# Ceratosauria

## Is the name Marsh's 1884 or his 1896?

*2026-10-16*

**Conclusion.** Marsh (1884), in *Nature*. He established the sub-order there for Ceratosauridae and *Ceratosaurus*, but the article prints it as "CERASTOSAURIA". That is a typesetting slip: the family on the same line is spelled correctly. A misprint corrected later is treated like an incorrect original spelling, which keeps the original author and date (compare ICZN Articles 32.5.1 and 19.2, though the Code does not govern unranked names), not like a deliberate emendation that credits the emender. Governs `erected_in` on [Ceratosauria](../../clades/Ceratosauria.yml).

**Evidence.**
- marsh1884c, p. 69: "(c) Sub-order CERASTOSAURIA.—(7) Family Ceratosauridæ.—Horn on skull. … Genus: Ceratosaurus."
- marsh1884a, the earlier *American Journal of Science* paper, names "a new family, which may be named the Ceratosauridæ" and erects the sub-orders Cœluria and Compsognatha, but gives the family no sub-order of its own.
- marsh1896a, p. 240: "Suborder Ceratosauria (Horned saurians)."
- carrano2008a credits "Ceratosauria *sensu* Marsh, 1884b", their 1884b being the *Nature* article.

**Ruled out.**
- *Marsh (1896) as the authority.* Ezcurra and Agnolín (2012) adopt it: "Marsh had coined the new taxon Cerastosauria (sic.) in 1884 but he emended this typographic mistake in Marsh (1896), in which the name Ceratosauria was used for the first time. Accordingly, we decided to use Marsh (1896) as the reference of the coining of Ceratosauria." A printer's error does not move the credit; the deliberate-emendation rule applied to Chaoyangsauridae does not extend to misprints.

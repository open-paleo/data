# Deinonychosauria

## Is Deinonychosauria kept as a node, and which paper is its `described_in`?

*2026-10-19*

**Conclusion.** It is kept as a node, with a dispute. Whether dromaeosaurids and troodontids form a clade to the exclusion of birds depends on the matrix. Analyses on the Theropod Working Group matrix and its descendants recover Deinonychosauria, with low support. Analyses on Cau's matrix put troodontids closer to birds, and Foth and colleagues (2025) put dromaeosaurids closer. The name is credited to Colbert and Russell (1969), who coined it for dromaeosaurids alone. The `described_in` is Sereno (1998), whose node-based definition is the one the analyses apply. Governs the node, `erected_in`, `described_in` and the dispute on [Deinonychosauria](../../clades/Deinonychosauria.yml).

**Evidence.**
- colbert1969a, p. 43: "It seems advisable to use a name for this infraorder based on the genus from the Cloverly Formation", for a group holding Dromaeosauridae alone.
- gauthier1986a, p. 11: Deinonychosauria Colbert and Russell, 1969, with included taxa "Troodontidae and Dromaeosauridae".
- sereno1998a, Table 3, p. 60: Deinonychosauria as a node-based taxon with reference taxa *Troodon* and *Velociraptor*; Table 4, p. 65, gives the definition.
- turner2012a, p. 124: "Deinonychosauria monophyly has significantly less support (GC = 3)"; p. 126: "collapsing the Deinonychosaurian node requires accepting trees only 1 step longer than the most parsimonious trees".
- pei2020a, p. 4037: group support "was generally very low within Paraves"; Fig. 1, p. 4036: Deinonychosauria with group support 40.
- freimuth2026a, p. 38: "As in the analysis of Pei et al., we recovered a monophyletic Deinonychosauria as sister to Avialae".
- hartman2019a, p. 32: "A Deinonychosauria including troodontids and dromaeosaurids was recovered as in many recent analyses. Positioning troodontids closer to Aves than dromaeosaurids only requires a single additional step".
- cau2021a, p. 16: trees with "the 'traditional Deinonychosauria', with troodontids being the sister group of dromaeosaurids" are 12 steps longer than the preferred trees.
- lee2022a, p. 6: "Deinonychosaurian monophyly is not supported by the strict consensus tree".
- foth2025a, Results: "Dromaeosauridae and not Troodontidae are now the sister group of Avialae".
- agnolín2019a, p. 25: the consensus tree "included a large polytomy at the base of Pennaraptora, encompassing Oviraptorosauria, Unenlagiidae, Microraptoria, Eudromaeosauria, Troodontidae, and Avialae".

**Ruled out.**
- *Collapsing the node, with Dromaeosauridae at Eumaniraptora and Troodontidae at Paraves.* This is the nearest parent that every hypothesis above accepts. The node is kept because the analyses on the most widely used paravian matrix recover it, and the dispute block records the alternatives.
- *Hartman and colleagues (2019) as a source for troodontids being closer to birds.* They recover Deinonychosauria. A move of troodontids toward birds is only an alternative they cost at one step.
- *`described_in: padian1999a`.* Padian and colleagues (1999, p. 73) give a stem-based definition, all maniraptorans closer to *Deinonychus* than to Neornithes. They credit it to an earlier work of their own, and the analyses cited here apply Sereno's definition instead.
- *`described_in: gauthier1986a`.* Gauthier lists the included taxa but gives no definition.

**Open.** Whether the matrices converge. The Foth and colleagues dataset has recovered all three arrangements across its versions: troodontids closer to birds in Foth and Rauhut (2017), Deinonychosauria in Rauhut and colleagues (2019), and dromaeosaurids closer to birds in Foth and colleagues (2025).

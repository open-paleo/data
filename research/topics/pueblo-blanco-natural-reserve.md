# Pueblo Blanco Natural Reserve

## Is the Pueblo Blanco Natural Reserve a different site from the Violante farm?

*2026-09-15*

**Conclusion.** No. It is the Violante farm under its later name. The papers describing *Taurovenator* (2016) and *Tralkasaurus* (2020) call the site the Violante farm; those describing *Chucarosaurus* (2023) and *Chakisaurus* (2024) call it the Pueblo Blanco Natural Reserve and gloss that as the former farm. All four print the same coordinate. The records use the current name, though the two older papers do not print it. Governs `location.locality: Pueblo Blanco Natural Reserve` and `location.coordinates` on [*Taurovenator violantei*](../../genera/T/Taurovenator.yml), [*Tralkasaurus cuyi*](../../genera/T/Tralkasaurus.yml), [*Chucarosaurus diripienda*](../../genera/C/Chucarosaurus.yml) and [*Chakisaurus nekul*](../../genera/C/Chakisaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- motta2016a, p. 231: "The Violante farm (39°23'52.37"S, 68°37'4.30"W) is located southeast of the Ezequiel Ramos-Mexía lake, at the northwest of Río Negro province".
- cerroni2020a, 3.1 Locality and horizon: "The material here described was found at the Violante farm (39°23'52.37"S, 68°37'4.30"W)".
- agnolín2023a, p. 2, Locality and horizon: "found at the Pueblo Blanco Natural Reserve (ex Violante farm; 39°23′52.37″ S, 68°37′4.30″ W)".
- nogueira2024a, p. 2, Introduction: "Huincul Formation beds outcropping at the Pueblo Blanco Natural Reserve (former Violante Farm), in northern Río Negro province".

**Ruled out.**
- *Two sites, or a reserve that contains the farm.* Both later papers give the reserve as the former farm, and all four papers print one position.

**Open.** Nothing.

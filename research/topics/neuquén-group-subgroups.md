## Why do eight Neuquén Group formations parent directly to the group, not to its subgroups?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** The Neuquén Group has three subgroups (Río Limay, Río Neuquén, Río Colorado), but no subgroup has a registry entry and no record names one, so eight formations take `parent: Neuquén`. Governs `parent` on [Bajo de la Carpa](../../stratigraphy/b/bajo-de-la-carpa.yml), [Candeleros](../../stratigraphy/c/candeleros.yml), [Portezuelo](../../stratigraphy/p/portezuelo.yml), [Huincul](../../stratigraphy/h/huincul.yml), [Anacleto](../../stratigraphy/a/anacleto.yml), [Plottier](../../stratigraphy/p/plottier.yml), [Cerro Lisandro](../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-lisandro.yml) and [Sierra Barrosa](../../stratigraphy/s/sierra-barrosa.yml), under [Neuquén](../../stratigraphy/n/neuquén.yml).

**Evidence.**
- garrido2010a, p. 121, abstract: "composed lithostratigraphically by three subgroups and nine formations".
- garrido2010a, p. 144: "Formación Los Bastos (nom. nov.)"; p. 146: "Formación Sierra Barrosa (nom. nov.)". These two bring the formation count to nine.

**Ruled out.**
- *A rank conflict for the Candeleros.* Calvo and Bonaparte (1991) place *Andesaurus* in the "Candeleros Member of the Río Limay Formation, Neuquén Group" (calvo1991a, Horizon and locality; read in English translation). That is the older scheme, in which the Río Limay was a formation with members; Garrido (2010) raises the members to formations and the former formations to subgroups. It is a superseded scheme, not a rival rank.

**Open.** If subgroup entries are ever added, these eight formations would move under them: Candeleros and Huincul under Río Limay; Cerro Lisandro, Portezuelo and Plottier under Río Neuquén; Bajo de la Carpa and Anacleto under Río Colorado. That allocation is the usual one in the literature and was not checked against Garrido (2010) for this note; Sierra Barrosa's subgroup was not checked at all.

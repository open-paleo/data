# Lance ceratopsian horizons in Niobrara County

## Do the published horizons of the Niobrara County ceratopsian types give them a `part` of the Lance Formation?

*2026-09-08*

**Conclusion.** Only for *Triceratops prorsus*, which Forster (1996) places "approximately at mid section" of the Niobrara County Lance. The other statements rank skulls against each other or describe the collecting ground, and the two that bear on *Triceratops horridus* disagree. Hatcher (1907) puts the *T. horridus* type "near the middle" of the fresh-water beds between the Fox Hills and the Fort Union, and two pages later "about the middle of the upper half" of the same deposits. Lull's (1915) level table puts it 31st of 32 skulls, second from the bottom of the collected sequence. For *Torosaurus latus*, Stanton's judgment, as the 1907 monograph reports it, that it is "the highest Ceratopsia specimen from this region" is made "from its geographical position", and Lull's table ranks it first of 32. Both rank it against other skulls and do not place it within the formation. Governs `location.part: middle` on [*Triceratops prorsus*](../../genera/T/Triceratops.yml), the absence of `location.part` on [*Triceratops horridus*](../../genera/T/Triceratops.yml), and the absence of `location.part` on [*Torosaurus latus*](../../genera/T/Torosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- forster1996a, p. 267: "In this region, YPM 1822 was found approximately at mid section, and BSP 1964 I 456 approximately two-thirds of the way up section."
- hatcher1907a, p. 117: the *T. horridus* skull "had weathered out of a stratum of soft, light, yellow, heavily bedded sandstone, about 20 feet thick, near the middle of that series of fresh-water sandstones and shales which in this region are interposed between the marine Fox Hills sandstones below and the leaf-bearing Fort Union beds above."
- hatcher1907a, p. 119: "The type of Triceratops horridus came from about the middle of the upper half of the Laramie deposits as they are represented in Converse County, Wyo."
- hatcher1907a, p. 180: "Stratigraphically T. horridus came 'from midway between the Fox Hills and Fort Union' of Converse County."
- hatcher1907a, p. 181: "From its geographical position Stanton believes T. latus to be the highest Ceratopsia specimen from this region."
- lull1915a, p. 343, Table 1: "High 1 | 19 | Y.M. | 1830 | Holo. | Torosaurus latus"; "31 | 1 | Y.M. | 1820 | Holo. | Triceratops horridus"; "32 Low | 9 | N.M. | 4720 | Holo. | Triceratops obtusus".
- lull1915a, p. 341: "with the exception of skulls Nos. 1 and 9 (Triceratops obtusus), which are the lowest in the series, most of the types come from the upper portion and are all quite near one another stratigraphically".
- hatcher1907a, p. 150: the *T. latus* type lay "near the summit of the bluff on the north side of Lightning Creek".

**Ruled out.**
- *`part: upper` for* Torosaurus latus. It rests on Stanton's ranking and Lull's "High 1". Both order the collected skulls against each other, and Stanton's is inferred from where the skull lay on the map. "Near the summit of the bluff" describes the outcrop. None of these places the skull in the upper part of the formation.
- *`part: middle` or `part: upper` for* Triceratops horridus. Hatcher's "near the middle" and "midway between the Fox Hills and Fort Union" support middle, and his "middle of the upper half" supports upper. Lull's table puts the skull near the base of the sampled sequence. Scannella and Horner (2010) repeat the "middle of the upper half" wording through Ostrom and Wellnhofer (1986), so it adds no second statement. With the primary statements disagreeing, no single position is adopted.

**Open.** Whether "the middle of the upper half" on p. 119 and "near the middle" on p. 117 measure the same thickness of beds. Hatcher's p. 119 sentence compares the skull with *Ceratops montanus* of the Judith River beds and may refer to the whole sequence above the marine beds. A measured section tying YPM 1820 to the Lance contacts would settle it.

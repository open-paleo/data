# Chinle and Dockum ranks

## Is the Chinle a group or a formation, and what rank do the units inside it and the Dockum take?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** The Chinle is a formation and the Dockum a group, so the Chinle's subdivisions are members and the Dockum's are formations. The rank of each child follows from its parent's, and the two parents are separate questions with opposite answers. The other scheme, from Lucas (1993) and its followers, makes the Chinle a group and the Dockum a single formation inside it, which makes the Chinle's members formations and the Dockum's formations members. Martz and Parker (2017) use the adopted combination in one list. Governs `rank: formation` on [Chinle](../../stratigraphy/c/chinle.yml); `rank: group` on [Dockum](../../stratigraphy/d/dockum.yml); `rank: member` with `parent: Chinle` on [Petrified Forest](../../stratigraphy/p/petrified-forest.yml), [Mesa Redondo](../../stratigraphy/m/mesa-redondo.yml) and [Rock Point](../../stratigraphy/r/rock-point.yml); and `rank: formation` with `parent: Dockum` on [Tecovas](../../stratigraphy/t/tecovas.yml), Colorado City, [Bull Canyon](../../stratigraphy/b/bull-canyon.yml) and [Cooper Canyon](../../stratigraphy/c/cooper-canyon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cather2013a, p. 110: Lucas "raised the Chinle to group rank, broadened its geographic extent, and raised numerous units within it to formation rank. Subsequent mapping has demonstrated, however, that many of these new formation-rank units are not viable map units and that the Chinle should be considered a formation, not a group, in much of New Mexico."
- cather2013a, p. 106: Lucas and colleagues "abandoned the Dolores Formation in Colorado and Dockum Group in eastern New Mexico, despite their unquestionable priority over the Chinle, and restricted the Dockum in Texas to the sole formation within the Chinle Group. Extension of the term Chinle to Colorado and Texas has not been widely accepted"; p. 107: "We designate all Upper Triassic strata east of 105.5° W in New Mexico as part of the Dockum Group".
- martz2017a, p. 112: the Otischalkian holochronozone "encompasses the Camp Springs Conglomerate, Santa Rosa Formation, Colorado City Member, the lowermost Cooper Canyon, the lowermost Tecovas Formation, and the lowermost Garita Creek Formation of the Dockum Group, and the Shinarump Member, Mesa Redondo Member, lowermost Cameron Member, and the lowermost Blue Mesa Member type section of the Chinle Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Chinle as a group.* Hunt and colleagues (1998) write that "All Upper Triassic nonmarine strata in the western United States have recently been united in the Chinle Group (Lucas 1993)", divided "into about 25 formations" (hunt1998a, p. 512). Cather and colleagues (2013) tested that scheme against mapping and rejected it (above).
- *The Dockum as a formation, with members.* The same scheme puts "Tecovas Member of Dockum Formation" and "Colorado City Member of Dockum Formation" inside the Chinle Group (hunt1998a, p. 512, Fig. 1 caption), and Lucas and colleagues write that "Upper Triassic strata in west Texas belong to the Dockum Formation of the Chinle Group consisting of five members" (lucas1995a, p. 5). It goes with the Chinle Group and fails with it, and Cather and colleagues restore the Dockum Group.
- *Hunt and colleagues' Chinle Group kept beside a Dockum Group.* Hunt and colleagues demote the Dockum in the same move that raises the Chinle, so taking their Chinle rank while keeping the Dockum a group gives a combination that none of the works cited here uses.
- *The Petrified Forest as a formation.* Hunt and colleagues write "Blue Mesa and Painted Desert Members of Petrified Forest Formation" (hunt1998a, p. 512, Fig. 1 caption). Martz and Parker write "Petrified Forest Member" and label formation rank as the other scheme's usage, "Petrified Forest Formation sensu Lucas et al., 2003, 2005" (martz2017a, p. 98).
- *The Rock Point as a formation.* Nesbitt and Sues name formation rank as Lucas and Hunt's house usage, "the Rock Point Member of that formation ('Rock Point Formation' in their nomenclature)" (nesbitt2020a, p. 152), and Cather and colleagues report the rename only to decline it (cather2013a, p. 110). Martz and colleagues (2013) are not a formation-rank source: their paper's only "Rock Point Formation" is in the title of Harris and Downs (2002) in its reference list (martz2013a, References). Martz and Parker (2017) write "Rock Point Member" (martz2017a, p. 112).

**Open.** Martz and Parker (2017) list the "Colorado City Member" among formations of the Dockum Group (p. 112), and Lehman and Chatterjee (2005) map the Otis Chalk beds into the upper Cooper Canyon; whether the Colorado City exists as a unit is a separate question, recorded on its entry. The Rock Point's age is in [Rock Point](../stratigraphy/r/rock-point.md), and the Bull Canyon and Cooper Canyon synonymy in [Bull Canyon and Cooper Canyon](bull-canyon-and-cooper-canyon.md).

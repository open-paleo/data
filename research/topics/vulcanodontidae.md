# Vulcanodontidae

## Is Vulcanodontidae a clade to recognize between Gravisauria and its two genera?

*2026-10-19*

**Conclusion.** No. Whether *Vulcanodon* and *Tazoudasaurus* are sister taxa depends on the matrix. The Allain, Pol and Wilson–Carballido datasets recover the pair, with low support. The Yates–McPhee lineage and several unrelated matrices recover the two as successive outgroups to Eusauropoda, or leave them in a polytomy. The two genera stay at the nearest node every analysis accepts, without a family node between. Governs `parent: Gravisauria` on [*Vulcanodon*](../../genera/V/Vulcanodon.yml) and [*Tazoudasaurus*](../../genera/T/Tazoudasaurus.yml), and the absence of a Vulcanodontidae node under [Gravisauria](../../clades/Gravisauria.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cooper1984a, p. 204: "Family VULCANODONTIDAE new family", with a diagnosis; Cooper would "include provisionally also the Indian Barapasaurus".
- allain2004a, p. 205: Cooper's grouping of *Vulcanodon* and *Barapasaurus* "is now widely considered as paraphyletic"; p. 206: "Vulcanodontidae is redefined here as all sauropods closer to Vulcanodon than to eusauropods".
- allain2008a, p. 404: "the monophyly of the stem based Vulcanodontidae (Allain et al. 2004) is confirmed here and supported by five unambiguous derived characters".
- läng2010a, Fig. 22: *Tazoudasaurus* and *Vulcanodon* as sister taxa, Bremer support 2, sister to Eusauropoda.
- holwerda2018a, p. 293: "Tazoudasaurus and Vulcanodon come out as sister-taxa, and more basal to Barapasaurus".
- pol2022a, Fig. 6: *Sanpasaurus* sister to *Vulcanodon* + *Tazoudasaurus* within Gravisauria; p. 152: "the position of Gongxianosaurus, Isanosaurus, and Vulcanodontidae outside Eusauropoda".
- remes2009a, Fig. 6A: *Vulcanodon*, *Cetiosaurus*, *Tazoudasaurus* and *Spinophorosaurus* as successive outgroups to Eusauropoda.
- nair2012a, p. 387: "Vulcanodon is the most basal divergence. This is followed successively by: (1) Tazoudasaurus + Spinophorosaurus"; p. 389: the result is "contrasting with Allain and Aquesbi (2008) who found a Tazoudasaurus-Vulcanodon clade".
- mcphee2014a, Results: "Isanosaurus is recovered as more derived than Vulcanodon, separating the latter from its 'vulcanodontid' (sensu Allain & Aquesbi, 2008) sister taxon, Tazoudasaurus".
- hu2026a, Fig. 12: *Vulcanodon*, *Isanosaurus* and *Tazoudasaurus* as successive branches below Eusauropoda.

**Ruled out.**
- *A Vulcanodontidae node holding Vulcanodon and Tazoudasaurus.* Jones (2026) classifies both genera in Vulcanodontidae within Gravisauria, but a reference work corroborates and does not decide. The analyses that recover the pair share a few matrix lineages and give it low support. Those built on other matrices, including the most recent analysis cited here (Hu and colleagues, 2026), do not recover it. If the node were adopted, its `erected_in` would be Cooper (1984), who named the family, and its `described_in` Allain and colleagues (2004), whose stem-based definition Allain and Aquesbi (2008) apply.
- *Cooper's Vulcanodontidae with Barapasaurus.* Allain and colleagues (2004) call that grouping paraphyletic, and none of the analyses above places *Barapasaurus* with *Vulcanodon*.

**Open.** Whether *Vulcanodon* belongs in Gravisauria at all. Allain and Aquesbi (2008) define Gravisauria from the common ancestor of *Tazoudasaurus* and *Saltasaurus*. Where *Vulcanodon* branches below *Tazoudasaurus*, as in Remes and colleagues (2009), Nair and Salisbury (2012), McPhee and colleagues (2014) and Hu and colleagues (2026), it falls outside the clade. McPhee and colleagues write that "This topology would require a redefinition of 'Gravisauria' sensu Allain & Aquesbi (2008) if Vulcanodon were to remain within that taxonomic unit" (mcphee2014a, Results).

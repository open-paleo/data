# Lias Group

## Do the Scunthorpe Mudstone and the Blue Lias belong to one Lias Group, and why does neither carry a parent?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Both are formations of the Lias Group as Cox and colleagues (1999) define it, the Scunthorpe Mudstone replacing the Blue Lias in the northern East Midlands Shelf. "Lower Lias Group" is not a second unit: "Lower Lias" is an older informal name that Cox and colleagues list under previous nomenclature. The registry has no Lias Group entry, so neither formation sets a `parent`. Governs the absence of `parent` on [Scunthorpe Mudstone](../../stratigraphy/s/scunthorpe-mudstone.yml) and [Blue Lias](../../stratigraphy/b/blue-lias.yml), and bears on `location.formation` of [*Sarcosaurus woodi*](../../genera/S/Sarcosaurus.yml), [*Dracoraptor hanigani*](../../genera/D/Dracoraptor.yml) and [*Dornraptor normani*](../../genera/D/Dornraptor.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cox1999a, p. 2: "in the northern part of the East Midlands Shelf, the Blue Lias Formation is replaced by the Scunthorpe Mudstone Formation"; p. 4: "The Blue Lias Formation, at the base of the Lias Group, is widespread"; §5.4 Blue Lias Formation and §5.11 Scunthorpe Mudstone Formation each give "Name of next higher ranking unit: Lias Group".
- cox1999a, §5.10 Redcar Mudstone Formation: "Previous nomenclature: Lower Lias".
- ezcurra2020a, online-first p. 6: the *Sarcosaurus* horizon in the "Scunthorpe Mudstone Formation, Lias Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *"Lias Group" and "Lower Lias Group" as two units.* Baron gives "Blue Lias Formation, Lower Lias Group, Charmouth, UK" (baron2024a, p. 6). Cox and colleagues, who define the group, treat "Lower Lias" as an earlier name, not a group.

**Open.** Whether to add a Lias Group entry and set it as the parent of both formations. Nothing in the sources argues against the containment.

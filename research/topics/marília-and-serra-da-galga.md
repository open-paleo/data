# Marília and Serra da Galga formations

## Does Soares and colleagues' "between the Santonian and the Maastrichtian" widen the Marília?

*2026-10-21*

**Conclusion.** No. The paper that defines the Marília reaches the range by tying its deposition to regional uplift and to volcanism dated 80 to 60 million years, not by dating the formation, and its Marília is the broad unit that still included the Serra da Galga. The 2026-10-01 entry stands. Governs `stages: [Maastrichtian]` on [Marília](../../stratigraphy/m/marília.yml).

**Evidence.**
- soares1980a, p. 184: "The sedimentation of this Formation was contemporaneous with the uplift of the northern part of the Serra do Mar and of the Canastra arch (Soares and Landim, 1975) or of the Alto Parnaíba (Saad et al., 1971), and with the associated volcanism, with ages between 80 and 60 million years. Thus, the sedimentation of the Marília Formation took place between the Santonian and the Maastrichtian" (translated).
- soares1980a, p. 184: the base of the unit is dated by a fauna "similar to that of the Adamantina Formation", including titanosaurid remains at Colina "to which a Senonian age was attributed" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Santonian or Campanian for the Marília.* An age for uplift and volcanism elsewhere is the age of other events, and CONTRIBUTING does not let a correlation date a unit. The 80 to 60 Ma figures are relayed without a method. The faunal Senonian is attributed in the passive, with no author, and dates only the lower part of the broad Marília.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does the Marília ruling govern *Ypupiara*?

*2026-10-10*

**Conclusion.** Yes. *Ypupiara* comes from Peirópolis, Uberaba, one of the northeastern localities the 2026-10-01 entry assigns to the Serra da Galga Formation, so its describing paper's "Marília Formation" is the Serra da Galga. Governs `location.formation: Serra da Galga` on [*Ypupiara lopai*](../../genera/Y/Ypupiara.yml), which the 2026-10-01 entry did not list.

**Evidence.**
- brum2021a, Locality and horizon: "The specimen was found at the Caiera site, also known as Ponto 1 do Price, in Serra do Veadinho, close to Peiropolis, a rural district of Uberaba … In this locality, the Marılia Formation crops out (Maastrichtian".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Marília Formation in its restricted sense.* That name now covers only the southeastern and northwestern outcrops.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which unit is "Marília Formation" in a given paper, and how old are the Marília and Serra da Galga formations?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Since Soares and colleagues (2020), the northeastern deposits of the former Marília Formation (Uberaba, Peirópolis, Triângulo Mineiro) are the Serra da Galga Formation, and "Marília Formation" means only the southeastern and northwestern outcrops (Monte Alto, Echaporã, Marília). A paper's sense of "Marília" is read from its locality, not its formation name. Both formations are Maastrichtian. Governs `stages: [Maastrichtian]` on [Marília](../../stratigraphy/m/marília.yml) and [Serra da Galga](../../stratigraphy/s/serra-da-galga.yml), and `location.formation` on species from the northeastern localities, such as [*Baurutitan britoi*](../../genera/B/Baurutitan.yml), [*Trigonosaurus pricei*](../../genera/T/Trigonosaurus.yml) and [*Uberabatitan ribeiroi*](../../genera/U/Uberabatitan.yml).

**Evidence.**
- soares2020a, p. 953 (early-view version, p. 3): "the use of the term Marília Formation is here restricted to the southeastern and northwestern parts of the Bauru Basin, traditionally considered as the Echaporã Member".
- iori2021a, p. 2: Soares and colleagues "unified the Serra da Galga and Ponte Alta members as the Serra da Galga Formation".
- arai2023a, p. 2, English abstract: "a Maastrichtian (late Maastrichtian?) age for the Marília and Serra da Galga formations". Only the Serra da Galga is dated directly, by charophytes and ostracods; the Marília's age follows from "the stratigraphic-spatial arrangement". The "(late Maastrichtian?)" appears only in the English abstract; the Portuguese abstract (p. 1) gives Maastrichtian alone.
- dias-brito2001a, p. 246: a "Maastrichtian (probably Late Maastrichtian)" age from a Ponte Alta sample, now in the Serra da Galga Formation.
- menegazzo2016a, p. 138: "Maastrichtian age is indicated for the Marília Formation", in the broad sense.

**Ruled out.**
- *Campanian–Maastrichtian.* The Campanian came from *Aeolosaurus* biochronology: Santucci and Bertini (2001) take it to indicate "a Late Campanian/Early Maastrichtian age" (santucci2001a, p. 307), and Bertini and colleagues (2001) give "Upper Campanian/Lower Maastrichtian age for the Echaporã and Serra da Galga members" (bertini2001a, p. 93). Martinelli and colleagues (2011) find "no characters indicating the presence of this Patagonian genus in the Late Cretaceous of Brazil" (martinelli2011a, p. 34), which removes that basis.
- *"Almost consensually" Campanian–Maastrichtian.* Silva Junior and colleagues (2022) write that the unit is "almost consensually accepted as of Campanian-Maastrichtian age (see Soares et al. 2021)" (silvajunior2022b, p. 408). Soares and colleagues give the basin's span, "from the Coniacian to the Maastrichtian", not a stage for this unit.
- *Ostracod Campanian–Maastrichtian.* Batezelli (2015) gives "Adamantina and Marília formations … with Campanian-Maastrichtian ages" (batezelli2015a, accepted article, p. 8), a joint range for two units, relayed from earlier ostracod work.
- *"Late Maastrichtian" from Cavalcanti and colleagues (2026).* The phrase is in a cited title in their reference list (cavalcanti2026a, p. 13); their own text gives "Upper Cretaceous (Maastrichtian)" (p. 6).
- *The broad Marília as current usage.* Before Soares and colleagues (2020), every source uses the three-member Marília: "subdividida nos membros Echaporã, Serra da Galga e Ponte Alta" ("subdivided into the Echaporã, Serra da Galga and Ponte Alta members", translated; fernandes2000a, p. 717, after Barcelos and Suguio 1987). Comparative papers still use it: "Serra da Galga Member, Marília Formation" (santucci2022a, p. 357). Since 2021 the Brazilian specialists write Serra da Galga Formation (silvajunior2022b, p. 408; iori2021a, p. 2; cavalcanti2026a, p. 6).

**Open.** The Adamantina below is late Santonian–early Campanian by inference, not by its own palynology (arai2023a, p. 2: palynology dates the São Carlos and Araçatuba). The pre-Marília hiatus spans at most "the mid-Campanian to early Maastrichtian" (arai2023a, p. 2).

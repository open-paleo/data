# Qinlingosaurus and Shanyangosaurus ages

## Do Qinlingosaurus and Shanyangosaurus have a stage?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** No. The naming paper gives Late Cretaceous for both and no stage; the stages in later compilations do not trace to a source that dates either type horizon. Governs `period: [Late Cretaceous]` with no `stage` on [*Qinlingosaurus luonanensis*](../../genera/Q/Qinlingosaurus.yml) and [*Shanyangosaurus niupanggouensis*](../../genera/S/Shanyangosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- xue1996a, p. 79: *Qinlingosaurus* from "陕西洛南红土岭，上白垩统红土岭组" ("Hongtuling, Luonan, Shaanxi; Upper Cretaceous Hongtuling Formation", translated).
- xue1996a, p. 76: *Shanyangosaurus* from "陕西山阳牛膀沟，晚白垩世" ("Niupanggou, Shanyang, Shaanxi; Late Cretaceous", translated), in the "上白垩统山阳组上段" ("upper member of the Upper Cretaceous Shanyang Formation", translated).
- weishampel2004b, p. 598: "Age: Late Cretaceous (Xue et al. 1996)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian.* Holtz and colleagues (2004) give "the mid- to late Maastrichtian Shanyang Formation … (Xue et al. 1996)" (holtz2004a, p. 108), citing a paper that gives no stage; their own Table 4.1 (p. 77) says Late Cretaceous. Lucas and Estep (1998) give Maastrichtian in a table of dinosaur egg localities "after Carpenter and Alf, 1994" (lucas1998a, p. 14, Table 1), which dates eggs, not these skeletons. That the Maastrichtian in later records came through Holtz and colleagues is an inference.
- *Campanian.* Horner and colleagues (2004, horner2004a, p. 441, Table 20.1) give Campanian for *Shantungosaurus giganteus* across the Wangshi Group and the Shanyang Formation; reading it as an age for the Shanyang taxa is an over-reading.
- *Early Cretaceous.* Zhao (2008) lists both the Hongtuling and the Shanyang formations as Early Cretaceous (zhao2008a, pp. 285, 307), against the naming paper's Upper Cretaceous.
- *Qinlingosaurus in the Shanyang Formation.* Weishampel and colleagues (2004, weishampel2004b, p. 598) list it in the Shanyang fauna; Xue and colleagues place it in the Hongtuling Formation at Luonan.

**Open.** The units themselves are less settled than the genera. The *Stratigraphic Lexicon of China* gives the Shanyang Formation as "Cretaceous-Paleocene" (zhang2009b, p. 971) and the Hongtuling Formation at Luonan as Eocene (p. 410). Neither entry dates the dinosaur-bearing levels.

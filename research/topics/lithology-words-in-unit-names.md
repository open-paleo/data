# Lithology words in unit names

## When papers print a unit both with a lithology word and with a rank word, which name does the registry use?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** The form with the lithology word. The registry drops the rank word and keeps any lithology word, so "Niobrara Chalk" and the hybrid "Niobrara Chalk Formation" both give `Niobrara Chalk`, and only "Niobrara Formation" gives a bare `Niobrara`. Where a paper prints the geographic name with a lithology word, that word is kept. For the Arén it also separates the unit from the town of Arén, which records name in their localities, and for the Poison Strip it is the form of the naming paper. Governs `name: Niobrara Chalk` on [Niobrara Chalk](../../stratigraphy/n/niobrara-chalk.yml), and `parent: Niobrara Chalk` on [Smoky Hill Chalk](../../stratigraphy/s/smoky-hill-chalk.yml); `name: Arén Sandstone` on [Arén Sandstone](../../stratigraphy/a/arén-sandstone.yml); `name: Poison Strip Sandstone` on [Poison Strip Sandstone](../../stratigraphy/p/poison-strip-sandstone.yml); and `location.formation: Niobrara Chalk` on [*Claosaurus agilis*](../../genera/C/Claosaurus.yml), [*Hierosaurus sternbergii*](../../genera/H/Hierosaurus.yml) and [*Niobrarasaurus coleii*](../../genera/N/Niobrarasaurus.yml), `location.formation: Arén Sandstone` on [*Blasisaurus canudoi*](../../genera/B/Blasisaurus.yml), and `location.member: Poison Strip Sandstone` on [*Cedrorestes crichtoni*](../../genera/C/Cedrorestes.yml), [*Planicoxa venenica*](../../genera/P/Planicoxa.yml) and [*Venenosaurus dicrocei*](../../genera/V/Venenosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- kirkland1997a, p. 77: "We propose the Poison Strip Sandstone as the official designation for the middle sandstone unit of Young (1960) at the top of the Yellow Cat Member in eastern Utah".
- csiki-sava2015a, p. 47: "the shallow marine-deltaic Arén Sandstone and the transitional-to-continental Tremp Formation".
- clarke2004a, p. 5: "the Late Cretaceous Niobrara Chalk of Kansas"; carpenter1995a, title: "The Dinosaurs of the Niobrara Chalk Formation (Upper Cretaceous, Kansas)".

**Ruled out.**
- *A bare "Niobrara".* Papers also write "Niobrara Formation", sometimes beside the lithic form in the same work (clarke2004a, p. 30: "Smoky Hill Chalk Member of the Niobrara Formation"). That is the same unit with the rank word in place of the lithology word, not a different name, and the lithology word is kept where papers print it.
- *"Niobrara Chalk Formation" as a fuller name.* Carpenter and colleagues (1995) and Weishampel and colleagues (2004) print the lithic word and the rank word together. Dropping the rank word gives the adopted name, so the hybrid is not a rival reading.
- *A bare "Arén".* Cruzado-Caballero and colleagues write "the upper part of the Arén Formation" (cruzado-caballero2010a, p. 1507). The same abstract places the Blasi 1 locality "of Arén", the town, which *Blasisaurus* and *Arenysaurus* carry in their localities.
- *A bare "Poison Strip", or "Poison Strip Member".* Later papers write "Poison Strip Member" (sprinkel2012a, p. 2: "Aptian for the Poison Strip Member"); Kirkland and colleagues named the unit as the Poison Strip Sandstone and use that form for it.

**Open.** The Demopolis. Carr and colleagues (2005) write "Demopolis Formation" and once "Demopolis Chalk"; the registry has `name: Demopolis` with `variants: [Demopolis Chalk]` on [Demopolis](../../stratigraphy/d/demopolis.yml). By the reading above the lithic form would be the name, and a lithology-word form of the same name is not a variant under the registry's rule on variants. Which way the Demopolis goes is undecided, and so is whether a single printing of a lithic form is enough to make it the name.

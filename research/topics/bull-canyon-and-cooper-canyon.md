# Bull Canyon and Cooper Canyon

## Are the Bull Canyon and Cooper Canyon formations synonyms?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** No, after Martz (2008): the Bull Canyon of New Mexico correlates only with the upper part of the Texas Cooper Canyon. Texas localities take Cooper Canyon and New Mexico localities take Bull Canyon (our working rule). Governs keeping [Bull Canyon](../../stratigraphy/b/bull-canyon.yml) and [Cooper Canyon](../../stratigraphy/c/cooper-canyon.yml) separate, and `location.formation` on [*Gojirasaurus quayi*](../../genera/G/Gojirasaurus.yml) (Bull Canyon, New Mexico).

**Evidence.**
- martz2013a, p. 342: "Lehman et al. (1992) identified a sandstone unit at the base of the Cooper Canyon Formation as the Trujillo Sandstone (… the 'Boren Ranch sandstone' of Frehlier 1986)"; "the Bull Canyon Formation is correlative only with the upper part of the Cooper Canyon Formation type section … and the Tecovas Formation is correlative with the lower part", the middle sandstones being "roughly correlative with the Trujillo Formation".
- martz2017a, pp. 102, 112: "to the Trujillo and middle Cooper Canyon Formations"; "the upper Cooper Canyon Formation, the Bull Canyon Formation".
- cather2013a, p. 108: "The extension of the term Cooper Canyon to southeastern New Mexico was later realized to be the result of a miscorrelation in Texas (Martz, 2008…)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Synonymy with Cooper Canyon priority.* Before Martz, "the Cooper Formation and Bull Canyon Formation are laterally equivalent and, following LEHMAN (1992), … Cooper Canyon had priority" (carpenter1997a, p. 203); Nesbitt and colleagues wrote "Bull Canyon (=Cooper Canyon) Formation in Quay County, New Mexico" (nesbitt2007a, p. 221). Both rest on the miscorrelated Boren Ranch sandstone.
- *Cooper Canyon for New Mexico Gojirasaurus.* Paul (2024) gives "New Mexico; Cooper Canyon" (paul2024a, p. 91).

**Open.** Rank and scope in later work: Nesbitt and colleagues (2015, nesbitt2015a, p. 515) adopt the correlation at member rank within a Dockum Formation, and Rinehart and colleagues still place the Texas Post Quarry "in the Revueltian Bull Canyon Formation" (rinehart2009a, p. 33).

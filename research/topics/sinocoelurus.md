# *Sinocoelurus*

## Is *Sinocoelurus* a dinosaur?

*2026-10-17*

**Conclusion.** Not demonstrably; the genus record is removed and *Sinocoelurus* is listed in [excluded.yml](../../excluded.yml) as non-dinosaurian. Wu and colleagues (2009) examined the four teeth and found them most probably plesiosaurian, and Rozhdestvensky (1975) had earlier proposed a crocodilian affinity; no later work defends a dinosaurian identity. The earlier treatments call it a theropod nomen dubium on general tooth form. The entry below governed the removed record's type specimen and now governs nothing.

**Evidence.**
- wu2009a: "*Sinocoelurus fragilis* is represented by four isolated teeth (IVPP V232-235) … Our examination shows that the vertically-striated teeth are slender and curved, and most probably belong to a plesiosaur."
- dong1983a: "Steel (1970) attributed this dentition to the Coeluridae although Rozhdestvesky (1975) proposed a crocodilian affinity."
- young1942a, the diagnosis: "Teeth long and slender. Compression and curvature moderate. Both anterior and posterior sides ridged but no trace of any serrations, enamel very thin."

**Ruled out.**
- *A theropod nomen dubium at Tetanurae incertae sedis.* The removed record's placement; no paper read argues for Tetanurae, and the latest first-hand examination finds the teeth not dinosaurian.

**Open.** A second study of the teeth confirming or rejecting the plesiosaur identity.

## How many teeth and numbers make up the Sinocoelurus type material?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** Four teeth catalogued under three numbers, V232–234; no holotype was designated, so they are syntypes (our reading). Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [IVPP V232, IVPP V233, IVPP V234]` and `specimen_type: syntype` on *Sinocoelurus fragilis*.

**Evidence.**
- young1942a, p. 297: "Four isolated teeth, V232-234 (Pl. I, Figs. 6-8)"; p. 298 gives four sets of dimensions and identifies the worn tooth as "(V232) which is worn".
- young1942a, pp. 300–301: Young uses the same convention for *Szechuanosaurus*: "One broken tooth, V235; another broken tooth, V236; three fragmentary teeth, V238 and one nearly complete tooth, V239", "the six teeth".

**Ruled out.**
- *Four numbers, V232–V235.* Dong and colleagues list "Four isolated teeth (V232, V233, V234, V235)" (dong1983a, English translation, p. 57) and call the damaged tooth V233 (p. 58); V235 is a *Szechuanosaurus* number, and Young names the worn tooth V232.

**Open.** Nothing.

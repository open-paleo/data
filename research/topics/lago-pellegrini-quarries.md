## Which formation are the Lago Pellegrini quarry dinosaurs from, and what stage does that give them?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** The formation is disputed between the Anacleto (early Campanian) and the lower Allen (late Campanian–early Maastrichtian). Both readings include the Campanian, so a Maastrichtian-only stage is wrong either way. Governs `stage` on [*Abelisaurus comahuensis*](../../genera/A/Abelisaurus.yml) (MPCA-PV 11098) and [*Pellegrinisaurus powelli*](../../genera/P/Pellegrinisaurus.yml) (MPCA-PV 1500): it must include the Campanian. It does not settle `location.formation`.

**Evidence.**
- bonaparte1985b (read in English translation, without the original folios): *Abelisaurus* from the "Lower part of the Allen Formation, Lago Pellegrini stone quarries", "early Maastrichtian".
- leanza2004a, pp. 79–80: "the tetrapod taxa found around Cinco Saltos and the Lago Pellegrini area belong to the Anacleto Formation and not to the Allen Formation (see Heredia and Salgado, 1999)"; p. 72: *Abelisaurus* "is now considered to come from the Anacleto Formation".
- carrano2008a, p. 190: "Lago Pellegrini stone quarries…; Anacleto Formation…; early-middle Campanian (Bonaparte & Novas 1985; Heredia & Salgado 1999; Dingus et al. 2000)".
- santucci2022a, p. 364: *Pellegrinisaurus* from "Lago Pellegrini… Anacleto Formation (lower-middle Campanian)".
- cerda2021a, p. 3: "Given that this last change… has not been to date formally proposed in a publication, we follow… Heredia and Salgado (1999)", declining a 2017 conference abstract that returns the quarry beds to the Allen; the Allen is given as "upper Campanian–lower Maastrichtian".
- gianechini2015a, pp. 1–2: *Abelisaurus* "has been considered as belonging to the Anacleto Formation after the inclusion of all dinosaur bearing deposits of the Lago Pellegrini to that unit", but "it is locally known that the specimen actually comes from the Allen Formation at the Sr. Fernandez field, close to Salitral Moreno"; "Since no witnesses are alive and no field notes were taken, more information is necessary".
- aranciagarolando2021a, p. 5 of the pre-proof: "a recent communication referred *Abelisaurus* to the lower member of the Allen Formation".
- dingus2000a, p. 8: palynology reported by Heredia and Salgado (1999) gives a "middle Campanian age… for strata from near the middle of the Allen Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Maastrichtian only.* This is Bonaparte and Novas's (1985) age for the lower Allen. Later dating of the Allen reaches back into the Campanian (dingus2000a, p. 8; cerda2021a, p. 3), and the Anacleto alternative is wholly Campanian, so no reading of either formation confines the quarry to the Maastrichtian.
- *Dingus and colleagues (2000) as a source for the quarry's formation.* They date the Anacleto at Auca Mahuevo ("C33R of the early or early-middle Campanian", dingus2000a, p. 10) and never discuss Lago Pellegrini. Carrano and Sampson (2008, p. 190) pair the citation with the quarry.
- *The genuine Allen localities.* Taxa from Salitral Ojo de Agua, Salitral Moreno and Bajo de Santa Rosa are Allen Formation on any reading and are not affected by this dispute.

**Open.** Which formation. Heredia and Salgado (1999) and the 2017 abstract that disputes them have not been read for this note; either would show what evidence each side has for the quarry beds. Gianechini and colleagues (2015) also raise a separate question for *Abelisaurus* alone: whether it came from Lago Pellegrini at all. Their source is local testimony with no field notes.

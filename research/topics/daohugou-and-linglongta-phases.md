# Daohugou and Linglongta phases

## Are the Daohugou paravians Haifanggou (Bathonian–Callovian) or Tiaojishan (Oxfordian)?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Haifanggou Formation, Bathonian–Callovian. The Yanliao Biota has an older Daohugou phase (Haifanggou, also called Jiulongshan) and a younger Linglongta phase (Tiaojishan, also called Lanqi); the Daohugou beds belong to the first. Governs `location.formation: Haifanggou` and `stage: [Bathonian, Callovian]` on [*Pedopenna daohugouensis*](../../genera/P/Pedopenna.yml) and [*Epidexipteryx hui*](../../genera/E/Epidexipteryx.yml), and the [Haifanggou](../../stratigraphy/h/haifanggou.yml) and [Tiaojishan](../../stratigraphy/t/tiaojishan.yml) ranges.

**Evidence.**
- xu2016b, p. 2229: "the Bathonian-Callovian Daohugou phase (about 168-164 million years ago) and the Oxfordian Linglongta phase (164-159 million years ago)"; p. 2231: the Haifanggou is "partially Bathonian but mainly Callovian".
- xu2016b, p. 2231: "A 206Pb/238U SHRIMP age of 165.0 ±1.2 Ma was obtained based on samples collected from the fossil-bearing layers (Yang and Li, 2008)"; the Tiaojishan's base gives "161.8±0.4 Ma based on samples from the basal portion of the formation (Chang et al., 2009; Chang et al., 2013)", from the basal Lanqi in western Liaoning.
- sullivan2014a, p. 246: the succession "comprises in ascending order the Jiulongshan (Haifanggou) Formation, the Tiaojishan (Lanqi) Formation", after Liu and colleagues (2006).

**Ruled out.**
- *Tiaojishan Formation, Oxfordian, for the Daohugou beds.* This is a published position, not a slip: Sullivan and colleagues (2014) say the beds "belong at least partly to the regionally widespread Tiaojishan Formation" (sullivan2014a, p. 243) and are "entirely referable to the upper Jiulongshan or lower Tiaojishan formation" (p. 248), following Liu and colleagues (2006, 2012) and Peng and colleagues (2012). Xu and colleagues (2016) assign them to the Haifanggou on the 165.0 Ma date from the fossil beds, which is older than the Tiaojishan's base. Rauhut and colleagues (2016) take the formation from Sullivan and colleagues and the Oxfordian from dates on the *Anchiornis* beds ("we consider the fossils from the Tiaojishan Formation to be Oxfordian in age", rauhut2016a, p. 43). Jones (2026) gives Oxfordian for every Yanliao paravian in its table, including "Haifanggou Fm., Late Jurassic, Oxfordian" for *Ambopteryx* (jones2026c, p. 508).
- *A Daohugou Formation.* Turner and colleagues (2012) write "the Middle to Late Jurassic Daohugou Formation" (turner2012a, p. 89); the name was "informally termed the 'Daohugou Formation' (Zhang, 2002), but this usage has received little acceptance" (xu2016b, p. 2231). Xu and Zhang (2005) name no formation: "Daohugou beds" (xu2005a, p. 174).

**Open.** The Daohugou beds' exact placement near the Haifanggou–Tiaojishan contact, which Sullivan and colleagues leave open. Xu and colleagues (2016, p. 2232) note scansoriopterygids and anchiornithines "occur in both" phases, so a taxon's affinity does not place it.

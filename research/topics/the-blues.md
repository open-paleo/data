# The Blues

## Is "The Blues" one site for the *Hagryphus*, *Talos* and *Teratophoneus* holotypes?

*2026-09-08*

**Conclusion.** No. "The Blues" is an area of Kaiparowits Formation exposures in Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monument, and each paper places only its own holotype somewhere in it. It stands as the locality because no paper names a finer site. For *Teratophoneus* the name is not in the describing paper, which gives only the monument: Loewen and colleagues (2013) take it, with the middle-unit horizon, from a 1981 newspaper account, and state that exact locality data for the holotype are not available. Governs `location.locality: The Blues` on [*Hagryphus giganteus*](../../genera/H/Hagryphus.yml), [*Talos sampsoni*](../../genera/T/Talos.yml) and [*Teratophoneus curriei*](../../genera/T/Teratophoneus.yml), and `location.part: middle` on *Teratophoneus curriei*.

**Evidence.**
- zanno2005a, p. 898: "The specimen was recovered just south of Powell Point, in an area of the monument known as 'The Blues'".
- zanno2011a, p. 3, Type Horizon, Locality, and Age: "UMNH VP 19479 was collected in Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monument from a region known as ''The Blues,'' within the northern portion of the Kaiparowits Plateau".
- carr2011a, p. 244, Locality and horizon: the holotype "was discovered in the upper Campanian Kaiparowits Formation, in what is now the Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monument of Utah".
- loewen2013a, pp. 4–5, Locality, horizon and age: "Exact locality data for the holotype BYU 8120 are not available; however, a contemporary newspaper article from 1981 describing the location of the site places the specimen in the middle member of the Kaiparowits Formation in 'The Blues' exposures northeast of Henrieville, Utah".

**Ruled out.**
- *Horse Mountain, or UMNH VP Locality 597, for Teratophoneus.* These are where the referred specimens UMNH VP 16690 and 16691 were found (loewen2013a, p. 5), not the holotype.

**Open.** Where in The Blues each holotype lies. The collecting records of the Natural History Museum of Utah (*Hagryphus*, *Talos*) and of Brigham Young University (*Teratophoneus*) would settle it.

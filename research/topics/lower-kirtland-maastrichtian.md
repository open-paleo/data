# Maastrichtian in the lower Kirtland members

## Does the 2026-11-25 ruling on the De-na-zin stand?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No, for the De-na-zin; it stands for the Farmington. Supersedes the 2026-11-25 entry on the De-na-zin. That entry re-placed Newman's (1987) early Maastrichtian by his definition of the stage from the *Baculites reesidei* zone; under CONTRIBUTING a stage the author names is carried over, and only a bare number is placed on the current chart, so Newman's stage stands for the upper shale member (see [De-na-zin](../stratigraphy/d/de-na-zin.md)). The same reading keeps Hunt and Lucas's (1992) "Edmontonian (early Maastrichtian)" for the pre-Naashoibito Kirtland (see [Kirtland](../stratigraphy/k/kirtland.md)). Governs nothing new beyond those entries.

**Evidence.**
- newman1987a, p. 159: "the upper shale member of the Kirtland is most likely early Maastrichtian in age, based on stratigraphic position alone".

**Ruled out.**
- *Re-placing a named stage by the author's boundary definition.*

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does Newman's (1987) early Maastrichtian put the Farmington or the De-na-zin in the Maastrichtian?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** No. Supersedes the Open line of the 2026-10-21 entry. Newman (1987) defines the lower Maastrichtian by the ammonite zones from *Baculites reesidei* to *B. clinolobatus*, and dates the Farmington and the upper shale member against that definition: at Durango, Colorado, from palynomorphs, and in the southwestern San Juan Basin from stratigraphic position alone. The GTS2020 Cretaceous chart places the *B. reesidei* and *B. jenseni* zones below the 72.17 Ma base of the Maastrichtian, so Newman's early Maastrichtian takes in what is now late Campanian, and none of his evidence places any part of either member above that base. Newman never names the De-na-zin; that his "upper shale member" is the De-na-zin is inferred from his listing it between the Farmington and the Naashoibito, the bounds Hunt and Lucas (1992) give the member. Wiersma and Irmis's (2018) "lower-most Maastrichtian" comes with dates of 73.04 and 73.49 Ma, both Campanian on the ICS chart v2024/12, and no other basis. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` on [De-na-zin](../../stratigraphy/d/de-na-zin.yml), which drops the Maastrichtian, and on [Farmington](../../stratigraphy/f/farmington.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- newman1987a, p. 160: "Lower Maastrichtian strata (defined by coexisting zones of ammonites Baculites reesidei to Baculites clinolobatus".
- newman1987a, p. 158: "the entire Farmington Sandstone through McDermott Formation interval is interpreted to be early Maastrichtian in age".
- newman1987a, p. 159: "includes the Farmington Sandstone, upper shale, and Naashoibito members in its upper part"; "the upper shale member of the Kirtland is most likely early Maastrichtian in age, based on stratigraphic position alone".
- gale2020a, Fig. 27.9, p. 1044: the Western Interior ammonoid column sets *Baculites reesidei* and *B. jenseni* below the 72.17 Ma base of the Maastrichtian.
- wiersma2018a, p. 65, Discussion: "lower-most Maastrichtian (73.04 ± 0.25 Ma (Fassett & Steiner, 1997); 73.49 ± 0.25 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian for the Farmington or the De-na-zin from Newman.* His Maastrichtian starts at the *B. reesidei* zone, which the current chart places in the late Campanian. The stage name changed with the chart; the palynomorphs and the stratigraphic position did not.
- *Maastrichtian for the De-na-zin from Wiersma and Irmis.* The label comes with two dates that are Campanian on the current chart and no other evidence.

**Open.** Whether the top of the De-na-zin reaches the Maastrichtian. A date from the upper part of the member would settle it.

## Does Hunt and Lucas's "Edmontonian (early Maastrichtian)" put the lower Kirtland members in the Maastrichtian?

*2026-10-21*

**Conclusion.** No. Hunt and Lucas (1992) date the four members below the Naashoibito together, and their Maastrichtian rests on a Campanian–Maastrichtian boundary at 74.0 Ma and on ash dates "consistent with an age of 73.5 ± 2.2 Ma". On the ICS chart v2024/12 the Maastrichtian begins at 72.2 Ma, so 73.5 Ma is late Campanian and the Maastrichtian label belongs to the 1989 chart. Fassett and Steiner's (1997) ashes in the upper Farmington, 73.37 and 73.04 Ma, are also Campanian on the current chart. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` on [Hunter Wash](../../stratigraphy/h/hunter-wash.yml) and [Farmington](../../stratigraphy/f/farmington.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- hunt1992a, p. 234: "Brookins and Rigby (1987) reviewed the age determinations from these ashes and concluded that they are all consistent with an age of 73.5 ± 2.2 Ma. The Campanian-Maastrichtian boundary is currently placed at 74.0 Ma (Harland et al., 1990)."
- hunt1992a, p. 234: "radiometric dates, palynology and the conformable position between Judithian and Lancian strata suggest that the lower Kirtland is Edmontonian (early Maastrichtian) in age."
- fassett1997a, p. 243: ash H, "in the upper part of the Farmington Sandstone Member of the Kirtland Shale", at 73.37 ± 0.18 Ma; ash J, "very near the top of the Farmington Member", at 73.04 ± 0.25 Ma.

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian for the Hunter Wash and Farmington from Hunt and Lucas.* The stage comes from the older boundary, not from evidence that differs from the Campanian dates. CONTRIBUTING's rule on reading ages against the chart a paper used takes the number over the older stage.

**Open.** Newman's (1987) palynological "early Maastrichtian" for "a portion of the upper Farmington Member, the De-na-zin Member and the Naashoibito Member", reported by Hunt and Lucas (1992, p. 233), has not been read. A palynological stage does not depend on a numeric boundary in the same way, so reading Newman would show whether it adds the Maastrichtian to the upper Farmington and supports it on the [De-na-zin](../../stratigraphy/d/de-na-zin.yml). The De-na-zin's own Maastrichtian rests on Wiersma and colleagues' (2018) "upper-most Campanian–lower-most Maastrichtian", given with dates of 73.04 and 73.49 Ma that are Campanian on the current chart; whether that reading stands has not been examined here.

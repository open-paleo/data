# Maastrichtian in the lower Kirtland members

## Does Hunt and Lucas's "Edmontonian (early Maastrichtian)" put the lower Kirtland members in the Maastrichtian?

*2026-10-21*

**Conclusion.** No. Hunt and Lucas (1992) date the four members below the Naashoibito together, and their Maastrichtian rests on a Campanian–Maastrichtian boundary at 74.0 Ma and on ash dates "consistent with an age of 73.5 ± 2.2 Ma". On the ICS chart v2024/12 the Maastrichtian begins at 72.2 Ma, so 73.5 Ma is late Campanian and the Maastrichtian label belongs to the 1989 chart. Fassett and Steiner's (1997) ashes in the upper Farmington, 73.37 and 73.04 Ma, are also Campanian on the current chart. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` on [Hunter Wash](../../stratigraphy/h/hunter-wash.yml) and [Farmington](../../stratigraphy/f/farmington.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- hunt1992a, p. 234: "Brookins and Rigby (1987) reviewed the age determinations from these ashes and concluded that they are all consistent with an age of 73.5 ± 2.2 Ma. The Campanian-Maastrichtian boundary is currently placed at 74.0 Ma (Harland et al., 1990)."
- hunt1992a, p. 234: "radiometric dates, palynology and the conformable position between Judithian and Lancian strata suggest that the lower Kirtland is Edmontonian (early Maastrichtian) in age."
- fassett1997a, p. 243: ash H, "in the upper part of the Farmington Sandstone Member of the Kirtland Shale", at 73.37 ± 0.18 Ma; ash J, "very near the top of the Farmington Member", at 73.04 ± 0.25 Ma.

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian for the Hunter Wash and Farmington from Hunt and Lucas.* The stage comes from the older boundary, not from evidence that differs from the Campanian dates. CONTRIBUTING's rule on reading ages against the chart a paper used takes the number over the older stage.

**Open.** Newman's (1987) palynological "early Maastrichtian" for "a portion of the upper Farmington Member, the De-na-zin Member and the Naashoibito Member", reported by Hunt and Lucas (1992, p. 233), has not been read. A palynological stage does not depend on a numeric boundary in the same way, so reading Newman would show whether it adds the Maastrichtian to the upper Farmington and supports it on the [De-na-zin](../../stratigraphy/d/de-na-zin.yml). The De-na-zin's own Maastrichtian rests on Wiersma and colleagues' (2018) "upper-most Campanian–lower-most Maastrichtian", given with dates of 73.04 and 73.49 Ma that are Campanian on the current chart; whether that reading stands has not been examined here.

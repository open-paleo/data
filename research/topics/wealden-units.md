# Wealden units

## How are the Wealden units ranked, and is the Grinstead Clay a formation or a member?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** The ranks differ by authority. The literature since 2011 treats the Wealden of the Weald Sub-basin as a supergroup (Hastings Beds and Weald Clay groups) and of the Wessex Sub-basin as a group, with a Grinstead Clay Formation; the British Geological Survey Lexicon has a Wealden Group in both sub-basins and a Grinstead Clay Member of the Tunbridge Wells Sand Formation. The registry follows the literature; that departs from the BGS. Governs `rank` and `parent` on [Wealden](../../stratigraphy/w/wealden.yml), [Hastings Beds](../../stratigraphy/h/hastings-beds.yml), [Weald Clay](../../stratigraphy/w/weald-clay.yml) and [Grinstead Clay](../../stratigraphy/g/grinstead-clay.yml), and the Grinstead Clay assignment of [*Hylaeosaurus armatus*](../../genera/H/Hylaeosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- raven2020a, p. 23: "In the Wessex Sub-basin, the Wealden is given the stratigraphic status of 'Group' rather than 'Supergroup'", with the "Ashdown, Wadhurst Clay, Lower Tunbridge Wells Sandstone and Upper Tunbridge Wells Sandstone formations" in the Weald; p. 3: "Grinstead Clay Formation (Lower Cretaceous, Valanginian; Austen and Batten, 2018)", and "Tilgate Forest, near Cuckfield, West Sussex … Grinstead Clay Formation".
- austen2018a, in-press version: "Ardingly Sandstone Member of the Lower Tunbridge Wells Sand Formation and overlying Grinstead Clay Formation".
- The British Geological Survey Lexicon: "Grinstead Clay Member", parent Tunbridge Wells Sand Formation, with "Grinstead Clay Formation" marked obsolete; Tunbridge Wells Sand Formation in the Wealden Group; "Hastings Beds (Subgroup)" obsolete.

**Ruled out.**
- *Raven and colleagues as internally inconsistent.* Their p. 23 summary omits the Grinstead Clay, but they use Formation throughout (pp. 1, 3, Fig. 1).
- *A single Bolney locality for Polacanthoides.* Nopcsa wrote "These pieces were all found in the Wealden at Bolney" (nopcsa1928a, p. 58); Raven and colleagues show the taxon is "a chimera consisting of a scapula from the Wealden of Sussex (NHMUK PV OR2584 …) and a humerus and tibia from the Wessex Formation of Brighstone Bay on the Isle of Wight" (raven2020a, p. 13), so [*Polacanthoides*](../../genera/P/Polacanthoides.yml) has no single horizon.

**Open.** Whether to follow the BGS ranks. Owen's *Hylaeosaurus* localities, "Tilgate Forest, Bolney and Battle" in "the Wealden formation" (owen1842a, p. 120), point to the Hastings Beds by geography; the Valanginian reading is an inference. The scapula of *Polacanthoides* has a "Wealden of Sussex" origin with no stage.

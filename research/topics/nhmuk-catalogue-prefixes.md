# NHMUK catalogue prefixes

## Does the *Pelorosaurus* humerus take a register letter?

*2026-10-13*

**Conclusion.** No, as for the *Ornithopsis* lectotype in the entry below. The works that discuss the humerus print the number bare; the one letter in print is a single table cell. Governs `specimen_id: [NHMUK PV 28626]` on the humerus in [*Pelorosaurus*](../../genera/P/Pelorosaurus.yml)'s notable specimens.

**Evidence.**
- ostrom1970a and white1973a: "BMNH 28626".
- upchurch2015a: "NHMUK 28626", four times, in a paper on this material.
- jones2026b, p. 380: "NHMUK 28626—humerus".
- mannion2011a, limb-ratio table: "*Pelorosaurus* (NHMUK R.28626)".

**Ruled out.**
- *NHMUK PV OR 28626.* No source read prints the OR.
- *NHMUK PV R 28626.* One table cell by Mannion and colleagues (2011), against the bare number in Upchurch, Mannion and Taylor (2015), with Mannion again an author.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does the *Ornithopsis hulkei* lectotype take a register letter?

*2026-10-13*

**Conclusion.** No. Every source read prints the number bare, and since the R, RU and OR prefixes are part of the number, a letter no source prints is not added by pattern. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [NHMUK PV 28632]` on [*Ornithopsis hulkei*](../../genera/O/Ornithopsis.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hulke1879a: "No. 28632, Brit. Mus. Catal."
- blows1995a: "BMNH 28632 is the lectotype of *Ornithopsis hulkei*".
- jones2026b: "NHMUK 28632".

**Ruled out.**
- *NHMUK PV R 28632.* No source read prints the R.

**Open.** Nothing.

## How is the *Abrictosaurus* holotype number spaced?

*2026-10-13*

**Conclusion.** "NHMUK PV RU B 54", with the number spaced off the register, as the data writes the Lesothosaurus numbers RU B 17 and RU B 23. Sereno (2012) prints "NHMUK RU B54"; the space is the house form, not a different number. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [NHMUK PV RU B 54]` on [*Abrictosaurus consors*](../../genera/A/Abrictosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sereno2012a, Table 1: "*Abrictosaurus consors* | *NHMUK RU B54".

**Ruled out.**
- *"RU B54" run together.* It differs from the house form only in spacing.

**Open.** Nothing.

## How should Natural History Museum, London specimen numbers be written?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** The palaeontological vertebrate collection is NHMUK PV, and BMNH is the same institution's older code; NHMUK VP is a misprint for PV. Within PV, the R, RU and OR prefixes are part of the number. Governs `type_specimen.institution: NHMUK` and the prefix in `specimen_id` on records such as [*Pantydraco*](../../genera/P/Pantydraco.yml), [*Abrictosaurus*](../../genera/A/Abrictosaurus.yml) and [*Archaeopteryx*](../../genera/A/Archaeopteryx.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sabaj2020a, p. 656: "Current as: NHMUK … NHMUK PV (paleovertebrate collections). Also as: BMNH."
- butler2008b, p. 8: "(BMNH RUB54, holotype; formerly UCL B54)"; sereno2012a, p. 17: "now catalogued as NHMUK RU B54". RU is prefixed to the original University College London number, so the Pant-y-ffynnon specimens are RU P24, RU P77/1 and so on (barrett2005b, p. 808: "BMNH RU P24"; chapelle2018a, Table 1: "NHMUK RU P24").
- hendrickx2022a, p. 963: "London specimen of Archaeopteryx (NHMUK OR 37001)"; about 20 papers read for this note give "BMNH 37001".

**Ruled out.**
- *NHMUK VP.* Isasmendi and colleagues (2024) write "NHMUK VP R9951" 40 times and "NHMUK PV 31806" four times in one paper (isasmendi2024a, pp. 3, 21–22); Mateus and Estraviz-López (2022) and Ibrahim and colleagues (2020) also use VP. The registry and the museum use PV.
- *RUP as one token.* "RU B54" and "RU P24" show RU followed by the UCL series letter.

**Open.** What RU and OR stand for; no source read for this note expands them.

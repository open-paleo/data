# Kuangyuan Series and Young's Locality 47

## Which unit is Young's Locality 47 in, and is "Kuangyuan" a formation?

*2026-10-02*

**Conclusion.** "Kuangyuan Series" is a retired name with no modern equivalent, and Locality 47 lies above the Qianfoya (Tsienfuyen) Formation, not in it. No formation is assigned. Governs `period: [Late Jurassic]` with no `stage` and no `location.formation` on [*Sinocoelurus fragilis*](../../genera/S/Sinocoelurus.yml), [*Szechuanosaurus campi*](../../genera/S/Szechuanosaurus.yml) and [*Chienkosaurus ceratosauroides*](../../genera/C/Chienkosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- young1942a, p. 293: "locality 47, belonging to the top part of the Kuangyuan Series and immediately below the Chentsianyen conglomerate". Young uses only "Kuangyuan Series", never Formation or Group. The *Szechuanosaurus campi* types are "V235 … V236 … V238 and one nearly complete tooth, V239. All loc. 47." (p. 300).
- young1943a, p. 17: "Overlying the Hsuchiaho Coal Series disconformably, the Tsienfuyen Series commences with an imposing basal conglomerate"; "The Kuangyuan Series follows the Tsienfuyen Series concordantly. The boundary between the two is arbitrary." The Chengtsiangyen above "represents a disconformity" (p. 18) and is "tentatively regarded as of Cretaceous in age by its stratigraphic position" (p. 19).
- zhang2009b, p. 1131, entry "Tsienfuyen Formation": "Type locality: Qianfoyan (Tsienfuyen) in Guangyuan County"; age "Mid Jurassic". luo2014a, p. 3: "The Qianfoyan (J2q) and Shaximiao (J2s) Formations mainly comprise the Middle Jurassic".
- dong1983a, English translation, p. 58: "Overlying the Qianfoya Fm. are the red beds of the Chungking (Guangyuan) Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *Qianfoya Formation.* Dong (1983) writes that the *Sinocoelurus* material "should be recognized as being derived from the Qianfoya Fm. which overlies the Baitianba Fm." and that it is "very possible that these specimens are Early to Middle Jurassic" (dong1983a, English translation, p. 58). Young's section puts Locality 47 at the top of the Kuangyuan Series, which overlies the Tsienfuyen (Qianfoya), and Dong's own geology puts the red beds above the Qianfoya.
- *Kuangyuan Formation.* Carrano and colleagues (2012) write "the Late Jurassic Kuangyuan Formation of Sichuan Province" (carrano2012a, p. 256), changing Young's rank without a source. Dong (1983) writes "Chungking (Guangyuan) Group" and says the beds were "formerly referred to as the Guangyuan Group" (English translation, p. 2). The *Stratigraphic Lexicon of China* has no Kuangyuan or Guangyuan entry. Kuangyuan is also a city ("Kuangyuan City", molnar1974a, p. 1015).
- *Early Jurassic by Young's correlation.* Young (1942) regards "the Tienfuyen and the Kuangyuan Series … as equivalent of Tzeliuching Series … and represent an upper Jurassic age" (young1942a, p. 307). Applying the modern Early Jurassic age of the Ziliujing to that correlation would make Locality 47 Early Jurassic (our inference), but its position below a probably Cretaceous conglomerate, and Young's later "deposited during the Upper Jurassic time (Purbeckian)" (young1943a, p. 20), keep it Late Jurassic.

**Open.** Which modern unit holds Locality 47. The candidates above the Qianfoya are the upper Shaximiao, the [Suining](../../stratigraphy/s/suining.yml) and the Penglaizhen formations ("the Late Jurassic Upper Shaximiao, Suining, and Penglaizhen formations", dong1983a, English translation, p. 133); none is sourced for this locality. If it were the Suining, that unit's disputed age would apply.

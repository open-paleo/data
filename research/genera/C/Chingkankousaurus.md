# *Chingkankousaurus*

## Does the provincial lithostratigraphy place the *Chingkankousaurus* holotype in a formation?

*2026-10-23*

**Conclusion.** Yes, the Hongtuya. The provincial lithostratigraphy lists "*Chinkangkousaurus fragivis*" from the Tianqiaotun area of Laiyang in the Hongtuya Formation; see the [Wangshi](../../stratigraphy/w/wangshi.md) entry of the same date. Governs `location.formation: Hongtuya` on [*Chingkankousaurus fragilis*](../../../genera/C/Chingkankousaurus.yml). Supersedes the 2026 entry below, which stopped at the Wangshi Group because no source read named a formation.

**Evidence.**
- zhang1996a, p. 247: "The area around Tianqiaotun, Laiyang, yields *Tsintaosaurus spinorhinus*, *Tanius chinkangkouensis*, *T. sinensis*, *Chinkangkousaurus fragivis*" (translated), in the Hongtuya fauna.

**Ruled out.**
- *The Jingangkou Formation.* The genus name takes the place Chingkankou, but the provincial Jingangkou Formation is the lake beds above the hadrosaur horizon.

**Open.** The volume lists the taxon by area, not by specimen; reading the entry as the holotype IVPP V836 is our inference.

## Which unit is the *Chingkankousaurus* holotype from?

*2026-08-15*

**Conclusion.** The Wangshi Group, with no formation. Every source read that names a unit for IVPP V836 stops at the group or series. Governs `location.group: Wangshi` and the empty `location.formation` on [*Chingkankousaurus fragilis*](../../../genera/C/Chingkankousaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- brusatte2013a, p. 3: Young "described a single fragmentary bone from the Late Cretaceous … Wangshi Series of Shandong Province as a new genus and species of giant theropod, *Chingkankousaurus fragilis*".
- buffetaut1995a, p. 685: the Wangshi Group "has yielded a rather diverse dinosaur assemblage, containing theropods (*Chingkankousaurus fragilis* Young, 1958)". On the same page, Young (1958) "placed the Jiangjunding (Chiang-Chun-Ting) locality, which yielded the type of *Tanius sinensis*, in the lower part of the Wangshi Group, below the Jingangkou locality which yielded *Tsintaosaurus spinorhinus*", so Young's Laiyang taxa came from more than one level of the group.

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Whether the scapula came from the Jingangkou Formation. The genus name takes the form Chingkankou (our reading of the name), which Buffetaut (1995) equates with Jingangkou ("the Wangshi Group at Jingangkou (Chingkankou)", buffetaut1995a, p. 684), and Zhang and colleagues (2019) place *Laiyangosaurus* in the Jingangkou Formation (zhang2019a, p. 1, Abstract) and write that Young's *Tanius chingkankouensis* was "based on material collected from the Jingangkou Formation in Jingangkou, Laiyang" (p. 2, Introduction). None of these sources names *Chingkankousaurus* in that formation, and a name taken from a place is not evidence of the horizon. Young's (1958) description, which would say where the scapula was found, would settle it. *Tanius chingkankouensis* is a hadrosaur from the same locality name, a different taxon from *Chingkankousaurus fragilis*.

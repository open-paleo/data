# *Cionodon*

## Is the status of *Cionodon* in dispute, and what is the type material?

*2026-10-17*

**Conclusion.** No dispute: the block is removed. Every treatment read since Lull and Wright (1942) finds the type undiagnostic, hadrosaurid and nothing finer. The type material follows Lull and Wright, who saw it: Cope's femur is the distal end of a third metatarsal and his tibia probably the proximal end of an ulna, and their holotype list has no caudal vertebra. Governs the absence of a dispute and `type_specimen.material` on [*Cionodon arctatus*](../../../genera/C/Cionodon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lull1942a: "Holotype: No. 3951 A.M.N.H. Portion of right (?) maxilla, two dorsal vertebrae, distal end of metatarsal III, proximal and distal ends of ulna"; "The femur described by Cope is clearly the distal end of a third metatarsal, which accounts for its small size, and the writers suspect that Cope's tibia is the proximal end of an ulna."
- brett-surman1989a: "This genus was based on a partial maxilla, two dorsal vertebrae, the distal end of the third metatarsal, and a partial ulna"; "None of these elements permit a diagnosis at any taxonomic level, therefore Cionodon should be considered a nomen dubium."
- carpenter2002a: "The material is clearly hadrosaurian, but little else can be added to Cope's original description."

**Ruled out.**
- *Cope's femur and tibia, and a caudal vertebra.* The earlier material line, after Cope (1874); Lull and Wright, who examined the bones, re-identify the limb elements and list no caudal.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Where is the Cionodon arctatus holotype (AMNH 3951) from?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Unresolved between the Laramie and the Denver Formation, Colorado. The "Lodge Pole Creek" locality is an error. Governs the empty `location.formation` on *Cionodon arctatus*.

**Evidence.**
- cope1874a, p. 10, footnote: the taxon, printed "Cinodon arctatus", is listed in "a collection from this formation", the Cretaceous, made by an assistant, with no locality.
- carpenter2002a, p. 246: "Laramie Formation, exact locality unknown, but believed to be Bijou Creek", and Cope (in Cross 1896) "stated that the specimen came from Bijou Creek"; p. 247: "Either the specimen came from this stretch of the Laramie, or the specimen actually came from the Denver Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Lodge Pole Creek.* Lull (1942) gives "Evidently from the watershed between the South Platte River and Lodge Pole Creek, Colorado", with "Denver or Arapahoe formation, Cretaceous" (lull1942a, p. 213); the geography comes from Cope's opening sentence about Pliocene beds, "The water-shed between the South Platte River and the Lodge-Pole Creek, is composed superficially of formations of the Pliocene epoch" (cope1874a, p. 9). Carpenter (2002, p. 246) notes "the strata here are mostly mid-Tertiary in age". White (1973) repeats "Denver Formation—North America, Colorado, Lodge Pole Creek" (white1973a, p. 127).

**Open.** Which formation. Weishampel and colleagues list the taxon under "Denver Formation (Carpenter, pers. comm.)" (weishampel2004b, p. 581), one of Carpenter's two alternatives; Cross (1896), where Cope named Bijou Creek, has not been read.

# *Charonosaurus*

## Which Jiayin site is the *Charonosaurus* type locality?

*2026-08-15*

**Conclusion.** Longgushan, on the southern bank of the Amur (Heilongjiang) River 8 km west of Jiayin village, at the coordinate Godefroit and colleagues (2000) print. Godefroit and colleagues give the bearing and coordinate but no site name; Xing and colleagues (2022) name the site Longgushan and identify it as the source of the *Charonosaurus* material. Governs `location.locality: Longgushan` and `location.coordinates: [48.883333, 130.45]` on [*Charonosaurus jiayinensis*](../../../genera/C/Charonosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- godefroit2000a, p. 878: "*Locus typicus*. Southern bank of the Amur River (Heilongjang), 8 km west of Jiayin village (Heilongjang Province, P.R. China; 48°53'N, 130°27'E)." The French version on p. 876 gives the same coordinate.
- xing2022a, p. e2085111-2: Chinese expeditions "undertook further fossil excavations at the Longgushan site since 1977", and "Godefroit et al. (2000, 2001) reevaluated a portion of the Longgushan hadrosaurid specimens housed in China, and regarded them as belonging to a new lambeosaurine, namely *Charonosaurus jiayinensis*".

**Ruled out.**
- *Wulaga.* Xing and colleagues (2022) print a GPS coordinate, "48° 23′40.73″N, 130°08′44.12″E", for an outcrop "in the west of Wulaga Town, Jiayin County" (xing2022a, p. e2085111-6). It is a referred locality of *Amurosaurus riabinini*, from middle Maastrichtian beds that they keep apart from the Longgushan horizon (p. e2085111-2). "Jiayin" in a locality line can mean either the village or the county, and only the Longgushan site is *Charonosaurus*'s.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the *Charonosaurus* holotype Maastrichtian or Turonian?

*2026-08-05*

**Conclusion.** Maastrichtian. Godefroit and colleagues (2000) give the type stratum as Late Maastrichtian, on palynology. Governs `stage: [Maastrichtian]` on [*Charonosaurus jiayinensis*](../../../genera/C/Charonosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- godefroit2000a, p. 878: "*Stratum typicum*. Yuliangze Formation (Late Maastrichtian, Late Cretaceous)."
- godefroit2000a, p. 877: "A Late Maastrichtian age is proposed for the Yuliangze Formation because *Wodehouseia spinata* is considered an excellent biostratigraphic marker for Late Maastrichtian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Turonian.* Weishampel and colleagues (2004) give "Yuliangze Formation (Heilongjiang) … Turonian" (weishampel2004a, p. 441, Table 20.1). No source read dates the formation to the Turonian (see [Yuliangzi](../../../stratigraphy/y/yuliangzi.yml)). Jones (2026) gives "Yuliangze Fm., China, Late Cretaceous, Maastrichtian" (jones2026a, p. 409).

**Open.** Nothing.

# *Camarasaurus*

## Which Garden Park quarry is the *Camarasaurus supremus* type material from, and in which unit?

*2026-08-14*

**Conclusion.** Cope Quarry No. 1, in the uppermost Morrison, as Osborn and Mook (1921) attribute it, with their own hedge: the type material "apparently" came from that quarry. The "Nipple" is a hill about 500 yards from it, not the quarry. Governs `location.locality: Cope Quarry No. 1`, `location.formation: Morrison` and `location.part: upper` on [*Camarasaurus supremus*](../../../genera/C/Camarasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- osborn1921a, p. 255: two Cope quarries are known, and "Both of these were excavated in the uppermost beds of the Morrison formation".
- osborn1921a, p. 256: Quarry No. 1 "is situated about 500 yards west to southwest of the small conical hill, locally known as the 'Nipple'"; "from it apparently came some of Cope's original types and cotypes, to which the number A. M. 5760 was assigned". Quarry No. 2 lies "near the base of the 'Nipple'" and "may have furnished some of Cope's types".
- osborn1921a, p. 263: Cope's vertebrae "probably did not belong to one individual, and therefore must be considered as cotypes"; "Type locality and level.—Uppermost Morrison beds near Canyon City, Colorado."

**Ruled out.**
- *The Dakota.* Cope's account of the Dakota vertebrates, read to the American Philosophical Society in December 1877 (needs an id), gives Hayden's view that "the formation from which the *Camarasaurus* was obtained, is the Dakota". Osborn and Mook's section and map put the quarries in the Morrison, below the Purgatoire sandstone that caps it at Quarry No. 1 (osborn1921a, p. 256), and their geological map gives the "Dakota and Purgatoire Formation" as a unit apart from the Morrison (osborn1921a, Fig. 1).
- *Cope's Nipple as the locality, or as part of it.* Osborn and Mook use the hill as a landmark and separate it from Quarry No. 1 by about 500 yards. A locality string joining the quarry and the hill names two places as one.

**Open.** Quarry No. 2. Carpenter and Galton (2018) read Mudge's letter of August 1877, which puts the *Nanosaurus* specimen "about ten feet above the big saurian [holotype of *Camarasaurus supremus*] he is now taking out", as placing that level "near the top of Cope's Nipple" (carpenter2018b, p. 170). That fits Quarry No. 2, at the foot of the hill, better than Quarry No. 1, though the bracket is their identification and the cotypes may not come from one quarry: Osborn and Mook assign the red-series bones "probably" to Quarry No. 1 and the yellow series to Quarry No. 2 (osborn1921a, p. 260). Lucas's or Cope's 1877 correspondence tying the cotype vertebrae to a pit would settle it. See [*Nanosaurus*](../N/Nanosaurus.md) for the hill as that genus's site.

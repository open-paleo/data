# *Coronosaurus*

## Is Bone Bed 138 in a named bed of the Oldman Formation?

*2026-08-17*

**Conclusion.** No bed is recorded. Ryan and colleagues (2012) call the site "referable to the Comrey sandstone (Eberth 2005)", but Eberth (2005) names the Comrey Sandstone Zone as the middle of three informal units of the Oldman Formation, distinguished by channel geometry, not as a bed. The position comes from the UTM reference both describing papers print for the bone bed. Governs `location.formation: Oldman`, the empty `location.bed`, `location.locality: Bone Bed 138, Dinosaur Provincial Park` and `location.coordinates: [50.745728, -111.523158]` on [*Coronosaurus brinkmani*](../../../genera/C/Coronosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ryan2005a, p. 1371: "Bone bed 138 (Fig. 2), Dinosaur Provincial Park, approximately 50 km from Brooks, Alberta, Canada (12 463090E, 5621680N (WGS 84)), Oldman Formation, 14.6 m below the contact with the Dinosaur Park Formation (645 m above sea level)".
- ryan2012a, p. 1262: the same type-locality line, ending "referable to the Comrey sandstone (Eberth 2005)".
- eberth2005a, p. 60: "The Oldman Formation is subdivided into three informal stratigraphic units based on paleochannel geometry and sandstone stacking patterns"; "Unit 2 is commonly referred to as the Comrey Sandstone Zone"; p. 65: "The Comrey Sandstone Zone (Unit 2) is partially exposed in the Park".

**Ruled out.**
- *`bed: Comrey`.* Eberth's Comrey is an informal zone, one of three subdivisions of the whole formation, not a bed.
- *The pair stored before 2026-08-17, [50.747601, -111.524002].* It matches no figure in either paper and lies about 200 m from the conversion of their UTM reference.

**Open.** Whether the record should take `part: middle`. That follows if the bone bed lies in Eberth's unit 2, as Ryan and colleagues' "referable to the Comrey sandstone" implies, but no source states the position in those words.

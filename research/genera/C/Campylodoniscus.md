# *Campylodoniscus*

## Is the *Campylodoniscus* holotype from the Bajo Barreal Formation?

*2026-08-14*

**Conclusion.** No formation can be given. Huene (1929) records only "variegated sandstones" on the western side of the Sierra de San Bernardo, and the later sources go no finer than the Chubut Group. Governs `location.group: Chubut`, the absence of `location.formation`, and `location.locality: western side of the Sierra de San Bernardo` on [*Campylodoniscus ameghinoi*](../../../genera/C/Campylodoniscus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huene1929a, pp. 82–83 (translated): the maxilla comes "from the 'variegated sandstones,' perhaps relatively deep dinosaur layers, on the west side of the Sierra de San Bernardo, west of Lake Musters".
- powell2003a, Western Slope of the Sierra de San Bernardo: the level is "indicated just as 'variegated sandstones'. This certainly corresponds to units of the Chubut Group."
- bonaparte1979c, p. 402 (translated): "Chubut Group, Castillo, Bajo Barreal, or Laguna Palacios Formation".
- martínez2016a, p. 61, Fig. 31 caption: MACN A-IOR63 came from "the western flank of the Sierra de San Bernardo west of Lago Musters (i.e., in the vicinity of the Estancia Ocho Hermanos); unfortunately, however, its stratigraphic position within the Chubut Group is not known".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Bajo Barreal Formation.* Bonaparte and Gasparini give it as one of three candidates, not as a reading. Martínez and colleagues (2016), who work the Bajo Barreal at Ocho Hermanos, say the position within the Chubut Group is not known. The Bajo Barreal's fossil levels are "well exposed in sierra San Bernardo" (casal2015a, p. 88, translated), but that says where the rock could be from, not where this specimen is from.

**Open.** Nothing in the sources read places the specimen within the group, and the collector's notes, if any survive, would be the only source that could.

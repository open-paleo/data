# *Cristatusaurus*

## Is *Cristatusaurus* a valid genus, and is it a baryonychine?

*2026-10-19*

**Conclusion.** Its validity is disputed, and its placement within Spinosauridae is unresolved. Carrano and colleagues (2012) treat it as an indeterminate baryonychine, almost certainly the same animal as *Suchomimus*. Lacerda and colleagues (2022) find it a valid species and recover it as the earliest-diverging spinosaurid, outside Baryonychinae. The parent is Spinosauridae, which both accept. Governs `parent: Spinosauridae`, `status: disputed` and the dispute on [*Cristatusaurus lapparenti*](../../../genera/C/Cristatusaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- carrano2012a, p. 253: "Cristatusaurus can only be identified as an indeterminate baryonychine spinosaurid. However, we also consider it unlikely that two baryonychines are present at Gadoufaoua, and therefore Cristatusaurus and Suchomimus almost certainly represent the same animal."
- sales2017a, p. 18: "a taxon that may be a nomen dubium and/or a synonym of Suchomimus"; the paper notes differences between the two Nigerien taxa with possible taxonomic significance.
- lacerda2022a, p. 2089, Abstract: "The phylogenetic inference recovered C. lapparenti outside Baryonychinae, which was composed by B. walkeri and S. tenerensis"; p. 2104: "a basally branching spinosaurid and the sister taxon of the clade Baryonychinae + Spinosaurinae"; p. 2105: "our results support that C. lapparenti is a valid species, distinct from both, Baryonyx walkeri and Su. tenerensis."

**Ruled out.**
- *Lacerda and colleagues (2022) as finding it "likely synonymous with Suchomimus".* Their stated conclusion is the reverse, and the only synonymy they entertain is hypothetical: should more complete material show *C. lapparenti* to be a senior synonym of *Su. tenerensis*, the differences could be ontogenetic or intraspecific (p. 2099).
- *Baryonychinae as the parent.* It takes Carrano and colleagues' side against the one phylogenetic analysis that includes both genera as terminals.
- *Jones (2026) as the source for its validity.* Jones reports Lacerda and colleagues' conclusion correctly (jones2026c, *Cristatusaurus* entry), but the claim belongs to the primary.

**Open.** Whether a later analysis including both genera supports the result. Lacerda and colleagues themselves, noting the debate over its status, "suggest caution with these results until new materials or better descriptions become public" (p. 2104).

## Is "niveau des Innocents" the *Cristatusaurus* holotype's locality or its bed?

*2026-09-16*

**Conclusion.** The locality. "Niveau des Innocents" names a site at Gadoufaoua, and GAD 5, the upper Elrhaz, is the level. Taquet and Russell's type-locality line gives both together. Galton and Taquet separate them under their own headings, and Taquet (1976) measures distances from the Innocents as from a place. Governs `location.locality: Gadoufaoua, niveau des Innocents` and `location.bed: GAD 5` on [*Cristatusaurus lapparenti*](../../../genera/C/Cristatusaurus.yml), matching [*Nigersaurus taqueti*](../../../genera/N/Nigersaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- taquet1998a, p. 350: "Type locality: Gadoufaoua; GAD 5, niveau des Innocents, Tegama Formation, Aptian. Niger."
- galton1982a, p. 153: under LOCALITY, "Gadoufaoua, the region of niveau des Innocents (see Taquet, 1976, fig. 7)"; under HORIZON, "GAD 5, upper part of El Rhaz formation, Tégama Series, Aptian".
- sereno1999a, p. 1346, note: the *Nigersaurus* holotype is "from the locality 'niveau des Innocents' in the Gadoufaoua region".
- taquet1976a, *Ouranosaurus nigeriensis*, translated pages: a second skeleton was found "4 km south of the 'level of the innocents'"; the paratype locality is "4 km south of the formation of the Innocents … GAD 5 (upper part of the formation of Elrhaz)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Innocents as the bed and GAD 5 as the locality.* That inverts the sources: GAD 5 is printed as the horizon and the Innocents as the place, and *niveau* in the name does not make it a level in the stratigraphic sense.

**Open.** Taquet's fig. 7, the map Galton and Taquet cite, is not in the translated pages held here. This entry answers the Open question in [*Elrhazosaurus*](../E/Elrhazosaurus.md) on whether the name is a site: it is.

## Is the *Cristatusaurus* holotype MNHN GDF 366?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** Yes: GDF 366 alone, with GDF 365 and the dorsal vertebrae as referred specimens. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [MNHN GDF 366]` on [*Cristatusaurus lapparenti*](../../../genera/C/Cristatusaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- taquet1998a, p. 350: "Type specimen: (figure, f-h) MNHN GDF 366, both premaxillae, portion of right maxilla and dentary"; "Referred specimens (figure, e, i, j) MNHN GDF 365 fused premaxillae; dorsal vertebrae 357-359, 361."

**Ruled out.**
- *GDF 500.* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi (2019) give "MNN GDF500" in their theropod speed table, in a row headed "*Cristatusaurus lapparenti (Suchomimus)*" (molina-pérez2019a). Jones (2026) gives MNN GDF500 as the *Suchomimus tenerensis* type (jones2026c, p. 498).
- *GDF 366 with 357, 358, 359, 361 and 365.* Jones (2026) lists all six as the type (jones2026c, p. 497). Taquet and Russell refer all but 366.

**Open.** Nothing.

# *Craterosaurus*

## Does Galton's 1981 monograph give its own basis for a Neocomian source?

*2026-10-23*

**Conclusion.** Yes, and it allows the Lower Aptian too. Galton (1981) reasons from the predominance of *Iguanodon* among the derived dinosaurs to a source "probably Neocomian or possibly Lower Aptian", which supports the 2026-10-10 Conclusion (Berriasian to Aptian) and answers its Open question. Governs `period.stage` and `location.notes` on [*Craterosaurus pottonensis*](../../../genera/C/Craterosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton1981b, p. 29: the bed's "indigenous fossils give an Upper Aptian age"; "the dinosaurs are chiefly represented by bones of Iguanodon (SEELEY 1874, CASEY 1961) so the original age of Craterosaurus is probably Lower Cretaceous (probably Neocomian or possibly Lower Aptian)."

**Ruled out.**
- *Berriasian to Hauterivian alone.* Galton himself allows the Lower Aptian.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What stages does the reworked *Craterosaurus* holotype take?

*2026-10-10*

**Conclusion.** Every stage from the Berriasian to the Aptian. The holotype is reworked into the Potton Nodule Bed, so it takes the age of its source beds, and no source read establishes them. The Neocomian rests on Galton (1985), who cites Casey (1961) for it, but Casey lists derived Lower Aptian ammonites as well as Neocomian sandstone in the bed and holds that worn bones there prove no older source. By the rule that an uncertain source takes every bed it may have come from, and the union rule's every stage between, the range runs from the Neocomian through the Aptian. Supersedes the source-bed reading of the [Woburn Sands](../../stratigraphy/w/woburn-sands.md) entry of 2026-09-30. Governs `period.stage` and the location notes on [*Craterosaurus pottonensis*](../../../genera/C/Craterosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton1985d, p. 213: "Potton Nodule Bed in upper part of Woburn Sands (Lower Cretaceous, probably reworked from beds of Neocomian age; Casey, 1961)".
- casey1961a, p. 566: the derived material of the Potton nodules includes "blocks of Neocomian sandstone, bones of Iguanodon" and Lower Aptian ammonites.
- casey1961a, p. 491: "No one would today accept these finds as proof of a Wealden origin: Iguanodon was still living in Aptian times and worn fossils are commonplace in contemporary Lower Greensand deposits".
- galton2009a, p. 214: "~112 Ma (reworked into late Aptian-early Albian) for the Potton Sandstone of Bedfordshire".
- gale2020a, p. 1024: the Neocomian is "subdivided into three stages—the Berriasian, Valanginian and Hauterivian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Berriasian to Hauterivian alone.* Its only basis is Galton's "probably", attributed to a paper that declines to date the bones.
- *The depositional age alone, Aptian to Albian.* The nodule bed is a deposit of derived material, and the holotype is recorded as reworked; the Albian is the bed's, not a possible source's.
- *Neocomian as the formation's age.* Galton and Coombs (1981) place the specimen in "the Woburn Sands (Neocomian, Lower Cretaceous)", a dating of the formation since superseded, not a statement about reworking.

**Open.** Galton's 1981 monograph on *Craterosaurus*, which is not held, may give its own basis for a Neocomian source.

## Does *Craterosaurus* carry diagnostic features?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** No. The features once listed came from Seeley's reading of the type as a skull; it is a partial dorsal neural arch, and the genus is a nomen dubium. Governs the absence of `diagnostic_features` on [*Craterosaurus*](../../../genera/C/Craterosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton1985d: Nopcsa (1912) "reinterpreted it as the incomplete neural arch of a stegosaurian dorsal vertebra"; the one diagnostic feature is "a very deep depression in the top of the arch posterior to the prezygapophyses".
- maidment2008a: "originally described by Seeley (1874) as a partial skull, but Nopcsa (1912) correctly identified it as the neural arch of a dorsal vertebra".

**Ruled out.**
- *Galton's depression as a diagnostic feature.* The record treats the genus as a nomen dubium whose features are widespread in stegosaurs (Maidment and colleagues, 2008); Galton's feature is kept in his reference note.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is "Potton Sands" the *Craterosaurus* locality or its unit?

*2026-10-02*

**Conclusion.** Neither field takes it. Seeley (1874) uses "Potton Sands" for the bed of phosphatic nodules at Potton, which he places within the Woburn sands, and Galton (1985) gives the holotype as from old coprolite workings for the nodules of the Potton Nodule Bed in the upper Woburn Sands. The locality is the workings, the formation the Woburn Sands and the bed the Potton Nodule Bed. Governs `location.locality: Old coprolite workings near Potton`, `location.formation: Woburn Sands` and `location.bed: Potton Nodule Bed` on [*Craterosaurus pottonensis*](../../../genera/C/Craterosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- seeley1874a, p. 690: the specimen "was found by Mr. Charlesworth in the Potton Sands, near Potton, in Bedfordshire. The bed of phosphatic nodules which there occurs in the brown sands, named by Conybeare and Fitton Woburn sands".
- galton1985d, p. 213: "From old "coprolite" workings for phosphatic nodules of the Potton Nodule Bed in upper part of Woburn Sands (Lower Cretaceous, probably reworked from beds of Neocomian age; Casey, 1961) from near Potton, Bedfordshire."

**Ruled out.**
- *Locality "Potton Sands, near Potton".* Seeley's "Potton Sands" names rock, the nodule bed, not a site.
- *"Potton Sands" as the formation.* Weishampel and colleagues list "Potton Sands (reworked ...)" as the first unit of Bedfordshire (weishampel2004b, p. 558), but Seeley places the nodule bed within the Woburn sands and Galton gives it as a bed in their upper part.

**Open.** Nothing.

# *Compsognathus*

## Which formation is the *Compsognathus longipes* holotype from, when its site is unknown?

*2026-08-17*

**Conclusion.** The Painten Formation, with no locality. Wagner (1861) gives only "near Kelheim", a later label gives Jachenhausen, and Ostrom (1978) found no way to decide between them. Reisdorf and Wuttke (2012) narrow the site to Jachenhausen, or Kapfelberg or Goldberg in the Kelheim area, and place each candidate in the Painten Formation. Their assignment for the Kelheim sites rests on a personal communication from G. Schweigert; Niebuhr and Pürner (2014), who define the formation, give it a thickness in the Kelheim basin, which agrees. Governs `location.formation: Painten` and the empty `location.locality` on [*Compsognathus longipes*](../../../genera/C/Compsognathus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- wagner1861a, p. 94: Oberndorfer found the specimen "in the lithographic slate of Kelheim" (translated).
- ostrom1978a, p. 78: "considerable doubt exists about the exact locality of *Compsognathus longipes,* with no apparent way to resolve it"; the printed label reads "Jachenhausen. Oberpfalz", a village "15 km northwest of Kelheim".
- reisdorf2012a, p. 124: "If it originated in Jachenhausen, then it can be considered to be from the Painten Formation (Zandt Member; cf. Zeiss 1977)"; p. 125: Kapfelberg "is the more probable locality", and "both of these Plattenkalk deposits are considered part of the Painten Formation (G. Schweigert, personal communication 2011)".
- niebuhr2014a, p. 38: the Painten Formation reaches "more than 100 m in the type region", and "in the Kelheim basin more than 90 m are preserved" (translated).
- foth2025a, p. 18: the "Solnhofen limestone" sensu stricto "is part of the Altmühltal Formation and restricted to the area northwest of Ingolstadt, whereas the contemporaneous plattenkalks more to the east belong to the Painten Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Solnhofen Limestone, or the Altmühltal Formation.* An appendix table in Pittman and Xu (2020) gives the occurrence as Kelheim, "Solnhofen Limestone (Altmühltal Formation)" (pittman2020a, p. 153). Foth and colleagues restrict the Altmühltal to the area northwest of Ingolstadt, and Reisdorf and Wuttke place every candidate site in the Painten. Ostrom's and Wagner's "lithographic" terms are the archipelago-wide usage of their time, not a formation.
- *Jachenhausen as the locality.* Holtz and colleagues (2004) give the holotype as from "the Kimmeridgian or basal Tithonian Solnhofen Lithographic Limestone of Jachenhausen, in the Reidenberg-Kelheim area of Bavaria, Germany (Wagner 1861)" (holtz2004a, p. 105), but Wagner names only Kelheim. The Jachenhausen name comes from a later label of unknown authorship, and Ostrom and Wellnhofer found no stratum in the Jachenhausen quarries that "matched the lithology of the *Compsognathus* slab", nor any *Saccocoma*, which the slab preserves (ostrom1978a, p. 78). Reisdorf and Wuttke still keep Jachenhausen as one candidate.
- *The Zandt Member.* Reisdorf and Wuttke give it only for the Jachenhausen alternative.

**Open.** The site, and with it the age within Late Kimmeridgian to Early Tithonian. Mäuser (1984), which Reisdorf and Wuttke cite on the locality question, has not been read.

## Is the *Compsognathus longipes* holotype AS I 563?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** Yes, AS I 563 of the Bavarian State Collection, as Ostrom (1978) gives it. The forms with BSP and with SNSB-BSPG are the same collection's older and current prefixes, and the registry lists both under BSPG. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [SNSB-BSPG AS I 563]` on [*Compsognathus longipes*](../../../genera/C/Compsognathus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ostrom1978a, p. 75: "Wagner, 1861, holotype specimen: B. S. P. A. S. I 563".

**Ruled out.**
- *BSP AS I 563 as another specimen.* Jones (2026) writes "BSP AS I 563" (jones2026c, p. 500), the older prefix for the same number.
- *BSP AS I 536.* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi (2019) give this in their chronology of the smallest theropods and in their text (molina-pérez2019a). It transposes the last two digits of Ostrom's number.

**Open.** Nothing.

# *Camelotia*

## Where does *Camelotia* sit among sauropodiforms?

*2026-10-15*

**Conclusion.** Sauropodiformes incertae sedis, near the origin of Sauropoda, with its finer position unresolved. Analyses since 2011 agree on Sauropodiformes but place it variously beside *Melanorosaurus*, beside Lessemsauridae, or prune it as unstable for missing data. Galton's (1985) Melanorosauridae is not recovered by later analyses. Governs `parent: Sauropodiformes` and the dispute on [*Camelotia*](../../../genera/C/Camelotia.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton1985a: "In addition to *Melanorosaurus*, the Melanorosauridae HUENE, 1929 includes *Camelotia borealis* nov. gen. and nov. sp."
- pol2011b: "the instability of Camelotia and Blikanasaurus are exclusively caused by the lack of information (i.e., missing data) and not by character conflict".
- mcphee2014a: "the position of *Camelotia* … is wildly unstable".
- rauhut2020a: "The only European Triassic sauropodomorph that probably represents a basal sauropodiform is the poorly known *Camelotia borealis*".
- barrett2024b: "Camelotia is either sister to the Melanorosaurus lectotype OTU … or alternatively sister to Lessemsauridae".

**Ruled out.**
- *Melanorosauridae.* Galton's (1985) family, not recovered by the analyses read.

**Open.** Galton's (1998) redescription has not been read.

## Does the *Camelotia* holotype have a recorded bed?

*2026-08-14*

**Conclusion.** No bed is recorded. Galton (1985) gives the type horizon as the "Wedmore Stone of Westbury Formation", and no source read gives a numbered bed for the holotype. Governs `location.formation: Westbury`, `location.locality: Wedmore Hill` and the absence of `location.bed` on [*Camelotia borealis*](../../../genera/C/Camelotia.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton1985a, p. 675, Type horizon and locality: "Wedmore Stone of Westbury Formation in Penarth Group (Rhaetic, Upper Triassic) of Wedmore, Somerset, England."

**Ruled out.**
- *"Bed 7".* An earlier version of the record carried "Wedmore Hill, bed 7". No source read gives a bed 7 for this material.

**Open.** Whether the Wedmore Stone is a named bed the record should carry. A passage describing it as "a local development near the base of the Westbury Formation in the Isle of Wedmore", bed 27 of Richardson (1911), has not been traced to the paper it comes from; if it is right, the Wedmore Stone is a local limestone rather than a formal bed. That paper, Richardson (1911), Storrs (1994, 1999) or Galton's later redescriptions (1998, 2005) would settle its standing.

## Which catalogue numbers make up the *Camelotia* holotype?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** Eight: R2870 to R2874 and R2876 to R2878. Galton (1985) lists them as the holotype, and R2875 is not among them. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [NHMUK PV R 2870, NHMUK PV R 2871, NHMUK PV R 2872, NHMUK PV R 2873, NHMUK PV R 2874, NHMUK PV R 2876, NHMUK PV R 2877, NHMUK PV R 2878]` on [*Camelotia borealis*](../../../genera/C/Camelotia.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton1985a, p. 674, Holotype: "BMNH R2870-R2874, R2876-R2878, 4 dorsal vertebrae, 5 caudal vertebrae".

**Ruled out.**
- *R2870 alone.* The record carried it until 2026-08-01. Galton's figure caption calls R2870 "part of holotype" (galton1985a, Fig. 1 caption).
- *BMNH R2870-R2874.* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi (2020) give this range in their chronology of the largest early sauropodomorphs and their neck table (molina-pérez2020a). It omits R2876 to R2878. Jones (2026) gives "NHMUK R2870-R2874, R2876-R2878" (jones2026b, p. 373), agreeing with Galton.

**Open.** Nothing.

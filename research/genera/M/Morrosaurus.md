# *Morrosaurus*

## Is the Morrosaurus holotype from the Snow Hill Island Formation or the López de Bertodano Formation?

*2026-08-30*

**Conclusion.** The Cape Lamb Member of the Snow Hill Island Formation. The describing paper's horizon line names the López de Bertodano Formation, but the same page reassigns the El Morro outcrops to the Snow Hill Island Formation after Pirrie and colleagues (1997), and places the specimen in the "Cape Lamb Member, Snow Hill Formation". Governs `location.formation: Snow Hill Island` and `location.member: Cape Lamb` on [*Morrosaurus antarcticus*](../../../genera/M/Morrosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- rozadilla2016a, p. 312: "The specimen here described was found at Cape Lamb Member, Snow Hill Formation, around 30 m below the Theropod found by Case, Martin, Chaney, and Reguero"; Pirrie and colleagues "restrict the spatial occurrence of this stratigraphic unit to Seymour (Marambio) Island and eastern Spath Peninsula", and the outcrops of "El Morro (The Naze) península" were "reassigned by Pirie et al. (1997) to the Snow Hill Island Formation".
- ely2019a, p. 3: the Comb Ridge section on the Naze Peninsula is "an outcrop of the Cape Lamb Member of the Snow Hill Island Formation on James Ross Island"; p. 14: *Morrosaurus* "was collected from the 'El Morro' locality at the base of the Naze Peninsula also from deposits of the Cape Lamb Mbr., some 30 m down section from the locality where Imperobator was collected".

**Ruled out.**
- *López de Bertodano Formation.* The describing paper's horizon line reads "Horizon: Cape Lamb Member of the Lopez de Bertodano Formation (Maastrichtian)" (rozadilla2016a, p. 312). That is the assignment of Del Valle and colleagues (1982), which the paper's own stratigraphic section rejects on the same page; the abstract also names the Snow Hill Island Formation (p. 311).

**Open.** Nothing.

# *Microceratus*

## Is the status of *Microceratus* in dispute?

*2026-10-16*

**Conclusion.** No; the dispute block is removed. Sereno (2000) declared the genus a nomen dubium and removed its best referred skeleton, which became *Graciliceratops*; later work follows him, and no paper read since defends the genus. Mateus (2008) replaced the preoccupied name *Microceratops* only because it remained in use. Governs `parent: Neoceratopsia` and the absence of a dispute on [*Microceratus*](../../../genera/M/Microceratus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sereno2000a: "Given the absence of any diagnostic features of the holotype material and the abundance of immature individuals at many Asian localities that have yielded ceratopsian remains, the genus *Microceratops* and the species *M. gobiensis* and *M. sulcidens* are regarded here as *nomina dubia*"; "There is no basis, therefore, for referral of this specimen to *Microceratops* or to the species *M. gobienisis*."
- mateus2008a: "*Microceratus* n. gen. … is proposed as the replacement"; "the name is still used by Le Loeuff et al. (2002), Lucas (2006), Alifanov (2003) and Xu et al. (2002), and such practice justifies the renaming of the genus."
- makovicky2006a: "Sereno (2000) recently reviewed the status of this taxon and separated it from Microceratops, which is a nomen dubium."

**Ruled out.**
- *A live dispute.* No paper read since 2008 argues for its validity.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which unit and age is the Microceratus holotype from?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Probably the lower part of the [Ulansuhai Formation](../../../stratigraphy/u/ulansuhai.yml), Santonian–Campanian, at Tsondolein-khuduk, Inner Mongolia. Governs `location.formation`, `location.region: CN-NM` and `stage: [Santonian, Campanian]` on *Microceratus gobiensis*.

**Evidence.**
- bohlin1953a, p. 34: "Microceratops gobiensis nov. gen., nov. sp.", from Tsondolein-khuduk (locality, p. 31).
- evans2021a, accepted manuscript, p. 20: "it seems likely that fossils from Tsondolien-Khuduk are from the lower part of the Ulansuhai Formation and below the Bayan Mandahu fossil horizon"; p. 10: "Tsondolien-Khuduk is located North of the Yellow River, and in the Nei Mongol".
- evans2021a, accepted manuscript, p. 20: "similar in age to either Baynshirenian or Barungoyotian (i.e., Santonian–Campanian), based on 'Microceratops'", relaying Lucas and Estep (1998).

**Ruled out.**
- *Minhe Formation, Gansu.* The locality was "first assigned to the Minhe Formation by Weishampel (1990, who cited a personal communication from N. J. Mateer)" (evans2021a, accepted manuscript, pp. 18–19); Weishampel and colleagues (2004) repeat "Minhe Formation (Bohlin 1953; Maryánska 1977; Mateer, pers. comm.)" (weishampel2004b, p. 598). The same compilation also lists *Microceratops gobiensis* under the Xinminbao Group, "Age: Barremian-Albian" (weishampel2004b, p. 567), contradicting itself.
- *Maastrichtian.* The only Maastrichtian is "Campanian-Maastrichtian (Mateer, pers. comm.)" with the Minhe assignment (weishampel2004b, p. 598).

**Open.** Evans and colleagues' assignment is hedged ("seems likely") and concerns the locality, not *Microceratus* by name.

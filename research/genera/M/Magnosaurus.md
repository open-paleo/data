# *Magnosaurus*

## How should the Magnosaurus horizon in the Wessex Inferior Oolite be recorded?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** As the [Inferior Oolite Group](../../../stratigraphy/i/inferior-oolite.yml) with the *Stephanoceras humphriesianum* Zone and Subzone, early Bajocian; no formation, member or part. Governs `location.group: Inferior Oolite`, the empty `location.part`, and `stage: [Bajocian]` on *Magnosaurus nethercombensis*.

**Evidence.**
- barron2012a, p. 23: "Apart from in the Wessex Basin (south Cotswolds to Dorset), the group is fully divided into formations", and "the succession is regarded herein as the indivisible Inferior Oolite Group"; p. 24: "no strong case has yet been made for any laterally persistent formal members", and "The chronostratigraphical 'Lower', 'Middle' and 'Upper Inferior Oolite' terms".
- benson2010b, p. 132: "collected from the Stephanoceras humphriesianum ammonite Zone and Subzone (Early Bajocian", relaying Waldman (1974).

**Ruled out.**
- *"Middle Inferior Oolite" as a part of the unit.* Paul (1988) and Walker (1964) give "Middle Inferior Oolite" (paul1988a, p. 293; walker1964a, p. 115: "Middle Inferior Oolite, Nether Combe, Sherborne, Dorset"), which Barron and colleagues treat as a chronostratigraphic term, not a lithostratigraphic part.
- *Inferior Oolite Formation.* Benson's wording, "Inferior Oolite Formation of Nethercombe", follows a mapping usage that Barron and colleagues accept for mapping (p. 23); the BGS Lexicon marks the Wessex "Inferior Oolite Formation" obsolete.

**Open.** Nothing.

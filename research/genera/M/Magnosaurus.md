# *Magnosaurus*

## Is Kuhn (1939) needed to settle the type species?

*2026-10-29*

**Conclusion.** No. This supersedes the Open item of the 2026-10-28 entry on how *M. nethercombensis* was fixed as the type species. Kuhn (1939) would matter only if it designated a species other than *nethercombensis*, and every work read gives *nethercombensis*: White (1973), Olshevsky (1991) and Benson (2010). White's (1973) designation stands as the earliest read. Governs `type_species: true` on *Magnosaurus nethercombensis* in [*Magnosaurus*](../../../genera/M/Magnosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- white1973a, p. 139: "*Magnosaurus* Huene, 1932 … Genotype: *nethercombensis*."
- olshevsky1991a, p. 107: "*M. nethercombensis* (von Huene, 1926) (Type)".
- benson2010b, p. 132: Huene "later made this the type species of the new genus *Magnosaurus*".

**Ruled out.**
- *Holding the question open for Kuhn (1939).* No work read points to a rival designation.

**Open.** Nothing.

## How was *M. nethercombensis* fixed as the type species?

*2026-10-28*

**Conclusion.** By subsequent designation, not by Huene (1932). Huene put three species in *Magnosaurus*, *M. woodwardi*, *M. (?) lydekkeri* and *M. nethercombensis*, and named no type species. White (1973) is the earliest designation read, "Genotype: *nethercombensis*", and Olshevsky (1991) agrees. Governs `type_species: true` on *Magnosaurus nethercombensis* in [*Magnosaurus*](../../../genera/M/Magnosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huene1932a, p. 220: the account of *M. nethercombensis* ends by setting it, "together with *Megalosaurus woodwardi*", apart from the typical genus "as the subgenus *Magnosaurus* n. subgen." (translated); the faunal list on p. 218 gives *woodwardi*, *(?) lydekkeri* and *nethercombensis* under *Magnosaurus*. No type species is named.
- white1973a, p. 139: "*Magnosaurus* Huene, 1932 … Genotype: *nethercombensis*." Read from the page image.
- olshevsky1991a, p. 107: "Genus: *Magnosaurus* von Huene, 1932 / *M. nethercombensis* (von Huene, 1926) (Type)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Huene (1932) as the designating work.* Benson (2010, benson2010b, p. 132) writes that Huene "made this the type species", but the 1932 text names none.

**Open.** Kuhn's *Fossilium Catalogus* (1939), which Maisch (2016, maisch2016a, p. 216) credits with the *Altispinax* genotype, may designate earlier than White (1973); it has not been read.

## How should the Magnosaurus horizon in the Wessex Inferior Oolite be recorded?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** As the [Inferior Oolite Group](../../../stratigraphy/i/inferior-oolite.yml) with the *Stephanoceras humphriesianum* Zone and Subzone, early Bajocian; no formation, member or part. Governs `location.group: Inferior Oolite`, the empty `location.part`, and `stage: [Bajocian]` on *Magnosaurus nethercombensis*.

**Evidence.**
- barron2012a, p. 23: "Apart from in the Wessex Basin (south Cotswolds to Dorset), the group is fully divided into formations", and "the succession is regarded herein as the indivisible Inferior Oolite Group"; p. 24: "no strong case has yet been made for any laterally persistent formal members", and "The chronostratigraphical 'Lower', 'Middle' and 'Upper Inferior Oolite' terms".
- benson2010b, p. 132: "collected from the Stephanoceras humphriesianum ammonite Zone and Subzone (Early Bajocian", relaying Waldman (1974).

**Ruled out.**
- *"Middle Inferior Oolite" as a part of the unit.* Paul (1988) and Walker (1964) give "Middle Inferior Oolite" (paul1988a, p. 293; walker1964a, p. 115: "Middle Inferior Oolite, Nether Combe, Sherborne, Dorset"), which Barron and colleagues treat as a chronostratigraphic term, not a lithostratigraphic part.
- *Inferior Oolite Formation.* Benson's wording, "Inferior Oolite Formation of Nethercombe", follows a mapping usage that Barron and colleagues accept for mapping (p. 23); the BGS Lexicon marks the Wessex "Inferior Oolite Formation" obsolete.

**Open.** Nothing.

# *Micropachycephalosaurus*

## Which formation of the Wangshi Group is the Micropachycephalosaurus holotype from?

*2026-08-30*

**Conclusion.** None is recorded; the record stops at `group: Wangshi`. Dong (1978) names only the "Wangshi Formation", the whole succession that is now a group of four formations, and the one formation-level assignment found, Jones's (2026) Jiangjunding Formation, comes from a compilation that cites Dong (1978) for the specimen. Governs `location.group: Wangshi` and the empty `location.formation` on [*Micropachycephalosaurus hongtuyanensis*](../../../genera/M/Micropachycephalosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dong1978b, p. 225 (translated): the specimen "was collected in the summer of 1972, when we observed a section at the red earth cliff to the southwest of Laiyang Railway Station", in "the Wangshi Formation of Laiyang".
- zhang2019a, p. 2: "the Late Cretaceous Wangshi Group, which comprises the Xingezhuang, Jiangjunding, Jingangkou, and Changwangpu formations (Hu et al. 2001)"; "all Laiyang hadrosauriods have been collected from the Jiangjunding and Jingangkou formations".
- jones2026a, p. 410: "IVPP V5542—partial pelvis and cranial fragments (Dong, 1978)", "Jiangjunding Fm., China".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Whether the red earth cliff exposes the Jiangjunding Formation. Laiyang yields dinosaurs from at least two formations of the group, and Jones gives no source for the formation. A paper placing the cliff, or IVPP V5542, in one formation of the group would settle it.

## What stage is the *Micropachycephalosaurus* holotype?

*2026-08-05*

**Conclusion.** Campanian. The describing paper gives the new species a Campanian age, and Butler and Zhao (2009) and Sullivan (2006) keep it. Governs `stage: [Campanian]` on [*Micropachycephalosaurus hongtuyanensis*](../../../genera/M/Micropachycephalosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dong1978b, p. 228, English abstract: "The age of new species is upper Cretaceous (Campanian)."
- butler2009b, p. 64: "Wangshi Formation (Late Cretaceous: Campanian, Dong, 1978; Buffetaut and Tong, 1995; late Campanian, Sullivan, 2006)".
- sullivan2006a, p. 361: "from the Campanian Wangshi Group (Shandong) ... which is considered to be late Campanian (late Barungoyotian) age (Lucas and Estep, 1998)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Coniacian–Santonian.* The record's earlier value; no source read here gives it for this taxon.
- *Campanian–Maastrichtian.* Jones (2026) gives "Jiangjunding Fm., China, Late Cretaceous, Campanian/Maastrichtian" (jones2026a, p. 410) and cites Dong (1978) for the specimen, who gives Campanian.

**Open.** Whether the holotype is from the Jiangjunding Formation (see the entry above). If it is, Borinder and colleagues' (2021) late Campanian–early Maastrichtian for that formation, listed in [Wangshi](../../stratigraphy/w/wangshi.md), would bear on the stage. That paper has not been read.

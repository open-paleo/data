# *Metriacanthosaurus*

## Which unit and site is the Metriacanthosaurus holotype from?

*2026-08-30*

**Conclusion.** The Weymouth Member of the Oxford Clay, at Jordan's Cliff, Overcombe, north of Weymouth. Huene (1926) gives only "the Oxford Clay, 1½ miles north of Weymouth"; the site name is Carrano and colleagues' (2012), who cite Huene and Walker for it. Governs `location.formation: Oxford Clay`, `location.member: Weymouth` and `location.locality` on [*Metriacanthosaurus parkeri*](../../../genera/M/Metriacanthosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huene1926a, p. 477: "These bones, from the Oxford Clay, 1½ miles north of Weymouth, Dorset".
- carrano2012a, p. 237: "Jordan's Cliff, Overcombe, 1.5 km north of Weymouth, Dorset, England; *Cardioceras cordatus* Zone, Weymouth Member, Upper Oxford Clay; early Oxfordian, Late Jurassic (Huene 1926a, Walker 1964)".
- naish2007a, p. 495: "Metriacanthosaurus parkeri from the Weymouth Member (formerly Upper Oxford Clay) of Weymouth, Dorset".

**Ruled out.**
- *Corallian Oolite Formation.* The systematic table of *The Dinosauria* gives "Corallian Oolite Formation (Dorset), England" (holtz2004a, p. 73, Table 4.1), and the distribution chapter lists the species under the Corallian Oolite Formation for Dorset (weishampel2004b, p. 558); these are one 2004 statement. The same chapter's text says "M. parkeri comes from the Upper Oxford Clay of Weymouth, England" (holtz2004a, p. 99), and Naish and Martill (2007) write that "Holtz et al. (2004a) and Weishampel et al. (2004) incorrectly stated that M. parkeri is from the Corallian Oolite Formation" (naish2007a, p. 495).
- *1.5 km north of Weymouth.* Carrano and colleagues' distance cites Huene, whose 1½ miles is about 2.4 km; the kilometer figure reads as a unit slip rather than a second measurement (our inference).

**Open.** Where the name Jordan's Cliff, Overcombe comes from. Huene (1926) does not use it, and Carrano and colleagues cite only Huene and Walker for the occurrence. Benson and Barrett's (2009) review of Dorset dinosaurs, not read, may be the source.

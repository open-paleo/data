# *Minmi*

## Is *Minmi paravertebra* valid, and where does it sit?

*2026-10-24*

**Conclusion.** Its validity is disputed, and it is placed at Thyreophora incertae sedis. Arbour and Currie (2015) call it a nomen dubium, while Leahey and colleagues (2015, 2019) keep it distinct and Raven and colleagues (2023) score it on that basis; no consensus on its own validity has been reached. Raven and colleagues recover the holotype outside Eurypoda in their preferred tree, and inside Ankylosauria in others, so Thyreophora is the nearest clade every result accepts. Supersedes the 2026-10-09 change to nomen dubium. Governs `status: disputed`, `parent: Thyreophora` and `placement: incertae sedis` on [*Minmi paravertebra*](../../../genera/M/Minmi.yml).

**Evidence.**
- arbour2015a, p. 41: "there are no diagnostic characters unique to the holotype of Minmi paravertebra, and this taxon must therefore be considered a nomen dubium."
- leahey2015a, p. 35: "we still consider Minmi paravertebra sufficiently different to other ankylosaurians, particularly within Australia and other Gondwanan landmasses, to represent a distinct genus."
- leahey2019a, p. 139: "like M. paravertebra and K. ieversi, the new taxon is a basal ankylosaurian."
- raven2023a, p. 9: "Eurypoda is monophyletic with Scelidosaurus and Minmi outside of the stegosaur/ankylosaur split"; p. 24: "the recovery of Minmi outside of Eurypoda is likely due to a lack of ankylosaur synapomorphies."

**Ruled out.**
- *Nomen dubium.* It rests on one paper, against two that keep the taxon.
- *Ankylosauria.* The authors who recover it outside Eurypoda attribute that to missing data, but it is their preferred tree; the nearest-agreed-parent rule takes the wider clade.

**Open.** The reappraisal of the holotype that Leahey and colleagues (2015) announced.

# *Massospondylus*

## Does the *Massospondylus carinatus* neotype keep Barrett and colleagues' coordinate?

*2026-10-11*

**Conclusion.** Yes. The pair is not a landmark near the site but the authors' identification of it: the one hill on the farm with fossiliferous outcrop, where vertebrate remains were collected. Their "most likely source" is a hedged reading that is the paper's own, which counts as it does for a stage. Kitching and Raath (1984) give only the farm's position. Governs `location.coordinates: [-28.962739, 27.430377]` on [*Massospondylus carinatus*](../../../genera/M/Massospondylus.yml), and settles the **Open** line of the 2026-08-29 entry.

**Evidence.**
- barrett2019a, p. 118: "significant vertebrate remains were collected from this hill at approximately 28.962739°S, 27.430377°E, at an elevation of 1712 m, and we consider this area to be the most likely source of the neotype".
- kitching1984a, table: "Bormansdrift (133) | 2827CD MEKOATLENGSNEK | 28° 57'S 27° 26'E | Elliot Fm. (U) : *Pachygenelus* sp., cf. *Massospondylus* sp.", a position for the whole farm.

**Ruled out.**
- *No coordinate.* The rule excludes positions for other places; this one is printed for the place the authors take the neotype to come from.
- *Kitching and Raath's farm position.* It marks the farm to the minute, about 1.4 km from the hill, not the site.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which site is the *Massospondylus carinatus* type locality, and what does its coordinate mark?

*2026-08-29*

**Conclusion.** Bormansdrift 133 Farm, Clocolan District, the site of the neotype BP/1/4934. The coordinate is the position Barrett and colleagues (2019) give for the one hill on the farm with fossiliferous outcrop, which they consider the most likely source of the neotype; the 1980 excavation itself was not recorded to a point in any source read. Governs `location.locality: Bormansdrift 133` and `location.coordinates: [-28.962739, 27.430377]` on [*Massospondylus carinatus*](../../../genera/M/Massospondylus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- barrett2019a, p. 118: "BP/1/4934 was collected in March 1980 from the upper Elliot Formation of Bormansdrift 133 Farm, in the Clocolan District"; "only one small hill on this farm preserves suitable outcrop for fossil prospecting … significant vertebrate remains were collected from this hill at approximately 28.962739°S, 27.430377°E, at an elevation of 1712 m, and we consider this area to be the most likely source of the neotype".
- barrett2019a, p. 119, Locality and horizon: "Bormansdrift 133 Farm, Clocholan District, Free State Province, South Africa".

**Ruled out.**
- *Beauchef Abbey 215, Harrismith District.* This is the farm of Owen's five syntype vertebrae, destroyed in 1941: "These specimens were from the farm Beauchef Abbey 215 in the Harrismith District" (barrett2019a, p. 116). The neotype replaced them, so the type locality is the neotype's.
- *"Bornmansdrift" as the farm's spelling.* Barrett and colleagues print it once ("also on Bornmansdrift 133", p. 118) against five uses of Bormansdrift, including the Locality and horizon line.

**Open.** Kitching and Raath (1984), cited for "locality details" (barrett2019a, p. 118), have not been read and may record where on the farm the neotype was dug.

## Which specimen is the *Massospondylus carinatus* type?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** The neotype BP/1/4934. Owen's syntypes were destroyed, and Yates and Barrett (2010) proposed BP/1/4934 in their place. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [BP/1/4934]` and `specimen_type: neotype` on [*Massospondylus carinatus*](../../../genera/M/Massospondylus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- barrett2019a, p. 117: "as the original specimens were no longer accessible, Yates & Barrett (2010) formally proposed BP/1/4934 as a neotype for M. carinatus, following the earlier suggestions of Sues et al. (2004) and Barrett (2009)."

**Ruled out.**
- *BMNH R.8171, MT 124 and BP/1/5347A.* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi use these for *M. carinatus* in their speed tables: "Massospondylus carinatus (M. harriesi) BMNH R.8171" and "(M. browni) MT 124" (molina-pérez2020a, p. 167), and the embryo BP/1/5347A (p. 168). They are referred specimens, two of them filed under junior synonyms, not the type.

**Open.** Nothing.

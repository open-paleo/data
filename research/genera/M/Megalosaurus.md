# *Megalosaurus*

## Is the Stonesfield Slate the *Megalosaurus* type formation?

*2026-08-06*

**Conclusion.** No. The Stonesfield Slate is one or more informal horizons inside the Taynton Limestone Formation, which is the formation. Governs `location.formation: Taynton Limestone` on [*Megalosaurus bucklandii*](../../../genera/M/Megalosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- benson2010c, p. 884: the type horizon is "One or more discrete horizons ('Stonesfield Slate'; Boneham & Wyatt, 1993) within the Taynton Limestone Formation (middle Bathonian Procerites progracilis ammonite zone)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Stonesfield Slate as the formation.* *The Dinosauria* gives "Stonesfield Slate (Oxfordshire), England, middle Bathonian" (holtz2004a, p. 72, Table 4.1). That is the informal horizon name, not a formation. Jones (2026) gives "Taynton Ls. Fm." (jones2026c, p. 497).
- *Stonesfield Slate as a member or bed.* Benson leaves the number of horizons open and fixes none of them, so the record names no bed.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What makes up the *Megalosaurus* type series?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** The lectotype is the right dentary with the part and counterpart of the rock that held it. The rest of the material Buckland (1824) figured is paralectotypes, but none can be referred to *M. bucklandii* with certainty, and the species is restricted to the lectotype. The paralectotypes are described in the lectotype's `type_specimen.notes` and get no `notable_specimens` entries. Governs `type_specimen.notes` on [*Megalosaurus bucklandii*](../../../genera/M/Megalosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- benson2008c, Systematic palaeontology: "Lectotype. OUMNH J.13505, a partial right dentary. OUMNH J. 13505b and 13505c, blocks of matrix containing the impressions of the lateral and medial sides of the dentary respectively and fragments of the superficial layers of the bone."
- benson2008c, Systematic palaeontology: "as none of the paralectotypes can be convincingly referred to the same taxon as the lectotype dentary we follow Allain and Chure (2002) in restricting the hypodigm of Megalosaurus bucklandii to the lectotype"; "As there is evidence for more than one large theropod taxon in the Stonesfield fauna ... none of these elements can be referred to M. bucklandii with any certainty."
- nicholls2025a, section 2.1: the part and counterpart "contain fossilised bone and enamel material and thus form part of the lectotype specimen."
- nicholls2025a, section 2.2: the material "was not found in association, represents more than one individual, and was acquired at different times"; "Of the 15 specimens figured in the publication, ten are known to be extant in the Museum's collections."

**Ruled out.**
- *The paralectotype sacrum OUMNH J.13576 as a notable specimen of* M. bucklandii. Nicholls and colleagues (2025) call it crucial to Buckland's interpretation, but Benson and colleagues (2008) exclude it and every other paralectotype from the species, so it is not a specimen of the taxon.
- *Listing the paralectotypes' catalog numbers in the record.* The schema has no paralectotype field, the specimens are not referable to the species, and the two papers do not agree on the numbers: Benson and colleagues (2008) give the right ilium as OUMNH J.29881 and two ribs as J.13580 and J.29792, where Nicholls and colleagues (2025) give the ilium as J.13560 (which their section 2.1 also calls the matrix that supported the ilium) and one rib as J.13585.
- *Molnar, Osmólska and Dodson as the authors of the 1990 Carnosauria chapter.* Nicholls and colleagues (2025) print that in their reference list; Benson and colleagues (2008) and the store give Molnar, Kurzanov and Dong.
- *A horizon for the type series from the sacrum.* Nicholls and colleagues (2025) relay Powell and Howlett (2025) that the sacrum may come from veins above the Stonesfield Slate. That concerns one paralectotype, not the lectotype. Benson and colleagues (2008) give the lectotype's horizon as "Unknown horizon within the Taynton Limestone Formation", agreeing with the entry above.

**Open.** The catalog form of Oxford numbers. Benson and colleagues (2008) print "OUMNH J.13505" and Nicholls and colleagues (2025) "OUMNH PAL-J.13505", while the record has "OUM J13505"; Oxford ids across the data are normalized as one batch, not here.

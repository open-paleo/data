# *Megaraptor*

## Which formation is the *Megaraptor* holotype from?

*2026-08-06*

**Conclusion.** The Portezuelo Formation. Novas (1998) gives the Río Neuquén Formation, which later work ranks as a subgroup containing the Portezuelo, and Calvo and colleagues (2004) place Novas's holotype in the Portezuelo. Governs `location.formation: Portezuelo` on [*Megaraptor namunhuaiquii*](../../../genera/M/Megaraptor.yml).

**Evidence.**
- novas1998a, p. 4: "Locality and Horizon—Upper Cretaceous (Turonian-Coniacian ...), Rio Neuquen Formation, Sierra del Portezuelo, Neuquen Province, Argentina."
- calvo2004b, p. 565: Novas's specimen is "from the Portezuelo Formation of the Río Neuquén Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *Río Neuquén Formation.* *The Dinosauria* gives "Río Neuquén Formation (Neuquén), Argentina" (weishampel2004a, p. 199, Table 10.1), the describing paper's name under the older ranks. It is now the subgroup above the Portezuelo (see [Neuquén Group subgroups](../../topics/neuquén-group-subgroups.md)). Jones (2026) gives "Portezuelo Fm." (jones2026c, p. 499).

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does the braincase study of MUCPv 595 make Paulina-Carabajal and Porfiri (2025) a redescription of *Megaraptor*?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** No. The paper redescribes the braincase of one referred juvenile, MUCPv 595, and proposes no diagnosis, so it does not qualify as `described_in` for [*Megaraptor namunhuaiquii*](../../../genera/M/Megaraptor.yml). It is cited on the specimen's `notable_specimens` entry instead, with Porfiri and colleagues (2014), who describe the specimen.

**Evidence.**
- paulina-carabajal2025a, main text: "Here, we re-describe the braincase of Megaraptor namunhuaiquii Novas, 1998, incorporating new information on its endocranial morphology and non-preserved soft tissues (brain, inner ear, and cranial pneumatic sinuses) using X-ray CT scans."
- The paper has no Diagnosis section. Its only statement close to one is that the prootic recess "could be a unique feature of Megaraptor within Megaraptoridae".
- The entry's category is `complementary` rather than `sensory`, because what makes the specimen notable is the skull itself: "This specimen offers for the first time information about the cranial anatomy of Megaraptor and of the snout and braincase of megaraptorans" (porfiri2014a, main text). The braincase's sensory anatomy is one part of that.

**Ruled out.**
- *`sensory` as the category.* It fits Paulina-Carabajal and Porfiri (2025) alone, not the specimen.
- *`ontogeny` as the category.* The specimen is a juvenile of about six years by bone histology (porfiri2014a, main text), but the description and later work treat it chiefly as the source of the skull.

**Open.** Nothing.

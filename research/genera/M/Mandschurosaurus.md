# *Mandschurosaurus*

## What is the Mandschurosaurus holotype's number, and how much of the mount is original?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** Riabinin catalogued it as No. 1644 of the Museum of the Geological Committee (now the Chernyshev museum, St. Petersburg); the mounted skeleton is heavily restored and includes bone from more than one individual. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id`, `institution` and the `material` note on *Mandschurosaurus amurensis*.

**Evidence.**
- riabinin1930a, p. 27: the comparative-measurements table heads its *Mandschurosaurus amurensis* column with No. 1644, Museum of the Geological Committee (a table header).
- riabinin1930a, p. 6: "количество гипса достигло в скелете 35%" ("the amount of plaster in the skeleton reached 35%", translated), quoting his 1925 account; the skeleton "был монтирован в Музее Геологического Комитета" ("was mounted in the Museum of the Geological Committee", translated) in 1925.
- bolotsky2014a, p. 318: the mount is "on display in the F. N. Chernishev Scientific Research Geological Institute, St. Petersburg".

**Ruled out.**
- *A single individual throughout.* Riabinin calls the mount a restoration of "единого образца" ("a single specimen", translated; p. 6) but assigns the right femur to "другому, более мощному индивидууму" ("another, more robust individual", translated; p. 25).

**Open.** How many individuals. Godefroit and colleagues read Riabinin as admitting that "the holotype skeleton is clearly reconstructed from several individuals" (godefroit2000a, p. 877); Riabinin himself names a second individual for one bone only.

## Is the *Mandschurosaurus* type locality the Jiayin (Longgushan) site, and does its printed position apply?

*2026-08-29*

**Conclusion.** No, as far as the sources show. Riabinin's material came from the Belye Kruchi ("White Cliffs") section between Kasatkino and Sagibovo, excavated in 1916 and 1917. Godefroit and colleagues (2011) print a position for the Jiayin (Longgushan) locality, excavated from 1975, and say only that Belye Kruchi "roughly corresponds" to Gurov's 1902 discovery spot near Jiayin. Governs `location.locality: Belye Kruchi` and the empty `location.coordinates` on [*Mandschurosaurus amurensis*](../../../genera/M/Mandschurosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- godefroit2011a, p. 162: "Jiayin ( Longgushan; N48° 51' 27. 6″/E130° 14' 37. 7″)", the heading of the locality's account; Renngarten studied "a geological section opened in the Belyie Kruchi' ( White Cliffs') ,. . . on the right shore of the Amur River … between the villages Kasatkino and Sagibovo' ( Riabinin,1925,fig. 1) . This place roughly corresponds to Gurov's discovery spot"; Gurov's spot "is located in the vicinity of the village of Jiayin".
- godefroit2011a, p. 163: "Between 1975 and 1979 the Heilongjiang Provincial Museum ( Harbin) undertook new excavations along the Chinese banks of the Amur River at Longgushan near Jiayin"; "The Jiayin dinosaur locality is situated along the cliffs on the right side of the Amur River … 8 km west of the town of Jiayin. Within these cliffs, also known as the Longgushan section".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Longgushan position, 48.8577, 130.2438.* The record once carried this pair. It is the position of the modern Longgushan locality, tied to Riabinin's section only through two approximations: Belye Kruchi roughly corresponds to Gurov's spot, which is in the vicinity of Jiayin.
- *Jiayin as the locality.* Later authors' name for the area, not Riabinin's for the site.

**Open.** Whether the Belye Kruchi section lies within the Longgushan cliffs. Riabinin's 1925 map (his fig. 1, cited by Godefroit and colleagues) would settle it; that paper has not been read for this note.

# *Marmarospondylus*

## Where were *Marmarospondylus* and its species named?

*2026-11-12*

**Conclusion.** Both in the same paper, Owen's monograph keyed 1874: he names the species *Bothriospondylus robustus* and proposes *Marmarospondylus* conditionally, as the genus to use if more of the skeleton should warrant one. A conditional proposal made before 1961 is available (ICZN Art. 11.5.1), so the genus dates from that paper too, and the record gives it as both the species' and the genus's `erected_in` (user ruling, 2026-11-12). Governs `erected_as: Bothriospondylus robustus` on the species and the genus-level `erected_in: owen1874a` on [*Marmarospondylus*](../../../genera/M/Marmarospondylus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- owen1874a, pp. 21–22: "propose to call it *Bothriospondylus robustus*"; "unless, indeed, modifications of other parts of the skeleton than are now known of *Bothriospondylus robustus* should be deemed to support a distinct generic name (*Marmarospondylus*, e. g.)".
- mannion2010a: Owen "also named a second species (*B. robustus*) … although within this same article, he also suggested a possible new generic name for it (*Marmarospondylus*)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Leaving out `erected_as`.* The species was named in *Bothriospondylus*, and the combination *Marmarospondylus robustus* is not printed in the paper.

**Open.** The paper's date. The store keys it 1874, while Mannion (2010), McIntosh (1990) and Olshevsky (1991) give 1875, and Owen's own *Omosaurus* monograph cites it as "1875" (owen1875a).

## Is the *Marmarospondylus* holotype from Bradford, Wiltshire?

*2026-08-29*

**Conclusion.** No source places it there. Owen gives the vertebra only as from "the Forest-marble of Wiltshire". Bradford enters his account with a different specimen: a tooth sent by Channing Pearce of Bradford, which Owen declines to refer to *Bothriospondylus*. Governs the empty `location.locality` and `location.region: GB-WIL` on [*Marmarospondylus robustus*](../../../genera/M/Marmarospondylus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- owen1874a, p. 21: "The earliest indications of the genus were afforded by fragmentary vertebræ or vertebral centrums from the Forest-marble of Wiltshire, of which the least mutilated is figured in Pl. VI."
- owen1874a, p. 22: "Mr. Channing Pearce, of Bradford, Wilts, a tooth from the Forest-marble near that town"; "I feel the insufficiency of the present grounds for referring these teeth to any otherwise defined species or genus of Dinosaur."
- mannion2010a, p. 279: Owen "named a second species ('B. robustus') from Wiltshire (Text-fig. 1) based on a single dorsal vertebra (NHM 22428) from the Forest Marble Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Bradford-on-Avon.* Bradford is the collector's town and the source of the tooth (owen1874a, p. 22), not of the vertebra.

**Open.** Where in Wiltshire the vertebra was found; no source read records it.

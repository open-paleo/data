# *Mussaurus*

## What stages does *Mussaurus* take?

*2026-10-13*

**Conclusion.** Sinemurian and Pliensbachian. Pol and colleagues (2021) date tuffaceous siltstones within the vertebrate-bearing interval at 192.78 and 192.74 Ma, which they call Sinemurian on the older scale; on the current chart the Pliensbachian begins at 192.9 Ma, so the dates fall at the boundary and the stage takes both sides. Governs `stage: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian]` on [*Mussaurus patagonicus*](../../../genera/M/Mussaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- pol2021a: "U–Pb geochronology (CA-ID-TIMS method) from tufaceous siltstones intercalated with the vertebrate-bearing interval that yielded two overlapping dates of 192.78±0.14 Ma and 192.74±0.14 Ma … now assign an Early Jurassic (Sinemurian) age".
- hesselbo2020a: the base of the Pliensbachian at 192.9 Ma.

**Ruled out.**
- *Sinemurian alone.* The dates sit at the boundary on the current chart.
- *Norian.* The older reading from the *Dicroidium* flora, which the dates replace.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which specimen is the *Mussaurus* holotype?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** PVL 4068, from the describing paper. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [PVL 4068]` on [*Mussaurus patagonicus*](../../../genera/M/Mussaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bonaparte1979b, Systematics, read in English translation: "Holotype specimen, PVL 4068, composed of most of a skeleton".

**Ruled out.**
- *PVL uncat (1) (6/73).* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi give "Mussaurus patagonicus (juvenile) PVL uncat (1) (6/73)" in a table of running speeds (molina-pérez2020a, p. 167). The same table gives PVL 4068 for a neonate, so the uncataloged specimen is a second individual.

**Open.** The Spanish original has not been read; the page is not given because the translation's folios are not the journal's.

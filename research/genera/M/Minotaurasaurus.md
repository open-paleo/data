# *Minotaurasaurus*

## What does Alicea and Loewen's abstract add on the *Minotaurasaurus* provenance?

*2026-10-24*

**Conclusion.** A dated second specimen, but no horizon for the holotype. The abstract, now read, refers a skull and neck from the Xanadu site at Ukhaa Tolgod (Djadokhta) to *Minotaurasaurus* and calls the holotype illegally acquired; it keeps "Mongolia or China" for the purchased skull. This settles the 2026-08 entry's Open item about the unread abstract; where the holotype came from stays open. Governs the `notable_specimens` entry for MAE 98 179 and the description on [*Minotaurasaurus ramachandrani*](../../../genera/M/Minotaurasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- alicea2013a, p. 76: "Minotaurasaurus ramachandrani was named from an illegally acquired specimen that was available at the Tuscon Gem and Mineral show. As with most specimens of uncertain provenance, the stratigraphy of the specimen was assigned to "Gobi Desert of either Mongolia or China.""; the new specimen comes "from the Xanadu site of the Uhkaa Tolgod locality within the Late Campanian Djodokta Formation"; "This new specimen places *Minotaurasaurus* in stratigraphic and geographic context".

**Ruled out.**
- *Mongolia as an established origin of the holotype.* Only Jones (2026) states it (jones2026a, p. 115); the primaries leave Mongolia or China.

**Open.** Where the holotype skull came from.

## What do the Minotaurasaurus country and region rest on?

*2026-08-30*

**Conclusion.** On the two competing referrals, not on any record of where the skull was found. It was bought with no find site, and its describers allow either Mongolia or China. Penkalski and Tumanova (2017) give the Djadokhta Formation of the Nemegt Basin, from a referred skull collected at Ukhaa Tolgod; Arbour and colleagues (2014) give the Baruungoyot Formation of the Mongolian Gobi, from its referral to *Tarchia kielanae* of Khulsan. Both put it in Mongolia, and both named sites lie in Ömnögovi (our inference from geography; neither paper names the aimag). Governs `location.country: MN`, `location.region: MN-053` and the empty `location.formation` on [*Minotaurasaurus ramachandrani*](../../../genera/M/Minotaurasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- miles2009a, p. 65: "The skull was originally purchased by V. S. Ramachandran"; the matrix "indicates a location in the Gobi Desert of either Mongolia or China".
- penkalski2017a, accepted manuscript, p. 19: "Type locality and horizon: Djadokhta Formation, Nemegt Basin"; p. 20: "MAE 98 179, a skull, axis, and first cervical half-ring, from the Djadokhta Formation at Ukhaa Tolgod, Mongolia".
- arbour2014b, p. 648: the referral to *Tarchia kielanae*, "the holotype of which was collected from the Baruungoyot Formation at Khulsan, strongly suggests that this specimen derives from the Baruungoyot Formation of the Mongolian Gobi".

**Ruled out.**
- *A documented Djadokhta horizon.* Penkalski and Tumanova's horizon line reads as a type-locality statement, but it rests on the referred skull, as their own discussion shows: "Assuming that MAE 98 179 is indeed from the Djadokhta, that formation hosted at least two ankylosaurids" (penkalski2017a, accepted manuscript, p. 15).

**Open.** Where the skull came from; China stays possible on the describing paper's evidence. Alicea and Loewen's (2013) abstract on new *Minotaurasaurus* material from the Djadokhta, which both later papers cite, has not been read.

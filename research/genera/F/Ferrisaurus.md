# *Ferrisaurus*

## Was the *Ferrisaurus* holotype found in the Tatlatui Member of the Tango Creek Formation?

*2026-08-23*

**Conclusion.** Probably, but the horizon was reconstructed, not observed. The bones were found loose in talus on the BC Rail grade in 1971. Arbour and colleagues (2020) relocated the probable source in 2017 from the collector's field notes, a turtle shell fragment preserved like the dinosaur, and matrix on the bones that matches the outcrop: the Turtle Site, a few hundred meters east of Birdflat Creek. They assign that outcrop to the Tatlatui Member on its lithology. Governs `location.formation: Tango Creek`, `location.member: Tatlatui` and `location.locality` on [*Ferrisaurus sustutensis*](../../../genera/F/Ferrisaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- arbour2019a, p. 4, Systematic Palaeontology: "the bones were found loose in the rubble during construction along the BC Rail line"; "Formation and Age: Tatlatui Member, Tango Creek Formation, Sustut Group".
- arbour2020a, accepted manuscript, p. 8, Results: the specimen "was discovered in a talus slope on the BC Rail grade near the confluence of Birdflat Creek and the Sustut River".
- arbour2020a, accepted manuscript, p. 17, Summary and conclusions: they consider "the presence of vertebrate fossils at a location matching the 1971 field notes to be compelling evidence in favour of the Turtle Site as the original collection site for RBCM P900".
- arbour2020a, accepted manuscript, pp. 14–15, Discussion: "The Turtle Site to the east of Birdflat Creek is in an area currently mapped as Brothers Peak Formation (McMechan et al. 2007)"; "We prefer the second hypothesis, because the lithology at this site more closely matches that of the Tatlatui Member of the Tango Creek Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *The lower Brothers Peak Formation, on the matrix.* Arbour and Graves (2008) assigned the specimen there from thin sections of the matrix, as Arbour and colleagues report it (arbour2020a, accepted manuscript, p. 12, Geologic age of the Sustut River dinosaur). Arbour and Evans replace that reading on the 2017 field data: the specimen "is likely from the Tango Creek Formation, rather than the Brothers Peak Formation as originally reported" (arbour2019a, p. 2, Introduction). The Brothers Peak itself remains the other reading for the Turtle Site (see Open).

**Open.** Whether the Turtle Site is in the Tango Creek or the Brothers Peak. Arbour and colleagues give both readings: its late Maastrichtian palynoflora is "younger than that reported for the Tango Creek Formation anywhere in the basin", and the area is mapped as Brothers Peak. They prefer the Tango Creek on lithology. A dated section through the Tango Creek–Brothers Peak contact near Birdflat Creek would settle it. Arbour and Graves (2008) has not been read.

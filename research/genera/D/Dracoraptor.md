# *Dracoraptor*

## What stages does *Dracoraptor* take?

*2026-10-14*

**Conclusion.** Rhaetian and Hettangian, for the holotype and for the Bull Cliff Member it comes from. Martill and colleagues (2016) argue for an Early Hettangian age while granting that these beds "have traditionally been regarded as Late Rhaetian age"; Simms and colleagues (2004) put the base of the Jurassic at Lavernock at the first *Psiloceras planorbis*, about 1.4 m above the top of the Bull Cliff Member, and call the beds below it Triassic. That the Bull Cliff at Lavernock lies within the beds Simms and colleagues call Triassic is our inference from their section. Hillebrandt and colleagues (2013) expect the ratified boundary "in the lowest few meters of the Blue Lias Formation", which could fall on either side of this level. Both readings are the authors' own, so the stage takes both. Governs `period` and `stage: [Rhaetian, Hettangian]` on [*Dracoraptor hanigani*](../../../genera/D/Dracoraptor.yml), and `stages` on the [Bull Cliff Member](../../../stratigraphy/b/bull-cliff.yml).

**Evidence.**
- martill2016a, p. 7: the horizon "lies below the first occurrence of the ammonite Psiloceras planorbis"; "these strata have traditionally been regarded as Late Rhaetian age"; p. 9: "the new dinosaur should be regarded as being of Early Hettangian age", and "it may never be possible to define the T/J boundary" in the British Isles.
- simms2004a, p. 115: "The first Psiloceras planorbis, marking the base of the Jurassic System, was recorded about 1.4 m above the Dual Bed (Hodges, 1994)", the Dual Bed being the base of the bed above the Bull Cliff Member; p. 23: the Lias Group takes in "the uppermost beds of the Triassic System (the 'Pre-Planorbis Beds' of earlier accounts)".
- hillebrandt2013a, p. 189: "The boundary should be expected to occur in the lowest few meters of the Blue Lias Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Hettangian alone.* It takes Martill and colleagues' argued reading and drops Simms and colleagues' reading of the same beds.
- *Hillebrandt and colleagues' sentence as support for the boundary at the base of the formation.* Martill and colleagues quote it so; it places the boundary within the lowest few metres, not at their base.

**Open.** Where the ratified boundary falls at Lavernock.

## Which bed of the Blue Lias is the *Dracoraptor* holotype from?

*2026-08-20*

**Conclusion.** The Bull Cliff Member, with no bed. The holotype was collected from fallen blocks at the foot of the cliff at Lavernock Point, and Martill and colleagues (2016) match it on lithology and bed thickness to bed 7 or bed 9 of their log without choosing between them. Governs `location.member: Bull Cliff` and the empty `location.bed` and `location.part` on [*Dracoraptor hanigani*](../../../genera/D/Dracoraptor.yml).

**Evidence.**
- martill2016a, p. 10, Locality and horizon: "collected from several limestone and mudstone blocks among debris from a small rock fall at the base of the cliff on the east side of Lavernock Point"; "the dinosaur comes from either bed 7 or bed 9 (Fig 4), of the Bull Cliff Member".
- martill2016a, p. 4, Fig. 3 caption: "The new specimen is most likely from the higher of two limestones indicated in yellow ... but it was recovered from the fallen blocks in front of the exposure."
- martill2016a, p. 7, Stratigraphic Position and Age: "constrained on lithological grounds to one of two limestone horizons within the Blue Lias Formation of the Lower Lias Group (Fig 4), and specifically within the lower Bull Cliff Member".

**Ruled out.**
- *The higher bed alone, from the figure caption.* The caption's "most likely" points to the higher of the two limestones (bed 9, if the two are beds 7 and 9, which is our reading of the log). The Locality and horizon section does not carry the preference and gives both beds.

**Open.** Whether "the lower Bull Cliff Member" places the two beds in the lower part of the member, which would give `part: lower`, or only names the member low in the formation. The paper uses the qualifier once and names the member without it elsewhere; the positions of beds 7 and 9 on its log (Fig. 4) against the member's base and top would settle it.

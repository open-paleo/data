# *Dysganus*

## Is the status of *Dysganus* in dispute, and what is its type?

*2026-10-17*

**Conclusion.** No dispute: the block is removed and the parent stays Ceratopsia. Coombs (1988), the one redescription, finds every designated type a single worn ceratopsian tooth and all four species nomina dubia, and later work agrees. The hadrosaurid material the removed block mentioned belongs to Cope's original mixed series, not to the designated types. The type stays a lectotype: the type tooth was chosen from the original series, which Coombs's figure captions call a neotype, a term for a replacement chosen when the original material is lost. Governs `parent: Ceratopsia`, `type_specimen.specimen_type: lectotype` and the absence of a dispute on [*Dysganus*](../../../genera/D/Dysganus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- coombs1988a: "The designated types of Dysganus encaustus, D. bicarinatus, D. haydenianus, and D. peiganus are each a single, worn, fragmentary ceratopsian tooth"; "the genus and all four species are here regarded as nomina dubia"; "W. D. Matthew designated as the type an individual tooth within each of the original suites of material (fide Lull and Wright, 1942)"; Figure 1 caption: "the neotype of D. encaustus".
- brett-surman1989a: "None of the teeth is diagnostic below the family level and all the species are therefore nomina dubia."
- mallon2016a lists *D. bicarinatus*, *D. encaustus*, *D. haydenanius* and *D. peiganus* among the invalid ceratopsids of the Judith River Formation.

**Ruled out.**
- *Hadrosaurid material as part of the type.* It belongs to the original series only.
- *Neotype.* Coombs's caption word; the type was selected from the original series.

**Open.** Nothing.

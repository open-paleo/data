# *Dryptosaurus*

## Which formation is the *Dryptosaurus* holotype from?

*2026-08-20*

**Conclusion.** The New Egypt Formation. Cope (1866) gives the holotype from the top of the "chocolate" bed in the West Jersey Marl Company's pits, about two miles south of Barnesboro, and Baird and Horner (1979) and Gallagher (1993) both identify that bed as the New Egypt Formation. Governs `location.formation: New Egypt` and `location.locality: West Jersey Marl Company's pits` on [*Dryptosaurus aquilunguis*](../../../genera/D/Dryptosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cope1866a, p. 276: found by workmen "of the West Jersey Marl Company's pits, about two miles south of Barnesboro, Gloucester Co., N. J. The bones were taken from about twenty feet below the surface, in the top of the "chocolate" bed".
- baird1979a, p. 7: the type specimen "(ANSP 9995, Fig. 3B) came from the "chocolate greensand bed" or New Egypt Formation of late Maestrichtian age near Barnsboro, Gloucester County, New Jersey".
- gallagher1993a, p. 105: "Type: ANSP 9995 ... Navesink (= New Egypt) Fm. (= Chocolate Marl), Maastrichtian."

**Ruled out.**
- *The Marshalltown or Mount Laurel Formation.* Weishampel and colleagues (2004) list *Dryptosaurus aquilunguis* under the Marshalltown, the Mount Laurel and the "Navesink Formation (including New Egypt Formation)" of New Jersey (weishampel2004b, p. 586). The list gathers every unit the species has been reported from and names no specimen; the holotype's bed is Cope's chocolate bed.

**Open.** Nothing.

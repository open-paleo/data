# *Dacentrurus*

## Where and at what level in the Kimmeridge Clay was the *Dacentrurus armatus* holotype found?

*2026-08-20*

**Conclusion.** In the brick-pit of the Swindon Brick and Tile Company at the foot of Old Swindon Hill, at about National Grid reference SU 142838, which converts to about 51.5529, -1.7966. The level is known only roughly. Galton (1985) says the exact horizon is unknown, and gives the lower Kimmeridge Clay from Arkell's (1933) remark that most of the vertebrates from these pits came from the lower beds. Sánchez-Fenollosa and Cobos (2025) repeat that with Galton among their sources. Governs `location.locality: Swindon Brick and Tile Company brick pit`, `location.coordinates: [51.5529, -1.7966]` and `location.part: lower` on [*Dacentrurus armatus*](../../../genera/D/Dacentrurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton1985d, p. 213: "From brick-pit of Swindon Brick and Tile Co. at foot of Old Swindon Hill (at west end of Swindon Hills at about SU 142838; numbers given with localities are the appropriate National Grid Reference)".
- galton1985d, p. 213: "The exact horizon is not known, and the productive pits were abandoned long ago, but Arkell (1933) noted that most of the vertebrate remains came from the lower beds of the Kimmeridge Clay", in the *A. eudoxus* and *A. mutabilis* zones "of the lower Kimmeridgian".
- sánchez-fenollosa2025a, p. 170: "Unknown horizon. The fossils were discovered in a clay pit at the municipality of Swindon (United Kingdom). Lower part of the Kimmeridge Clay Formation, Upper Jurassic (Kimmeridgian) (Davies 1876; Galton 1985; Martill et al. 2006)."

**Ruled out.**
- *51.558, -1.785.* The pair stored before, which no source read prints; it lies about 1 km northeast of the grid reference.
- *"Clay pit" as the site's name.* Sánchez-Fenollosa and Cobos describe the site as "a clay pit", citing Galton, and Galton's own line calls it a brick-pit.

**Open.** Whether SU 142838 marks the pit or the west end of the hill above it: the parenthesis follows "Old Swindon Hill", and the grid reference is qualified "at about". Davies (1876), on the excavation, has not been read and would settle both the spot and the level.

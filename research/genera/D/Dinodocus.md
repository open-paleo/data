# *Dinodocus*

## Which formation of the Lower Greensand is the *Dinodocus* material from?

*2026-08-19*

**Conclusion.** None is recorded; the record stops at the Lower Greensand at group rank. Owen (1851) places the bones in the "Green sand Quarries" near Hythe, and later work on the specimen names no finer unit. Governs `location.group: Lower Greensand`, the absence of `location.formation`, and `location.locality: near Hythe` on [*Dinodocus mackesoni*](../../../genera/D/Dinodocus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- owen1851a, p. 48: the bones "were discovered, in 1840, by H. B. Mackeson, Esq., of Hythe, in the Green sand Quarries, near that town".
- woodward1908a, p. 204: "In 1840 Mr. H. B. Mackeson discovered a group of bones of a large reptile in the Lower Greensand near Hythe, Kent".
- mannion2013a, p. 153: "'*Dinodocus mackesoni*' is known from poorly preserved appendicular material ... from the Aptian–Albian Lower Greensand of England".
- jones2026b, p. 379 (table): "Atherfield Clay Fm., United Kingdom *Early Cretaceous, Aptian*".

**Ruled out.**
- *Atherfield Clay Formation.* It appears only in the table row of Jones (2026), which cites no source for it, and a compilation does not set a value alone. None of Owen (1851), Woodward (1908) or Mannion and colleagues (2013) names a formation.

**Open.** Which formation the Hythe quarries worked. The Hythe Formation takes its name from the town, and quarries in the Lower Greensand there would most likely be in it, but that is our inference from the name and the word "quarries", not a statement in any source read. A source on the 1840 quarry, or on Lower Greensand quarrying at Hythe, would settle it.

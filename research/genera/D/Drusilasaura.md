# *Drusilasaura*

## Is the *Drusilasaura* holotype from the Bajo Barreal or the Lago Colhué Huapi Formation, and from which part?

*2026-08-20*

**Conclusion.** The Bajo Barreal, in the upper half of its Upper Member, which is an informal subdivision and so is recorded as `part: upper`. Casal and colleagues (2015) erect the Lago Colhué Huapi Formation above the Upper Member, not in place of it, and name *Drusilasaura* as a record of the Upper Member. Governs `location.formation: Bajo Barreal` and `location.part: upper` on [*Drusilasaura deseadensis*](../../../genera/D/Drusilasaura.yml).

**Evidence.**
- navarrete2011a, p. 2: the formation "is informally subdivided into two members, Lower and Upper (Sciutto, 1981)" (translated); p. 4, Stratigraphic location: a sandstone bank between mudstones "of the upper half of the Upper Member of the Bajo Barreal Formation" (translated).
- casal2015a, p. 77, English abstract: "This unit overlies the Bajo Barreal Formation (Upper Member)".
- casal2015a, p. 88: "there is a record of the titanosaur *Drusilasaura deseadensis* in the Upper Member of the Bajo Barreal Formation on the south margin of the Deseado River valley, in Santa Cruz Province" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *The Lago Colhué Huapi Formation.* Ibiricu and colleagues (2019) write that specimens earlier reported as "coming from the Upper Member of the Bajo Barreal Formation" belong to the Lago Colhué Huapi (ibiricu2019a, p. 277). That sentence concerns the *Sektensaurus* material from Lago Colhué Huapi in Chubut. The paper that erects the formation keeps *Drusilasaura* in the Upper Member.
- *The Lower Member.* Mannion and Otero (2012) count *Drusilasaura* among the titanosaurs "named from the lower member of the Bajo Barreal Formation" (mannion2012a, p. 633). The describing paper places it in the upper half of the Upper Member.

**Open.** Nothing.

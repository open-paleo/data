# *Dasosaurus*

## Is the *Dasosaurus* site in the Itapecuru or the Codó Formation?

*2026-08-17*

**Conclusion.** The lower Itapecuru Formation, which the type-locality line of Mayer and colleagues (2026) gives. The same paper notes that regional geological maps place the site in the outcrop area of the Codó Formation. The authors assign it to the Itapecuru on its lithology, and ask for further work. Governs `location.formation: Itapecuru` and `location.part: lower` on [*Dasosaurus tocantinensis*](../../../genera/D/Dasosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- mayer2026a, p. 7, Type locality and horizon: "from deposits referred to the lower part of the Itapecuru Formation, late Aptian of the Grajaú Basin (Ferreira et al., 2020, 2021)".
- mayer2026a, p. 2: "Recent regional geological maps (Costa Neto et al., 2014; Klein & Sousa, 2012) place the site into the exposure area of the Codó Formation"; "The lithology of the site, on the other hand, is more reminiscent of the Itapecuru Formation"; "Hence, the fossil site most likely exposes the lower portion of the Itapecuru Formation amid a more general incidence of the Codó Formation".
- mayer2026a, p. 3: "Enclaves of the Itapecuru Formation seem to be common in the area; see purple areas in Figure 2, corresponding to the 'Varjão Formation' of Costa Neto et al. (2014). In any case, further studies are needed to better place the fossil bearing beds"; the unit's recent accounts "have agreed upon a Formation, rather than Group" status.

**Ruled out.**
- *Codó Formation.* The mapped unit at the site on the two regional maps the paper cites, which its authors set aside on the lithology of the section.
- *Itapecuru Group.* Rossetti and Truckenbrodt's (1997) rank, as the paper reports it; the paper follows the later accounts that treat the Itapecuru as a formation.
- *Varjão Formation.* Costa Neto and colleagues' (2014) name for the Itapecuru enclaves on their map, as the paper reports it; the paper does not use it for the site.

**Open.** The unit, by the authors' own statement that the beds still need to be placed. Ferreira and colleagues (2020, 2021), cited for the horizon and its age, have not been read.

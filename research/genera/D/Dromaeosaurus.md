# *Dromaeosaurus*

## Is the *Dromaeosaurus albertensis* holotype from the Oldman or the Dinosaur Park Formation?

*2026-08-20*

**Conclusion.** Neither is recorded, and `formation` stays empty. Matthew and Brown (1922) give only the Belly River formation. Colbert and Russell (1969) write "Oldman Formation" in the sense of 1969, which still included the beds now in the Dinosaur Park Formation, and the level they discuss is not measured for this specimen: Brown logged the holotype "150 feet above the river", his designation for every specimen from the Little Sandhill Creek badlands, and Colbert and Russell only presume that it came from about the level at which Sternberg (1950) relocated the *Centrosaurus nasicornis* quarry, which carries the same designation. Turner and colleagues (2012) give each formation once. Governs `location.formation` (empty) and `location.locality: right bank, Little Sandhill Creek` on [*Dromaeosaurus albertensis*](../../../genera/D/Dromaeosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- matthew1922a, p. 383: "Belly River formation, Red Deer River, Alberta. Found by Barnum Brown, Amer. Mus. Exped., 1914."
- colbert1969a, p. 1: the fossil "was found in beds then designated as belonging to the Belly River Formation, but which are now designated as being within the Oldman Formation".
- colbert1969a, p. 3, Horizon and locality: "From the Oldman Formation, Belly River Series"; Brown's field records place it "on the right bank of the Red Deer River, 12 miles downstream from Steveville, Alberta, at a level 150 feet above the river. This was Brown's general locality designation for all the specimens he collected in the Little Sandhill Creek (Sand Creek) badlands".
- colbert1969a, p. 6: Sternberg relocated the *Centrosaurus* quarry "at about the base of the upper third of the dinosaur-bearing section of the Oldman Formation in this region. Presumably the type of *Dromaeosaurus albertensis* was found at about this level."
- currie1995a, p. 576: the locality "was not marked in any way".
- turner2012a, p. 60: "DISTRIBUTION: Campanian, Oldman Formation, Alberta, Canada"; p. 20, Table 2: *Dromaeosaurus albertensis*, "Dinosaur Park Fm."

**Ruled out.**
- *Oldman Formation, on Colbert and Russell (1969).* Their Oldman is the name of its time for the whole dinosaur-bearing section, not a placement below the Dinosaur Park Formation (see [Oldman and Dinosaur Park](../../topics/oldman-and-dinosaur-park.md)).
- *Either formation on Turner and colleagues (2012).* The species account gives the Oldman and the paper's Table 2 the Dinosaur Park, and neither names a quarry or a level.
- *A stratigraphic position from "150 feet above the river".* The figure is a height above the river at the collecting point, and Brown applied it to all his specimens from these badlands.

**Open.** Where the quarry lay relative to the Oldman–Dinosaur Park contact. A source that ties a quarry in the Little Sandhill Creek badlands, or Sternberg's elevation of 2261.5 feet for the *Centrosaurus* quarry (colbert1969a, p. 6), to the contact would settle the formation, and even then the *Dromaeosaurus* level rests on Colbert and Russell's presumption.

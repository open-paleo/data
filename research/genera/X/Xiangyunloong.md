# *Xiangyunloong*

## Does the *Xiangyunloong* holotype take a member or a `part`?

*2026-09-14*

**Conclusion.** Neither. Hu and colleagues (2026) place it in "the upper part of First Member of Fengjiahe Formation", a numbered member of a two-member local subdivision that the record does not carry, and a position stated against that member cannot be restated against the formation. Governs the absence of `location.member` and `location.part` on [*Xiangyunloong fengming*](../../../genera/X/Xiangyunloong.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hu2026a, p. 4, 4.3 Type locality and horizon: "the upper part of First Member of Fengjiahe Formation, Lower Jurassic".
- hu2026a, p. 2, 2. Geological setting: "In the study area that yields the new specimen, the Fengjiahe Formation is subdivided into two members", the First and the Second.
- hu2023a, p. 760: the Fengjiahe "can be subdivided into two lithological members", Member 1 (J1f1), "the main dinosaur-bearing horizon", and Member 2 (J1f2) (translated); their measured section (pp. 760–761) lists Member 2 above Member 1.

**Ruled out.**
- *`member: First Member`.* It is a number within a local two-part subdivision, not a name anyone erects.
- *`part: upper`.* "Upper" attaches to the First Member, which is the lower of the two, so the holotype lies in the upper part of the lower member. `part: upper` on the formation would put it in the formation's upper half, which no source does.

**Open.** The correlation with the Lufeng Formation, which bears on `stage`. Hu and colleagues (2026, p. 3) call the First Member "roughly equivalent to the Zhangjiaao Member", citing the 2024 paper, but the 2024 paper says that "the middle and lower sandstone and mudstone layers of the Fengjiahe Formation in the study area roughly correspond to the Shawan Member of the Lower Lufeng Formation" (p. 766, translated). Neither paper dates the horizon by other means. A statement from the authors on which member they meant, or a study tying the Luming section to the Lufeng members, would settle it.

# *Xiaosaurus*

## Is *Xiaosaurus dashanpensis* a nomen dubium?

*2026-10-08*

**Conclusion.** No, on the work since 2005. Barrett and colleagues (2005) found one autapomorphy, a proximally straight humerus, and provisionally accepted the genus; later work follows them. The characters of the original diagnosis are shared by ornithischians generally and no longer serve as diagnostic features. Governs `status: valid` and `diagnostic_features` on [*Xiaosaurus dashanpensis*](../../../genera/X/Xiaosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- barrett2005a, Systematic Paleontology, *Xiaosaurus*: "although Xiaosaurus is usually considered to be a nomen dubium (Weishampel and Witmer, 1990; Sereno, 1991; Norman et al., 2004a), it can be distinguished from all other basal ornithischians on the basis of a single humeral autapomorphy ... we provisionally accept Xiaosaurus as a valid genus".
- barrett2005a, emended diagnosis: "possession of a proximally straight humerus that lacks the medial curvature seen in all other basal ornithischians."
- butler2008b: it "appears to be diagnosable" on that humerus, but "the whereabouts of the holotype and referred material are currently unknown".
- boyd2015a: "all of the apomorphies proposed by Dong & Tang (1983) are actually symplesiomorphies of Ornithischia", while the humeral autapomorphy "indicates it is a valid taxon".

**Ruled out.**
- *Nomen dubium.* The reading of Norman and colleagues (2004) and earlier works, answered by Barrett and colleagues (2005); no later work read returns to it.
- *Dong and Tang's (1983) characters as diagnostic features.* Barrett and colleagues (2005) and Boyd (2015) show them to be ornithischian symplesiomorphies.
- *A straight femur as the autapomorphy.* Jones (2026) writes "femur"; every primary source gives the humerus.

**Open.** The type material (IVPP V6730A, B) is of unknown whereabouts, so the autapomorphy has not been checked against the specimen since 2005.

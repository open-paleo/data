# *Nuthetes*

## Is the Nuthetes jaw from Willcox's Feather Quarry or from Brodie's dirt-bed at Durlston Bay?

*2026-08-31*

**Conclusion.** The Feather Quarry, as Owen's 1854 description gives it, in a footnote to the paragraph on the jaw. The page has one footnote, attached to the sentence comparing the jaw with *Varanus crocodilinus*, so "the specimen above described" is read as the jaw and not the tibia slab of the next paragraph. Owen's 1861 monograph gives the jaw a bed number that his 1854 paper uses for Brodie's dirt-bed, and is not followed. Governs `location.locality: Feather Quarry` on [*Nuthetes destructor*](../../../genera/N/Nuthetes.yml).

**Evidence.**
- owen1854b, p. 420: the fossils were "transmitted for my examination by W. R. Brodie, Esq., and Charles Willcox, Esq., of Swanage, by whom they were discovered … in certain members of the Purbeck formation at Durdlestone Bay, near that town", and the reptiles described first are "those transmitted by Mr. Willcox".
- owen1854b, p. 421, footnote to the paragraph on the jaw: "The specimen above described was obtained at the Feather Quarry, and from the division of the Chert-beds marked J. 81–84. in the stratigraphical list in the Rev. Mr. Austen's".
- owen1854b, p. 423: Brodie's *Macellodus* block came from "a part of the Purbecks, marked K. 93. in Mr. Austen's 'Guide,' and called the 'dirt-bed, containing shells,' high up the cliff, at Durdlestone Bay".
- owen1861a, p. 31: Wilcox's specimens, "including a portion of jaw with teeth, were discovered in the Purbeck formation, from the bed marked k 93 in Mr. Austen's 'Guide'"; p. 32: "in the same division of the Purbeck strata, viz., from the 'Feather Quarry,'" came long bones of a small saurian; p. 33: Brodie's *Saurillus* came from the "'Dirt-bed,' No. 93".

**Ruled out.**
- *Bed k 93, the dirt-bed.* Owen (1861) assigns the jaw to it, but in 1854 that number is Brodie's dirt-bed at Durdlestone Bay, a different collector's bed, while the 1854 footnote ties Willcox's jaw to the Chert-beds, J. 81–84. The 1861 text still treats the Feather Quarry as the jaw's division, which fits the 1854 account rather than the bed number.

**Open.** The modern member, and which unit Austen's J. 81–84 falls in. Milner's (2002) redescription, not read, is the likeliest source for both.

# *Neuquensaurus*

## Which formation is the Neuquensaurus australis holotype from?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** Probably the Bajo de la Carpa Formation; the Anacleto is ruled out for the holotype. Governs `location.formation: Bajo de la Carpa` on *Neuquensaurus australis*. The assignment is conditional, and the record's notes should keep it so.

**Evidence.**
- bonaparte1979c, p. 396 (read in English translation): Bonaparte and Gasparini quote Huene's locality, "in the elevated right cliff of the Río Neuquén, some kilometers (2-4) from the railway bridge", then give their own conditional assignment: "If this provenance is correct… Río Colorado Formation, possibly Bajo de la Carpa Member".
- powell2003a, p. 39: "Neuquén Group. Río Colorado, Bajo de La Carpa Member? (Holotype)".
- huene1929a, pp. 12–13 (translated): in the Boca del Sapo section, layer c, "approximately 15 m of red clays and variegated marls", is where "the sauropod remains described by Lydekker in 1893 as 'Neuquén' remains probably also come from". Layer c lies below layer b, whose base yielded *Notosuchus*. Huene's section predates the name Bajo de la Carpa (1938) and does not use it.
- leanza2004a, pp. 65, 70: the Bajo de la Carpa is a formation of the Río Colorado Subgroup, "divided into the sandy Bajo de la Carpa Formation below and the argillaceous Anacleto Formation above"; its age "is Santonian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Anacleto Formation for the holotype.* D'Emic and Wilson (2011) give "Type horizon: Anacleto Formation; Santonian−Campanian" (d'emic2011a, p. 64), citing Bonaparte and Gasparini, who said Bajo de la Carpa. Santucci and Filippi (2022) give Anacleto (santucci2022a, p. 370) for holotype and referred material together. Huene's section puts the type beds below the *Notosuchus* level, which is Bajo de la Carpa fauna (leanza2004a, p. 70), so the type cannot come from the overlying Anacleto.
- *A "Lower La Carpa Member".* Powell (2003, p. 8) prints "Río Colorado Formation, Lower La Carpa Member?", rendering *Bajo*, part of a place name, as "Lower". His own Neuquensaurus entry (p. 39) has Bajo de La Carpa. No unit called La Carpa exists.
- *Laplatasaurus evidence as Neuquensaurus evidence.* Gallina and Otero's (2015) "right bank of the Neuquén River, near Neuquén city [probably Bajo de la Carpa Formation…]" (gallina2015a, p. 495) concerns MLP-26-28, caudals of *Laplatasaurus*, not *Neuquensaurus*.

**Open.** Huene's layer c, red clays and marls, fits Leanza and colleagues' description of the underlying Plottier Formation ("light red massive claystones", leanza2004a, p. 70) at least as well as the Bajo de la Carpa. Huene's section rules out the Anacleto but does not decide between Bajo de la Carpa and Plottier; that step is an inference from Bonaparte and Gasparini's conditional assignment.

## Where was the referred material collected, and does it bear on the record's age?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** The referred material from Cinco Saltos and General Roca is Anacleto Formation, early Campanian. Under the rule that a record's stages follow its type horizon, it belongs in the notes and does not set `stage`. Governs the record's `location.notes`. The record's current `stage: [Santonian, Campanian]` takes its Campanian from this hypodigm or from the Bajo de la Carpa's formation-wide range, not from the holotype.

**Evidence.**
- otero2010a, p. 401: "Those 'Dinosaurs beds' where Neuquensaurus' remains were found correspond to the Anacleto Formation"; and "Lydekker did not give… the specific stratigraphic position" of the holotype.
- leanza2004a, p. 71: *Neuquensaurus* "Near the Cinco Saltos locality… was recorded from beds belonging to this unit"; the Anacleto's "age is considered to be Early Campanian (Leanza, 1999; Dingus et al., 2000…)". No members are named.

**Ruled out.**
- *Lower Allen Formation for the Cinco Saltos material, and the Maastrichtian with it.* Powell (2003, p. 39) gives "Lower Member of the Allen Formation" for the referred material, the reading that brought the Maastrichtian into the record. The Cinco Saltos beds were reassigned to the Anacleto (leanza2004a, pp. 79–80, following Heredia and Salgado 1999).
- *Anacleto Member.* Dingus and colleagues (2000) call the unit the "Anacleto Member" of the Río Colorado Formation (dingus2000a, pp. 1–3), the pre-1999 rank. Leanza and colleagues (2004) treat it as a formation with no members.

**Open.** Whether the record should keep the Campanian. The holotype's formation is Santonian by Garrido (2010) and Leanza and colleagues (2004), with a thinly supported Campanian top; see [Bajo de la Carpa](../../stratigraphy/b/bajo-de-la-carpa.md).

## Is "Sierra Roca" a valid locality for the holotype?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** It is a historical name for the same area, not an error, but Huene's description is more specific. Governs `location.locality`, now "Right bank of the Río Neuquén, 2 to 4 km from the railway bridge near Neuquén".

**Evidence.**
- powell2003a, p. 8: "This area, known long ago as 'Sierra Roca', was crossed by people going to the west of the Neuquén and Limay rivers."
- bonaparte1979c, p. 396: Huene's locality, quoted above.

**Ruled out.**
- *"Sierra Roca" as a distinct site.* Powell uses it as the old name for the Neuquén-city outcrops, not for a separate locality.

**Open.** Nothing.

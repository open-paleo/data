# *Nopcsaspondylus*

## Is *Nopcsaspondylus alarconensis* diagnosable?

*2026-10-29*

**Conclusion.** No; it is a nomen dubium, a rebbachisaurid of undetermined position. Apesteguía (2007) named it on a vertebra already lost and known only from Nopcsa's (1902) figure, diagnosing it by very reduced centra, a forked medial centropostzygapophyseal lamina and a composite lateral lamina of the neural spine. Lerzo (2026) reassessed the figure and found no autapomorphy: the fossa and the lamina configuration recur across Rebbachisauridae. The record had already called the genus dubious and its validity tentative; Lerzo's reassessment settles it, and no held paper disagrees. With no diagnostic character left, the genus sits at Rebbachisauridae `placement: incertae sedis` and carries no diagnostic features. Governs `status: nomen dubium`, `placement: incertae sedis`, the removal of `diagnostic_features` and of the `dispute` block on [*Nopcsaspondylus*](../../../genera/N/Nopcsaspondylus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- apesteguía2007a, p. 544: "Diagnosis: diplodocoid sauropod characterized by mid to posterior dorsal vertebrae with very reduced centra, forked medial centropostzygapophyseal lamina, lateral lamina of the neural spine formed by the spinodiapophyseal lamina plus a lateral spinopostzygapophyseal".
- lerzo2026a, p. 2753: "This dorsal vertebra does not present any autapomorphic characters that distinguish it from other rebbachisaurid sauropods; the presence of a laterodiapophyseal fossa is well distributed within Rebbachisauridae as well as the laminae configuration of the neural arch (including the neural spine)."
- lerzo2026a, p. 2749: the vertebra "was housed in the University of Vienna but now it is lost".

**Ruled out.**
- *Keeping `status: valid`.* Apesteguía's characters are not unique to the specimen.
- *Lerzo's argument from ICZN Recommendations 73B and 73C* (a holotype known only from a figure, with its horizon unsettled). Recommendations do not bear on a name's availability, and the record's status rests on diagnosability alone.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Where was the Nopcsaspondylus vertebra found, and in which unit?

*2026-08-31*

**Conclusion.** Near Villa El Chocón, as Canale and colleagues place the 1889 discovery from Zapalowicz's report and travel diary, with no formation recorded. Apesteguía's Candeleros and Canale and colleagues' basal Huincul are both stated as presumptions. Governs `location.locality: near Villa El Chocón` and the empty `location.formation` and `location.part` on [*Nopcsaspondylus alarconensis*](../../../genera/N/Nopcsaspondylus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- nopcsa1902a, section 3, "Vertebra of a South American Sauropod" (translated): "According to the label, the specimen is from the left bank of the Limay River, approximately 80 km above its junction with the Neuquén"; the matrix is "a fine-grained, yellow, friable, quartz-rich sandstone", unlike the "red or green, partly clayey strata" of earlier finds.
- huene1929a, p. 4 (translated): "Alarcón, on the left bank of the Limay River; a beautiful complete vertebra was found by Major Zapalowicz".
- calvo1995a, p. 27: "Nopcsa's vertebra was collected in the Alarcon Barda, 80 km southwest of Neuquén City".
- apesteguía2007a, p. 544: "coarse sandy levels of the lower section of the Candeleros Formation (presumably), Coniacian (Leanza and Hugo, 1997); Barda Alarcón, NE from Picún Leufú town".
- canale2024a, abstract (translated): the discovery area "is close to the present-day Villa El Chocón, possibly in the area called Barda Aquebeque"; the material was taken "40 meters below the maximum height of the barda", and since the Huincul Formation crops out in the upper thirds of the bardas there, "it can be presumed that *Nopcsaspondylus* comes from the basal section of this lithostratigraphic unit".

**Ruled out.**
- *Barda Alarcón.* The name is not on the label as Nopcsa (1902) reports it. "Alarcón" first appears in Huene (1929) and "Barda Alarcón" in Calvo and Salgado (1995), and Canale and colleagues place the find elsewhere from the collector's own account.
- *Barda Aquebeque as the locality.* Canale and colleagues offer it only as "possibly".

**Open.** Which unit. Apesteguía's lower Candeleros is marked "presumably" and rests on what crops out in the area. Canale and colleagues' basal Huincul is also a presumption, but it rests on two facts about the specimen, the measured 40 m below the top of the barda and the yellow quartz-rich sandstone Nopcsa saw on the bone. Neither is recorded, and with no formation the earlier `part: lower` has nothing to attach to. The age is also open: the record keeps the Cenomanian, Apesteguía dates his horizon Coniacian, and the Huincul that Canale and colleagues propose is Cenomanian–Turonian in their abstract. A full paper by Canale and colleagues, or a dated section at Villa El Chocón tied to the 40 m level, would settle both.

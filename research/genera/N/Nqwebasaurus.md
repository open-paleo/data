# *Nqwebasaurus*

## Can de Klerk and colleagues' printed position for Nqwebasaurus be repaired?

*2026-08-31*

**Conclusion.** No. The paper prints "25°15′83″E", with 83 in the seconds field, and the typeset page has it so; it is not damage in a copy. Repairing it means choosing which figure is wrong, so no coordinate is recorded. Governs the empty `location.coordinates` on [*Nqwebasaurus thwazi*](../../../genera/N/Nqwebasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- deklerk2000a, p. 325: "17 km west of Kirkwood village (33°25′01″S; 25°15′83″E)".

**Ruled out.**
- *−33.384998, 25.250999.* The pair stored before is about 3.7 km from either reading below (our calculation) and matches nothing printed.

**Open.** Two readings of the longitude are plausible: transposed seconds (25°15′38″, giving −33.416944, 25.260556) or decimal minutes printed with a seconds mark (25°15.83′, giving −33.416833, 25.263833 if the latitude is read the same way). They lie about 0.3 km apart (our calculation). Choiniere and colleagues (2012) give only "approximately 17 km west of Kirkwood Village" (choiniere2012a, p. 2). A later paper that prints the position, or the Albany Museum's locality record, would settle it.

## Which member of the Kirkwood Formation is the Nqwebasaurus horizon?

*2026-08-31*

**Conclusion.** No named member is recorded. De Klerk and colleagues call the horizon the formation's "unnamed uppermost member", which the record holds as `part: upper`. Governs `location.part: upper` and the empty `location.member` on [*Nqwebasaurus thwazi*](../../../genera/N/Nqwebasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- deklerk2000a, p. 325: "unnamed uppermost member (coarse red-brown mudstone paleosol facies) of the Lower Cretaceous Kirkwood Formation (Berriasian–Valanginian), Uitenhage Group".
- mcphee2016b, accepted manuscript, section 1.1: in the Swartkops and Colchester members "No vertebrate fossils have been discovered", and "The remaining, stratigraphically higher sediments of the Kirkwood Formation have not been formally named, but they contain all of the vertebrate fossil material so far discovered."
- forster2023a, p. 3 (online-first version): "The Kirkwood Fm is divided into the Bethelsdorp, Colchester, and Swartkops Members"; p. 12, for *Iyuku*: "Kirkwood Formation, uppermost member, paleosol facies".

**Ruled out.**
- *Swartkops or Colchester Member.* McPhee and colleagues (2016) report no vertebrates from either, and both lie below the beds that hold them.

**Open.** Whether the Bethelsdorp Member is the interval de Klerk and colleagues left unnamed. Forster and colleagues (2023), describing *Iyuku* from the same formation, divide the Kirkwood into three members after Muir and colleagues (2017), Bethelsdorp among them, yet give their own horizon only as the uppermost member. The paper by Muir and colleagues that names the three members (*South African Journal of Geology* 120(2): 281–293) has not been read, and it would settle whether the vertebrate-bearing upper beds are the Bethelsdorp. [*Iyuku*](../../../genera/I/Iyuku.yml) carries `part: upper` on the same reading.

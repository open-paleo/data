# *Notoceratops*

## Which side of Lago Colhué Huapí is the Notoceratops jaw from?

*2026-08-31*

**Conclusion.** The record keeps Tapia's (1919) own wording, which names the northwestern bluffs of the lake and the source of the Río Chico in one sentence. Huene's (1929) systematic account and Bonaparte (1984) place the find at the Río Chico end of the lake, east of it, and the formation follows that reading: Casal and colleagues (2015) erect the Lago Colhué Huapi Formation with its type locality at the headwaters of the Río Chico, and none of these works assigns the jaw to it directly. Governs `location.locality` and `location.formation: Lago Colhué Huapi` on [*Notoceratops bonarellii*](../../../genera/N/Notoceratops.yml).

**Evidence.**
- tapia1919a, p. 369 (translated): collected "in the bluffs northwest of Lake Colhué-Huapí, in the vicinity of the source of the Río Chico"; the original reads "en las barrancas del noroeste del lago Colhué-Huapí, en las inmediaciones del nacimiento del río Chico".
- huene1929a, p. 147 (translated): found "north of the outlet of the Río Chico at the southeastern corner of Lake Colhué Huapí"; p. 6 lists *Notoceratops* under "Area east of Lake Collhué Huapí and north of the outlet of the Río Chico".
- bonaparte1984a, p. 142 (translated): the material comes "from the eastern region of Lago Colhué-Huapi, slightly north of the headwaters of the Río Chico".
- casal2007a, p. 60 (translated): on the Río Chico "the only Cretaceous continental stratigraphic unit cropping out in that region is the upper member of the Bajo Barreal Formation", and "there is in the region a dubious record of a mandible assigned to the ceratopsian *Notoceratops bonarelli*".
- casal2015a, p. 90 (translated): "Type locality (Art. 14): headwaters of the Río Chico, Sarmiento Department, southern Chubut Province".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Tapia's two directions point to opposite ends of the lake if Huene is right that the Río Chico leaves it at the southeastern corner. Huene himself also writes "west of Lake Colhué Huapí" (huene1929a, p. 4). If the bluffs are in fact northwest of the lake, the formation inference from the Río Chico headwaters does not apply to them, although Casal and colleagues (2015) say the unit's outcrops extend widely through the basin. The jaw is lost; a collection record from the Dirección General de Minas, or a source that relocates Tapia's 1917 site, would settle it.

# *Nodosaurus*

## Which slope of Como Bluff is the Nodosaurus holotype from, and at what level?

*2026-08-31*

**Conclusion.** Como Bluff, with no slope named, and the Belle Fourche Member of the Frontier Formation, which Carpenter and Kirkland (1998) work out from Reed's field letters and a published measured section. Governs `location.locality: Como Bluff` and `location.member: Belle Fourche` on [*Nodosaurus textilis*](../../../genera/N/Nodosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lull1921a, p. 98: Reed was "searching for mammals and reptiles in the Morrison strata on the western slope of Como Bluff" when he found the specimen; p. 99: it "lay on the easterly slope of the Como anticline was therefore considerably above the Morrison stratigraphically", and Marsh's label reads "400 feet above the Dacota sandstone".
- carpenter1998c, p. 251: "Belle Fourche Member (Middle Cenomanian: Merewether et al., 1978; Merewether, 1983), Frontier Formation, probably sec. 9 or 10, T22N, R76W., Albany County, Wyoming."
- carpenter1998c, p. 253: Reed's letter of June 19, 1882 puts the specimen "about one mile south east from [Quarry] 13 and in strata about 400 feet above the dacota [sic] sand stone"; a zone of septarian nodules "about 311 ft (95 m) above the Dakota Sandstone" in Dunbar's (1944) section is the horizon it "most likely was collected" from, and the 400 ft figure is "probably due to his not taking into account the dip of the beds".

**Ruled out.**
- *The west slope of Como Bluff.* Lull (1921) uses "western slope" for where Reed was collecting in the Morrison that season, and on the next page places the specimen itself on the easterly slope, in younger beds.
- *A level 400 ft above the Dakota.* Reed's estimate, which Carpenter and Kirkland replace with the nodule zone about 311 ft above it, explaining the difference by the dip.

**Open.** The distance from Quarry 13 is given two ways: "about 1½ miles east and south" (lull1921a, p. 98) and Reed's own "about one mile south east" (carpenter1998c, p. 253). Neither is recorded, and the survey section is not converted to a coordinate. Dunbar (1944) and Merewether (1983), on which the member assignment rests, have not been read.

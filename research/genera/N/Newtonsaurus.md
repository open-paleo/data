# *Newtonsaurus*

## Is the *Newtonsaurus* dentary from the Cotham Member or the Westbury Formation?

*2026-08-31*

**Conclusion.** The Cotham Member of the Lilstock Formation, as Evans and colleagues (2025) assign it. The block was obtained from a mason at Stormy Down, so the assignment rests on its lithology and two bivalve impressions, and the authors call it presumed. Governs `location.formation: Lilstock` and `location.member: Cotham` on [*Newtonsaurus cambrensis*](../../../genera/N/Newtonsaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- evans2025a, p. 7, Systematic palaeontology: "Stratigraphy: Sandstone of the Cotham Member of the Lilstock Formation, mid to late Rhaetian (c., 202 Ma)."
- evans2025a, p. 2, Fig. 1 caption: the bivalves "confirm a Rhaetian (Penarth Group) age, and presumed assignment to the sandstones of the Cotham Member of the Lilstock Formation, exposed widely on Stormy Down".
- evans2025a, p. 3: "the general lithology of the block containing the dentary mould matches horizons of the lower Lilstock Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Near the base of the Westbury Formation.* Galton (2007) writes that "the remains of large dinosaurs are known from sedimentary beds near the base of the Westbury Formation (Rhaetian) in England and Wales", and that "These include … a dentary and teeth of the theropod *Megalosaurus cambrensis* (Newton, 1899)" (galton2007a, p. 507). The sentence covers several specimens and was written before the block was restudied; Evans and colleagues match its lithology to the lower Lilstock.

**Open.** The evidence for the member is not all one way. Of the two bivalves, Evans and colleagues identify one as probably *Pteromya langportensis*, "typical of the Langport Member of the Lilstock Formation", and the other as *P. crowcombeia*, "occurring in the Westbury Formation and Cotham Member" (evans2025a, p. 2), and they note that "it is difficult to distinguish the Cotham Member from some harder beds in the upper Westbury Formation, as in South Wales the transition is gradual" (p. 3), which on our reading leaves the upper Westbury possible. No source read gives the bed the stone was quarried from.

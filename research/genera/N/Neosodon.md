# *Neosodon*

## Is the *Neosodon* tooth from the Sables et Grès à Trigonia gibbosa?

*2026-10-14*

**Conclusion.** Not shown; `formation` stays empty. The name comes from two reference works, which list *Neosodon* under "Sables et Gres a Trigonia gibbosa" and cite four papers for the unit. Of those, only Cuny and colleagues (1991) has been read, and it does not place the tooth: it studies two other sites, La Rochette and Montrouge, and uses the *Trigonia gibbosa* name there only for beds of an older published section, not as a formation. The describing note gives the tooth's bed as the "ferruginous sands of Wimille" and names no unit. Governs the empty `location.formation` on [*Neosodon* sp.](../../../genera/N/Neosodon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- delamoussaye1884a: "On a tooth of Neosodon found in the ferruginous sands of Wimille"; the only *Trigonia* it lists is *Trigonia incurva*, from the hard building limestone above the sands, with "other mollusks of the Upper Portlandian".
- weishampel2004b, p. 547: "Sables et Gres a Trigonia gibbosa (Bonte et al. 1985; Cuny et al. 1991; Buffetaut and Martin 1993; Buffetaut 1995b) … Sauropoda indet. (= Neosodon sp.)".
- jones2026b, *Neosodon* entry: "Occurrence: Sables et Grès à Trigonia gibbosa".
- cuny1991a: the sites are "at the pointe de la Rochette, in the cliff just north of Wimereux" and "the former Montrouge quarry, about three kilometres southeast of the first"; the Montrouge sands "correspond to the upper part of the beds with *Trigonia gibbosa*", following an earlier section, and at La Rochette "We are either at the top of the sandstones with *Trigonia gibbosa*, or above them" (translated). Wimille and the *Neosodon* tooth do not appear in the text.

**Ruled out.**
- *Restoring "Sables et Grès à Trigonia gibbosa".* The two reference works cannot be its only source, and the one primary source they cite that has been read does not place the tooth in it.

**Open.** The other three papers Weishampel and colleagues (2004) cite for the unit have not been read; any of them placing the Wimille tooth would settle it.

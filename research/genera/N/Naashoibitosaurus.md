# *Naashoibitosaurus*

## Which member is the *Naashoibitosaurus* holotype from, and is "head of Willow Wash" its locality?

*2026-08-31*

**Conclusion.** The De-na-zin Member of the Kirtland Formation, and no named site. The genus is named for the Naashoibito Member, which Hunt and Lucas (1993) took to be the source of NMMNH P-16106, but later work places the specimen in the underlying De-na-zin Member. No source read names a site for this holotype. Governs `location.member: De-na-zin` and the empty `location.locality` on [*Naashoibitosaurus ostromi*](../../../genera/N/Naashoibitosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sullivan1999a, p. 127: "Even the hadrosaur *Naashoibitosaurus ostromi*, a probable junior subjective synonym of *Kritosaurus navajovius*, is not from the Naashoibito Member but is from the De-na-zin Member (Williamson, 1998)."
- lehman2016a, p. 353: "Although the holotype of 'N.' ostromi was originally attributed to the overlying Naashoibito Member of the Kirtland Formation (Maastrichtian), this was later shown to be incorrect"; the holotypes of *K. navajovius* and *'N.' ostromi* "were collected in the same area and from the same stratigraphic level in the De-na-zin Member".

**Ruled out.**
- *Naashoibito Member.* Hunt and Lucas write that "The generic name derives from the Naashoibito Member of the Kirtland Formation that yielded the holotype (NMMNH P-16106)" (hunt1993a, p. 82). Sullivan (1999) and Lehman and colleagues (2016) correct the horizon, both crediting Williamson; Sullivan cites Williamson (1998) and Lehman and colleagues cite Williamson (2000).
- *Head of Willow Wash.* This is the type locality of a different animal: Williamson and Carr give the holotype and only referred specimen of *Sphaerotholus goodwini* as "from the head of Willow Wash within the Bisti/De-na-zin Wilderness Area … De-na-zin Member, Kirtland Formation, NMMNH locality L-3921" (williamson2003a, p. 780). No source ties NMMNH P-16106 to that site.

**Open.** Where in the De-na-zin Member the holotype was collected. Williamson's (2000) review of San Juan Basin hadrosaurs, which the later papers cite for the correction, would be the place to look.

# *Raptorex*

## Why does the Raptorex record carry no coordinate, unit or country?

*2026-09-03*

**Conclusion.** The holotype was collected privately and has no collection record. The one published position is an approximation attached to a Chinese attribution whose only age evidence was later shown to be misidentified, and the authors who showed it conclude that the provenance is unknown. The location block holds only a note. Governs the empty `location.coordinates`, `location.formation`, `location.country` and `location.region` on [*Raptorex kriegsteini*](../../../genera/R/Raptorex.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sereno2009a, p. 422, note 15: "Locality: Approximately 41°20′N and 119°40′E, collected privately in the border area between Liaoning Province and the Nei Mongol Autonomous Region"; "Horizon and associations: Lujiatun Beds of the Yixian Formation".
- fowler2011b, p. 2, Results and Discussion: the fish centrum's "morphology and size exclude the possibility of it being Lycoptera"; p. 3: "at no time had the specimen been represented as coming from China"; "LH PV18 is of currently unknown provenance".

**Ruled out.**
- *41°20′N, 119°40′E as the site.* Sereno and colleagues label it approximate and give it in whole minutes for a specimen collected privately. It locates their Lujiatun attribution rather than a recorded dig site (our reading), and falls with that attribution.
- *China, the Lujiatun Beds of the Yixian.* The age rested on a centrum identified as cf. *Lycoptera*. Fowler and colleagues find it consistent with Ellimmichthyiformes, which "in China range from the Early Cretaceous to the Eocene" (fowler2011b, p. 2), leaving "no biostratigraphic evidence for placement of the specimen in the Lower Cretaceous".
- *Mongolia.* Fowler and colleagues report from the seller and the buyer that the specimen was sold as a juvenile *Tarbosaurus* from Mongolia, but note that "variability in personal communications regarding the specimen makes it difficult to ascertain exact details" (fowler2011b, p. 3), and conclude only that the provenance is unknown.

**Open.** The species' age. The only support for `period` (Late Cretaceous, Maastrichtian) among the sources read is Fowler and colleagues' judgment that LH PV18 "more likely represents the juvenile of a larger tyrannosaurid from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, such as *Tarbosaurus*" (fowler2011b, p. 5, Conclusions), a likelihood rather than a horizon. A collection record, or a study of the matrix, would settle the age and the provenance together.

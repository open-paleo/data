# *Rhoetosaurus*

## What stage is the Rhoetosaurus holotype?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** Late Callovian or younger: a U-Pb maximum age from the holotype's own matrix, which on the current timescale falls in the late Callovian, at most touching the base of the Oxfordian. Governs `stage` on *Rhoetosaurus brownei*: Callovian (or Callovian–Oxfordian), not Oxfordian alone and not Bathonian–Callovian.

**Evidence.**
- todd2019a, accepted manuscript, p. 2: "The *Rhoetosaurus* maximum depositional age determined is 162.6 ± 1.1 Ma, no older than early Oxfordian"; p. 9 argues the youngest zircon population "effectively records the true depositional age".
- hesselbo2020a: base of the Oxfordian at 161.5 ± 1.0 Ma in GTS 2020 (163.5 Ma in GTS 2012). The [Walloon Coal Measures](../../../stratigraphy/w/walloon-coal-measures.yml) record reads the date the same way on the ICS chart.

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Oxfordian.* Todd and colleagues (2019) read 162.6 Ma as "no older than early Oxfordian" against the 2012 timescale, on which the Oxfordian began at 163.5 Ma. On the 2020 timescale 162.6 ± 1.1 Ma (163.7–161.5 Ma) is late Callovian. The stage name changed with the timescale; the date did not.
- *Late Bathonian–middle Callovian.* Nair and Salisbury (2012) give "The material derives from the lowermost section of the Walloon Coal Measures … The age of the lowermost WCM, based on palynostratigraphy … is late Bathonian–middle Callovian" (nair2012a, p. 371), relaying earlier palynology. Todd and colleagues agree "the site falls within the base of the Walloon Coal Measures" (accepted manuscript, p. 9); the conflict is palynological age against the zircon date for the same interval, and the zircon date is younger.

**Open.** How far above the maximum age the true age lies. Todd and colleagues argue it is close; a date from above the horizon would settle it. Jones (2026) gives "Late Jurassic, Oxfordian" in a specimen table (jones2026b, p. 375) without citing Todd and colleagues; that it derives from them is an inference.

# *Yehuecauhceratops*

## Is the *Yehuecauhceratops* holotype from the lower or the upper member of the Aguja Formation?

*2026-09-09*

**Conclusion.** The basal part of the upper member, as the 2016 description of CPC 274 gives it. The naming paper's "Lower member" is not followed. Governs `location.part: upper` on [*Yehuecauhceratops mudei*](../../../genera/Y/Yehuecauhceratops.yml).

**Evidence.**
- rivera-sylva2016a, p. 4: "The lower part of the upper member documents deltaic coastal marsh and coastal floodplain environments in which CPC 274 occurs."
- rivera-sylva2016a, p. 7, Material: "The sediments are consistent with the basal portion of the upper member of the Aguja, documenting the transition from marine to terrestrial sedimentation."

**Ruled out.**
- *Lower member.* Rivera-Sylva and colleagues (2017) give "Horizon and age: Lower member of the Aguja Formation (Late Cretaceous, Late Campanian) (Rivera-Sylva et al., 2016)" (rivera-sylva2017a, p. 67). The 2016 paper they cite does not place CPC 274 in the lower member. Its one "lower member" sentence dates the member: "Biostratigraphic correlation using marine invertebrates suggests a late Campanian age for the lower member of the Aguja Formation" (p. 4). Earlier in the same paragraph the marine member is followed by "a transitional sequence of sandstones, mudstones, and limonites", and "The vertebrate remains occur in these layers." That the 2017 line was taken from the age sentence is an inference; the 2017 paper gives no other basis for it.

**Open.** The exact horizon. The 2016 paper says "It is not yet clear where in the Aguja Formation the locality occurs" and that "Further work on the geology of the area is required" (p. 7), and its measured section could not be tied to a contact with the formations above or below (Fig. 2). A section linking the site near La Salada to the base or top of the Aguja would settle it.

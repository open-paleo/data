# *Yamaceratops*

## Is the *Yamaceratops* holotype from the middle Javkhlant Formation?

*2026-09-09*

**Conclusion.** The Javkhlant Formation, at no stated level within it. Eberth and colleagues (2009) erect the formation and confirm that the type and referred material came from the Khugenslavkhant badlands within it, but no source read gives the holotype's interval. Governs `location.formation: Javkhlant` and the absence of `location.part` on [*Yamaceratops dorngobiensis*](../../../genera/Y/Yamaceratops.yml).

**Evidence.**
- eberth2009a, p. 295: locality data "for the type and referred materials of Yamaceratops dorngobiensis confirms that they were collected from the Shine Us Khudag redbeds in a localized area of badlands called Khugenslavkhant".
- eberth2009a, p. 298: "Most of the vertebrate fossils seen by us and collected by MAE (see Introduction) occur preferentially, but patchily, in this middle interval"; p. 297, Fig. 2E, a photograph labelled "in situ postcrania of Yamaceratops" set against the middle Javkhlant. The holotype is a skull, so that photograph shows other material.
- makovicky2006a, p. 2: "The specimen derives from a fluvial, pale red sandstone layer at Khugenetslavkant."

**Ruled out.**
- *Son and colleagues' (2022) horizon as a statement about the holotype.* Their "middle unit of the Javkhlant Formation" (son2022a, p. 6, Locality, horizon, and age) follows both the holotype line and the line for the juvenile MPC-D 100/553, and the sentence after it ("The specimen was preserved on top of a trough cross-bedding sandstone") describes the juvenile's bed. It places the juvenile found in 2014, not the skull collected in 1991.
- *Makovicky and colleagues (2011) as a statement about the holotype.* "within a limited stratigraphic interval in the middle of the Javkhlant Formation" (makovicky2011a, p. 627) describes the specimens of their new ornithopod; *Yamaceratops* appears in the next sentence only as part of the locality's fauna.

**Open.** Whether the holotype lies in the middle interval. Eberth and colleagues put most of the locality's fossils, and the *Yamaceratops* postcrania of their Fig. 2E, in that interval, and no source read contradicts it, but none gives the holotype's level. The GPS-derived locality data that Eberth and colleagues reviewed but did not print, placed on their type section, would settle it.

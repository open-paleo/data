# *Gigantoscelus*

## Which unit and age does *Gigantoscelus* take now that the Bushveld Sandstone is the Clarens?

*2026-10-14*

**Conclusion.** The Clarens Formation, Sinemurian to Toarcian. Its type comes from the Bushveld Sandstone as Haughton (1924) and Van Heerden (1979) give it, which Bordy and Head (2018) record as an older name for the Clarens; with no statement about its own horizon, the record takes the formation's range. Supersedes the entry below, which kept the unit's epochs while its correlation was unsettled. Governs `location.formation: Clarens` and `stage` on [*Gigantoscelus molengraaffi*](../../../genera/G/Gigantoscelus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bordy2018a: the Clarens "has also been referred … as the Bushveld Sandstone in the Lebombo, Springbok Flats, Tshipise, Tuli and Ellisras/Lephalale Basins".

**Ruled out.**
- *The lower Elliot, from Haughton's (1924) tie of the femur type to the base of the Red Beds.* A correlation by kind of fossil; the paper defining the unit settles which unit the name means.

**Open.** Whether van Hoepen's (1916) limestone, which "may even" belong to the uppermost Bushveld Sandstone, lies within the Clarens or just below it.

## What age does *Gigantoscelus* take?

*2026-10-13*

**Conclusion.** The Bushveld Sandstone's epochs, Late Triassic and Early Jurassic, with no stage. The record's Hettangian–Sinemurian followed the unit's correlation with the Clarens Formation, the route by which *Massospondylus* is dated, while Haughton (1924) ties this femur's type to the base of the Red Beds, the lower Elliot. Neither correlation dates the bed, as the [Bushveld Sandstone](../../stratigraphy/c/clarens.md) note records, so the horizon takes the unit's range. Governs `period: [Late Triassic, Early Jurassic]` and the absent `stage` on [*Gigantoscelus molengraaffi*](../../../genera/G/Gigantoscelus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- haughton1924a, p. 427: "it is of a type that occurs apparently only near the base of the Red Beds in the Cape Province".
- weishampel2004a, p. 528: the Bushveld Sandstone row that includes *Gigantoscelus*, "Age: late Carnian or early Norian (?= Lower Elliot Formation; Kitching, pers. comm.)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Hettangian to Sinemurian.* It follows the Clarens correlation, which no cited source applies to this bed, against the one statement about this femur.
- *Late Carnian or early Norian.* A compilation row resting on a personal communication and on the Elliot correlation.

**Open.** Which correlation holds for the Bushveld Sandstone.

## What is the Gigantoscelus holotype?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** TM 65, the distal end of a femur, in the Transvaal Museum (now DNMNH); the toe bone found with it is not part of the type. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: TM 65`, `institution: DNMNH` and `material` on *Gigantoscelus molengraaffi*.

**Evidence.**
- vanheerden1979a, p. 68: "Holotype: TM 65, the weathered distal end of a femur"; p. 24: "Gigantoscelus molengraaffi: TM 65 (holotype)".
- vanhoepen1916a, p. 113: "het kleine beentje een teenkootje van een Dinosaurier, waarschijnlijk behoorende tot de Theropoden" ("the small bone a toe bone of a dinosaur, probably belonging to the Theropoda", translated).
- sabaj2020a, p. 648: DNMNH, "Also as: TM (fossils)".

**Ruled out.**
- *TrM 65.* Jones (2026) writes "a single femur (TrM 65)" (jones2026b, p. 363), a form no other source uses.
- *MT 65.* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi print "MT 65" (molina-pérez2020a, p. 69), a transposition.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the *Gigantoscelus* holotype from the Bushveld Sandstone?

*2026-08-24*

**Conclusion.** The Bushveld Sandstone, as Haughton (1924) and Van Heerden (1979) give it. Van Hoepen (1916), who described the find, left the bed's position open: the femur lay in a limestone that he placed between the gritstone below and the Bushveld Sandstone, possibly in the uppermost part of the sandstone. Haughton states the horizon without discussing that hedge, and Van Heerden cites Haughton and Brink (1955) for it. Governs `location.formation: Bushveld Sandstone` on [*Gigantoscelus molengraaffi*](../../../genera/G/Gigantoscelus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- vanhoepen1916a, p. 112: according to the Pienaarsrivier map sheet, Haakdoornbult "bestaat … hoofdzakelijk uit grindsteen. Slechts de uiterste Noord-Oosthoek ervan is bedekt door den hooger[en] Boschveld Zandsteen" ("consists … mainly of gritstone. Only its extreme north-eastern corner is covered by the higher Bushveld Sandstone", translated).
- vanhoepen1916a, p. 115: "Wel ligt deze kalkbank niet ver boven den grindsteen van de 'Coal-measures', doch hij ligt blijkbaar ook niet ver van den Boschveld Zandsteen en het kan zelfs zijn dat hij tot het bovenste deel van dezen laatsten behoort" ("This limestone bank does lie not far above the gritstone of the 'Coal-measures', but it evidently also lies not far from the Bushveld Sandstone, and it may even belong to the uppermost part of the latter", translated).
- haughton1924a, p. 427: "Locality. Haakdoornbult, 344. Waterberg Dist., Transvaal. Horizon. Bushveld Sandstone."
- vanheerden1979a, p. 68: "Horizon: Bushveld Sandstone (Haughton & Brink, 1955)".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Whether the limestone is inside the Bushveld Sandstone or in the beds just below it. Van Hoepen's own words allow either, and the later statements give the unit without new field evidence in the sources read. Haughton and Brink (1955), which Van Heerden cites, has not been read.

# *Gryponyx*

## Is *Gryponyx africanus* a valid taxon?

*2026-10-20*

**Conclusion.** Its validity is disputed. It has been sunk into *Massospondylus carinatus* and listed as a nomen dubium, then revived as a distinct basal massospondylid, and later authors still report doubts. Every placement on record is massospondylid. Governs `status: disputed`, `parent: Massospondylidae` and the dispute on [*Gryponyx africanus*](../../../genera/G/Gryponyx.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton1990a, Table 15.1: "M. carinatus Owen, 1854 (including … Gryponyx africanus Broom, 1911".
- galton2004a, p. 236, Table 12.1, Nomina dubia: "Gryponyx africanus Broom, 1911 | Vertebrae, both manus, pelvis and hindlimbs".
- knoll2010a, p. 814: "Vasconcelos & Yates (2004) revived Gryponyx africanus Broom, 1911 as a basal massospondylid".
- yates2011a, p. 610: "Gryponyx africanus, a taxon that has been sunk into synonymy with Massospondylus carinatus, was resurrected by Vasconcelos and Yates (2004), although doubts about its distinctiveness still linger".
- mcphee2017b, p. 448: "some suggestion that the enigmatic 'Gryponyx' … may be taxonomically distinct from Massospondylus".
- barrett2019a, p. 172: earlier decisions "were made on the basis of the provenance of material, and the assumption that there was only a single valid upper Stormberg Group sauropodomorph taxon, rather than on detailed consideration of anatomy"; p. 173, Table 10: "Junior subjective synonym of M. carinatus … or nomen dubium, Sauropodomorpha indet."

**Ruled out.**
- *A settled synonym or nomen dubium.* Both rulings predate the revival by Vasconcelos and Yates (2004), and Barrett and colleagues (2019) note that they did not rest on anatomy.
- *Jones (2026) as a source for distinctiveness.* Jones cites Lü and colleagues (2010) and Yates and colleagues (2010) for that view. Yates and colleagues' (2010) description of *Aardonyx* does not mention *Gryponyx*; the description of *Chuxiongosaurus* by Lü and colleagues (2010) has not been read.

**Open.** An anatomical comparison of *G. africanus* with *Massospondylus*, which Vasconcelos and Yates (2004) give only in an abstract.

## What places the *Gryponyx africanus* type material in the upper Elliot Formation?

*2026-08-24*

**Conclusion.** A compilation only. Broom (1911) and Haughton (1924) both give the Red Beds of the Stormberg, the name of their time, and neither names the Elliot. The distribution chapter of *The Dinosauria* lists *G. africanus* under the Upper Elliot Formation of the Free State. The record keeps the Elliot and the upper part on that entry until a primary source places the Foutani site. Governs `location.formation: Elliot` and `location.part: upper` on [*Gryponyx africanus*](../../../genera/G/Gryponyx.yml).

**Evidence.**
- broom1911a, p. 294: "Recently there was discovered on the farm of Mr. J. L. Harries, Foutani, near Fouriesburg, in the Orange Free State, a considerable number of remains of Dinosaurs … All the remains are from the Red beds."
- haughton1924a, p. 474: "*Massospondylus* occurs from about half-way up to near the top, both in Herschel and in the north at Fouriesburg. At the latter place it is associated with the somewhat larger but still light-limbed *Gryponyx africanus*".
- weishampel2004b, p. 536: "81.2. FREE STATE PROVINCE, SOUTH AFRICA 1. Upper Elliot Formation (Haughton 1924; …)", with "*G. africanus*" among the Prosauropoda indet.

**Ruled out.**
- *A height in the section from Haughton (1924).* His "about half-way up to near the top" is the range of *Massospondylus* in Herschel and at Fouriesburg. He says only that *Gryponyx africanus* occurs with it at Fouriesburg, so the sentence fits the upper part but gives no level for the type.

**Open.** A primary source that places the Foutani site in the upper Elliot Formation. The distribution chapter cites Kitching (1979, 1981) and Grine and Kitching (1987), among others, for the Free State entry; none of them has been read.

# *Giraffatitan*

## Which specimen is the *Giraffatitan brancai* lectotype?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** MB.R.2180, formerly HMN SI, with MB.R.2181 (formerly HMN SII) the paralectotype. Taylor (2009) designated SII the lectotype in the belief that Janensch had fixed no type; Taylor (2011) withdrew that, citing Janensch (1935–1936) for the smaller individual as the type, and gave both specimens' current Berlin numbers. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [MB.R.2180]` and `specimen_type: lectotype` on [*Giraffatitan brancai*](../../../genera/G/Giraffatitan.yml).

**Evidence.**
- taylor2011b, p. 727: "I wrongly stated that Janensch never explicitly designated these two specimens as a syntype series or nominated either specimen as a lectotype"; "their correct designations are now MB.R.2180 and MB.R.2181, respectively. Therefore, MB.R.2180 is the lectotype specimen of the species *Giraffatitan brancai*, and MB.R.2181 (previously SII) is the paralectotype."

**Ruled out.**
- *HMN SII (MB.R.2181) as the type.* Paul (1988) calls SII the "holotype" (paul1988b, Fig. 1), Taylor (2009) designates "Lectotype—HMN SII" (taylor2009a, p. 799), and Molina-Pérez and Larramendi (2020) write "the holotype specimen HMN SII" (molina-pérez2020a, Animal Intelligence). Taylor's 2011 correction supersedes the 2009 designation.
- *HMN XV2.* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi head their chronology of the largest sauropods with "*Giraffatitan brancai* HMN XV2" (molina-pérez2020a, Chronology of the largest sauropods throughout history), a fibula they use for size, not a type.

**Open.** Nothing.

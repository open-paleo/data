# *Kaatedocus*

## Which member of the Morrison Formation holds the *Kaatedocus* type site?

*2026-08-28*

**Conclusion.** None is recorded. Tschopp and Mateus (2012) place SMA 0004 in the Howe Quarry near Shell and assign the quarry no member; their geological setting says the members recognized farther south are difficult to recognize in northern Wyoming. The "Brushy Basin Member" in the type-locality table of Tschopp and colleagues (2015) conflicts with that statement and gives no reason for the assignment. Governs the empty `location.member` and `location.locality: Howe Quarry` on [*Kaatedocus siberi*](../../../genera/K/Kaatedocus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- tschopp2012a, p. 5 of the online-first file, Locality and horizon: "SMA 0004 was recovered from the Howe Quarry in the vicinity of Shell, Bighorn County, north-central Wyoming, USA (44° 40′ 2.95″ N/107° 49′ 8.12″ W)", "in the upper part of the lower Morrison Formation".
- tschopp2012a, p. 2 of the online-first file, Geological setting: "The members of the Morrison Formation, as identified further south, are difficult to recognize in northern Wyoming."
- tschopp2015a, p. 6, Table 2: "Howe Quarry, Bighorn County, WY, USA", "Kimmeridgian/Tithonian; Brushy Basin Member, Morrison Form."

**Ruled out.**
- *Brushy Basin Member.* Tschopp and colleagues (2015) give it in a table of type specimens and localities. The 2012 description, by the table's first two authors, assigns the quarry to no member and says the members are hard to recognize in this part of Wyoming.
- *Howe-Stephens Quarry.* It is a second site on the Howe Ranch, "approximately 10 m higher in stratigraphy" than the Howe Quarry (tschopp2012a, p. 2 of the online-first file).

**Open.** The `part`. The horizon line gives "the upper part of the lower Morrison Formation", and the same paper's geological setting divides the formation at a clay change about 20 m above the quarry, after Turner and Peterson (1999), while adding that "several authors propose a higher stratigraphical position for the Howe Quarry" (tschopp2012a, p. 2 of the online-first file). That the horizon line's "lower" rests on that clay change is an inference. The record carries no `part`. Tschopp, Mehling and Norell (2020), an account of the Howe Quarry's specimens and history, has not been read and may settle the quarry's level.

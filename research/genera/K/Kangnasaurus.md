# *Kangnasaurus*

## Does the *Kangnasaurus* holotype have a formation?

*2026-08-06*

**Conclusion.** No. No paper on the Kangnas site names a formation; De Wit and colleagues (1992) read the fossil-bearing beds as crater-lake fill above a Late Cretaceous intrusion. Governs the empty `location.formation` on [*Kangnasaurus coetzeei*](../../../genera/K/Kangnasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dewit1992a, p. 504: "the Kangnas dinosaur site is more likely to be related to the infill of a crater lake above a Late Cretaceous olivine–melilitite intrusion than to a palaeodrainage system"; p. 506: "The *Kangnasaurus coetzeei* fossils are likely to be associated with a crater facies deposit above an olivine–melilitite intrusion of Late Cretaceous age".
- rogers1915a, p. 267: "the age of the bones is not known". The paper uses "Kalahari" for a region, "the southern Kalahari".
- haughton1915a, p. 264: "speculation as to the exact age of the remains would be very premature".
- cooper1985a, introduction: "The occurrence of dinosaur bones at the base of the Kalahari succession in the northern Cape … The material was obtained from a poorly sorted, immature, coarse clastic unit of colluvial rubble exposed in a well at a depth of approximately 34 m."
- weishampel2004b, Early Cretaceous, Cape Province, South Africa: "1. Kalahari Deposits (Haughton 1915; Cooper 1985) Ornithopoda Iguanodontia indet. (= *Kangnasaurus coetzeei*) Age: ?Late Jurassic–Valanginian (Dingle 1983)".
- jones2026a, Ornithopoda table: "Unnamed Fm., South Africa".

**Ruled out.**
- *Kalahari Deposits.* Weishampel and colleagues (2004) cite Haughton (1915), who names no unit, and Cooper (1985), who places the bones "at the base of the Kalahari succession" in colluvial rubble without naming a formation. Rogers (1915) uses "Kalahari" as a region. De Wit and colleagues (1992) read the beds as crater facies rather than as part of a drainage system.

**Open.** Nothing.

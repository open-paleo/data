# *Kerberosaurus*

## Do the papers on *Kerberosaurus* print a position for the Blagoveschensk bonebed?

*2026-10-06*

**Conclusion.** Yes. Bolotsky and Godefroit (2004) give the holotype's locality as the upper part of Nagornaia Street in Blagoveschensk, at 50° 16′ 11.6″ N, 127° 28′ 33.1″ E, and Godefroit and colleagues (2011) print the same pair for the Blagoveschensk locality. It converts to 50.269889, 127.475861. Governs `location.coordinates` on [*Kerberosaurus manakini*](../../../genera/K/Kerberosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bolotsky2004a, p. 352, *Kerberosaurus manakini*: "Holotype—AENM 1/319, the caudal part of a cranium"; "Locality and Horizon—Upper part of Nagornaia street (50° 16′ 11.6″ N; 127° 28′ 33.1″ E), west of Blagoveschensk City, Amur Region, Russia. Middle part of Tsagayan Formation".
- bolotsky2004a, p. 351: the lambeosaurine *Amurosaurus riabinini* comes "from the same locality"; p. 352: the bones "form a very large and continuous bonebed extending over several hundreds of square meters".
- godefroit2011a, p. 166: "Blagoveschensk ( N50° 16′ 11.6″ / E127° 28′ 33.1″)"; "The Blagoveschensk dinosaur locality is situated within the city limits of Blagoveschensk".
- xing2022a, p. e2085111-6, *Amurosaurus riabinini*, Locality and Horizon: "Type locality in the Russian Far East (GPS coordinates: 50°16′07.63′′N, 127°28′50.24′′E), outcrop along the south part of Nagornaia Street at the southwest corner of Blagoveschensk City", citing Lauters and colleagues (2008).

**Ruled out.**
- *No coordinate in the describing paper.* The earlier deletion held that "The paper prints none". The page carrying the locality has no text layer in the corpus copy, so a text search finds nothing; the page image prints the position.
- *Blagoveschensk with no finer site name.* The describing paper names Nagornaia Street.

**Open.** Which of two printed positions marks the bonebed. The pair of Lauters and colleagues (2008), as Xing and colleagues (2022) give it for the *Amurosaurus* type locality, lies about 360 m east-southeast of the 2004 pair, in a bonebed described as extending over several hundred square meters. [*Amurosaurus riabinini*](../../../genera/A/Amurosaurus.yml) carries the 2008 pair. Lauters and colleagues (2008) has not been read.

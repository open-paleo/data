# *Kazaklambia*

## Is the *Kazaklambia* holotype's formation contested as a name or as a horizon?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** As a horizon. The Darbazin and the Syuksyuk are two formations, the Darbazin lying on the Syuksyuk, and the holotype comes from beds at or near their contact. Rozhdestvensky (1964) puts it in the lower part of the Darbazin and the Stratigraphic Dictionary of the USSR (1979) in the Syuksyuk, so the disagreement is over which side of the contact the skeleton lies on, not over two names for one unit. The record follows Rozhdestvensky and Bell and Brink (2013). Governs `location.formation: Darbazin` on [*Kazaklambia convincens*](../../../genera/K/Kazaklambia.yml).

**Evidence.**
- vereshchagin1979a, p. 118, Darbazin Formation: "Lies transgressively on the Syuksyuk Formation" (translated); p. 378, Syuksyuk Formation: "most of the S. Formation corresponds to the Dinosaur Horizon, and its top to the base of the Lower Darbazin subformation" (translated).
- rozhdestvensky1964a, Syuk-Syuk wells (translated): the band of layers containing the skeleton "has been assigned, according to G. A. Belen’kii (1961), to the lower part of the Darbaza Suite".
- nessov1995a, p. 101, locality 99 (translated): "Lower part of the Darbazin Formation (Rozhdestvensky, 1964a). Syuk-Syuk Formation (Stratigraphic Dictionary, 1979)".

**Ruled out.**
- *One unit under two names.* The record's location note says that "Which unit name applies to the horizon is contested rather than the horizon itself". The dictionary describes two formations, the Darbazin lying on the Syuksyuk, and puts the top of the Syuksyuk at the base of the Lower Darbazin subformation.

**Open.** Which side of the contact the holotype lies on. Belenky (1961, 1966), on whose work both readings rest, has not been read. The rewording of the record's note is a separate fix.

## Is the *Kazaklambia* holotype from Kazakhstan, and from Syuk-Syuk rather than Shakh-Shakh?

*2026-08-28*

**Conclusion.** Yes to both. The holotype, PIN 2230/1, comes from the Syuk-Syuk well site in southern Kazakhstan, about 45 km from Tashkent, which lies across the border in Uzbekistan. Governs `location.country: KZ`, `location.region` and `location.locality: Syuk-Syuk` on [*Kazaklambia convincens*](../../../genera/K/Kazaklambia.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bell2013a, p. 265: "discovered in rocks of the Dabrazinskaya Svita at the Syuk–Syuk well site, 45 km northwest of Tashkent (Uzbekistan) in southeastern Kazakhstan".
- nessov1995a, p. 101, locality 99: "Syuk-Syuk (=Syok-Syok). in the vicinity of wells in the south of the Chimkent region, 10-12 km north of the Dzhilga (=Zhilga) station, Kazakhstan" (translated).
- rozhdestvensky1968a, *Procheneosaurus convincens*, Holotype: "PI No. 2230 … Senonian (Dabrazin Formation) Southern Kazakhstan, Shakh-Shakh Formation, 45 km north of Tashkent" (translated).
- rozhdestvensky1968a, *Aralosaurus tuberiferus*, Holotype: "Paleontological Institute No. 2229/1 … Central Kazakhstan, Shakh-Shakh plateau, approx. 80 km to the north of Karmachka" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Uzbekistan.* Bell and Brink's type-locality line reads "Syuk–Syuk well site, southeastern Uzbekistan" (bell2013a, p. 266). Their own introduction puts the site in southeastern Kazakhstan, with Tashkent as the reference point, and their title and Fig. 1 (a locality map of Kazakhstan) agree with the introduction.
- *Shakh-Shakh.* The English translation of Rozhdestvensky (1968) names a "Shakh-Shakh Formation" in the *P. convincens* holotype line, and its introduction says the 1961 find came from "the Shakh-Shakh deposit". The same paper places the Shakh-Shakh plateau, the *Aralosaurus* type locality, in central Kazakhstan about 80 km north of Karmachka, while the *P. convincens* line places that holotype 45 km north of Tashkent in southern Kazakhstan. One site cannot be in both places, and Nessov (1995) and Bell and Brink (2013) give Syuk-Syuk.

**Open.** Whether the Russian text of Rozhdestvensky (1968) names Shakh-Shakh in the *P. convincens* holotype line, or the translation introduced it. Only the translation has been read.

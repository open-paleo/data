# *Sinocephale*

## Which formation is the *Sinocephale* locality, Tsondolein-Khuduk, in?

*2026-09-05*

**Conclusion.** The Ulansuhai Formation, in Nei Mongol, following Evans and colleagues (2021), who place the site north of the Yellow River and its red beds "likely" in the Ulansuhai. The locality keeps the spelling of Bohlin (1953), who names the site; Evans and colleagues print "Tsondolien" in their locality statement and "Tsondolein" elsewhere. Governs `location.formation: Ulansuhai`, `location.region: CN-NM` and `location.locality: Tsondolein-Khuduk` on [*Sinocephale bexelli*](../../../genera/S/Sinocephale.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bohlin1953a, p. 31, under the heading "TSONDOLEIN-KHUDUK": "About 250 km west of Ulan-tsonch the caravan road passes a comparatively small area occupied by brick red sediments of the same type as those at Ulan-tsonch."
- evans2021a, Locality and horizon: "Tsondolien-Khuduk is located somewhere west of the towns of Ulan Suhai (Suhongtu) and Bayan Mod (Wuliji)"; "The red sediments in this area likely belong to the Ulansuhai Formation, and are constrained by radiometric dates to the Late Cretaceous, post ~92 Ma (Kobayashi et al. 2003)."
- evans2021a, Discussion: the site "was first assigned to the Minhe Formation by Weishampel (1990, who cited a personal communication from N. J. Mateer)"; "the Minhe Formation is only exposed in the Xining and Minhe basins south of the Yellow River"; Bohlin's map "clearly shows that the locality is North of the Yellow River".

**Ruled out.**
- *Minhe Formation.* Weishampel and colleagues (2004) list "*Troodon*" *bexelli* under the Minhe Formation in their Nei Mongol entries, citing Bohlin (1953) and "Mateer, pers. comm." (weishampel2004b, pp. 598–599). Evans and colleagues show that the Minhe crops out only south of the Yellow River and the locality lies north of it.

**Open.** Two hedges. Evans and colleagues give the Ulansuhai as "likely", and in the Discussion write that "it seems likely that fossils from Tsondolien-Khuduk are from the lower part of the Ulansuhai Formation", from the "presumed close proximity" of the *Sinornithomimus* type locality. They add that "more precise locality data is needed". The record carries no `part` for that reason. Relocating Bohlin's site would settle both.

## What stage is the *Sinocephale bexelli* holotype?

*2026-08-05*

**Conclusion.** Turonian to Santonian. The red beds are younger than about 92 Ma, and Evans and colleagues (2021) place the site in the lower Ulansuhai, below the Bayan Mandahu horizon, which they treat as most likely Campanian. Governs `stage: [Turonian, Coniacian, Santonian]` on [*Sinocephale bexelli*](../../../genera/S/Sinocephale.yml).

**Evidence.**
- evans2021a, Locality and horizon: the red sediments "likely belong to the Ulansuhai Formation, and are constrained by radiometric dates to the Late Cretaceous, post ~92 Ma".
- evans2021a, Discussion: the Bayan Mandahu red beds "are considered most likely Campanian in age"; "it seems likely that fossils from Tsondolien-Khuduk are from the lower part of the Ulansuhai Formation and below the Bayan Mandahu fossil horizon".

**Ruled out.**
- *Campanian–Maastrichtian.* The record's earlier value. No source read places the type horizon in either stage.
- *Turonian alone.* Jones (2026) gives "Ulansuhai Fm., China *Late Cretaceous, Turonian*" (jones2026a, p. 414). Evans and colleagues give only a lower limit at about 92 Ma, not a Turonian age.

**Open.** The upper limit is our inference: it reads "below the Bayan Mandahu fossil horizon" together with that horizon's Campanian. Evans and colleagues hedge both placements as "likely", and add that "more precise locality data is needed". They also relay an earlier suggestion that the assemblage is Santonian–Campanian. Relocating Bohlin's site would settle it.

# *Suchosaurus*

## Which work named *Suchosaurus cultridens*, and which erected *Suchosaurus*?

*2026-11-12*

**Conclusion.** Owen named the species *Crocodilus cultridens* in the *Odontography*, by name with a reference to its figure, and erected *Suchosaurus*, as a sub-genus, in his 1842 report on British fossil reptiles. Governs `erected_in: owen1841b` and `erected_as: Crocodilus cultridens` on the species, and the genus-level `erected_in: owen1842a`, on [*Suchosaurus*](../../../genera/S/Suchosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- owen1841b, p. 287: "*Crocodilus cultridens* of the Wealden strata (Pl. 62 A, fig. 10)".
- owen1841b, explanation of Pl. 62 A: "Fig. 10. a, b, *Crocodilus* (*Suchosaurus*) *cultridens*."
- owen1842a, pp. 67–68: "I have described this form of tooth (Odontography, pl. lxii. A, figs. 9 and 10) … under the name of *Crocodilus cultridens*", followed by the proposal of *Suchosaurus* as a sub-genus.

**Ruled out.**
- *Owen (1842) as the species' authority.* Owen cites his own earlier naming in the *Odontography* and erects only the sub-genus.

**Open.** The year of the part that carries p. 287, which the store gives as 1841 but no copy read prints; the title page reads "1840–1845". And when the plate explanation was issued: it already prints "(*Suchosaurus*)", so if it appeared before the 1842 report, the sub-genus would date from the *Odontography*. Owen's 1842 wording presents the sub-genus as new there.

## Which unit is the Suchosaurus cultridens holotype tooth from?

*2026-09-15*

**Conclusion.** The Grinstead Clay. Governs `location.formation: Grinstead Clay` on [*Suchosaurus cultridens*](../../../genera/S/Suchosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- barker2021a, p. 2: teeth referred to the nomen dubium "*Suchosaurus cultridens*", "from the Valanginian Grinstead Clay Formation of West Sussex".
- barker2023a, p. 4, Fig. 1 caption: "the Grinstead Clay Formation, which subdivides the Tunbridge Wells Sands Formation in [Batten & Austen (2011)] and from which the ''*Suchosaurus cultridens*'' type specimen was discovered".

**Ruled out.**
- *Wadhurst Clay.* Tucker and colleagues list "the Valanginian–Hauterivian Wahurst Clay Formation of England (e.g., the nomen dubium "*Suchosaurus cultridens*" (Mateus et al., 2011)" (tucker2022a, p. 16). Mateus and colleagues (2011), the work they cite, name no unit for the tooth (mateus2011b, searched for unit and place names), and Barker and colleagues give the Grinstead Clay.
- *Tunbridge Wells Sand Formation.* Carrano and colleagues give it for the *Valdoraptor* metatarsals from Cuckfield (carrano2012a, p. 256), not for this tooth. That the Grinstead Clay is a member of the Tunbridge Wells Sand in other schemes is a question of rank, settled for the registry in [Wealden units](../../topics/wealden-units.md).

**Open.** Nothing.

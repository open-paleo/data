# *Siluosaurus*

## Is *Siluosaurus* from the Zhonggou Formation?

*2026-09-05*

**Conclusion.** Yes. You and colleagues (2014) report that fieldwork in 2010 and 2011 assigned the whole dinosaur-bearing Xinminpu Group of the Mazongshan area to the Zhonggou Formation. Their report cites a personal communication, not a published section. Governs `location.formation: Zhonggou` on [*Siluosaurus zhanggiani*](../../../genera/S/Siluosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- you2014a, p. 75: "Our 2010 and 2011 field work confirms that the dinosaur-bearing Xinminpu Group in the Mazongshan area belongs to Zhonggou Formation (Liu, Y.-Q., pers. comm.)"; "An Albian age interpretation for the Zhonggou Formation is further supported by the palynological analysis of the lower red unit in the Gongpoquan Basin (Tang et al., 2001)".
- tang2001b, p. 120: the upper part of the Lower Red Unit "has yielded many vertebrate fossils, including ceratopsians, iguanodontids, hypsilophodontids, ankylosaurids, theropods".
- tang2001b, p. 117, Fig. 2: "7, the hypsilophodontid site".
- weishampel2004b, p. 567: under "1. Xinminbao Group", "Euornithopoda indet. (= *Siluosaurus zhangqiani*)", the only euornithopod in the list.

**Ruled out.**
- *The Xinminbao Group with no formation.* Weishampel and colleagues (2004) list the taxon at group rank (weishampel2004b, p. 567). Their list predates the fieldwork You and colleagues report, which places the dinosaur-bearing beds of the area in the Zhonggou.

**Open.** The horizon within the formation. Tang and colleagues (2001) place hypsilophodontid material in the upper part of their informal Lower Red Unit, and *Siluosaurus* is the only euornithopod listed for the area, so the unit probably holds it (our inference). That rests on a faunal list. Their Fig. 4 gives sections for six other localities and none for the hypsilophodontid site (tang2001b, p. 119). A section at that site, or Dong's (1997) description, would settle it.

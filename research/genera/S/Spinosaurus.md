# *Spinosaurus*

## What stage is the *Spinosaurus aegyptiacus* neotype?

*2026-08-05*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian. The neotype comes from the base of the upper unit of the Kem Kem beds, which Ibrahim and colleagues (2020) date as Cenomanian. Governs `stage: [Cenomanian]` on [*Spinosaurus aegyptiacus*](../../../genera/S/Spinosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ibrahim2014a, Fig. 1 caption: "Stratigraphic position at the base of the upper unit of the Kem Kem beds".
- ibrahim2020a, p. 1: "the fossiliferous, Cenomanian-age deltaic sediments in eastern Morocco, generally referred to as the 'Kem Kem beds'".

**Ruled out.**
- *Albian.* Jones (2026) gives "Baharija Fm., Egypt Kem Kem Fm.; Continental Red Beds, Morocco *Early Cretaceous, Albian Late Cretaceous, Cenomanian*" (jones2026c, p. 498). This is a range across every occurrence the row lists, not a date for the neotype's horizon.

**Open.** Nothing on the stage.

## Which specimen is the *Spinosaurus aegyptiacus* name-bearing type?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** FSAC-KK 11888, the neotype that Ibrahim and colleagues (2014) designated after Stromer's holotype was destroyed. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [FSAC-KK 11888]` and `specimen_type: neotype` on [*Spinosaurus aegyptiacus*](../../../genera/S/Spinosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ibrahim2014a, p. 1613: bones of the species "first came to light over a century ago from Upper Cretaceous rocks in Egypt … but were destroyed in WWII"; "The subadult skeleton, here designated the neotype of *Spinosaurus aegyptiacus*".
- ibrahim2014a, Fig. 2 caption: the reconstruction uses "the neotype (FSAC-KK 11888), referred specimens, and drawings of original bones".

**Ruled out.**
- *BSP 1912 VIII 19.* Jones (2026) lists Stromer's holotype first, marked "(destroyed)" (jones2026c, p. 498). A destroyed holotype is replaced by the neotype.
- *MSNM V4047.* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi (2019) give "MSNM V4047 Adult | Incomplete skull" for the species (molina-pérez2019a, p. 259). Ibrahim and colleagues use it as a referred specimen, the "anterior half of an adult skull" (ibrahim2014a, p. 1613).

**Open.** Whether the neotype designation stands. The record's notes report that it is contested and that the ICZN has not ruled; no paper read for this entry argues either side.

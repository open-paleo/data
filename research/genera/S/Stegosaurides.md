# *Stegosaurides*

## Is *Stegosaurides* a stegosaur, an ankylosaur, or neither?

*2026-10-19*

**Conclusion.** It is a nomen dubium that cannot be placed below Thyreophora. Its describer referred it provisionally to Stegosauridae; later reviews refer it to Ankylosauria; and Arbour and Currie (2015) find that its spine base occurs in both groups and its affinities cannot be determined. No work argues that it is valid. Governs `status: nomen dubium`, `parent: Thyreophora` with `placement: incertae sedis`, and the dispute on [*Stegosaurides excavatus*](../../../genera/S/Stegosaurides.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bohlin1953a, p. 66: "FAMILY ? STEGOSAURIDAE … Type: A couple of vertebrae and the base of a dermal spine. These remains are provisionally referred to the family Stegosauridae".
- maryańska1977a, p. 98: "Very incomplete and badly preserved material of Stegosaurides excavatus and Peishansaurus philemys is the main basis for the view that these two species are nomina dubia"; p. 92: "nomen dubium ?ankylosaur".
- carpenter2001d, Table 21.2: "Ankylosaur taxa not accepted as valid because the material does not allow the taxon to be diagnosed separately from other taxa", including *Stegosaurides excavatus*.
- arbour2015a, p. 43: "Although conical spikes are present in stegosaurs, this osteoderm morphology also occurs in ankylosaurs"; "it is not possible to determine the affinities of 'Stegosaurides'"; "Status. Nomen dubium; ?Thyreophora indet."

**Ruled out.**
- *Stegosauria or Ankylosauria as the parent.* Each rests on the spine base, which Arbour and Currie show does not distinguish the two.
- *A valid taxon.* Bohlin's placement was provisional, and every later review treats the name as dubious.

**Open.** Nothing on the placement. Ankylosaurid remains have since been found in the area (Arbour and Currie, 2015), which bears on the fauna but not on this material.

## What stages does *Stegosaurides* take?

*2026-10-11*

**Conclusion.** Barremian to Albian, the range of the Xinminpu Group, since no source dates the Hui-hui-p'u beds. The narrower Barremian–Aptian came with the record's import and has no source, as with [*Peishansaurus*](../P/Peishansaurus.md). Governs `stage: [Barremian, Aptian, Albian]` on [*Stegosaurides excavatus*](../../../genera/S/Stegosaurides.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bohlin1953a: the region's "badlands of Late Mesozoic age", and the Hui-hui-p'u deposit "is evidently not contemporaneous with the badlands" at Chia-yü-kuan; no age is given for the species.

**Ruled out.**
- *Barremian–Aptian.* No source read narrows the group's range for this locality.

**Open.** A dating of the Hui-hui-p'u beds.

## Is the Stegosaurides type from the Xinminpu Group?

*2026-09-06*

**Conclusion.** The group is assigned because Hui-hui-p'u lies in the Jiuquan Basin near Chia-yü-kuan (Jiayuguan), where Tang and colleagues (2001) place Bohlin's material and recognize the group. No source places the type in a formation. Governs `location.group: Xinminpu` and the empty `location.formation` on [*Stegosaurides excavatus*](../../../genera/S/Stegosaurides.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bohlin1953a, p. 62: the Hui-hui-p'u badlands "lie between the small isolated mountains of Hei-shan and Ku'an-t'ai-shan".
- tang2001b, p. 116: Lower Cretaceous sediments "are developed in the neighbouring Jiuquan Basin (Jiayuguan area) where some fragmentary vertebrate fossils were recovered by the Sino-Swedish expedition in the 1920s (Bohlin, 1953)"; p. 117: Qi (1984) "suggested that the Xinminbao Group throughout northern Gansu, including the Jiuquan Basin, should be regarded as the Early Cretaceous".

**Ruled out.**
- *One horizon with the Chia-yü-kuan badlands.* Bohlin writes that "The Hui-hui-p'u deposit, situated almost within sight of Chia-yü-kuan, is evidently not contemporaneous with the badlands at this place" (bohlin1953a, Summary), so a dating or unit for the Chia-yü-kuan beds does not carry over.

**Open.** A formation. Tang and colleagues do not separate Hui-hui-p'u from the Chia-yü-kuan badlands, and that Hui-hui-p'u lies in the Jiuquan Basin is our reading of Bohlin's description and map.

# *Santanaraptor*

## Where does *Santanaraptor* sit within Coelurosauria?

*2026-10-20*

**Conclusion.** Unresolved; it stays at Coelurosauria incertae sedis. Some analyses recover it as a tyrannosauroid, but the preferred result of the most recent analysis places it among the earliest maniraptoromorphs, and others recover it as a basal maniraptoran or prune it as unstable. Governs `parent: Coelurosauria`, `placement: incertae sedis` and the dispute on [*Santanaraptor placidus*](../../../genera/S/Santanaraptor.yml).

**Evidence.**
- holtz2004b, p. 133: among taxa "not examined phylogenetically for this analysis but for which there is some tentative evidence for a position within Tyrannosauroidea".
- rauhut2010a, p. 189: "the characters listed (Holtz, 2004: 133) indicate only that this taxon is a non-maniraptoran basal coelurosaur".
- porfiri2014a, Fig. 12, p. 50: *Santanaraptor* as the first branch of a tyrannosauroid clade with Megaraptora and the tyrannosaurid lineage.
- delcourt2018a, p. 1: "Santanaraptor and Timimus were tyrannosauroids of a type that was more derived than Dilong paradoxus and more basal than Xiongguanlong baimoensis".
- dalsasso2011a, p. 123: "Santanaraptor placidus is found to be a basal maniraptoran that is more advanced than Ornitholestes".
- novas2012a, p. 68: "the lack of resolution close to the base of Coelurosauria is mostly a consequence of a single wild-card taxon, the enigmatic South American coelurosaur Santanaraptor"; p. 71: "recovered as an indeterminate basal coelurosaur".
- delcourt2025a, p. 1: "Our preferred phylogenetic hypothesis places S. placidus and M. asymmetrica in the earliest-branching maniraptoromorph clade"; p. 24: "Under equal weights, S. placidus is placed as an early branching Megaraptora, with that clade nested within Tyrannosauroidea".

**Ruled out.**
- *Tyrannosauroidea as the parent.* The preferred result of Delcourt and colleagues (2025) and the result of Dal Sasso and Maganuco (2011) contradict it.
- *Maniraptora or Maniraptoromorpha as the parent.* The tyrannosauroid results of Porfiri and colleagues (2014) and Delcourt and Grillo (2018) contradict it.

**Open.** Whether further analyses settle it.

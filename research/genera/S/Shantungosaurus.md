# *Shantungosaurus*

## Which diagnosis and holotype does *Shantungosaurus giganteus* take, and does Hu and colleagues' Xingezhuang change its formation?

*2026-10-23*

**Conclusion.** The amended diagnosis of Xing and colleagues (2014), and the holotype as they and Hu and colleagues (2001) restrict it, the posterior skull GMV 1780-1. Hu and colleagues (2001) redescribe the type material bone by bone but give a diagnosis that restates Hu (1973); Xing and colleagues revise it, keeping only the gigantic size and the dentary count and dismissing the rest as common among hadrosaurines. The formation stays Hongtuya: Hu and colleagues place the holotype in their Xingezhuang Formation, but Xing and colleagues put Longgujian in the lower Hongtuya, as the provincial lithostratigraphy does. Governs `described_in: xing2014b`, `diagnostic_features`, `type_specimen.material` and the synonym reasons on [*Shantungosaurus giganteus*](../../../genera/S/Shantungosaurus.yml), and `location.formation: Hongtuya`, unchanged. Supersedes the 2026-08-06 entry's ruling that no source read places the Longgujian bones in the Xingezhuang.

**Evidence.**
- xing2014b, p. 1631, amended diagnosis: "Gigantic hadrosaurine hadrosaurid that has the following autapomorphies: maxilla with 62 or more tooth positions, dentary with 60 or more alveoli, and paired exoccipitals with a strongly expanded posterodorsally central shelf that is 2.5–3.0 times as tall as the foramen magnum."
- xing2014b, pp. 1632–1633: "Most of the diagnostic characters of *Shantungosaurus giganteus* noted by Hu et al. (2001) are common among hadrosaurines. They are therefore of little help in diagnosing this species. Only two characters are retained for the diagnosis of *S. giganteus*: 'the gigantic body size' and '60–63 alveoli for the dentary tooth row'".
- xing2014b, p. 1630: "Holotype: GMV 1780-1, partial, articulated posterior portion of a skull, including most of the braincase, and some neighboring dermal bones of the skull roof (paired postorbitals and squamosals)"; the maxillae, premaxillae, dentaries and other cranial bones are the paratype GMV 1780-2. hu2001a, p. 8, gives the same division between GMV 1780-1 and GMV 1780-2.
- xing2014b, p. 1631: the bone beds run "from the top of the Xingezhuang Formation to the middle of the Hongtuya Formation", with Longgujian in the lower Hongtuya; zhang1996a, p. 247: "Longgujian at Lübiao, Zhucheng, yields *Shantungosaurus giganteus*" (translated), in the Hongtuya fauna.
- hu2001a, p. 8: "Longgujian, Kugou Village, Lübiao Township, Zhucheng City, Shandong Province; lower part of the Wangshi Group, Xingezhuang Formation, Upper Cretaceous" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Hu and colleagues' (2001) diagnosis.* It repeats the 1973 characters, which Xing and colleagues set aside, and the same book says tooth-groove counts "have no taxonomic significance" (hu2001a, p. 63, translated).
- *The Xingezhuang Formation for the holotype.* Hu and colleagues' Xingezhuang belongs to their parallel scheme of the Wangshi Group (see [Wangshi](../../stratigraphy/w/wangshi.md)); Xing and colleagues and the province put Longgujian in the Hongtuya.
- *Cenomanian–Turonian.* Hu and colleagues date the Xingezhuang beds that hold the holotype to the Cenomanian–Turonian (hu2001a, pp. 55–57), on hadrosaur grade and sporopollen, an age the Campanian dating of the Hongtuya bone beds (an2015a) does not support.

**Open.** Xing and colleagues' osteology covers only the features *Shantungosaurus* shares with *Edmontosaurus*; Hu and colleagues (2001) remain the only bone-by-bone description.

## Which unit is the *Shantungosaurus giganteus* holotype from?

*2026-08-06*

**Conclusion.** The Hongtuya Formation of the Wangshi Group. Hu (1973) places the bones in the middle part of the Wangshi Group, and An and colleagues (2015) treat the Kugou–Longgujian bone beds as Hongtuya. Governs `location.formation: Hongtuya` on [*Shantungosaurus giganteus*](../../../genera/S/Shantungosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hu1973a, introduction: the fossils are from Longguyu, "located between Xijianü and Kugou", "belonging to the middle part of the Wangshi Group" (translated).
- an2015a, p. 76: "Detrital zircons were separated from six samples of sandstones from the dinosaur bone beds in the Hongtuya Formation … Four samples … were collected from a section in Kugou–Longgujian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Shanyang Formation.* Horner and colleagues (2004) give "Wangshi Group (Shandong), Shanyang Formation (Shaanxi)" (horner2004a, p. 441, Table 20.1). That cell lists the genus in two provinces, and only the Shandong half bears on the holotype.
- *Xingezhuang Formation.* Jones (2026) gives "Xingezhuang Fm., China" (jones2026a, p. 408). No source read places the Longgujian bones in it.

**Open.** Two links are inferences. That Hu's "middle part of the Wangshi Group" at Longguyu is the Hongtuya, and that An and colleagues' sampled section holds the holotype's bed, are both our readings. A paper that ties GMV 1780-1 to a level in the Kugou–Longgujian section would settle it.

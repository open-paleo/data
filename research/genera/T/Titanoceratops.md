# *Titanoceratops*

## Is the *Titanoceratops* holotype from the Fruitland or the Kirtland Formation?

*2026-09-08*

**Conclusion.** The upper Fruitland or the lowermost Kirtland; the quarry maps are lost and the two cannot be separated on what survives. The record gives the Fruitland, which Longrich (2011) favors on the quarry photographs. Governs `location.formation: Fruitland` on [*Titanoceratops ouranos*](../../../genera/T/Titanoceratops.yml).

**Evidence.**
- longrich2011a, p. 265: "The holotype was collected in San Juan County, New Mexico, from the upper Fruitland Formation or the lower Kirtland Formation"; "Maps showing the location of the quarry have been lost, and so the stratigraphic position of the specimen is uncertain"; "Quarry photographs (Fig. 2A) show that OMNH 10165 was excavated from soft shales overlain by resistant sandstones; this matches the beds of the upper Fruitland Formation".
- lehman1998a, p. 895: "The Fruitland Formation and lower part (lower shale member) of the Kirtland Shale are exposed along this part of Coal Creek, and this is consistent with the lithology of the specimen's matrix".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Naashoibito member of the Kirtland.* Suggested because all earlier triceratopsins are Maastrichtian. Longrich found only the Fruitland and the lowermost Kirtland exposed where the skeleton was recovered, "The closest outcrops of the Naashoibito are more than 5 km to the northeast", and a matrix lithology "common in the upper Fruitland and lower Kirtland … but not in the Naashoibito" (longrich2011a, p. 265).

**Open.** Fruitland or lower Kirtland. Finding the excavation, which Lehman (1998), after Wolberg and colleagues (1988), says may still be visible, would settle it.

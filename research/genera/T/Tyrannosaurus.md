# *Tyrannosaurus*

## Is the *Tyrannosaurus rex* type from the Hell Creek Formation?

*2026-08-06*

**Conclusion.** Yes. Osborn (1905) took the type from "the true Laramie of Hell Creek", the beds now called the Hell Creek Formation, and Longrich and Saitta (2024) place the same skeleton in the uppermost Hell Creek Formation. Governs `location.formation: Hell Creek` on [*Tyrannosaurus rex*](../../../genera/T/Tyrannosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- osborn1905a, p. 262: "Type. — The larger portion of a skeleton from the true Laramie of Hell Creek, Dawson County, Montana, 220 feet above the Ft. Pierre, American Museum No. 973."
- longrich2024d, p. 3: "He collected the second, AMNH 973 (sold to the Carnegie Museum, now CM 9380), in 1902 from the uppermost Hell Creek Formation of Montana".

**Ruled out.**
- *"Multiple".* Jones (2026) fills the occurrence cell with "Multiple; United States, Canada" (jones2026c, p. 502). It summarizes every unit that has yielded the species, not the type's horizon, and names no formation.
- *Laramie.* Osborn's "Laramie" is the name of its time for the Hell Creek beds, not a rival unit.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What is the number of the *Tyrannosaurus rex* holotype?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** CM 9380. Osborn (1905) designated the skeleton as American Museum No. 973; it was sold to the Carnegie Museum and now carries CM 9380. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [CM 9380]` on [*Tyrannosaurus rex*](../../../genera/T/Tyrannosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- osborn1905a, p. 262: "Type. — The larger portion of a skeleton ... American Museum No. 973."
- longrich2024d, p. 3: "AMNH 973 (sold to the Carnegie Museum, now CM 9380)".

**Ruled out.**
- *AMNH 973.* Jones (2026) gives "AMNH 973—partial skull and skeleton" (jones2026c, p. 502). It is the same skeleton under its former number.
- *"CMN 9380 or AMNH 973".* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi (2019) print this in their "Chronology of theropods considered largest historically" (molina-pérez2019a, Largest and Smallest Theropods). CMN is the Canadian Museum of Nature's prefix, not the Carnegie Museum's, and the other *T. rex* numbers in the book's size tables (FMNH PR2081, MOR 555, UCMP 118742, UCMP 137538) are referred specimens.

**Open.** Nothing.

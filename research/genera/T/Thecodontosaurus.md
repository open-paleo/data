# *Thecodontosaurus*

## What deposit and site is the *Thecodontosaurus* type material from?

*2026-09-08*

**Conclusion.** A Late Triassic fissure fill in Early Carboniferous limestone at Quarry Steps Quarry, Clifton, Bristol, a deposit no source names as a formation. Benton and colleagues (2000) give the site the Ordnance Survey reference ST 572747. Governs the absence of `location.formation`, and `location.locality: Quarry Steps Quarry, Clifton` and `location.coordinates`, on [*Thecodontosaurus antiquus*](../../../genera/T/Thecodontosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- benton2000b, p. 82, Locality and Horizon: "All materials are from the Durdham Down locality at Quarry Steps, off Belgrave Terrace, Clifton, Bristol, England (ST 572747). Specimens were found in fissure fills of Late Triassic age contained within Early Carboniferous (Dinantian) marine limestones."
- galton2007a, p. 515: Benton and colleagues "conclude that it represent a Triassic (Rheatian) dolomitized cave breccia or fissure fill (not in Magnesian/ Dolomitic Conglomerate …) in Lower Carboniferous (Dinantian) marine limestone".

**Ruled out.**
- *Magnesian Conglomerate.* The unit named in the title of the paper that erects the genus: remains "discovered in the autumn of 1834, in the Magnesian Conglomerate on Durdham Down, near Bristol" (riley1836a, p. 397). Galton (2007) records that the deposit "was long equated with the Magnesian Conglomerate, which occurs very close by" (galton2007a, p. 514), and excludes it, following Benton and colleagues (2000).
- *Redland.* Riley and Stutchbury (1840) moved the site to Redland, "on the south-eastern extremity of Durdham Down" (galton2007a, p. 513). Galton places the quarry in Clifton on Sanders's geological sections and an 1875 map.

**Open.** Nothing.

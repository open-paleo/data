# *Tsintaosaurus*

## Is the *Tsintaosaurus* holotype from the Hsikou gully near Chingkankou?

*2026-09-08*

**Conclusion.** The record names the village, Chingkankou, which is where Prieto-Márquez and Wagner (2013) place the species. No source read ties the gully name Hsikou to the holotype, IVPP V725. In the passage Chure (2000) quotes from Young (1958), Hsikou is the find site of IVPP K87, a theropod dorsal vertebra. Young's statement that IVPP V829 came from "the same gully" as the type of *Tsintaosaurus* is relayed without naming the gully. Governs `location.locality: Chingkankou` on [*Tsintaosaurus spinorhinus*](../../../genera/T/Tsintaosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- prieto-márquez2013b, p. 2, Occurrence: "Jingangkou Formation, Wangshi Group, near the village of Chingkankou, 20 km south of the city of Laiyang, Shandong Province".
- prieto-márquez2013b, p. 4, Nasal: "According to Young [17], the specimen was found in the same gully where the type and hypodigm of Tsintaosaurus spinorhinus were excavated", of IVPP V829.
- chure2000a, p. 527, IVPP K87: "from a gully (Hsikou) about 1 kilometer N.W. of the village of Chingkankou about 120 kilometres south of Laiyang", quoting Young (1958, pp. 99–100).

**Ruled out.**
- *Hsikou as the holotype's site.* The only source read that names Hsikou gives it for IVPP K87. It may be the *Tsintaosaurus* gully as well, but that has not been checked.

**Open.** Two things that Young (1958) would settle, since that monograph has not been read: which gully yielded IVPP V725, and the distance from Laiyang. Chure's quotation of Young gives about 120 km, and Prieto-Márquez and Wagner give 20 km for the same village.

## Is *Tsintaosaurus spinorhinus* Campanian or Maastrichtian?

*2026-08-05*

**Conclusion.** Campanian. The one statement that dates the type horizon itself puts it in the lower Campanian; the Maastrichtian readings concern the Jingangkou Formation or the Wangshi Group as a whole. Governs `stage: [Campanian]` on [*Tsintaosaurus spinorhinus*](../../../genera/T/Tsintaosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- prieto-márquez2013b, p. 2, Occurrence: "Lower Campanian [33] Jingangkou Formation, Wangshi Group, near the village of Chingkankou, 20 km south of the city of Laiyang"; p. 2, Introduction: "a bonebed in the Campanian (Upper Cretaceous) Jingangkou Formation".
- horner2004a, p. 442, Table 20.1: "?Campanian" for *T. spinorhinus*.

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian.* Jones (2026) prints "Jingangkou Fm., China *Late Cretaceous, Maastrichtian*" (jones2026a, Lambeosaurinae table) with no source for the age. The Maastrichtian in the literature is formation-wide: Yao and colleagues (2026) give the Jingangkou as "?late Campanian to early Maastrichtian" (yao2026a, p. 2), and the Wangshi Group's Coniacian–Maastrichtian goes back to a personal communication (see [Wangshi](../../stratigraphy/w/wangshi.md)). Neither dates the *Tsintaosaurus* bonebed.

**Open.** Prieto-Márquez and Wagner take their lower Campanian from their reference 33, which has not been read. A date from the Chingkankou bonebed would settle whether the horizon reaches the Maastrichtian.

## Is the Wangshi Group a rival to the Jingangkou Formation for *Tsintaosaurus*?

*2026-08-03*

**Conclusion.** No. The Jingangkou is a formation of the Wangshi Group, so "Wangshi Group" names the container of the record's unit, not a different one. Governs `location.formation: Jingangkou` on [*Tsintaosaurus spinorhinus*](../../../genera/T/Tsintaosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- prieto-márquez2013b, p. 2, Occurrence: "Jingangkou Formation, Wangshi Group, near the village of Chingkankou".

**Ruled out.**
- *Wangshi Group as the formation.* Horner and colleagues (2004) give "Wangshi Group (Shandong), People's Republic of China" (horner2004a, p. 442, Table 20.1). It is the group, one rank above the Jingangkou.

**Open.** An and colleagues (2015) list *Tsintaosaurus spinorhinus* among taxa "reported in recent years from the Hongtuya Formation" (an2015a, p. 81), in a sentence citing papers on Zhucheng finds. The type locality is at Chingkankou near Laiyang, which Prieto-Márquez and Wagner place in the Jingangkou; that An and colleagues' list does not rest on a reading of the Laiyang site is an inference. A source placing the Chingkankou bonebed in the Hongtuya would reopen the question.

# *Thespesius*

## Is the ranch of Horace Dwito the *Thespesius* type locality?

*2026-09-08*

**Conclusion.** No. Lull and Wright (1942) give the ranch as "a more definite site in this area" for a separate hadrosaur collection of the Colorado Museum of Natural History, not for the syntypes Hayden and Sully collected. The only locality any source gives for the syntypes is Grand River, and for the vertebra Sully obtained secondhand that locality is Hayden's supposition. Governs `location.locality: Grand River` on [*Thespesius occidentalis*](../../../genera/T/Thespesius.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lull1942a, p. 24, Locality 38: "Established by Dr. F. V. Hayden and Capt. A. Sully in the Lance formation on Grand River, where they collected the cotypes of Thespesius occidentalis"; "A more definite site in this area is given by the Colorado Museum of Natural History as the ranch of Horace Dwito, located in the southwest corner, T. 20 S., R. 21 E., Watauga, Corson Co. Their collection consists of lower jaws, leg bones, and vertebrae of a hadrosaur".
- leidy1856a, pp. 311–312: "The phalanx and one vertebra were discovered by Dr. Hayden in the lowest member of the Lignite formation of Grand River, Nebraska. The other vertebra was obtained by Capt. Alfred Sully from an Indian … The specimen Dr. Hayden supposes to have been derived from the same locality."

**Ruled out.**
- *The ranch of Horace Dwito, Watauga, Corson County.* Lull and Wright file it under the same numbered locality as the syntypes, but tie it only to the museum's own collection; no source connects USNM 219–221 to the ranch.

**Open.** Nothing.

## References

- `leidy1856a`: Leidy, J. (1856). Notices of Extinct Vertebrata Discovered by Dr. F. V. Hayden, during the Expedition to the Sioux Country under the Command of Lieut. G. K. Warren. *Proceedings of the Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia* 8: 311-312.

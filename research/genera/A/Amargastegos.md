# *Amargastegos*

## Does all of MACN N-43 come from one horizon?

*2026-08-12*

**Conclusion.** Not certainly. All but one bone were found together near the type locality of *Vincelestes*, in the Puesto Antigual Member; the remaining cervical vertebra was found about 100 m away, near the *Amargasaurus* type locality, and may come from an older bed. Governs `location.member: Puesto Antigual` and `location.locality: La Amarga Creek` on [*Amargastegos brevicollus*](../../../genera/A/Amargastegos.yml), which describe the main group of bones.

**Evidence.**
- pereda-suberbiola2013a, p. 2 of the online-first file, Geological setting: "Most of the vertebrate remains found in the La Amarga Formation come from the basal Puesto Antigual Member (Leanza et al. 2004)", from "several stratigraphical levels".
- pereda-suberbiola2013a, p. 2: "all the remains but one were found near the type locality of the mammal Vincelestes neuquenianus. The less complete cervical vertebra, which has a lighter colour than the other remains, was recovered at about 100 m from the type locality of Amargasaurus cazaui. It is possible that this vertebra comes from a stratigraphically older bed than the remainder of the material (J.F. Bonaparte, pers. comm.)."
- pereda-suberbiola2013a, p. 8, Discussion: "With the exception of the cervical vertebra of Fig. 3F–H, the bones from the La Amarga Creek were closely associated and presumably belong to a single individual."

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** The bed of the cervical vertebra in Fig. 3F–H, and whether it is older than the Puesto Antigual Member or only lower within it. The evidence is a personal communication from the collector. Bonaparte's own account of the 1985 find (*Dinosaurios de América del Sur*, 1996) has not been read and may give the field record.

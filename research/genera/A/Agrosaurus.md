# *Agrosaurus*

## Where does the *Agrosaurus* type material come from?

*2026-08-11*

**Conclusion.** Durdham Down, Clifton, Bristol, the bone breccia that also yielded *Thecodontosaurus*. The type material's own label names the northeastern coast of Australia; the matrix and the trace elements of the bone outweigh it. Governs `location.country: GB`, `location.region: GB-BST` and `location.locality: Durdham Down, Clifton, Bristol` on [*Agrosaurus macgillivrayi*](../../../genera/A/Agrosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- seeley1891a, p. 164: the bones are "labelled, in a small, delicate handwriting, "'Fly,' 1844. Jn. Macgillivray, from the N.E. coast of Australia.""
- vickers-rich1999a, p. 195: Huene (1906) described the enclosing rock as "a grey breccia full of bone fragments", "extremely reminiscent of the bone breccia at Durdham Down near Bristol" (Vickers-Rich and colleagues' translation).
- vickers-rich1999a, p. 197, Fig. 7: the trace-element abundances "in the fossil bone from the holotype of Agrosaurus macgillivrayi and the sample from Durdham Down closely track one another, while the two objects found on Cape York Peninsula have a quite different trace element abundance signature".
- vickers-rich1999a, p. 198: the holotype "did not, in fact, come from northeastern Australia despite the label associated with the specimen"; "it is quite likely that it came from" Durdham Down.
- galton2007a, p. 510: Vickers-Rich and colleagues (1999) showed the species "to be based on mislabeled Durdham Down material of *Thecodontosaurus antiquus* (BMNH 49984 …)".

**Ruled out.**
- *The northeastern coast of Australia.* The label is the only evidence for it, and later catalogues repeat it ("Lower Jurassic—Australia, Queensland, York Peninsula", white1973a, p. 120). Vickers-Rich and colleagues prospected the Helby Beds of eastern Cape York, the unit they judged the most likely source on the ship's 1844 movements. They found nothing comparable, and the trace elements of the bone match Durdham Down instead (vickers-rich1999a, pp. 195–198).

**Open.** Which Durdham Down quarry. The material reached the museum in 1879 through the sale of a private collection after "the material had already passed through several hands" (vickers-rich1999a, p. 196), and no source read for this note ties it to one quarry.

# *Altispinax*

## Is NHMUK R1828 from Battle or from Hollington Quarry?

*2026-10-29*

**Conclusion.** Battle, near Hastings. Huene (1926) describes two separate British Museum specimens: the *Megalosaurus dunkeri* material from "the quarry of Hollington", teeth and incomplete bones, and "another specimen from the Wealden of Battle", the three tall-spined dorsal vertebrae that are R1828. Maisch (2016) gives Hollington Quarry as the type locality "according to Stovall & Langston (1950)", but Stovall and Langston give no locality for the vertebrae, and every other treatment read places them at Battle. Hollington is the locality of the other specimen. Also supersedes the 2026-08-06 entry, which says the record keeps the Wadhurst Clay: since 13 October 2026 the record has given only the Hastings Beds group, because no source read places R1828 in a formation. Governs `location.locality: Battle, near Hastings` and `location.group: Hastings Beds` on [*Altispinax dunkeri*](../../../genera/A/Altispinax.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huene1926a, p. 482: "This specimen, belonging to the British Museum, was found in the quarry of Hollington near Hastings, Sussex, in the Wadhurst Clay of the Lower Wealden. It consists of two teeth, one incomplete opisthocœlous anterior dorsal vertebra …"; then "There is another specimen from the Wealden of Battle, near Hastings, Sussex, consisting of three articulated middle dorsal vertebræ, with extremely high neural spines."
- olshevsky1991a, p. 111: "BMNH R1828, a series of three caudal dorsal vertebrae with very tall neural spines from the Lower Wealden of Battle-near-Hastings, East Sussex."
- naish2007a, p. 503: the vertebrae "with tall, robust neural spines (Fig. 7) from the Hastings Beds Group of Battle, East Sussex".
- carrano2012a, p. 252: "Three dorsal vertebrae (NHMUK R1828; Fig. 10) from the Hastings Beds Group (late Berriasian–Valanginian) of Battle, England".
- maisch2016a, p. 216: "Type specimen: According to Stovall & Langston (1950): NHMUK 1828 … Type locality: Hollington Quarry, Hastings, East Sussex".
- stovall1950a, p. 722: "Von Huene is nowhere explicit as to just which material he considers the type of the genus Altispinax"; the discussion gives no locality for the vertebrae.

**Ruled out.**
- *Hollington Quarry.* Maisch (2016) alone gives it, with no source for it; Huene (1926) assigns it to the other *M. dunkeri* specimen.
- *The Wadhurst Clay Formation.* The 2026-08-06 entry inferred it from Battle lying on its outcrop; no source read names a formation for R1828, and Huene's "Wadhurst Clay" belongs to the Hollington specimen.

**Open.** Owen's (1855) original account of the vertebrae and Lydekker's (1888) catalogue entry for R1828 have not been read; either could give the collector's locality.

## What is the type material of *Altispinax dunkeri*?

*2026-10-28*

**Conclusion.** The three dorsal vertebrae NHMUK R1828, as Maisch (2016) gives it, following the earlier entry on the name. The record's material, completeness and diagnostic features had described Dames's tooth; they now describe the vertebrae, with the diagnosis from Maisch. *Becklespinax* shares the type specimen and is a junior objective synonym. The species is still named for Wilhelm Dunker, but Dames credits Dunker with lending the tooth, not with finding it. Governs `type_specimen.material`, the `diagnostic_features`, the species etymology and the *Becklespinax* synonym type on [*Altispinax*](../../../genera/A/Altispinax.yml).

**Evidence.**
- maisch2016a, p. 216: "Type specimen: According to Stovall & Langston (1950): NHMUK 1828. Three articulated nearly complete posterior dorsal vertebrae (probably numbers 9-11 or 10-12)"; "Diagnosis: Large theropod; posterior dorsal vertebrae bearing high neural spines possibly forming a short sail in front of the hip region, longest neural spines have at least 5 times the height of centrum and are distinctly constricted just dorsal to the neural arches."
- maisch2016a, p. 218: "The replacement names Acrocanthosaurus altispinax proposed by Paul (1988) and Becklespinax altispinax proposed by Olshevsky (1991) thus become junior objective synonyms."
- dames1885a, pp. 187–188: the tooth was "kindly lent to the speaker by Privy Councillor Dunker of Marburg"; "It shall be called Megalosaurus Dunkeri, after the monographer of our Wealden, who also entrusted me with the specimen for study" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *A tooth as the type material.* That is Dames's species, which the earlier entry rules is not the name-bearer.

**Open.** Maisch (2016, p. 216) gives the type locality as Hollington Quarry, Hastings; the record has Battle, near Hastings, on which the formation entry relies.

## Is *Altispinax dunkeri* Huene, 1923 the name for the Battle vertebrae?

*2026-10-28*

**Conclusion.** The record follows Maisch (2016): *Altispinax dunkeri* Huene, 1923 is the name for NHMUK R1828, credited to Huene (1923) rather than to Dames (1884), with *Becklespinax* a junior objective synonym. Maisch argues that Huene founded the species on the vertebrae, which Lydekker had referred to Dames's tooth species, by a deliberate use of a misidentification (ICZN Art. 11.10). No later paper tests that argument; since 2016 some authors use *Altispinax dunkeri*, others *Becklespinax altispinax*, in passing. That is divided usage rather than a dispute, so the record carries no `dispute` block. Governs `erected_in: huene1923a` on *Altispinax dunkeri* and the *Becklespinax* synonym on [*Altispinax*](../../../genera/A/Altispinax.yml).

**Evidence.**
- maisch2016a, p. 218: "according to Art. 11.10 of the ICZN (4th edition), the species Altispinax dunkeri v. Huene, 1923 non Dames, 1884 can be retained as a valid taxon, since the use of the name Megalosaurus dunkeri by v. Huene (1923) was evidently a deliberate use of misidentification."
- maisch2016a, p. 216: of the two species Huene included, "None of them was explicitly designated as the type species, but Altispinax dunkeri (Dames) was considered as such and made the genotype by Kuhn (1939)."
- huene1923a, p. 453: "The species described as M. dunkeri by Lydekker (Dames), from the English (and German) Lower Wealden, is distinguished from Megalosaurus by its enormously high neural spines in the dorsal region. I therefore propose to establish a new genus, Altispinax, for it."
- Following Maisch: jones2026c, p. 159 ("the convoluted nomenclature was settled (hopefully) once and for all, leaving us with Altispinax dunkeri (Maisch, 2016)"); lockwood2025a, p. 2 ("Altispinax dunkeri Huene 1923"); barker2023a, p. 6.
- Using *Becklespinax*, without discussion: molina-pérez2019a, p. 39; raven2020a, p. e1826956-23; tucker2022a, p. 17. morrison2026a, p. 2, leaves it open: "attributable to either Altispinax or Becklespinax (Maisch [20] regarding the taxonomy of this material)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Treating the name as disputed.* No paper since 2016 argues against Maisch; the *Becklespinax* uses are passing mentions.
- *Adopting a reading of our own.* Both points under **Open** are this project's readings of the Code, not published arguments.

**Open.**
- Art. 11.10 requires a deliberate misidentification, which Arts. 67.13 and 70.4 tie to fixing a type species or to an expressly stated misidentification. Huene (1923) fixed no type species and states no misidentification, still crediting Dames. His earlier-written but later-printed account reads as an ordinary misidentification: "if it were certain that such dorsal vertebrae belong to Megalosaurus dunkeri, it would be necessary to put it into a distinct genus, for which the name Altispinax, gen. nov., might be reserved", calling the Dames tooth "The type-specimen" (huene1926a, p. 483). Olshevsky (1991, p. 22) likewise reads *Altispinax* as "a conditional name, without a type species".
- If Maisch's route fails, Art. 70.3.2 lets an author fix as type species the species actually involved in a misidentified type species, citing both names and the article. That would make *Acrocanthosaurus altispinax* Paul, 1988 the type, giving *Altispinax altispinax* (Paul, 1988), with *Becklespinax* still a junior objective synonym. No paper has made this designation.

## Where does *Altispinax* sit within Tetanurae?

*2026-10-15*

**Conclusion.** At Tetanurae incertae sedis, with the finer placement disputed. Carrano and colleagues (2012) exclude the vertebrae from Spinosauridae and Carcharodontosauridae; Naish (2003), Maisch (2016) and Morrison and colleagues (2026) favour an allosauroid or carcharodontosaurian affinity, none from a phylogenetic analysis. Tetanurae is the level all of them share. Governs `parent: Tetanurae` and the dispute on [*Altispinax*](../../../genera/A/Altispinax.yml).

**Evidence.**
- carrano2012a, p. 252: "Other than to exclude *Becklespinax* from Spinosauridae and Carcharodontosauridae, we cannot place it more specifically within Tetanurae"; the long spines "led many workers (Walker 1964; Romer 1966; Carroll 1988; Kurzanov 1989) to assign *Becklespinax* to Spinosauridae".
- naish2003a: *B. altispinax* "is regarded here as an allosauroid (pending further work)".
- maisch2016a: "Family *incertae sedis* (? Carcharodontosauridae … because of the striking similarity to the recently described carcharodontosaurid *Concavenator*)"; "*Altispinax dunkeri* v. Huene, 1923 is a valid taxon according to the ICZN … the generic name *Becklespinax* Olshevsky, 1991 [is a] junior objective synonym".
- morrison2026a: "a possible carcharodontosaurian that is attributable to either *Altispinax* or *Becklespinax*".

**Ruled out.**
- *"In one analysis to Carcharodontosauria."* The earlier summary. Jones (2026) writes "One analysis went further, more specifically placing in within Carnosauria (Naish, 2003)" (jones2026c), but Naish (2003) gives an opinion, not an analysis, and the carcharodontosaurid reading is Maisch's (2016).

**Open.** No phylogenetic analysis has included the vertebrae.

## Is Jones's "Hastings Grp." a rival to the Wadhurst Clay for *Altispinax dunkeri*?

*2026-08-06*

**Conclusion.** No. The Hastings Beds is the group that contains the Wadhurst Clay Formation, so Jones (2026) and Carrano and colleagues (2012) give the coarser unit. The record keeps the Wadhurst Clay. Governs `location.formation: Wadhurst Clay` on [*Altispinax dunkeri*](../../../genera/A/Altispinax.yml).

**Evidence.**
- carrano2012a, p. 252: "Three dorsal vertebrae (NHMUK R1828; Fig. 10) from the Hastings Beds Group (late Berriasian–Valanginian) of Battle, England".
- jones2026c, p. 498: "Hastings Grp., United Kingdom Early Cretaceous, Berriasian/Valanginian".
- The [Wadhurst Clay](../../../stratigraphy/w/wadhurst-clay.yml) registry entry records `parent: Hastings Beds`.

**Ruled out.**
- *Hastings Group as a rival claim.* It names the containing group, not a different unit.

**Open.** No source read names the formation for NHMUK R1828. The Wadhurst Clay rests on Battle lying on that formation's outcrop, which is an inference from the locality, not a reading. A paper that places the specimen in a formation would settle it.

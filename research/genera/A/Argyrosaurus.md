# *Argyrosaurus*

## Was the *Argyrosaurus* forelimb found near Pampa Pelada or at La Angostura?

*2026-08-12*

**Conclusion.** Near Pampa Pelada, on the left (northwest) bank of the Río Chico, northeast of Lago Colhué Huapi. Huene (1929) gives the site on the word of Carlos Ameghino, who found the specimen, and Mannion and Otero (2012) follow him. Governs `location.locality: near Pampa Pelada, northeast of Lago Colhué Huapi` on [*Argyrosaurus superbus*](../../../genera/A/Argyrosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huene1929a, p. 77 (translated): "It was found by Carlos Ameghino. According to verbal communication from this gentleman"; "The place of discovery is on the left margin, or northwest, of the Río Chico del Chubut, almost 5 kilometers from it, in the Pampa Peluda or de los Boers".
- mannion2012a, p. 616: "Left bank (northwest) of the Río Chico, near Pampa Pelada, northeast of Lago Colhué Huapi, Chubut Province, Argentina".

**Ruled out.**
- *La Angostura, on the south bank of the Río Chico.* Powell (2003) reports that Florentino Ameghino (1898) put the site "in the angle between the Rfo Chico and Lago Musters", which "seems to coincide with what was discovered by Riggs, located now in the Estancia 'La Angostura', on the south bank of Rio Chico (Marshall 1978)" (powell2003a, p. 14). Powell sets this beside Huene's account without choosing. Huene's account comes from the collector, and in Powell's sentence La Angostura is the site of Riggs's find, which is not shown to be Carlos Ameghino's.

**Open.** Casal and colleagues (2015) say the specimen was "discovered by Ameghino on the south margin of the río Chico" (casal2015a, p. 89, translated), which agrees with Florentino Ameghino's placement rather than Huene's; they cite no source for it. The passage on *Argyrosaurus* read in Ameghino (1898) gives only "found by Carlos Ameghino at Lake Colhue" (ameghino1898a, p. 239, translated), and the phrase Powell quotes is not on the pages read. Marshall (1978) has not been read. Either source would show whether the south-bank placement rests on anything other than Florentino Ameghino's sentence.

## Which number and which paper fix the *Argyrosaurus superbus* type?

*2026-07-30*

**Conclusion.** MLP 77-V-29-1, a left forelimb, as Mannion and Otero (2012) give it. Lydekker (1893) took "the fore-limb as the type" and gave no number, so the 2012 reappraisal is the first paper read that does. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [MLP 77-V-29-1]` on [*Argyrosaurus superbus*](../../../genera/A/Argyrosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lydekker1893a, *Argyrosaurus superbus* n. g. et sp.: "Taking, then, the fore-limb as the type of the present species, and provisionally associating with it the femur and caudal vertebræ".
- mannion2012a, p. 616: "Holotype—MLP 77-V-29-1, a partly articulated left forelimb consisting of the humerus, ulna, radius, probably two carpals (now lost), and all five metacarpals."
- jones2026b, p. 381: "MLP 77-V-29-1—forelimb"; molina-pérez2020a, List of Sauropods, Titanosauria: "MLP 77-V-29-1 Indeterminate age".

**Ruled out.** Nothing. Both reference works give the same single number, and "Indeterminate age" in Molina-Pérez and Larramendi (2020) is their note on ontogeny, not part of the number.

**Open.** Whether the femur and caudal vertebrae that Lydekker associated "provisionally" belong to the type. The record's material lists them, but Mannion and Otero's holotype line lists only the forelimb.

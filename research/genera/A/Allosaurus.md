# *Allosaurus*

## Which quarry yielded the *Allosaurus fragilis* types, and does "lower Morrison" contradict the Brushy Basin Member?

*2026-08-11*

**Conclusion.** Felch Quarry 1 at Garden Park, Fremont County, Colorado, for both Marsh's holotype YPM 1930 and USNM 4734, at a level in the Brushy Basin Member. Governs `location.locality: Felch Quarry 1, Garden Park`, `location.region: US-CO` and `location.member: Brushy Basin` on [*Allosaurus fragilis*](../../../genera/A/Allosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- gilmore1920a, p. 3: the type of *Allosaurus fragilis* "is from "Garden Park," near Canon City, Fremont County, Colorado"; p. 7: "No. 4734, U.S.N.M. … Nearly complete skeleton from "Quarry No. 1," Canon City, Fremont County, Colorado. Collected by M. P. Felch, 1883-4."
- chure2000a, p. 166: USNM 4734 is "from Felch Quarry 1, Garden Park, the same quarry as the type specimen"; p. 167: "TYPE LOCALITY: Felch Quarry 1, Garden Park Colorado. HORIZON: 47.9 m above the J5 unconformity, Brushy basin member of the Morrison Formation".
- chure2000a, p. 386, for *Labrosaurus ferox*, also from Felch Quarry 1: "Brushy Basin Mbr., 47.9 m above the J5 unconformity and 2.2 m below the changes from greenish gray non-smectitic to purplish gray smectite clays separating the lower and middle Morrison Formation".
- paul2010a, p. 53: YPM 1930 is "from the lower Morrison Formation at Garden Park, Fremont County, central Colorado".

**Ruled out.**
- *Quarry 1, Como Bluff, Wyoming.* Chure gives "Quarry I, Como Bluff, WY" as the type locality of *Allosaurus atrox*, whose type YPM 1890 is the type of *Creosaurus atrox* (chure2000a, p. 183); Paul and Carpenter put YPM 1890 in "the middle Morrison Formation of southern Wyoming" (paul2010a, p. 54). It is a different quarry with the same number.
- *"Lower Morrison" as a rival to the Brushy Basin Member.* Chure's horizon line for the quarry puts it in the Brushy Basin Member and also 2.2 m below the change that separates the lower from the middle Morrison, so Paul and Carpenter's "lower Morrison" and Chure's member describe the same level. That the lower, middle and upper Morrison are an informal division cutting across the members is our reading of the two texts.

**Open.** Evanoff and Carpenter (1998), the Felch Quarry 1 study that Chure and Paul and Carpenter both cite for the level and for the two specimens' common quarry, has not been read.

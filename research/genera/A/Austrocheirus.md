# *Austrocheirus*

## Is the *Austrocheirus* holotype from the Pari Aike, the Mata Amarilla or the Cerro Fortaleza Formation?

*2026-08-13*

**Conclusion.** The Cerro Fortaleza Formation. In Sickmann and colleagues' (2018) scheme the describing paper's Pari Aike Formation is an informal name for terrestrial beds north of Lago Viedma, and the terrestrial beds between Lago Viedma and Lago Argentino, including the Río Leona area where the holotype was found, are the Cerro Fortaleza, which takes in rocks sometimes mapped as the Mata Amarilla. Governs `location.formation: Cerro Fortaleza` on [*Austrocheirus isasii*](../../../genera/A/Austrocheirus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ezcurra2010b, p. 3: "Hoyada Arroyo Seco locality (49°57'17'' S, 72°05'31'' W), close to the western coast of the Leona River and four kilometers south of the De los Hornos Hill"; "Pari Aike Formation, Late Cretaceous (early Maastrichtian".
- sickmann2018a, Nomenclature: "terrestrial deposits north of Lago Viedma are referred to with the informal delineation "undifferentiated Pari Aike Formation;" and (5) terrestrial deposits between Lago Viedma and Lago Argentino and east past Tres Lagos are referred to as "Cerro Fortaleza Formation." This final delineation "Cerro Fortaleza Formation" includes rocks that have sometimes been named "Mata Amarilla Formation"".
- sickmann2018a, Stratigraphic Correlations and Mapping: "Eastward from the Rio Leona area (Fig 4A), the Cerro Fortaleza Formation is traceable another 30 km E-ENE".

**Ruled out.**
- *Pari Aike Formation.* The describing paper's name (ezcurra2010b, p. 3), which Sickmann and colleagues keep only as an informal name north of Lago Viedma.
- *Mata Amarilla Formation.* Novas and colleagues (2013) caption a photograph "Outcrops of the Mata Amarilla Formation (Upper Cretaceous) at the shore of Viedma Lake, the fossil site where the holotype of Austrocheirus isasii was collected" (novas2013a, Fig. 9). The describing paper puts the site on the Leona River, 4 km south of the De los Hornos Hill, not on the lake shore, and Sickmann and colleagues fold the rocks mapped as Mata Amarilla between the lakes into the Cerro Fortaleza.

**Open.** Nothing.

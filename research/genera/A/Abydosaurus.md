# *Abydosaurus*

## Which member of the Cedar Mountain Formation holds DNM 16?

*2026-10-02*

**Conclusion.** None is recorded. Chure and colleagues (2010) put the bone bed in the base of a paleovalley fill cut into the Ruby Ranch Member and call that level the base of the Mussentuchit Member, and Sprinkel and colleagues (2012) hold that the Mussentuchit name does not apply to the top of the formation in northeastern Utah. Governs `location.member: (none)` on [*Abydosaurus mcintoshi*](../../../genera/A/Abydosaurus.yml); the record's location note gives both statements.

**Evidence.**
- chure2010a, p. 383: "The bonebearing horizon is 27 m above the base of the Cedar Mountain Formation, near the base of the Mussentuchit Member"; the bone-bearing sandstones and the mudstone below them "mark the base of 12-m thick paleovalley incised into the Ruby Ranch Member of the Cedar Mountain Formation".
- sprinkel2012a, p. 13: "it is unwarranted to use the member names of the Cedar Mountain Formation defined by Kirkland and others (1997) and Kirkland and others (1999) in central Utah for the Cedar Mountain Formation in northeastern Utah"; if they are used, "the Mussentuchit Member should not be applied to the uppermost part of the Cedar Mountain Formation in northeastern Utah".

**Ruled out.**
- *Ruby Ranch Member.* Chure and colleagues name it only as the unit the paleovalley is cut into, so the bones lie in the fill above it. Sprinkel and colleagues allow that the Ruby Ranch name might be used in northeastern Utah, but that does not place the valley fill in it.
- *Mussentuchit Member.* It is the describing paper's reading, set aside on Sprinkel and colleagues' restriction of the name to central Utah.

**Open.** Which member name, if any, applies to the paleovalley fill at Dinosaur National Monument. A stratigraphic study of the Cedar Mountain Formation in northeastern Utah that assigns the fill to a member would settle it.

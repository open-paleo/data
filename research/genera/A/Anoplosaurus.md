# *Anoplosaurus*

## Can a group stand in for the unit the syntypes came from?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** No. The two candidate units straddle the base of the Chalk Group, so no group contains both, and the record names no unit. Governs the absence of `location.formation` and `location.group` on [*Anoplosaurus curtonotus*](../../../genera/A/Anoplosaurus.yml), whose `location.notes` give the two candidates.

**Evidence.**
- pereda-suberbiola1999a, p. 186: "phosphatite-washing pit near Reach … ?Upper Gault Clay or ?Cambridge Greensand, Upper Albian"; p. 185: the associated material "may have been recovered from the phosphate beds of the uppermost Gault Clay rather than from the Cambridge Greensand".
- barrett2020a, p. 2, Geological background: the Cambridge Greensand "forms the basal-most unit of the West Melbury Marly Chalk Formation (Grey Chalk Subgroup)", and much of its fauna was "reworked from the underlying Albian Gault Formation".
- hopson2005a, p. 15, Chalk Group: the group rests "on the underlying Lower Cretaceous strata (Upper Greensand and Gault formations in the Southern Province …)", and its age is "Upper Cretaceous, Cenomanian to Maastrichtian".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Chalk Group, as the group containing both candidates.* It contains the Cambridge Greensand but not the Gault, which lies beneath the group's base.
- *Upper Greensand.* Seeley (1879) judged that the bones "show no signs of being derivative, and appear to me to be of Upper Greensand age" (seeley1879a, p. 600). Barrett and Bonsor (2020, p. 2) note that the deposit was "sometimes referred to … incorrectly as the 'Cambridge Upper Greensand'", the Upper Greensand Formation being "a lithostratigraphically distinct unit" deposited mainly in the Wessex and Wealden sub-basins.

**Open.** Which of the two units yielded the syntypes; nothing read for this note gives evidence beyond Pereda-Suberbiola and Barrett's taphonomic argument.

## Is SMC B55670 part of the *Anoplosaurus curtonotus* type?

*2026-08-06*

**Conclusion.** No. Pereda-Suberbiola and Barrett (1999) designated SMC B55731, a partial right scapula, as lectotype, and made the remaining syntypes, B55670 among them, paralectotypes. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [CAMSM B55731]` and `specimen_type: lectotype` on [*Anoplosaurus curtonotus*](../../../genera/A/Anoplosaurus.yml). SMC is an alias of CAMSM in the institution registry.

**Evidence.**
- pereda-suberbiola1999a, p. 186: "Designated lectotype. SMC B55731, a partial right scapula. *Anoplosaurus curtonotus* was erected on the basis of 77 syntypes (Seeley 1879). No holotype specimen was designated by Seeley (1879)."; next line: "Paralectotypes. SMC B55670, partial left dentary".
- jones2026a, p. 398: "SMC B55731, SMC B55670–55742—partial skeleton".

**Ruled out.**
- *SMC B55670 to 55742 as the type.* Jones (2026) adds the paralectotype series to the lectotype. After the 1999 designation those numbers no longer belong to the name-bearing type.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the *Anoplosaurus curtonotus* type Albian or Cenomanian?

*2026-08-05*

**Conclusion.** Albian. Both candidate source beds give a late Albian age for the syntypes. The Cenomanian is the depositional age of the Cambridge Greensand, which reworked older fossils. Governs `stage: [Albian]` on [*Anoplosaurus curtonotus*](../../../genera/A/Anoplosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- pereda-suberbiola1999a, p. 186: "?Upper Gault Clay or ?Cambridge Greensand, Upper Albian".
- barrett2020a, p. 2, Geological background: "Although the depositional age of the Cambridge Greensand Member is thought to be Cenomanian on the basis of its foraminiferan fauna (Hart, 1973), many fossils within it … were reworked from the underlying Albian Gault Formation", "suggesting an upper Albian age for these specimens".
- weishampel2004a, p. 367: "Cambridge Greensand (Cambridgeshire), England", "late Albian"; jones2026a, p. 398: "Early Cretaceous, Albian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Cenomanian.* It is the Cambridge Greensand's own age. A type reworked from older beds takes the age of those beds (see [methods](../../methods.md)), and the Gault alternative is Albian outright.

**Open.** Pereda-Suberbiola and Barrett's "Upper Albian" cites Rawson and colleagues (1978) together with a personal communication. Which of the two units yielded the syntypes is open (see the entry above on the group).

# *Archaeopteryx*

## Where and from what horizon was the Solnhofen specimen collected?

*2026-09-27*

**Conclusion.** Neither was recorded. The specimen reached science from a private collection whose owner kept no data on where it was found, and Elżanowski (2001), making it the holotype of *Wellnhoferia grandis*, gives its type horizon and type locality as unknown, each with only a probable unit or area. Governs no `location` field of [*Archaeopteryx*](../../../genera/A/Archaeopteryx.yml) itself, whose location is the London neotype's. It protects the unrecorded horizon and locality of the "Solnhofen specimen" in `notable_specimens` (`specimen_id: [BMMS 500]`), which carries neither, and of any later field or record built on that specimen.

**Evidence.**
- wellnhofer1988a, p. 1790: "Exact locality data are not available. According to the collector, an amateur, the specimen was found many years ago and no data were kept."
- wellnhofer1988a, p. 1790: "Although the quarry site is no longer known, it must have been located in the Eichstätt area where the Berlin and Eichstätt specimens were found"; the slab is "Solnhofen limestone of the so called "Fäule" facies, a soft marly limestone".
- elżanowski2001a, pp. 519–520: "This specimen, of unknown origin in terms of exact horizon and locality, was identified as another *Archaeopteryx* specimen".
- elżanowski2001a, p. 522: "Type horizon: unknown (probably Upper Solnhofen Lithographic Limestone), Late Jurassic (probably Malm zeta 2b, lower Lower Tithonian)"; "Type locality: unknown (in all probability Altmühl Valley, Bavaria, Germany)".
- foth2025a, p. 19: "Within the Altmühltal Formation around Solnhofen and Eichstätt, the lithographic limestones of the Eichstätt Member are somewhat older than the Solnhofen Member".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Altmühltal Formation, or its Eichstätt or Solnhofen Member, as the specimen's recorded unit.* Altmühltal is the current name for the "Solnhofen limestone" in this area (foth2025a, p. 18), but Elżanowski gives the horizon as unknown and the Upper Solnhofen Lithographic Limestone only as probable. The formation now holds two members of different age, and no source read places this specimen in either; reading "Upper Solnhofen" as the Solnhofen Member would be a correlation no source read makes.
- *The Eichstätt area as the locality.* Wellnhofer (1988) infers it from where two other specimens were found, not from any record of this one, and Elżanowski (2001) still calls the locality unknown, giving the Altmühl Valley as the probable area.
- *Wellnhofer (1988) as the source for an unknown horizon.* He reports that the locality data were not kept and identifies the rock of the slab; the statement that the horizon is unknown is Elżanowski's (2001).

**Open.** Where the collector found the slab. Wellnhofer's full description of the specimen, which Elżanowski cites as Wellnhofer (1988b) in the journal *Archaeopteryx*, has not been read, and might record more of the collector's account.

## Which quarry produced the London specimen?

*2026-08-12*

**Conclusion.** The former Ottmann quarry on the Langenaltheimer Haardt, from the lowest layers of the Upper Solnhofen layers. Governs `location.locality: Ottmann quarry, Langenaltheimer Haardt` on [*Archaeopteryx lithographica*](../../../genera/A/Archaeopteryx.yml), whose neotype is the London specimen, BMNH 37001.

**Evidence.**
- wellnhofer1993a, p. 5: "A further 300 m to the north was located the Ottmann quarry, which no longer exists today, the discovery site of the London specimen of 1861"; "The London specimen thus comes from the deepest layers of the Upper Solnhofen layers" (translated).
- dames1884a, p. 119: a second skeleton "lay in the lithographic shales on the Blumenberg near Eichstätt in Dürr's quarry, while the first came from the Langenaltheimer Haardt near Pappenheim" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Blumenberg quarry, Eichstätt.* The find site of the Berlin specimen, which Dames (1884) sets against the London specimen's Langenaltheimer Haardt in the same sentence.

**Open.** A member. Wellnhofer (1993) places the site in the Upper Solnhofen layers (Malm zeta 2b), and no source read equates those layers with the Solnhofen Member of the Altmühltal Formation.

## Is the London neotype from the Altmühltal, the Solnhofen or the Mörnsheim Formation?

*2026-08-03*

**Conclusion.** The Altmühltal Formation. Niebuhr and Pürner (2014) defined it for the plattenkalks formerly called the "Solnhofener Schichten", and Wellnhofer (1993) places the London specimen in the Upper Solnhofen layers. Governs `location.formation: Altmühltal` on [*Archaeopteryx lithographica*](../../../genera/A/Archaeopteryx.yml), whose neotype is the London specimen, BMNH 37001.

**Evidence.**
- niebuhr2014a, p. 7, Abstract: "The Altmühltal Formation contains the typical lithographic plattenkalks of the western Southern Franconian Alb, which formerly has been termed 'Solnhofener Schichten'".
- foth2025a, p. 18: "the 'Solnhofen limestone' *sensu stricto* is part of the Altmühltal Formation"; "The geologically younger Mörnsheim Formation lies on top of both these formations".
- wellnhofer1993a, p. 5 (translated): "The London specimen thus comes from the deepest layers of the Upper Solnhofen layers".
- jones2026c, p. 508: "Mörnsheim Fm.; Solnhofen Fm., Germany".

**Ruled out.**
- *Solnhofen Formation.* Jones (2026) uses the former name of the beds that Niebuhr and Pürner (2014) put in the Altmühltal Formation.
- *Mörnsheim Formation.* Jones (2026) gives it for the species as a whole. It overlies the Altmühltal and yields other specimens, but the London specimen comes from the Upper Solnhofen layers.

**Open.** Nothing.

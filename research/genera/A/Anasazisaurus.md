# *Anasazisaurus*

## Are Kimbeto Arroyo and Betonnie Tsosie Wash two localities for BYU 12950?

*2026-08-12*

**Conclusion.** No. Hunt and Lucas (1993) and Lucas and colleagues (2006) both identify the site as locality 4 of Rigby (1981): the first calls it Kimbeto Arroyo, and the second places it on the northwest flank of Betonnie Tsosie Wash, in the Farmington Member. Governs `location.locality: Betonnie Tsosie Wash` and `location.member: Farmington` on [*Anasazisaurus horneri*](../../../genera/A/Anasazisaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hunt1993a, p. 80: "BYU 12950 is a partial skull from the Kirtland Formation in Kimbeto Arroyo (Fig. 7; Horner, 1992, pl. 45). This locality is number 4 of Rigby (1981, text-fig. 5.2)", and the specimen "comes from strata low in the Kirtland Formation that are probably correlative with the Farmington Member".
- lucas2006b, p. 293: the skull was found "in the Kirtland Formation on the northwestern flank of Betonnie Tsosie Wash in the San Juan Basin, New Mexico (locality 4 of Rigby, 1981, textfig. 5.2; SE1/4 sec. 8, T22N, R9W, San Juan County). This locality is in the Farmington Member of the Kirtland Formation".
- lucas2006b, p. 294, Horizon and locality of holotype: "Farmington Member of Kirtland Formation, northwest flank of Betonnie Tsosie Wash".

**Ruled out.**
- *Kimbeto Arroyo as a separate site.* It carries the same Rigby locality number as the 2006 statement, and both papers cite Rigby's paper for "the local stratigraphy of Betonnie Tsosie Wash" (lucas2006b, references). Hunt and Lucas are both authors of the 2006 paper.
- *A hedged Farmington assignment.* Hunt and Lucas (1993) wrote "probably correlative with the Farmington Member"; Lucas and colleagues (2006) state the member without the hedge.

**Open.** Nothing.

## References

- `hunt1993a`: Hunt, A. P.; Lucas, S. G. (1993). Cretaceous vertebrates of New Mexico. *New Mexico Museum of Natural History and Science Bulletin* 2: 77-91.
- `lucas2006b`: Lucas, S. G.; Spielmann, J. A.; Sullivan, R. M.; Hunt, A. P.; Gates, T. (2006). Anasazisaurus, a hadrosaurian dinosaur from the Upper Cretaceous of New Mexico. *New Mexico Museum of Natural History and Science Bulletin*.

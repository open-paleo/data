# *Agustinia*

## Where on Cerro de los Leones was the *Agustinia* holotype found?

*2026-08-11*

**Conclusion.** At Cerro de los Leones, in fossiliferous level 3 of the Cullín Grande Member. The three papers that place the quarry agree on the hill and the level and disagree on which side of the hill it lies, and on its distance from Picún Leufú. Governs `location.locality: Cerro de los Leones` and `location.member: Cullín Grande` on [*Agustinia ligabuei*](../../../genera/A/Agustinia.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bonaparte1999c, p. 2: "Upper section of the Lohan Cura Formation, Aptian; north side of Cerro El León, 8 km west from Picún Leufú, southern Neuquén Province".
- martinelli2007b, p. 538: Cerro de Los Leones lies "approximately 8 km southwest of the town of Picún Leufú"; p. 548: "Agustinia was unearthed from fossiliferous level 3 on the northwestern side of the hill (39°35′38″S/69°24′00″W), while the holotype and referred specimen of Ligabuesaurus were discovered 6 m below the level of Agustinia, on the south side of the hill (39°35′18″S/69°23′54″W)".
- bellardini2022b, p. 2408: Cerro de los Leones is "12 km southwest to Picún Leufú town", and the bones came from level n°3 "on the south-western flank of the hill"; p. 2409, Fig. 1 caption: "about 10 kilometres to the southwest of Picún Leufú city", the holotype "recovered in 1997 from the southwestern flank of the hill (yellow waypoint)"; p. 2412: "in the southern flank of the Cerro de los Leones".

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian.* Bonaparte (1999) dates the horizon with the whole Lohan Cura Formation, which he calls "the continental Aptian Lohan Cura Formation" (bonaparte1999c, p. 1). Martinelli and colleagues (2007) give the formation's two members separate ages, "the Puesto Quiroga Member (Late Aptian) and the Cullín Grande Member (Early Albian)" (martinelli2007b, p. 539), and Bellardini and colleagues (2022) give the Albian.

**Open.** Which side of the hill, and whether the printed coordinate can be used. Martinelli and colleagues' two pairs put the *Agustinia* site about 0.6 km south and 0.1 km west of the *Ligabuesaurus* site. That fits Bellardini and colleagues' southern or south-western flank. It does not fit the "northwestern side" and "south side" in the same sentence that prints the pairs. The comparison is arithmetic on the printed numbers; it has not been checked against a map. Either the side words or the numbers in that sentence are wrong. A source that places the quarry independently, such as the satellite waypoint behind Bellardini and colleagues' Fig. 1C if it is ever published as a value, would settle it.

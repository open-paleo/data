# *Asylosaurus*

## Which quarry, and which unit, did the *Asylosaurus* holotype come from?

*2026-08-12*

**Conclusion.** Quarry Steps Quarry on Durdham Down, with no formation. Galton (2007) identifies the 1834 quarry from Sanders's geological work, describes the deposit as a Rhaetian fissure fill in Carboniferous limestone rather than a named rock unit, and gives the *Asylosaurus* horizon and locality as those of the *Thecodontosaurus antiquus* types. Governs `location.locality: Quarry Steps Quarry, Durdham Down` and the empty `location.formation` on [*Asylosaurus yalensis*](../../../genera/A/Asylosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton2007a, p. 560: for *Thecodontosaurus antiquus*, "Upper Triassic (Rhaetian) fissure fill in Quarry Steps Quarry off Upper Belgrave Road in Clifton, Bristol, England (UK National Grid Reference ST 572747"; for *Asylosaurus yalensis*, "Horizon and type (only) locality : as for types of *Thecodontosaurus antiquus* (see above)."
- galton2007a, p. 514: the site "can be positively identified as the Quarry Steps Quarry based on Sanders' original geological work rather than on hearsay published over 55 years later."

**Ruled out.**
- *The Avenue Quarry, near Worrall Road.* Perceval (1908) placed the find at an exposure "on the west side at the south end of Worrall Road", which Galton identifies as the Avenue Quarry; Huene (1908) and Kelloway and Welch (1993) also give that quarry. Galton notes that "Perceval (1908) gave no reasons for identifying this quarry" and that Huene "apparently misunderstood Moore (1881)" (galton2007a, p. 514).
- *The Magnesian (Dolomitic) Conglomerate.* The deposit "was long equated with the Magnesian Conglomerate, which occurs very close by" (galton2007a, p. 514); Galton follows Benton and colleagues (2000) in treating it as "a Triassic (Rheatian) dolomitized cave breccia or fissure fill (not in Magnesian/ Dolomitic Conglomerate" (p. 515).

**Open.** Nothing.

## References

- `galton2007a`: Galton, P. M. (2007). Notes on the remains of archosaurian reptiles, mostly basal sauropodomorph dinosaurs, from the 1834 fissure fill (Rhaetian, Upper Triassic) at Clifton in Bristol, southwest England. *Revue de Paléobiologie* 26(2): 505-591.

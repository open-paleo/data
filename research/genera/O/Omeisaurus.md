# *Omeisaurus*

## Does the *O. junghsiensis* location follow Young's lost type or Dong's neotype?

*2026-08-31*

**Conclusion.** The neotype. Young's (1939) material from Junghsien (Rongxian in later papers) was mostly lost, and Dong and colleagues (1983) chose a neotype from the Wujiaba Dam quarry at Zigong, which they place at the base of the Upper Shaximiao. The location block follows the type specimen. Governs `location.locality: Wujiaba Dam, Zigong` and `location.part: upper` on [*Omeisaurus junghsiensis*](../../../genera/O/Omeisaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dong1983a, *Omeisaurus junghsiensis*, Justification for synonymy: "a majority of the data was lost in transport during World War II. Consequently, a neotype is hereby selected"; its table lists specimen 001, "Occiput, cranium, frontal, and parietal", as "Neotype (Fig. 16)" (translated).
- dong1983a, III History of dinosaur research in the Sichuan Basin: the Wujiaba Dam quarry "is confirmed to lie at the base of the Upper Shaximiao Fm. approximately 15 m above a conchostracan-bearing shale" (translated).
- he1984b, p. 25: all sauropods with procoelous distal caudals "occur in the Upper Shaximiao Formation, such as at Wujiaba in Zigong" (translated). He and colleagues place the Wujiaba site in the same unit as Dong and colleagues.

**Ruled out.**
- *Hsikuashan, Junghsien, in the lower Shaximiao.* This is the site of Young's lost material: "at Hsikuashan near Junghsien (Szechuan)", in sandstone "approximately 200 meters above the Tzeluting limestone" (young1939a, p. 280). He and colleagues place that site in "the upper part of the lower member of the Shaximiao Formation (commonly called the Lower Shaximiao Formation)" (he1984b, p. 25, translated), while Dong and colleagues say the Rongxian type "was derived from what is now recognized as the base of the Upper Shaximiao Fm." (dong1983a, Justification for synonymy, translated). That disagreement concerns the lost type's site and does not bear on the neotype's.

**Open.** Whether later authors accept the neotype. He and colleagues (1984) compare their species with the Rongxian material and do not mention a neotype. A later revision of the species that ruled on the neotype would settle it.

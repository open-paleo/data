# *Ornithotarsus*

## What stages does *Ornithotarsus* take while its formation is open?

*2026-10-09*

**Conclusion.** Campanian and Maastrichtian, the stages of the two open readings of the horizon taken together. This supersedes nothing in the 2026-10-02 entry, which left the formation open; it settles the stage that entry's Open line said the two readings imply. Governs `stage: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [*Ornithotarsus immanis*](../../../genera/O/Ornithotarsus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- prieto-márquez2006a, p. 91: Colbert (1948) questionably assigned the taxon "to the Campanian Woodbury Formation".
- prieto-márquez2006a, p. 92: the Navesink, Lull and Wright's horizon for the type (lull1942a, p. 9), is "Maastrichtian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Campanian alone.* It takes the Woodbury side of an open question, and matches the Horner and colleagues (2004) row for *Hadrosaurus foulkii*, already ruled out as a reading for this taxon.
- *Period alone.* Both open readings agree that the horizon is Campanian or Maastrichtian; dropping the stages would discard that.

**Open.** As in the 2026-10-02 entry: Weller (1907) and Colbert (1948).

## Which formation is the *Ornithotarsus* holotype from?

*2026-10-02*

**Conclusion.** Unsettled, so the record names none. The readings are two: the Navesink, which Lull and Wright (1942) place within their Monmouth formation, and the Woodbury, which Prieto-Márquez and colleagues (2006) report Colbert (1948) assigning with a query. Governs the empty `location.formation` on [*Ornithotarsus immanis*](../../../genera/O/Ornithotarsus.yml), and `location.locality: Union, 2½ miles east of Keyport`.

**Evidence.**
- cope1869c, p. 123: the bones are "in the collection of Dr. Samuel Lockwood, of Keyport, Monmouth County, N. J.", discovered "in the lower cretaceous clays on the shores of Raritan Bay".
- lull1942a, p. 4: "the type of *Ornithotarsus immanis* was collected at Union near Keyport, New Jersey"; p. 146: "Horizon: Lower Cretaceous clays beneath the marl. Monmouth formation. Locality: Shore of Raritan Bay, at Union, 2½ miles east of Keyport".
- lull1942a, p. 9: the eastern hadrosaur horizons "include the Merchantville and Woodbury of the Matawan formation and the Navesink clays of the Monmouth formation"; *Hadrosaurus foulkii* "came from the Woodbury clay horizon near Haddonfield, N. J., and that of *Ornithotarsus immanis* from the younger Navesink near Keyport on Raritan Bay (Weller, 1907)".
- prieto-márquez2006a, p. 91: "Colbert (1948) pointed out that the stratigraphic position of this taxon is unclear (questionably assigning it to the Campanian Woodbury Formation)", and "No further details were given as to where within the Lower Cretaceous the bones came from"; p. 92, Fig. 6 caption: "Keyport, New Jersey, Monmouth Formation, Lower Cretaceous?".

**Ruled out.**
- *Keyport as the site.* Cope names Keyport as the collector's town; Lull and Wright put the site at Union, east of it.
- *Monmouth and Navesink as two readings.* Lull and Wright's systematic entry says "Monmouth formation" and their distribution chapter says "the younger Navesink", but the same chapter treats the Navesink clays as part of the Monmouth formation (p. 9), so the two lines are one reading at two ranks.
- *Woodbury from the Hadrosaurus synonymy.* Horner and colleagues list "Merchantville Formation, Woodbury Formation, Marshalltown Formation" in the row for "*H. foulkii* Leidy, 1858 (including *Ornithotarsus immanis* Cope, 1869c)" (horner2004a, p. 440, Table 20.1), so the units are those of *H. foulkii*, whose type is from the Woodbury at Haddonfield. Lull and Wright distinguish the two horizons in one sentence (p. 9), and Prieto-Márquez and colleagues (2006) keep *O. immanis* apart from *H. foulkii*. Colbert's queried Woodbury is a separate reading and stays open.

**Open.** Whether the Navesink or the Woodbury reading holds. Weller (1907), the source Lull and Wright cite for the Navesink, and Colbert (1948) have not been read. The two readings imply different stages: Prieto-Márquez and colleagues call the Navesink "Maastrichtian" (p. 92) and the Woodbury "Campanian" (p. 91).

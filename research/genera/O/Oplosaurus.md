# *Oplosaurus*

## Is the *Oplosaurus* tooth from Brixton Bay or "Brighton Bay"?

*2026-08-31*

**Conclusion.** Brixton Bay on the Isle of Wight, which Galton (1974) equates with Brightstone Bay. Wright (1852), who found the tooth, names it; "Brighton-Bay" is a later edition of Gervais's book. Governs `location.locality: Brixton Bay` and `location.region: GB-IOW` on [*Oplosaurus armatus*](../../../genera/O/Oplosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- wright1852a, p. 89: "I had the good fortune to meet with a very perfect reptilian tooth in the Wealden clay of Brixton Bay".
- gervais1852a, p. 263: the genus is for an animal "dont M. Wright a décrit une dent trouvée par lui dans les terrains wéaldiens de l'île de Wight" ("of which Mr. Wright has described a tooth he found in the Wealden beds of the Isle of Wight", translated). The first edition names no bay.
- galton1974a, p. 15: *Hypsilophodon* remains "have been found only in Brightstone (= Brixton) Bay".

**Ruled out.**
- *Brighton Bay.* A later edition of Gervais's *Zoologie et paléontologie françaises* (p. 464) says Wright collected the tooth "dans l'argile wéaldienne de Brighton-Bay, à l'île de Wight". No bay of that name is given for the Isle of Wight in the other sources read here, and Wright's own account, which this sentence cites, gives Brixton Bay. The first edition, which the record cites, has no bay name at all.

**Open.** Nothing.

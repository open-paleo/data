# *Jingshanosaurus*

## Is *Jingshanosaurus* a sauropodiform or a massospondylid?

*2026-10-19*

**Conclusion.** A sauropodiform. Most analyses of the Yates matrix and its successors recover it on the sauropod side of Massospondylidae, often as the sister of *Seitaad*, which is within Sauropodiformes as McPhee and colleagues (2014) define it. Several papers place it just outside a narrower, node-based Sauropodiformes, but still on the sauropod side of Massospondylidae, so it falls within the stem-based clade. Governs `parent: Sauropodiformes` and the dispute on [*Jingshanosaurus xinwaensis*](../../../genera/J/Jingshanosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- mcphee2014a, Systematic Palaeontology: Sauropodiformes "is allocated the stembased definition of all taxa sharing a more recent common ancestor with Saltasaurus Bonaparte & Powell, 1980, than with Massospondylus"; Results: *Seitaad* "is a sister taxon to Jingshanosaurus in a relatively derived position between Yunnanosaurus and Anchisaurus".
- zhang2020a, p. 759, Abstract: the analysis "finds Jingshanosaurus to be one of the earliest diverging sauropodiforms".
- fernández2022a, p. 808, Table 14: Sauropodiformes, "The most inclusive clade containing Saltasaurus but not Massospondylus", includes *Jingshanosaurus*.
- hu2026a, p. 17: characters unite *Xiangyunloong* "with the sister group formed by Jingshanosaurus and Seitaad, and Sauropodiformes to the exclusion of Yunnanosaurus and Qianlong".
- rauhut2020a, p. 32: Massospondylidae includes the usual genera "but also Jingshanosaurus, Seitaad, and Yunnanosaurus, which form a distinct subclade within massospondylids".
- chapelle2018a, Fig. 54, p. 74: *Jingshanosaurus* + *Seitaad* as the sister of Massospondylidae plus the clade with *Yunnanosaurus* and sauropods.

**Ruled out.**
- *Massospondylidae.* Rauhut and colleagues (2020) recover it there. Fernández and Werneburg (2022), on a version of the same matrix, recover it at the base of Sauropodiformes, and no later analysis cited here repeats the massospondylid result. The nearest is one of their constrained iterations, in which "Massospondylidae is nested within a clade with Seitaad … Yunnanosaurus and Jingshanosaurus in 60% of the MPTs" (fernández2022a, p. 803).
- *Massopoda, the clade that also covers the arrangement of Chapelle and Choiniere (2018).* Their placement outside both Massospondylidae and Sauropodiformes is a single analysis that later work on the Yates and McPhee matrices does not repeat.
- *Jones (2026) as the source of the placement.* Jones reports the majority result correctly ("more commonly shown … as being among the basalmost Sauropodiformes"), but the placement rests on the analyses.
- *The basal-Plateosauria or basal-Sauropoda positions on the matrices of Galton and Upchurch (2004) and Upchurch and colleagues (2007).* Those matrices recover a monophyletic Prosauropoda and have been superseded for this question by the Yates matrix and its successors.

**Open.** Its position relative to *Yunnanosaurus*, *Xingxiulong* and *Seitaad* varies between analyses.

## Is the town at the *Jingshanosaurus* type site Jingshan or Jinshan?

*2026-08-27*

**Conclusion.** Jinshan. Zhang and colleagues (2020) write "Town of Jinshan" for the holotype's village and for the referred specimen's, and the report they cite on the referred specimen has "Jinshanosaurus" in its title. The genus name's "Jingshan" is a spelling of the name, not evidence of the place. Governs `location.locality: Xinwa, Jinshan` on [*Jingshanosaurus xinwaensis*](../../../genera/J/Jingshanosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- zhang2020a, p. 760: the holotype was found "from the Shawan Member (Dull Purplish Beds) of Lufeng Formation at Xinwa Village, Town of Jinshan"; p. 761: "CXM-LT9401 is from Tanghai Village of Town of Jinshan, Lufeng County, Yunnan Province".
- zhang2020a, p. 770, references: "Wang G. 2004. The Jinshanosaurus discovered at Tanghai. Lufeng Yunnan Geol 23:77–82. in Chinese."

**Ruled out.**
- *Jingshan as the town.* It follows the genus name. The only source read for this note that names the town spells it Jinshan, for two villages.

**Open.** How Zhang and Yang (1995), the describing monograph, spell the town; it has not been read.

## Is the *Jingshanosaurus* holotype LFGT-ZLJ0113 or LV003?

*2026-08-06*

**Conclusion.** LFGT-ZLJ0113 is the catalogue number and LV003 the field number. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [LFGT-ZLJ0113]` on [*Jingshanosaurus xinwaensis*](../../../genera/J/Jingshanosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- zhang2020a, p. 761: "Holotype. LFGT-ZLJ0113 (field number: LV003), a well-preserved skeleton (around 9 m in total length)".
- molina-pérez2020a, List of Sauropods, Sauropodiformes, and the speed table for bipedal sauropodomorphs: "LV003".
- jones2026b, p. 373: "LFGT-ZLJ0113 [aka LV003]—skull and skeleton".

**Ruled out.**
- *LV003.* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi (2020) print the field number in place of the catalogue number.

**Open.** Nothing.

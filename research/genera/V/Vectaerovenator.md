# *Vectaerovenator*

## Which unit at Knock Cliff did the *Vectaerovenator* vertebrae come from?

*2026-09-09*

**Conclusion.** The Ferruginous Sands Formation, identified from the matrix. The vertebrae were picked up loose on the foreshore, and Barker and colleagues (2020) assign them by matching the sandstone stuck to them against the three formations exposed in the cliff. Governs `location.formation: Ferruginous Sands` and the absence of `location.member` on [*Vectaerovenator inopinatus*](../../../genera/V/Vectaerovenator.yml).

**Evidence.**
- barker2020a, Geological Setting: "All were surface finds, discovered with adhering glauconitic sandstone matrix."
- barker2020a, Geological Setting: the cliff exposes "the uppermost members of the Ferruginous Sands Formation", "the Sandrock Formation" and "the Monk's Bay Sandstone Formation"; "This renders the relatively fossiliferous Ferruginous Sands Formation as the most plausible provenance."
- barker2020a, Geological Setting: "The glauconitic, fine to medium-grained sandstone matrix adhering to the vertebrae matches that of the New Walpen (XIII) Member", one of "Eleven informal (='local'; Rawson 2006) 'members'"; "the material may originate from the 'Urchin bed', located on top of Horse Ledge".
- barker2020a, Systematic Palaeontology, Type locality and horizon: "Ferruginous Sands Formation, Member XIII".

**Ruled out.**
- *Sandrock and Monk's Bay Sandstone formations.* Both crop out at Knock Cliff. Barker and colleagues exclude the Monk's Bay because its sediment "is gritty and dark brown", and the Sandrock for its fine yellow and white sands; neither matches the matrix.

**Open.** Whether the record should carry Member XIII, the New Walpen Chine Member. It is an informal local member, and the assignment rests on the matrix alone, as the formation does. Insole and colleagues (1998), the source Barker and colleagues cite for the members, has not been read.

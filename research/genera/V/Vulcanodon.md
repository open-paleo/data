# *Vulcanodon*

## Is the *Vulcanodon* holotype from the Batoka Basalt Formation?

*2026-09-09*

**Conclusion.** No. It comes from the top of the Forest Sandstone, below the Lower Basalt of the Batoka Basalt Formation. Governs `location.formation: Forest Sandstone` and `location.part: upper` on [*Vulcanodon karibaensis*](../../../genera/V/Vulcanodon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, 4.3 Revised Stratigraphy for *Vulcanodon karibaensis*: "no basalt layer could be located stratigraphically below the *Vulcanodon* type locality", and "*Vulcanodon* was found in the uppermost Forest Sandstone, and not within interbedded sandstones of the Batoka Basalt Formation".
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, 1. Introduction: Marsh and Jackson (1974) "confirmed the presence of the Forest Sandstone and the *Vulcanodon*-bearing horizon below the Lower Basalt on Island 126/127".
- cooper1984a, p. 230, Sauropod Ecology: the remains "were found in a bedded siltstone/sandstone sequence cut by a fluviatile channel, at the top of the Forest Sandstone".

**Ruled out.**
- *A sandstone between two lava flows of the Batoka Basalt Formation.* Bond and colleagues (1970) and Raath (1972), as Viglietti and colleagues (2018) report them, placed the type locality "within a sandstone layer that was sandwiched stratigraphically between two lava flows within the Batoka Basalt Formation", and later dinosaur papers kept that scheme. Viglietti and colleagues found no basalt below the horizon on Island 126/127 or on the islands to the west, and suggest the earlier workers "mistook a dense layer of fallen basalt blocks, which fringes much of the shoreline" for a lower flow. Cooper (1984) had already given the Forest Sandstone but, in Viglietti and colleagues' words, "provided no evidence of his stated stratigraphic position".

**Open.** The quarry's position on the island: "the exact location of the *Vulcanodon* material could not be determined" (viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, 3. Results). Raath (1972), the describing paper, has not been read; it is the source of the Batoka Basalt reading and the only likely source of a quarry position.

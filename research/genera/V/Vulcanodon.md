# *Vulcanodon*

## Is the *Vulcanodon* holotype from the Batoka Basalt Formation?

*2026-09-09*

**Conclusion.** No. It comes from the top of the Forest Sandstone, below the Lower Basalt of the Batoka Basalt Formation. Governs `location.formation: Forest Sandstone` and `location.part: upper` on [*Vulcanodon karibaensis*](../../../genera/V/Vulcanodon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, 4.3 Revised Stratigraphy for *Vulcanodon karibaensis*: "no basalt layer could be located stratigraphically below the *Vulcanodon* type locality", and "*Vulcanodon* was found in the uppermost Forest Sandstone, and not within interbedded sandstones of the Batoka Basalt Formation".
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, 1. Introduction: Marsh and Jackson (1974) "confirmed the presence of the Forest Sandstone and the *Vulcanodon*-bearing horizon below the Lower Basalt on Island 126/127".
- cooper1984a, p. 230, Sauropod Ecology: the remains "were found in a bedded siltstone/sandstone sequence cut by a fluviatile channel, at the top of the Forest Sandstone".

**Ruled out.**
- *A sandstone between two lava flows of the Batoka Basalt Formation.* Bond and colleagues (1970) and Raath (1972), as Viglietti and colleagues (2018) report them, placed the type locality "within a sandstone layer that was sandwiched stratigraphically between two lava flows within the Batoka Basalt Formation", and later dinosaur papers kept that scheme. Viglietti and colleagues found no basalt below the horizon on Island 126/127 or on the islands to the west, and suggest the earlier workers "mistook a dense layer of fallen basalt blocks, which fringes much of the shoreline" for a lower flow. Cooper (1984) had already given the Forest Sandstone but, in Viglietti and colleagues' words, "provided no evidence of his stated stratigraphic position".

**Open.** The quarry's position on the island: "the exact location of the *Vulcanodon* material could not be determined" (viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, 3. Results). Raath (1972), the describing paper, has not been read; it is the source of the Batoka Basalt reading and the only likely source of a quarry position.

## Is the "Vulcanodon Beds" a unit other than the Forest Sandstone?

*2026-08-06*

**Conclusion.** No. It is a name for the type horizon from the period when that horizon was read as a sandstone between basalt flows. The horizon is the top of the Forest Sandstone (see the 2026-09-09 entry below). Governs `location.formation: Forest Sandstone` on [*Vulcanodon karibaensis*](../../../genera/V/Vulcanodon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, Abstract: "*Vulcanodon* is from lower in the stratigraphy than previously documented, lying within the uppermost Forest Sandstone rather than the interbedded sandstones of the Batoka Basalt Formation".
- cooper1984a, p. 230, Sauropod Ecology: the remains were found "at the top of the Forest Sandstone".

**Ruled out.**
- *"Vulcanodon Beds".* Upchurch and colleagues (2004) give "Vulcanodon Beds (Mashonaland North), Zimbabwe" (upchurch2004a, Table 13.1). That table predates Viglietti and colleagues (2018). That the name comes from Raath's (1972) basalt-interbed scheme is an inference: Raath has not been read.

**Open.** Nothing beyond the 2026-09-09 entry.

## What stage is *Vulcanodon karibaensis*?

*2026-08-05*

**Conclusion.** Sinemurian–Pliensbachian. Viglietti and colleagues (2018) date the horizon by its place below the Toarcian Lower Basalt and by correlating the upper Forest Sandstone with the Clarens Formation. Governs `period.stage: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian]` on [*Vulcanodon karibaensis*](../../../genera/V/Vulcanodon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, Abstract: "Sedimentological data suggest that the upper part of the Forest Sandstone correlates with the Clarens Formation of the main Karoo Basin, implying that *Vulcanodon* is likely Sinemurian–Pliensbachian in age".
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, 4.3 Revised Stratigraphy for *Vulcanodon karibaensis*: the stratum "could be as old as the Rhaetian or as young as the Pliensbachian. A Sinemurian–Pliensbachian age seems most likely for this taxon, given its stratigraphic proximity to the Toarcian-aged Lower Basalt".

**Ruled out.**
- *?Hettangian.* Upchurch and colleagues (2004) give "?Hettangian" for the "Vulcanodon Beds" (upchurch2004a, Table 13.1). Viglietti and colleagues place a Rhaetian–Hettangian maximum only on the lower Forest Sandstone, from *Coelophysis* and *Massospondylus*, not on the type horizon at the top.
- *Sinemurian alone.* Jones (2026) gives "Upper Forest Sandstone, Zimbabwe *Early Jurassic, Sinemurian*" (jones2026b, Sauropoda table), narrower than the source that set the horizon.
- *Toarcian or late Pliensbachian–Toarcian.* The age of Yates and colleagues (2004) as Viglietti and colleagues report it, which assumed the horizon lay within the Batoka Basalt.

**Open.** Nothing. No radiometric age exists for the horizon.

## Does the *Vulcanodon* holotype number carry an NMZ prefix?

*2026-07-30*

**Conclusion.** No. Cooper (1984) gives the catalogue number as QG24, the museum's own letter series, with the institution named in words. The record keeps QG24 with `institution: NMZB`, for which the registry lists QG as an alias. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [QG24]` on [*Vulcanodon karibaensis*](../../../genera/V/Vulcanodon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cooper1984a, p. 203, Systematic Palaeontology: "The type material … is now housed in the National Museum of Zimbabwe, Bulawayo, and bears the catalogue number QG24."

**Ruled out.**
- *NMZ QG24.* The record's value before 2026-07-30, which joins the institution's initials to a number that already carries the collection's letters. Jones (2026) prints "**QG24**—partial skeleton" (jones2026b, Sauropoda table) and Molina-Pérez and Larramendi (2020) "QG 24" (molina-pérez2020a, List of sauropods), agreeing with Cooper.

**Open.** Nothing.

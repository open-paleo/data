# *Vouivria*

## Which unit at Damparis is the *Vouivria* holotype from?

*2026-09-14*

**Conclusion.** The Calcaires de Clerval Formation, with no member. "Calcaires fins" is the older name of the 12 m local unit at Damparis that Mannion and colleagues (2017) assign to the Calcaires de Clerval. Their Tidalites de Mouchard Member is offered as a possibility on a qualified argument. Governs `location.formation: Calcaires de Clerval` and the absence of `location.member` on [*Vouivria damparisensis*](../../../genera/V/Vouivria.yml).

**Evidence.**
- mannion2017a, p. 6, Lithological description, fossil content, and environmental context: "The fossil assemblage was found in a 12 m thick formation formerly named the *calcaires fins* (Boullier, Contini & Pernin, 1975)".
- mannion2017a, p. 9, Stratigraphic position and age: "The Damparis fossils thus belong to the *Calcaires de Clerval* Formation."
- mannion2017a, pp. 9–10: "As far as this argument is reliable, the presence of about 10% of quartz in the Damparis fossil layer (Dreyfuss, 1934), and in the basal clay bed of the *calcaires fins*, implies that the fossils might belong to the *Tidalites de Mouchard* Member"; "However, the exact position within the *Calcaires de Clerval* Formation cannot currently be ascertained."
- mannion2017a, p. 18, Type horizon and stratigraphic age: "*Calcaires de Clerval* Formation, possibly from the *Tidalites de Mouchard* Member".

**Ruled out.**
- *Calcaires fins Formation.* Mannion and colleagues use the name for the local unit and place that unit within the Calcaires de Clerval, so it is not a rival assignment.

**Open.** The member. The Tidalites de Mouchard rests on the quartz content alone, an argument the authors themselves qualify, and nothing else read for this note excludes the lower member, the Calcaires de l'Isle-sur-le-Doubs. A finer position for the Damparis bed within the Calcaires de Clerval would settle it.

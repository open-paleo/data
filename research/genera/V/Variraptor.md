# *Variraptor*

## Where does *Variraptor* belong, and does Cau and Madzia (2021) discuss it?

*2026-10-09*

**Conclusion.** Paraves incertae sedis. The name rests on a sacrum and last dorsal vertebra that cannot be shown to be dromaeosaurid. The two analyses that include the taxon both put it inside Dromaeosauridae as this tree defines it, but they score a hypodigm whose association is doubted, while every statement about the holotype itself stops short of Dromaeosauridae. Cau and Madzia (2021) do include *Variraptor*: it appears only in their Fig. 6, so a search of their text finds nothing, and the citation removed earlier the same day is restored. Supersedes the placement at Dromaeosauridae incertae sedis in the record's dispute history. Governs `parent`, `placement` and `dispute` on [*Variraptor*](../../../genera/V/Variraptor.yml).

**Evidence.**
- leloeuff1998a, p. 106: "Holotype. An articulated posterior dorsal vertebra (MDE-D168) and sacrum (MDE-D169)"; the referred humerus, cervicodorsal, sacrocaudal and femur were "used to augment the diagnosis"; pp. 110–111: "the position of Variraptor mechinorum among dromaeosaurids is uncertain".
- allain2003a, p. 37: "the identification of the holotypic material as dromaeosaurid is far from certain"; "the association of the referred material ... is very doubtful"; "Variraptor mechinorum is probably a chimera and can only be regarded as a nomen dubium".
- senter2004a, p. 3: "could neither confirm nor falsify deinonychosaurian status for Variraptor (Fig. 4)".
- turner2012a, p. 52: the holotype elements "preserve no characters diagnostic of Dromaeosauridae, or other less inclusive clades of coelurosaurs"; the referred cervicodorsal "is thus referable only to Maniraptora".
- hartman2019a, p. 34, Phylogenetic results: "We recover Bambiraptor as an early branching microraptorian ..., with Variraptor as its sister taxon"; "Variraptor can join Unenlagiinae with the other European taxa in only three steps".
- cau2021a, p. 14, Fig. 6: the key to pruned terminals reads "k: *Variraptor*", and "k" marks only branches within Unenlagiinae (the *Buitreraptor* plus *Unenlagia* node and two nodes within *Unenlagia*), which the figure places inside its Dromaeosauridae.
- moutrille2025a, pp. 24–25: the referred cervicodorsal "is very similar to the diagnostic cervicodorsal vertebrae of the Shri species"; the type-locality ilium differs from most eumaniraptorans and resembles early-diverging dromaeosaurids and avialans; the sacral features are not autapomorphic.

**Ruled out.**
- *Dromaeosauridae incertae sedis.* Both analyses land there, but on the whole hypodigm; Allain and Pereda-Suberbiola (2003), Senter and colleagues (2004) and Turner and colleagues (2012) each find the holotype not demonstrably dromaeosaurid or deinonychosaurian, and a nomen dubium is placed at the broadest node its material supports.
- *Maniraptora, on the oviraptorosaur resemblance.* Allain and Pereda-Suberbiola (2003, p. 37) relay from an unpublished thesis that the referred cervicodorsal "is almost indistinguishable from that of the caenagnathid Chirostenotes". It concerns a referred vertebra from another locality, not the holotype, and Moutrille and colleagues (2025) later liken the same vertebra to *Shri*.
- *Removing the Cau and Madzia (2021) citation.* The paper names *Variraptor* only in the raster Fig. 6, so its text layer has no match; Jones (2026) cites the same paper for the same point.

**Open.** Whether the holotype sacrum falls with the eudromaeosaur or the non-eudromaeosaurian paravian that Moutrille and colleagues (2025) see in the hypodigm. The thesis was not read.

# *Velociraptor*

## Is the *Velociraptor mongoliensis* holotype Maastrichtian?

*2026-08-05*

**Conclusion.** No. The holotype AMNH 6515 comes from the Djadokhta beds at Shabarakh Usu (Bayn Dzak), and the Djadokhta Formation and its Bayn Dzak Member are Campanian (see [Djadokhta](../../stratigraphy/d/djadokhta.md)). Governs `period.stage: [Campanian]` on [*Velociraptor mongoliensis*](../../../genera/V/Velociraptor.yml).

**Evidence.**
- osborn1924b, p. 1: "TYPE.-Amer. Mus. 6515, skull and jaws, one front claw and adjoining phalanges. Djadochta beds at Shabarakh Usu, August 11, 1923".
- dashzeveg2005a, p. 18: the Djadokhta falls in the "late part of the Campanian incorporating the end of Chron 33 and Chron 32 between about 75 and 71 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian.* Jones (2026) gives "Barun Goyot Fm., Mongolia Djadokhta Fm., Mongolia *Late Cretaceous, Maastrichtian*" (jones2026c, Eudromaeosauria table). The cell covers the genus's referred material from two formations, and no source read for the Djadokhta places it in the Maastrichtian; the Cenomanian to early Maastrichtian span of older work is a list of other authors' views (dashzeveg2005a, p. 17).

**Open.** Nothing.

## What does Bindellini and colleagues' (2025) review change on *Velociraptor*?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** Two things. The generic placement of *V. osmolskae* is a live dispute, recorded on [*Velociraptor*](../../../genera/V/Velociraptor.yml) with the species kept in the genus, since no replacement genus has been named. The quill-knob ulna stays evidence for *V. mongoliensis*, with its referral marked as provisional; the review's claim that the specimen cannot be referred to the genus misreads its source.

**Evidence.**
- evans2013a, Phylogenetic analysis: "Velociraptor is found to be paraphyletic in this analysis, with V. osmolskae being more closely related to Tsaagan than to Velociraptor mongolienesis from Mongolia."
- czepiński2023a, Fig. 6: in both the tree from the modified matrix of Powers and colleagues (2022) and the tree from that of Napoli and colleagues (2021), "Velociraptor" osmolskae is sister to *Linheraptor exquisitus* + *Tsaagan mangas*, while *V. mongoliensis* groups with *Shri devi*. The text never discusses the species' placement; the result is read from the figure.
- wang2022a, Supplementary Figure S4 (strict consensus of 12 parsimony trees): *Velociraptor osmolskae* lies outside a clade of *V. mongoliensis* + (*Tsaagan* + *Linheraptor*). Fig. 4 (Bayesian tip-dated tree): the two *Velociraptor* species pair with a posterior probability of 0.52, sister to *Linheraptor* + *Tsaagan*. Both read from the figures, whose labels are images.
- bindellini2025a, Results: "None of the two consensus trees resulting from analyses A and B recovered "Velociraptor" osmolskae in a monophyletic clade with Velociraptor mongoliensis and/or Velociraptor sp." The authors "once again support the proposal to establish a new genus for "Velociraptor" osmolskae".
- napoli2021a, Note on Holotype Specimen: the quill-knob specimen from Zos Wash shows "the first direct evidence of feathers in a eudromaeosaur (Turner et al., 2007a)"; "Turner et al. (2007a) erroneously identified the Zos Wash specimen as IGM 100/981. The correct specimen number for the Zos Wash specimen is IGM 100/3503. This individual is currently referred to Velociraptor mongoliensis and is under renewed study." IGM 100/981 is the holotype of *Kuru kulla*.

**Ruled out.**
- *The quill-knob specimen as Velociraptorinae indet.* Bindellini and colleagues (2025) write that MPC-D 100/981 "is preferably referred to Velociraptorinae indet. or ?Velociraptor (Napoli et al., 2021)". Napoli and colleagues (2021) say neither: they renumber the specimen IGM 100/3503 and keep it in *V. mongoliensis*, provisionally. The review carries the superseded number and attributes a reassignment its source does not make.
- *Moving* V. osmolskae *to a new genus.* Most analyses read here place it away from *V. mongoliensis*, but no genus has been named for it, and a recombination waits for one and for its adoption by later work.
- *Wang and colleagues (2022) as support for* V. osmolskae *in* Velociraptor. The review cites them as the exception ("but see Wang et al., 2022"). Only their Bayesian tree groups the two species, at a posterior probability of 0.52; their parsimony tree does not.
- *A hip height of about 1 m.* The review gives "around 1 m tall at the hip", citing earlier work; it is a relayed figure and was not used.
- *The review's Table 1 locality and claw for AMNH 6515.* The table gives "Tugrikin-Shire" and "one of the raptorial second toe claws", while the review's own text gives the Flaming Cliffs (Bayn Dzak) and a manual ungual of digit III, agreeing with Osborn (1924). These are slips within one document.

**Open.**
- Powers and colleagues (2021a), which the review cites as also placing *V. osmolskae* away from *V. mongoliensis*, has not been read.
- The review credits Czepiński (2023) with a single autapomorphy of *Velociraptor*, denticle size (character 85). The extraction of czepiński2023a found no such statement in its text; it may sit in the paper's supplement.
- The review notes there is no up-to-date diagnosis of *Velociraptor* and proposes none, so the genus `diagnostic_features` are unchanged.

# *Velafrons*

## Is the *Velafrons* 7A quarry on Cerro de los Dinosaurios?

*2026-09-09*

**Conclusion.** Probably, by inference. Gates and colleagues (2007) name only the "7A quarry" near Rincon Colorado. The hill name comes from Kirkland and colleagues (2006), who call the excavation that produced the skull "Quarry 7". The record joins the two. Governs `location.locality: 7A Quarry, Cerro de los Dinosaurios` on [*Velafrons coahuilensis*](../../../genera/V/Velafrons.yml).

**Evidence.**
- gates2007a, p. 918, Type Locality: "7A quarry, about 1 km northeast of the town Rincon Colorado, Coahuila, Mexico".
- gates2007a, pp. 917–918: Dinamation "returned to Rincon Colorado and established several quarries in the area, including quarry 7A"; in 2002 "the Utah Museum of Natural History, the Royal Tyrrell Museum of Paleontology, and the Museo del Desierto in Saltillo, Coahuila, reinitiated excavations at the 7A quarry", recovering the skull.
- kirkland2006a, p. 299: at Rincon Colorado, quarries were set up "around a hill that became known as Cerro de los Dinosaurios", and with the Royal Tyrrell Museum and the Utah Museum of Natural History, "Renewed excavations at the Cerro de los Dinosaurios Quarry 7 (Fig. 3) resulted in the recovery of the skull of a new genus of lambeosaurine hadrosaur currently under study at the UMNH (Gates et al., 2005)".

**Ruled out.** Nothing; whether Quarry 7 and quarry 7A are one excavation is the open question.

**Open.** Neither paper says that Quarry 7 and quarry 7A are the same. The match rests on the shared account: the same three institutions, renewed excavation, a lambeosaurine skull, and Kirkland and colleagues' citation of Gates and colleagues' 2005 abstract announcing the new lambeosaurine. A source that numbers the Rincon Colorado quarries, or places quarry 7A on the hill, would settle it.

# *Utahceratops*

## Is the *Utahceratops* holotype from UMNH VP locality 145?

*2026-09-08*

**Conclusion.** No source places it there. Locality 145 yielded UMNH VP 12198, which Sampson and colleagues (2010) list as a referred specimen; the holotype is the partial skull UMNH VP 16784, and no source cited here gives its locality. Levitt (2013) calls UMNH VP 12198 "the holotype of *Utahceratops*" in its account of locality 145, which is a misreading of Sampson and colleagues, the paper that sentence cites. Governs the absence of `location.locality` on [*Utahceratops gettyi*](../../../genera/U/Utahceratops.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sampson2010a, Systematic Paleontology: "The holotype specimen is UMNH VP 16784, a partial skull"; "Referred Specimens. UMNH VP 12198, a fragmentary but mostly complete skull about 2.3 m long together with an associated postcranium".
- loewen2013b, p. 496: "The holotype specimen (UMNH VP 16784) consists of a partial disarticulated skull"; "UMNH VP 12198 is the first specimen recovered of this taxon"; "This locality (UMNH VP Loc. 145) represents the most extensive ceratopsid excavation".
- levitt2013a, p. 10, UMNH VP locality 145: "the holotype of Utahceratops (UMNH VP 12198) (Sampson et al., 2010a)".

**Ruled out.**
- *UMNH VP locality 145.* It is the site of UMNH VP 12198, a referred specimen in both Sampson and colleagues (2010) and Loewen and colleagues (2013). Levitt (2013) is the only source that calls that specimen the holotype, and it cites Sampson and colleagues for the claim.

**Open.** Where UMNH VP 16784 was collected. Sampson and colleagues give a horizon for "the holotype and assigned specimens" together and no locality; field records at the Natural History Museum of Utah would settle it.

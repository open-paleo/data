# *Brontomerus*

## Which part of the Ruby Ranch Member, and which formation, is the *Brontomerus* holotype from?

*2026-08-14*

**Conclusion.** The upper part of the Ruby Ranch Member of the Cedar Mountain Formation, the describing paper's assignment. A later stratigraphic account puts the same specimen low in the member, and two papers put the site in the Burro Canyon Formation; neither reading has displaced the describing paper's. Governs `location.formation: Cedar Mountain`, `location.member: Ruby Ranch` and `location.part: upper` on [*Brontomerus mcintoshi*](../../../genera/B/Brontomerus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- taylor2011a, p. 75, abstract: "from the Hotel Mesa Quarry in Grand County, Utah, USA, in the upper part of the Ruby Ranch Member (Aptian–Albian) of the Lower Cretaceous Cedar Mountain Formation".
- kirkland2016a, p. 150: of the excavation "on the east side of the Colorado River at Hotel Mesa", "Preliminary investigation revealed that the site was low in the Ruby Ranch Member"; Fig. 29 caption: "Aptian dinosaurs from lower Ruby Ranch Member … Holotype juvenile *Brontomirus* ilium (OMNH 66430) from Hotel Mesa site (OMNH v857)".
- kirkland2016a, p. 104: the Colorado River "has served as the arbitrary boundary between the Cedar Mountain (northwest) and Burro Canyon (southeast) Formations".
- kirkland2007a, p. 15, figure caption: "Juvenile brachiosaurid ilium in Oklahoma Museum of Natural History collections from Hotel Mesa Site east of Colorado River (actually in Burro Canyon Formation, figure 2)".
- d'emic2012b, p. 646: the material comes "from the Early Cretaceous Burro Canyon Formation (equivalent to the Ruby Ranch Member of the Cedar Mountain Formation) of Utah".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Two readings are unresolved. The level: Kirkland and colleagues (2016) place OMNH 66430 low in the Ruby Ranch Member against the describing paper's upper part, and only the describing paper's abstract has been read, so the basis for its "upper part" is unknown. The formation: the site lies east of the river that serves as the conventional boundary, where Kirkland and Madsen (2007) and D'Emic (2012) write Burro Canyon, while Kirkland and colleagues (2016) keep it in the Cedar Mountain. The full text of Taylor and colleagues (2011) would settle the first question.

# *Brachylophosaurus*

## Is the *Brachylophosaurus* holotype from the Oldman or the Dinosaur Park Formation?

*2026-08-14*

**Conclusion.** The Oldman Formation. Cuthbertson and Holmes (2010) place CMN 8893 below the base of the Dinosaur Park Formation, so Sternberg's (1953) "Oldman formation" here is not the older name for beds now in the Dinosaur Park. Governs `location.formation: Oldman` and `location.locality: Little Sandhill Creek, near Steveville` on [*Brachylophosaurus canadensis*](../../../genera/B/Brachylophosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sternberg1953a, p. 275: "Horizon. Oldman formation, Belly River Series, Upper Cretaceous"; "Locality. Little Sandhill Creek, NE. 1/4, sec. 6, tp. 21, rge. 11, W. of 4th mer., 60 feet above Red Deer River, Alberta"; the skeleton lay "half a mile above the mouth of Little Sandhill Creek, 60 feet above the level of Red Deer River, quarry No. 58 on the Steveville map".
- cuthbertson2010a, p. 374, Type locality and horizon: "Oldman Formation of Belly River Group … 7.5 m below the Dinosaur Park Formation-Oldman Formation contact (David Evans, pers. comm.)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Dinosaur Park Formation.* Until the Dinosaur Park Formation was separated in 1993, the beds now in it were included in the Oldman (see [Oldman and Dinosaur Park](../../topics/oldman-and-dinosaur-park.md)), and the site lies "in what is now Dinosaur Provincial Park" (cuthbertson2010a, p. 373), where both units crop out. Sternberg's "Oldman" there could have meant either; Cuthbertson and Holmes place this specimen below the contact.
- *Canadian Creek, Judith River Formation, Montana.* The record once carried "Brachylophosaurus site, Canadian Creek" with the Judith River Formation of Montana. Sternberg's type line places the holotype in Alberta; the Montana specimens Cuthbertson and Holmes compare, MOR 794 and 1071, are referred material from the "lower Judith River Formation of north-eastern Montana" (p. 374).

**Open.** The position below the contact rests on a personal communication, not a published section. A measured section through Sternberg's quarry 58 would settle it.

# *Burianosaurus*

## Do the Korycany Beds have member rank?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** Yes, in current usage. The Czech Geological Survey, through the lead author of the 1980 revision of the basin's stratigraphy, writes of the Peruc, Korycany and Pecínov members of the Peruc-Korycany Formation, and the *Burianosaurus* femur lies in the Korycany unit. No source read formally redefines the unit at member rank, and the 1980 revision itself has not been read. Governs `location.member: Korycany` on [*Burianosaurus augustai*](../../../genera/B/Burianosaurus.yml) and the [Korycany](../../../stratigraphy/k/korycany.yml) registry entry. Settles the 2026-09-30 entry's **Open** line on rank.

**Evidence.**
- čech2011a, p. 20: "fluvial deposits of the Peruc Member", and "Ammonites of *Calycoceras guerangeri* and *Metoicoceras geslinianum* zones of the Upper Cenomanian are known from the Korycany and Pecínov members of the Peruc-Korycany Formation".
- caracciolo2009a: the "shallow-marine Korycany Member" and "Individual units recognized within the Peruc and Korycany members".
- fejfar2005a, p. 297, Fig. 2: the femur plotted at the level of the Korycany Beds within the Peruc-Korycany Formation; madzia2017a, p. 4: "Korycany Beds of the Peruc-Korycany Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Formation rank and no finer.* The earlier record and registry notes said Fejfar and colleagues place the remains no finer than the formation; their Fig. 2 plots them in the Korycany Beds.
- *"Not a mappable subdivision".* The registry note attributed this to Madzia and colleagues (2017); the phrase is not in their paper.
- *Rank still unresolved.* Svoboda (1989) wrote that "the question of elevating the Korycan beds to formation rank remains unresolved" (svoboda1989a, p. 41, translated); the later member usage supersedes it.

**Open.** Whether the 1980 revision (Čech and colleagues 1980) defines the units as members or as beds.

## Which part of the Peruc-Korycany Formation is the Burianosaurus holotype from?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** The Korycany Beds, the near-shore marine part of the [Peruc-Korycany Formation](../../../stratigraphy/p/peruc-korycany.yml), late Cenomanian. Whether the Korycany Beds have member rank is unchecked, so the record keeps formation Peruc-Korycany with no member, and its notes should place the find in the Korycany Beds. Governs `location.formation`, the location notes, and `stage: [Cenomanian]` on *Burianosaurus augustai*.

**Evidence.**
- fejfar2005a, p. 296: "the dinosaur remains can be described as originating from the near-shore marine sediments of the Peruc-Korycany Formation"; p. 297, Fig. 2: the femur symbol sits in the band labelled "Korycany Beds", in the upper Cenomanian.
- madzia2017a, online-first version, p. 4, Type locality and horizon: "Korycany Beds of the Peruc-Korycany Formation".
- fejfar2005a, p. 297: "middle part of late Cenomanian … *Inoceramus pictus–Inoceramus pictus bohemicus* inoceramid zone (Stanislav Čech, personal communications 2004), and/or, probably to the lower part of *Metoicoceras geslinianum* ammonite zone"; Madzia and colleagues (2017, p. 4) add the *Calycoceras naviculare* zone as an alternative.

**Ruled out.**
- *Formation rank and no finer.* Fejfar and colleagues' text names only the formation, but their own Fig. 2 places the find in the Korycany Beds, as Madzia and colleagues do.
- *Peruc Beds.* The Peruc Beds are the continental facies; the find is from marine sediments.

**Open.** The rank of the Peruc and Korycany Beds. Fejfar and colleagues describe the boundary between them as "irregular" and parts of the two as "isochronous (Houša 1987)", and use "Peruc-Korycany Formation" for both (p. 296). That they are facies of one formation rather than members is an inference; the formal Czech lithostratigraphy has not been read for this note.

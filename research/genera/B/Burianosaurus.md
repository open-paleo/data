# *Burianosaurus*

## Which part of the Peruc-Korycany Formation is the Burianosaurus holotype from?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** The Korycany Beds, the near-shore marine part of the [Peruc-Korycany Formation](../../../stratigraphy/p/peruc-korycany.yml), late Cenomanian. Whether the Korycany Beds have member rank is unchecked, so the record keeps formation Peruc-Korycany with no member, and its notes should place the find in the Korycany Beds. Governs `location.formation`, the location notes, and `stage: [Cenomanian]` on *Burianosaurus augustai*.

**Evidence.**
- fejfar2005a, p. 296: "the dinosaur remains can be described as originating from the near-shore marine sediments of the Peruc-Korycany Formation"; p. 297, Fig. 2: the femur symbol sits in the band labelled "Korycany Beds", in the upper Cenomanian.
- madzia2017a, online-first version, p. 4, Type locality and horizon: "Korycany Beds of the Peruc-Korycany Formation".
- fejfar2005a, p. 297: "middle part of late Cenomanian … *Inoceramus pictus–Inoceramus pictus bohemicus* inoceramid zone (Stanislav Čech, personal communications 2004), and/or, probably to the lower part of *Metoicoceras geslinianum* ammonite zone"; Madzia and colleagues (2017, p. 4) add the *Calycoceras naviculare* zone as an alternative.

**Ruled out.**
- *Formation rank and no finer.* Fejfar and colleagues' text names only the formation, but their own Fig. 2 places the find in the Korycany Beds, as Madzia and colleagues do.
- *Peruc Beds.* The Peruc Beds are the continental facies; the find is from marine sediments.

**Open.** The rank of the Peruc and Korycany Beds. Fejfar and colleagues describe the boundary between them as "irregular" and parts of the two as "isochronous (Houša 1987)", and use "Peruc-Korycany Formation" for both (p. 296). That they are facies of one formation rather than members is an inference; the formal Czech lithostratigraphy has not been read for this note.

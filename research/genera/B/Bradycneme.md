# *Bradycneme*

## Is *Bradycneme* a valid taxon, and where does it belong?

*2026-10-19*

**Conclusion.** It is a nomen dubium, placed no closer than Tetanurae. No work since the 1975 description argues that the tibiotarsus is diagnostic. The two reassessments of the specimen disagree on its group: Csiki and Grigorescu (1998) exclude it from the derived coelurosaurs, and Naish and Dyke (2004) regard it as an indeterminate maniraptoran. Tetanurae covers both. Governs `status: nomen dubium`, `parent: Tetanurae` with `placement: incertae sedis`, and the dispute on [*Bradycneme draculae*](../../../genera/B/Bradycneme.yml).

**Evidence.**
- harrison1975a, p. 563: the tibiotarsi "belong to the owls (Strigiformes)"; p. 565: "Holotype only. The distal end of a right tibiotarsus, A 1588".
- osmólska1987a, p. 136: "These genera and species cannot be, however, considered valid because the distal portions of tibiotarsi are very alike in all troodontid species".
- csiki1998a, p. 87: "Without diagnostic remains of small theropods, it is inappropriate to give the reported material generic names"; p. 100: the specimen "has a synapomorphy that seems to exclude it from the derived coelurosaurs", and "'Bradycneme draculae' is informally retained".
- naish2004b, p. 392: the status of the material "must also remain uncertain", and "A1588 exhibits the craniocaudally compressed rectangular shape seen in maniraptorans: consequently we suggest that A1588 be regarded as Maniraptora indet."
- makovicky2004b, p. 185, Table 9.1: *Bradycneme draculae* among the nomina dubia.

**Ruled out.**
- *An alvarezsaurid.* Jones (2026) and Paul (2024) report an alvarezsaurid referral for *Bradycneme*, but Naish and Dyke (2004), the work Jones cites, refer only *Heptasteornis* (BMNH A4359) to Alvarezsauridae and regard this specimen as an indeterminate maniraptoran.
- *Maniraptora as the parent.* Csiki and Grigorescu (1998) exclude the specimen from the derived coelurosaurs, a reading later work has not answered on the specimen.
- *An owl or a troodontid.* The owl identification was abandoned once the bones were recognized as non-avian, and the troodontid referral rests on a resemblance that Osmólska (1987) shows to be shared by all troodontid tibiotarsi.
- *A valid taxon.* No work after the description argues for a diagnosis.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which form does the *Bradycneme* locality take?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** The modern Romanian name, Sânpetru, as other Hațeg records give it; the describing paper's Hungarian form goes in the location notes. Governs `location.locality: Sânpetru` on [*Bradycneme draculae*](../../../genera/B/Bradycneme.yml), replacing "Szentpeterfalva, Hătszeg", a misreading of the printed form.

**Evidence.**
- harrison1975a, p. 565, as printed: "Szèntpeterfalva, Hàtszeg, Transylvania, Romania".
- csiki1998a: Andrews's material, including BMNH A.1588, is from the Sinpetru Formation of the Sibișel Valley; the 2026-09-30 entry equates Szentpéterfalva with Sânpetru.

**Ruled out.**
- *"Szentpeterfalva, Hătszeg".* The record's earlier string, a transcription of the printed page with the wrong diacritics.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the Bradycneme holotype (BMNH A 1588) from the Sânpetru Formation?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Probably, by inheritance: the specimen came from Szentpéterfalva (Sânpetru) in the Hațeg Basin, and the referred Hațeg bird and theropod material has been assigned to the Sibișel Valley, the [Sânpetru Formation](../../../stratigraphy/s/sânpetru.yml)'s type section, on a personal communication. Governs `location.formation: Sânpetru` and `stage: [Maastrichtian]` on *Bradycneme draculae*.

**Evidence.**
- harrison1975a, p. 565: "Szèntpeterfalva, Hàtszeg", "presented by Lady Smith-Woodward, 1923".
- csiki1998a, p. 89: "BMNH A.1588, A.1528. All the referred material comes from the Sibisel Valley (Weishampel, pers. comm.)".
- stoicescu2024a, p. 73: the Sânpetru Formation's type section is in the Sibișel Valley.

**Ruled out.**
- *A formation from the describing paper.* Harrison and Walker (1975) give only the village; the formation is inferred from the locality.

**Open.** How reliable the Sibișel Valley attribution is: it rests on a personal communication (csiki1998a, p. 89), and the specimen came by a different donor route from Nopcsa's material (harrison1975a, p. 566; our reading of the donor names). Stoicescu and colleagues (2024, p. 75) report *Bradycneme* as a junior synonym of *Elopteryx* without saying whose view it is.

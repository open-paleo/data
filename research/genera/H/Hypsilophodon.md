# *Hypsilophodon*

## Where was the *Hypsilophodon foxii* holotype found, and in which formation?

*2026-08-27*

**Conclusion.** About 210 yards east of Barnes High, in the Hypsilophodon Bed at the top of the Wessex Formation. Galton (1974) takes the position from a letter of Fox's quoted by Owen (1874): the skull was found about 60 yards east of a slab that lay "about 150 yards east of Barnes High", near the western end of the bed's exposure. Governs `location.locality: about 210 yd east of Barnes High`, `location.formation: Wessex` and `location.bed: Hypsilophodon Bed` on [*Hypsilophodon foxii*](../../../genera/H/Hypsilophodon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton1974a, p. 7, R197: "The holotype, a skull of a small individual plus a partial atlas, a cervical vertebra and a dorsal centrum. Found about 210 yd east of Barnes High (Fox in letter quoted by Owen 1874 : 13)."
- galton1974a, p. 16, quoting Fox: "This slab [R190] was found in the fallen cliff, about 150 yards east of Barnes High. . . . The skull [R197 – holotype] and broken jaw [R189] were found about 60 yards further eastward."
- galton1974a, p. 14: "The *Hypsilophodon* Bed is exposed in the cliff at beach level about 100 yd west of Cowleaze Chine and rises in the cliff to end about ¾ mile further west just beyond Barnes High".
- galton2009a, p. 215: "*Hypsilophodon foxii* Huxley, 1869 comes from the *Hypsilophodon* Bed [at top of Wessex Formation (=Wealden Marls), not at base of Vectis Formation (=Wealden Shales) as in Benton & Spencer, 1995".

**Ruled out.**
- *Cowleaze Chine as the site.* Galton (1974) reports that Lydekker (1888) listed the holotype and the specimens found with it as from Cowleaze Chine, "whereas the actual site is at the opposite end of the bed, a little over half a mile further west", and that the catalogue entry "'Cowleaze Chine' is equivalent to *Hypsilophodon* Bed" (galton1974a, p. 16). Huxley (1870) quotes Fox on the bed "which forms the floor of Cowleaze Chine, and rises to the top of the sea cliff at Barne's High" (huxley1870a, p. 3), which describes where the bed crops out, not the find spot. The name also belongs to a member of the overlying formation: "The Vectis Formation comprises the Cowleaze Chine Member and the younger Shepherd's Chine Member" (bonsor2023a, p. 3), so a "Cowleaze Chine" locality is easily read as that member.
- *The base of the Vectis Formation.* Galton (2009) reports this placement by Benton and Spencer (1995) and corrects it to the top of the Wessex Formation (galton2009a, p. 215).

**Open.** Nothing.

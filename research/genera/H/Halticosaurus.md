# *Halticosaurus*

## Where on the Stromberg was the *Halticosaurus* holotype found, and is "Stormberghöhe" a separate place?

*2026-08-26*

**Conclusion.** The Weisser Steinbruch, the sandstone quarry at Pfaffenhofen on the Stromberg. Huene (1908) prints the hill as "Stormberghöhe", a misprint for the Stromberghöhe that Galton (1985) writes for the same 1906 find; Rauhut and Hungerbühler (2000) and Sues and Schoch (2025) name the quarry. Governs `location.locality: Weisser Steinbruch` and the spelling "Stromberghöhe" in `location.notes` on [*Halticosaurus longotarsus*](../../../genera/H/Halticosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huene1908a, p. 231: the remains were found "in einem mergeligen Zwischenlager des Stubensandsteins auf der Stormberghöhe bei Pfaffenhofen, SW. von Brackenheim" ("in a marly interlayer of the Stubensandstein on the Stormberghöhe near Pfaffenhofen, SW of Brackenheim", translated), in the summer of 1906, by E. Fraas. The spelling was checked on the page image.
- galton1985c, Plate 4 caption: "*Halticosaurus longotarsus* v. Huene 1908, part of holotype SMNS 11839 from Middle Stubensandstein of Pfaffenhofen, Stromberghöhe".
- rauhut2000a, p. 78: "**Occurrence:** Middle Stubensandstein, Weißer Steinbruch, Pfaffenhofen, Baden-Württemberg, Germany".
- sues2025a, p. 464: "**Type locality.** 'Weisser Steinbruch' near Pfaffenhofen, Heilbronn district, Baden-Württemberg".

**Ruled out.**
- *Stormberghöhe as a site name.* Only Huene's 1908 text prints it. Galton uses "Stromberghöhe" for this find and for the *Teratosaurus minor* holotype found with it ("Holotype SMNS 11838 from Stromberghöhe, near Pfaffenhofen southwest of Brackenheim", galton1985c, p. 12), and the bibliographies of Rauhut and Hungerbühler and of Sues and Schoch list Berckhemer's (1938) "Wirbeltierfunde aus dem Stubensandstein des Strombergs". A reader of Huene's page who restores "Stormberghöhe, Pfaffenhofen" restores a misprint.

**Open.** The holotype's number. Rauhut and Hungerbühler state that the fragments are "all bearing the collection number SMNS 12353" (rauhut2000a, p. 78), and the record follows them; Galton's Plate 4 caption and Sues and Schoch's holotype line give "SMNS 11839" (sues2025a, p. 464), while Sues and Schoch keep SMNS 12353b for the *Apatosuchus* skull from the same lot. The Staatliches Museum für Naturkunde Stuttgart catalogue would settle which number the *Halticosaurus* bones carry.

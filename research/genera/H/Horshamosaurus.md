# *Horshamosaurus*

## Which unit is the *Horshamosaurus* holotype from?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** The Weald Clay Group, with no formation. Blows (1996) gives "Wealden Marls (Wessex Formation)" for the Rudgwick quarry, but the Wessex Formation is the Isle of Wight unit of the Wessex Sub-basin, and Raven and colleagues (2020) place the specimen in the Weald Clay Group of Sussex. Governs `location.group: Weald Clay` and the empty `location.formation` on [*Horshamosaurus rudgwickensis*](../../../genera/H/Horshamosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- blows1996a, p. 672: "*Type horizon.* Uniformly grey-green marl beds about 30 cm thick, resting on brown clay at the quarry floor level in Wealden Marls (Wessex Formation), Lower Cretaceous (Barremian)"; the type locality is "Rudgwick Brickworks Company quarry, Rudgwick, Sussex, England [N.G.R.TQ085 343]".
- raven2020a, p. 22: HORSM 1988.1546 "was found in the Weald Clay Group (Hauterivian–Barremian) of Sussex, on the mainland of southeast England"; p. 23: the Weald Sub-basin contains the Weald Clay Group, "which contains the Upper and Lower Weald Clay formations".
- raven2020a, p. 13: the *Polacanthoides* humerus and tibia come "from the Wessex Formation of Brighstone Bay on the Isle of Wight".

**Ruled out.**
- *Wessex Formation.* Raven and colleagues use it for Isle of Wight strata (p. 13) and place this specimen in the Weald Clay Group of mainland Sussex, which is in the Weald Sub-basin (our reading of their geography; see [Wealden units](../../topics/wealden-units.md)).
- *Weald Clay as a formation.* Raven and colleagues rank it as a group containing the Upper and Lower Weald Clay formations (p. 23); see the [Weald Clay](../../../stratigraphy/w/weald-clay.yml) entry.

**Open.** Which of the two formations. Blows's "Barremian" and Raven and colleagues' "Hauterivian–Barremian" for the group do not decide it, and no paper read names the formation exposed at the Rudgwick quarry floor.

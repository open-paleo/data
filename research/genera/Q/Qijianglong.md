# *Qijianglong*

## Did the holotype come from Heba Village?

*2026-09-02*

**Conclusion.** No. Heba Village is where a farmer found sauropod vertebrae in the early 1990s; the holotype QJGPM 1001 came from the Beidu site, found during a later survey. Governs `location.locality: Beidu site` on [*Qijianglong guokr*](../../../genera/Q/Qijianglong.yml).

**Evidence.**
- xing2015a, p. e889701-3: "a local farmer (Cai Changming) discovered sauropod vertebrae in his backyard in Heba Village, Beidu Township, in the early 1990s. In 2006, construction workers working 500 m from the original locality spotted a 0.7 m long neopterygian fish (*Lepidotes*). These discoveries prompted Qijiang District to commission a survey of the area".
- xing2015a, p. e889701-4: "Locality—Beidu site (29°0′25″N, 106°34′55″E), Qijiang Petrified Wood and Dinosaur Footprint National Geological Park".

**Ruled out.**
- *Heba Village, or "Heba Village, Beidu Site".* The paper ties Heba Village to the earlier vertebrae, and its locality line for the holotype names only the Beidu site.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the holotype from the Suining Formation?

*2026-09-02*

**Conclusion.** Yes, on the published evidence. The describing paper places it in the Suining; the only rival reading is an unpublished survey. Governs `location.formation: Suining` on [*Qijianglong guokr*](../../../genera/Q/Qijianglong.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dai2025b, p. 11: "*Qijianglong guokr*, as a mamenchisaurid, was excavated from the Suining Formation, and further geological surveys from the Zigong Dinosaur Museum reported the materials belong to the Upper Shaximiao Formation (unpublished)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Upper Shaximiao Formation.* It rests on an unpublished survey. The same paper's introduction lists *Qijianglong guokr* among the genera of "The Middle-Late Jurassic Upper Shaximiao Formation of Sichuan Basin" (dai2025b, p. 1), stating as fact what its discussion attributes to unpublished work; it is one statement, not two.

**Open.** A published account of the Zigong Dinosaur Museum survey would settle the formation. Xing and colleagues note that the park exposes "the Shangshaximiao, Suining, and Penglaizhen formations" (xing2015a, p. e889701-4), so a reassignment within the park is possible.

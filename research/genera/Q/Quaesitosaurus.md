# *Quaesitosaurus*

## Are the Shara-Tsav beds Barungoyot or Nemegt?

*2026-09-02*

**Conclusion.** Barungoyot (Baruungoyot). Kurzanov assigned the Shara-Tsav beds to the Nemegt in 1981 and to the Barungoyot in 1983, from the same associated fauna; the later paper is the describing paper and gives the lithology. Governs `location.formation: Baruungoyot` and `stage: [Campanian]` on [*Quaesitosaurus orientalis*](../../../genera/Q/Quaesitosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- kurzanov1983a, introduction (translated): the skull came from "the western part of the Shara-Tsav bluffs, located 8 km to the north of the well-known Upper Cretaceous reptile locality at Bayshin-Tsav", whose "mottled marls, clays, siltstones, sandstones, and grits" are of "the Barungoyot Formation"; *Gallimimus* and *Avimimus* from the locality "also place the deposits in the Barungoyot". Holotype line: "Shara-Tsav; Upper Cretaceous, Barungoyot Formation".
- jerzykiewicz1991a, p. 368: Udan Sayr and "Shara Tsav locality … occur within lithologies similar to that of the Barun Goyot Formation", citing Kurzanov (1987).

**Ruled out.**
- *Nemegt Formation.* Kurzanov (1981), describing *Avimimus*, places Shara-Tsav "7 km north from the Bayshin-Tsav" and concludes that "the deposits in this location are of the Nemegetsk suite" (kurzanov1981a, translated), from *Gallimimus* and a *Nemegtosaurus* skull. The 1983 paper reads the same assemblage as Barungoyot, so the 1981 assignment is superseded by its own author, not a rival reading.
- *Djadokhta Formation.* Weishampel and colleagues (2004) list *Quaesitosaurus orientalis* with *Avimimus* and *Gallimimus* under a "Djadokhta Svita (Kurzanov 1987; Fastovsky et al. 1998)" (weishampel2004b, p. 596), against the describing paper's Barungoyot.

**Open.** Ivakhnenko and Kurzanov (1988), on the geological structure and age of the Udan-Sayr and Shara-Tsav localities, is the source the later Barun Goyot correlations rely on and has not been read. Whether the "skull of the sauropod *Nemegtosaurus*" that Kurzanov (1981) reports from Shara-Tsav is the skull later named *Quaesitosaurus* is not stated in either paper.

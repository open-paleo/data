# *Plateosaurus*

## Is the P. trossingensis holotype from the Trossingen or the Löwenstein Formation?

*2026-09-01*

**Conclusion.** The Trossingen Formation. Yates (2003) assigns the Trossingen quarry to the upper Löwenstein Formation, following Brenner and Villinger (1981); later work places the same quarry in the Trossingen Formation. Governs `location.formation: Trossingen` on [*Plateosaurus trossingensis*](../../../genera/P/Plateosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sues2025a, p. 462, *Plateosaurus trossingensis*: "Type locality. Slope ("Rutschete") east of Obere Mühle at Deibhalde, east of Trossingen … Type horizon. "Untere Saurierschicht," Trossingen Formation, Middle Keuper Subgroup."
- fernández2022a, p. 773, Table 1: material "collected from the upper bone bed from the Obere Mühle, an outcrop of the Trossingen Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Upper Löwenstein Formation.* Yates (2003) writes that "the famous Plateosaurus specimens from Trossingen actually derive from a muddy facies of the Löwenstein Formation … (Brenner and Villinger 1981)" (yates2003b, p. 317) and that "SMNS 13200 is from the Plateosaurus Quarry, Trossingen … upper Löwenstein Formation" (p. 332). The later sources above place the quarry in the Trossingen Formation; that Yates's assignment follows the older scheme rather than a different reading of the rock is our inference, not checked against Brenner and Villinger.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Where was the P. engelhardti lectotype found?

*2026-09-01*

**Conclusion.** Probably the loam pit at Buchenbühl on the Haidberg, south of Heroldsberg, as Moser (2003) concludes, with the qualifier he gives it. Governs `location.locality: Buchenbühl` on [*Plateosaurus engelhardti*](../../../genera/P/Plateosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- moser2003a, p. 13: "Locus typicus: wahrscheinlich Lehmgrube am Buchenbühl auf dem Haidberg, ca. 2 km S Heroldsberg, NE Nürnberg, Mittelfranken" ("probably a loam pit at Buchenbühl on the Haidberg, about 2 km S of Heroldsberg", translated).
- moser2003a, p. 17: of the workings on the Haidberg, the *Plateosaurus* conglomerate was exposed "nur in der Lehmgrube am Buchenbühl" ("only in the loam pit at Buchenbühl", translated), and it "erscheint unwahrscheinlich" ("seems unlikely", translated) that Engelhart would have named his locality after Heroldsberg if it lay at the more distant Buchberg.

**Ruled out.**
- *A quarry between Heroldsberg and Günthersbühl, on the Buchberg.* Dorn (1930) proposed it because the conglomerate is traceable at the Haidberg "nur an einigen Stellen" ("only in a few places", translated), and Haarländer (1966) thought it probable because the conglomerate there swells to 170 cm (moser2003a, p. 15). Moser answers with a *Plateosaurus* vertebra that "ziemlich sicher" ("almost certainly", translated) came from the Buchenbühl conglomerate and bone fragments later found in the pit itself (p. 15), and with the naming argument above.
- *Heroldsberg.* It is the nearest town, the name the find was reported under, not the site.

**Open.** Whether the pit is the site. Moser keeps "wahrscheinlich" ("probably") in the type-locality line, and the pit has since been filled in and overgrown (p. 16).

## What is the number of the P. gracilis holotype?

*2026-09-01*

**Conclusion.** SMNS 5715. Yates's (2003) formal holotype line prints SMNS 5175, but his own figure captions, Galton's (1985) holotype line and later papers give SMNS 5715. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: SMNS 5715` on [*Plateosaurus gracilis*](../../../genera/P/Plateosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton1985c, p. 12: "Holotype: SMNS 5715, a partial postcranial skeleton from the Middle Stubensandstein of Heslach in Stuttgart".
- yates2003b, p. 321, Text-fig. 3 caption: "P. gracilis (Huene, 1908), SMNS 5715, in dorsal view"; the same number in the captions of Text-figs 5–7 and in Table 1.
- fernández2022a, p. 781, reporting McPhee and colleagues (2015): "the OTU P. gracilis as containing GPIT 18392 and SMNS 5715".

**Ruled out.**
- *SMNS 5175.* Yates prints it in the holotype line, "Holotype. SMNS 5175, incomplete postcranium" (yates2003b, p. 331), three more times in his text and once in the caption of Text-fig. 11, against SMNS 5715 in the captions of Text-figs 3 and 5–7 and in Table 1. Which form is the slip is our judgment from the other sources.

**Open.** The museum's catalogue would confirm it. Huene's (1905) list of new names gives the specimen only as "Skeleton, Stuttgart" (translated).

## Is the P. engelhardti lectotype from the Trossingen, Feuerletten or Klettgau Formation?

*2026-08-03*

**Conclusion.** The Trossingen Formation. "Feuerletten" is the Bavarian name for the same beds, and Klettgau is a Swiss unit. Governs `location.formation: Trossingen` on [*Plateosaurus engelhardti*](../../../genera/P/Plateosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sues2025a, p. 462: the lectotype UEN 552 is "from the Feuerletten (Trossingen Formation) of Heroldsberg near Nürnberg, Bavaria".
- moser2003a, title: "*Plateosaurus engelhardti* Meyer, 1837 … aus dem Feuerletten (Mittelkeuper; Obertrias) von Bayern".

**Ruled out.**
- *Klettgau.* The record held it until 2026-08-03. Jones (2026) lists "Klettgau Fm., Switzerland" among the occurrences of *P. trossingensis* (jones2026b, p. 372); the Heroldsberg lectotype is from Bavaria. That the value came from the Swiss material is an inference.
- *Feuerletten Formation as a separate unit.* Weishampel and colleagues (2004) give "Feuerletten Formation (Bayern), Germany" (weishampel2004a, p. 236, Table 12.1). Sues and Schoch (2025) equate it with the Trossingen Formation, so this is the unit's older regional name, not a rival reading.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which specimen is the P. engelhardti type?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** The sacrum UEN 552, as lectotype. Meyer (1837) named the species on a series of bones and fixed no type; Moser (2003) chose the lectotype from it. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [UEN 552]` and `specimen_type: lectotype` on [*Plateosaurus engelhardti*](../../../genera/P/Plateosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- moser2003a, p. 161: "Of the prevailing 11 syntypes of *Plateosaurus engelhardti* MEYER, 1837 … the sacrum (UEN 552) received the highest attention and is therefore fixed in this work as the lectotype".

**Ruled out.**
- *POL 76.* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi list "*Plateosaurus engelhardti* POL 76" and note that "It was known as *Dimodosaurus poligniensis*" (molina-pérez2020a, table "Chronology of the largest early sauropodomorphs in history"). It is a French skeleton referred to the species, not part of Meyer's series.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Are SMNS 17928 or SMNS 11838 the P. gracilis holotype?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** Neither; the holotype is SMNS 5715 (see the entry on its number). Both are specimens of the old *Sellosaurus gracilis* hypodigm that Yates (2003) moves to *Efraasia minor*. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [SMNS 5715]` on [*Plateosaurus gracilis*](../../../genera/P/Plateosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- yates2003b, p. 329, *Efraasia minor*: "Holotype. SMNS 11838, incomplete adult postcranium"; "Referred material. SMNS 12188–92 … 12684, 17928".
- galton1985c, p. 12: "Holotype: SMNS 5715, a partial postcranial skeleton".

**Ruled out.**
- *SMNS 17928 and SMNS 11838.* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi give both under *Plateosaurus gracilis* (molina-pérez2020a, table "Sauropod speed"; SMNS 17928 also in its size table, "Partial skeleton - Galton 1985"). SMNS 11838 is the holotype of *Teratosaurus minor*, now *Efraasia minor*, and 17928 is referred to that species.

**Open.** Nothing.

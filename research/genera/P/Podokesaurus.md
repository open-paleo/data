# *Podokesaurus*

## What is the Portland Formation of Podokesaurus based on?

*2026-09-02*

**Conclusion.** On the rock of a glacial boulder, not on a known outcrop. Talbot (1911) names no formation, and the compilations that give one hedge it. The record keeps the Portland with a note that its provenance is uncertain. Governs `location.formation: Portland` on [*Podokesaurus holyokensis*](../../../genera/P/Podokesaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- talbot1911a, p. 469: "In a bowlder of Triassic sandstone which the glacier carried two or three miles, possibly, and deposited not far from the site of Mount Holyoke College".
- weishampel2004b, p. 532: *Podokesaurus holyokensis* is listed under "Unnamed unit (=?Portland Formation; Galton 1980c)".
- tykoski2004a, p. 48, Table 3.1: "?Portland Formation (Massachusetts)"; paul2024a, entry *Podokesaurus* (or *Coelophysis*) *holyokensis*, Distribution and formation: "Massachusetts; Portland?".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Which unit the boulder came from. White (1973) catalogues the type from the "Upper Triassic, Newark Series, Longmeadow Sandstone—North America, Massachussetts, Hampden County, Holyoke" (white1973a, entry *Podokesaurus*) without giving a source; whether that rests on an identification of the boulder's rock, and whether the Longmeadow would now be recorded as a unit of its own, is unresolved. A study that matches the boulder's rock to an outcrop would settle it.

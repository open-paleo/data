# *Pelorosaurus*

## Which unit are the Pelorosaurus brevis syntypes from, and is Cuckfield Stone a member?

*2026-09-01*

**Conclusion.** The Grinstead Clay Formation, with no member. The syntypes come from Tilgate Forest near Cuckfield, which the literature places in the Grinstead Clay (see [Wealden units](../../topics/wealden-units.md)); applying that to these specimens is an inference from the locality. "Cuckfield Stone" is a quarrying name for a rock within the unit. Governs `location.formation: Grinstead Clay` and the empty `location.member` and `location.group` on [*Pelorosaurus brevis*](../../../genera/P/Pelorosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- raven2020a, p. 3, Type Locality: "An imprecisely located quarry in Tilgate Forest, near Cuckfield, West Sussex, U.K.. Grinstead Clay Formation", for *Hylaeosaurus*.
- norman2013a, p. 389: specimens in the Mantell Collection "retain the calcareous, gritty sandstone (Cuckfield Stone) that was quarried for road repairs and general building work".

**Ruled out.**
- *member: Cuckfield Stone.* Norman (2013) describes a rock quarried for road and building work, not a named subdivision of the formation.
- *group: Hastings Beds.* The group is right but redundant once a formation is recorded.

**Open.** Upchurch, Mannion and Barrett (2011) are reported to give the Tunbridge Wells Sand Formation for these specimens; their chapter has not been read, and it is the source that would show whether the Grinstead Clay assignment holds for this series.

## Is the Pelorosaurus brevis type series from the Tunbridge Wells Sand or the Grinstead Clay?

*2026-08-06*

**Conclusion.** The Grinstead Clay Formation, which Jones (2026) also gives; the record's earlier Tunbridge Wells Sand names the unit that contains the Grinstead Clay in the scheme that ranks it as a member. Governs `location.formation: Grinstead Clay` on [*Pelorosaurus brevis*](../../../genera/P/Pelorosaurus.yml). Which quarry the series came from is in the 2026-09-01 entry.

**Evidence.**
- norman2013a, p. 390: the Whiteman's Green quarries "exposed Hastings Group Beds of the Grinstead Clay Formation".
- jones2026b, p. 380: "Grinstead Clay Fm., United Kingdom *Early Cretaceous, Valanginian*".

**Ruled out.**
- *Tunbridge Wells Sand Formation.* The record's earlier value. Norman (2010) places the same quarries in "the Grinstead Clay Member, a diachronous lithological unit within the Tunbridge Wells Sand Formation" (norman2010a, p. 47), so the value was the containing unit under that ranking, coarser rather than a different rock.
- *Hastings Beds.* Weishampel and colleagues (2004) give "Hastings Beds (West Sussex), England" for *P. conybearei* (weishampel2004a, Table 13.1). It is the group, and a formation inside it is now recorded.

**Open.** The rank of the Grinstead Clay, formation or member, differs between Norman (2010) and Norman (2013); see [Wealden units](../../topics/wealden-units.md).

## Is the Mantell humerus part of the Pelorosaurus brevis type series?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** No. The name-bearing material is the four caudal vertebrae and three chevrons from Cuckfield that Owen (1842) named *Cetiosaurus brevis*; *Pelorosaurus conybeari* is an objective synonym of that name. The humerus Mantell (1850) based *Pelorosaurus* on is a referred specimen. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id` (NHMUK PV R 2544 to NHMUK PV R 2550) and `specimen_type: syntype` on [*Pelorosaurus brevis*](../../../genera/P/Pelorosaurus.yml), and the humerus's place under `notable_specimens`.

**Evidence.**
- taylor2007a, p. 1559: "Four proximal caudal vertebrae (BMNH R2544–2547) and three chevrons (BMNH R2548–2550) from the Hastings Beds Group of Cuckfield … were named Cetiosaurus brevis Owen, 1842"; "Pelorosaurus conybeari and 'C.' brevis are objective synonyms, with the latter having priority".
- taylor2007a, p. 1559: the humerus was "Discovered adjacent to the Cuckfield 'C.' brevis vertebrae and chevrons", and Mantell (1850) referred it.

**Ruled out.**
- *NHMUK 28626, the humerus, as the type.* Jones (2026) lists it first: "NHMUK 28626—humerus; NHMUK R2544–2547—four anterior caudal vertebrae; NHMUK R2548–2550—three chevrons" (jones2026b, p. 380), and Molina-Pérez and Larramendi give "*Pelorosaurus conybearei* BMNH 28626" (molina-pérez2020a, table "Chronology of the largest sauropods throughout history"). Both follow the *Pelorosaurus conybeari* name, whose type is the *C. brevis* series; the humerus was added to the name, not made its type.

**Open.** Nothing.

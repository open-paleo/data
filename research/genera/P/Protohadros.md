# *Protohadros*

## Is *Protohadros* a hadrosauromorph?

*2026-10-19*

**Conclusion.** Yes, as the record defines the clade. Norman (2014) defines Hadrosauromorpha as *Parasaurolophus walkeri* and all taxa closer to it than to *Probactrosaurus gobiensis*. Nearly every analysis that includes both genera puts *Protohadros* closer to hadrosaurids than *Probactrosaurus*, often as the sister of *Eolambia*. Only analyses on Norman's own matrices put it below *Probactrosaurus*. Governs `parent: Hadrosauromorpha` and the dispute on [*Protohadros byrdi*](../../../genera/P/Protohadros.yml).

**Evidence.**
- norman2014b, Hadrosauromorpha clade nov.: "This stem-based clade is defined as Parasaurolophus walkeri and all taxa positioned more closely to P. walkeri than to Probactrosaurus gobiensis"; in its tree *Protohadros* falls below *Probactrosaurus*, "the most derived non-hadrosauromorphan neoiguanodontian in this analysis".
- norman2004a, p. 435: *Protohadros* "is placed closer to Hadrosauridae, as proposed by Head (2001)" than *Altirhinus* and *Eolambia*, while *Probactrosaurus* is the sister of Hadrosauridae.
- prieto-márquez2010c, Fig. 5, p. 454: *Eolambia* + *Protohadros* (decay 2, bootstrap 51) above *Probactrosaurus* in the strict consensus.
- xing2014a, p. 17: "Eolambia caroljonesa and Protohadros byrdi together form a North American clade of basal hadrosauroids that is more derived than Probactrosaurus gobiensis".
- wang2017c, p. 6: of the five most basal Asian hadrosauroids, which include *Probactrosaurus*, "Eolambia and Protohadros are slightly more derived than the five taxa".
- head1998a, p. 730: "An expanded Hadrosauridae that includes Protohadros is diagnosed by all cranial characters previously mentioned with the exception of the loss of the surangular foramen and the medial migration of the angular".

**Ruled out.**
- *Hadrosauridae.* It holds only under the expanded definition of Head (1998). Every later analysis read for this entry places *Protohadros* outside Hadrosauridae as those papers define it.
- *Hadrosauroidea, the clade that also covers Norman's arrangement.* Norman's result comes from a single matrix line (Norman 2004 and 2014, and You and colleagues 2003 on the matrix of Norman 2002), against many independent matrices that agree with each other.
- *Norman (2014) as the source for Protohadros retaining the surangular foramen.* Norman's text does not say it of *Protohadros*. The point comes from Head's (1998) diagnosis of his expanded Hadrosauridae.
- *Jones (2026) as the source of the placement.* Jones reports it, citing Gates and Lamb (2021) and Alarcón-Muñoz and colleagues (2023).

**Open.** Its sister taxon: *Eolambia* in most analyses, *Jeyawati* in some.

## Is the *Protohadros* type from the Woodbine Formation or the Lewisville Formation?

*2026-08-06*

**Conclusion.** The Lewisville Formation of the Woodbine Group, Arlington Member. Head (1998) placed SMU 303 in the Arlington Member of what was then the Woodbine Formation; the revised scheme that Tykoski and colleagues (2023) follow raises the Woodbine to a group and makes the Arlington a member of the Lewisville inside it. Governs `location.formation: Lewisville` and `location.member: Arlington` on [*Protohadros byrdi*](../../../genera/P/Protohadros.yml). The rank question for the units is in [the rank that moves with the parent](../../topics/rank-that-moves-with-the-parent.md).

**Evidence.**
- head1998a, p. 718: SMU 303 "is located within a six meter thick section of the Arlington Member of the Woodbine Formation (Dodge, 1969)".
- tykoski2023a, p. e2257238-2: "The Lewisville Formation is the upper formation of the Woodbine Group following recent revision of Woodbine stratigraphy and nomenclature by Denne et al. (2016)"; a site "in either the Arlington Member or Tarrant Member of the Lewisville Formation".
- tykoski2023a, p. e2257238-7: "Protohadros byrdi is the most common and widespread herbivorous dinosaur taxon known in the Lewisville Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Woodbine Formation.* Head's rank, repeated in Norman's table as "Woodbine Formation (Texas), United States" (norman2004a, p. 417, Table 19.1). It names the same rock under the scheme that preceded the revision, not a different unit.

**Open.** Head puts the Tarrant Member in the Eagleford Group (head1998a, p. 718), while Tykoski and colleagues place it in the Lewisville, so the two schemes differ on more than rank. Denne and colleagues (2016), the revision itself, have not been read.

# *Priodontognathus*

## Does the *Priodontognathus* record keep a Yorkshire region?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** Yes. The region follows the formation the record already takes from the matrix, the Calcareous Grit of Yorkshire; Etheridge's alternative, the shore at Hastings, would mean a Lower Cretaceous rock, which the matrix identification argues against. Governs `location.region: GB-NYK` on [*Priodontognathus phillipsii*](../../../genera/P/Priodontognathus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- seeley1875a, p. 443: Etheridge "thought that the block containing the fossil was either Calcareous Grit or Coral Rag", probably "a fragment on the sea-shore", in which case "there were only two points from which it could have come, namely, the Yorkshire coast and Hastings".

**Ruled out.**
- *No region.* Dropping it would leave the record naming a Yorkshire formation with no region, half-committed rather than cautious.

**Open.** The locality, as before.

## Where is the Priodontognathus holotype from?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** No locality is known, and none can be recovered from the sources. A formation can be given only from the matrix: probably the [Calcareous Grit](../../../stratigraphy/c/calcareous-grit.yml), Oxfordian. Governs `location.formation: Calcareous Grit`, the empty `location.locality`, and the region on *Priodontognathus phillipsii*.

**Evidence.**
- seeley1875a, p. 439: "AMONG the few Reptilian fossils collected by the late Dr. Forbes-Young"; "It was in association with bones from the Wealden of Tilgate Forest, but may be of Great Oolite age".
- seeley1875a, p. 443, discussion: Etheridge regretted "that the locality of this interesting fossil was unknown", suggested "either Calcareous Grit or Coral Rag", and gave "only two points from which it could have come, namely, the Yorkshire coast and Hastings".
- seeley1893a, p. 57: the executors "presented it to the University of Cambridge in 1861, but I am unable to trace its earlier history"; "There was no evidence of the stratigraphical position of the fossil other than the matrix".
- hoffstetter1957a, p. 540: "sans provenance connue" ("of no known provenance", translated), listing Great Oolite or Wealden (Seeley), Corallian (Etheridge), Cambridge Greensand (Woodward and Sherborn) and Oxfordian (Hennig), and calling the Calcareous Grit "l'estimation la plus probable" ("the most probable estimate", translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *A locality from Seeley (1875).* Weishampel and colleagues cite "?Lower Calcareous Grit (Seeley 1875; Galton 1980e)" (weishampel2004b, p. 545), but Seeley gives no locality and the Calcareous Grit is Etheridge's suggestion in the discussion.
- *Slingsby.* Seeley (1893, p. 57) himself floated that the specimen "may be a portion of the head of the Slingsby reptile"; it is a guess, not a provenance.

**Open.** The region. Etheridge's alternatives were the Yorkshire coast and Hastings; a Yorkshire region follows only from preferring the Calcareous Grit.

## Is *Priodontognathus phillipsii* a nomen dubium?

*2026-10-08*

**Conclusion.** Its validity is disputed. Compilations list it as a nomen dubium without argument, while Naish and Martill (2008) argue from its teeth that the status is probably unjust. Governs `status: disputed` and the retained `diagnostic_features` on [*Priodontognathus phillipsii*](../../../genera/P/Priodontognathus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- vickaryous2004a lists it among ankylosaur nomina dubia in a table, with no discussion, as does Carpenter (2001).
- naish2008a: "The specimen exhibits unusual teeth combining denticulate cingula and bowllike lingual depressions (Barrett 2001), and its recent consignment to the status of nomen dubium (Vickaryous et al. 2004) is probably unjust."

**Ruled out.**
- *Nomen dubium by consensus.* Taxa founded on teeth or a single tooth-bearing element are now generally treated as dubious for want of autapomorphies, which explains the listings, but none of them argues the point for this maxilla, and the one paper that examines its teeth argues the other way. That is the shape of the *Priconodon* case: a label against an argument.

**Open.** Barrett (2001), the source of the tooth characters Naish and Martill cite, has not been read.

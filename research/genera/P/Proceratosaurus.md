# *Proceratosaurus*

## Does the *Proceratosaurus* holotype take a formation within the Great Oolite?

*2026-10-11*

**Conclusion.** No. The two papers that describe the specimen give only the Great Oolite, and the two later formation assignments disagree with each other and state no basis. Governs `location.group: Great Oolite`, the empty `location.formation` and the location note on [*Proceratosaurus bradleyi*](../../../genera/P/Proceratosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huene1926a: the skull is "from the Great Oolite of Minchinhampton, Gloucestershire".
- rauhut2010a, Type locality and Horizon: "Minchinhampton, Gloucestershire, England"; "*Horizon and age:* Great Oolite, Late Bathonian, Middle Jurassic".
- carrano2012a, p. 240: "Minchinhampton Reservoir, Gloucestershire, England; White Limestone Formation, Great Oolite Group; middle–late Bathonian".
- naish2022a: "*Proceratosaurus bradleyi* from the Bathonian Taynton Limestone Formation of the UK (Rauhut, Milner & Moore-Fay, 2010)".

**Ruled out.**
- *White Limestone Formation.* Carrano and colleagues give no basis, and another paper names a different formation.
- *Taynton Limestone Formation.* Naish and Cau cite Rauhut and colleagues, who name only the Great Oolite.

**Open.** Which bed of the Great Oolite at Minchinhampton yielded the skull.

## Which unit of the Great Oolite yielded the holotype?

*2026-09-02*

**Conclusion.** The Great Oolite Group, at group rank. The primary descriptions give only "Great Oolite". Governs `location.group: Great Oolite` and the empty `location.formation` on [*Proceratosaurus bradleyi*](../../../genera/P/Proceratosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huene1926a, p. 474: "a skull, incomplete in its upper half, from the Great Oolite of Minchinhampton, Gloucestershire".
- rauhut2010a, p. 157: "Type locality: Minchinhampton, Gloucestershire, England. Horizon and age: Great Oolite, Late Bathonian, Middle Jurassic."

**Ruled out.**
- *Taynton Limestone Formation.* Naish and Cau (2022) write "*Proceratosaurus bradleyi* from the Bathonian Taynton Limestone Formation of the UK (Rauhut, Milner & Moore-Fay, 2010)" (naish2022a, p. 3). The paper they cite gives only "Great Oolite" for the horizon (rauhut2010a, p. 157) and names no formation.

**Open.** The formation. Carrano and colleagues (2012) give "Minchinhampton Reservoir, Gloucestershire, England; White Limestone Formation, Great Oolite Group" (carrano2012a, p. 240) without stating the evidence. Woodward's (1910) original description, the account of the find, would show whether the reservoir excavation can be placed in a formation.

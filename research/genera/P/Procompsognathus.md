# *Procompsognathus*

## Which quarry yielded the holotype: the Weisser Steinbruch or the Burrer Quarry?

*2026-09-02*

**Conclusion.** The "Weisser Steinbruch" near Pfaffenhofen. Governs `location.locality: Weisser Steinbruch` on [*Procompsognathus triassicus*](../../../genera/P/Procompsognathus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sues2025a, p. 464: "Type locality. "Weisser Steinbruch" near Pfaffenhofen, Heilbronn district, Baden-Württemberg. Type horizon. Middle Stubensandstein (S2), Löwenstein Formation".
- sues2025a, p. 459: the "Weisser Steinbruch" "also yielded skeletal remains of other archosaurian reptiles including the coelophysid theropod *Procompsognathus triassicus*".
- fraas1913a, p. 1099: the holotype "was made in the red marls of the upper 'Fäule' in the Pfaffenhofen quarry in the spring of 1909" (translated), with no name for the quarry.

**Ruled out.**
- *Burrer Quarry, Pfaffenhofen.* "Burrer Quarry" is the name of a different site: Sues and Schoch list a *Cyamodus rostratus* cranium "from Burrer Quarry, Gaismühle near Crailsheim, Baden-Württemberg" in the Middle Triassic Muschelkalk part of their synopsis (sues2025a, p. 422). Jones (2026) names Albert Burrer as the man who found the *Procompsognathus* holotype "in his quarry" (jones2026c, *P. triassicus* entry), which makes Burrer the quarry's owner rather than the quarry's name.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the *Procompsognathus* holotype from the Löwenstein Formation or the "Stubensandstein"?

*2026-08-03*

**Conclusion.** The Löwenstein Formation, in its middle part. "Stubensandstein" is the older name for the same unit, and the Middle Stubensandstein is a division of it, not a rival unit. Governs `location.formation: Löwenstein` and `location.part: middle` on [*Procompsognathus triassicus*](../../../genera/P/Procompsognathus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sues2025a, p. 464: for *Procompsognathus triassicus*, "Type horizon. Middle Stubensandstein (S2), Löwenstein Formation. Age: Late Triassic (Norian: Lacian-Alaunian)."
- fraas1913a, p. 1099: the holotype "was made in the red marls of the upper 'Fäule' in the Pfaffenhofen quarry" (translated), within the beds Fraas calls "the Stubensandstein of the Stromberg near Pfaffenhofen" (p. 1097, translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *"Stubensandstein" as the formation.* Jones (2026) gives "Stubensandstein, Germany" (jones2026c, *Procompsognathus triassicus* table row). It is the traditional name, which Sues and Schoch (2025) keep only for the divisions S1 to S4 inside the Löwenstein Formation.
- *"Middle Stubensandstein" as the formation.* Weishampel and colleagues (2004) give "Middle Stubensandstein (Baden-Württemberg), Germany" (weishampel2004a, *P. triassicus* table row). It names the division that `location.part: middle` records.

**Open.** Nothing.

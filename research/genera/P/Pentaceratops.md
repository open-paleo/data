# *Pentaceratops*

## Is the Pentaceratops holotype from the Fruitland or the lowest Kirtland?

*2026-09-01*

**Conclusion.** The Fruitland Formation, as the describing paper and the latest revision give it, with no finer position. Governs `location.formation: Fruitland` on [*Pentaceratops sternbergii*](../../../genera/P/Pentaceratops.yml).

**Evidence.**
- osborn1923a, p. 1: "At a locality nine miles northeast of Tsaya, New Mexico, in the Cretaceous formation described in 1916 by Bauer as the Fruitland Beds, the veteran explorer Charles H. Sternberg discovered in 1922 a fine skull"; p. 3, Fig. 1 caption: "from the Fruitland Beds, 9 miles N. E. of Tsaya".
- fowler2020a, p. 10: AMNH 6325, 1624 and 1625 "are all recorded as having been collected in the Fruitland Formation (with no better stratigraphic resolution available for the AMNH specimens; see Supporting Information 1)"; their Fig. 1 marks only a "purported collection area of AMNH 6325".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Whether "9 miles N. E. of Tsaya" is the Coal Creek area of the Fossil Forest. Sullivan and Williamson (1999) note that hadrosaur bones collected in 1922 "9 miles N. E. of Tsaya, New Mexico" are "consistent with the Coal Creek locality" (sullivan1999b, p. 36), and that this area "encompasses either the upper part of the Fruitland Formation of some workers or the lowest member of the Kirtland Formation, Hunter Wash Member" (p. 35). They do not mention *Pentaceratops*, and they credit those bones to C. M. Sternberg, while Osborn credits the skull to Charles H. Sternberg, so the shared bearing is not shown to be the same site; applying their reading to AMNH 6325 is an inference. Fowler and Freedman Fowler's Supporting Information 1, which discusses the AMNH localities, has not been read and is the place to look first.

# *Pulanesaura*

## Is *Pulanesaura* a sauropod?

*2026-10-19*

**Conclusion.** It depends on the definition of Sauropoda, which the record does not yet fix. The analyses agree that *Pulanesaura* lies just outside the clade of *Vulcanodon* and Eusauropoda and outside Gravisauria. Under Yates's stem-based definition that position is inside Sauropoda; under the node-based definition of Salgado and colleagues (1997) it is the sister of Sauropoda. The parent stays at Sauropoda until the clade's definition is settled. Governs `parent: Sauropoda` and the dispute on [*Pulanesaura eocollum*](../../../genera/P/Pulanesaura.yml).

**Evidence.**
- mcphee2015a, p. 6: "Because of differing taxonomic opinions on the node-or-stem-based definition for Sauropoda, our phylogenetic hypothesis of Pulanesaura places it as either a basal sauropod … or as the sister taxon to Sauropoda".
- yates2007a, p. 104: "I propose that Sauropoda be defined as the most inclusive clade that includes Saltasaurus loricatus but not Melanorosaurus readi".
- mcphee2017a, p. 831, Abstract: the stem-based definition "places Pulanesaura as an unambiguous sauropod, whereas another commonly cited definition (the least inclusive clade containing Vulcanodon and Saltasaurus) suggests it is the most proximate sauropod outgroup"; Fig. 15, p. 850, marks the node-based definition as "preferred".
- rauhut2020a, p. 7: "Concerning the definition of Sauropoda, we follow the emerging consensus to use the node-based definition originally proposed by Salgado et al. (1997)".
- barrett2024b, p. e2337802-28: "For the time being, we advocate abandonment of any stem-based definition of Sauropoda using Melanorosaurus".
- pol2022a, p. 132: "Other recent analyses followed the definition of Yates (2007) that points to the node of sauropodomorphs closer to Saltasaurus loricatus than to Melanorosaurus readi".
- rincón2022a, p. e2077112-4: under the stem-based definition "Pulanesaura, Antetonitrus, lessemsaurids (Lessemsaurus, Ingentia), Amygdalodon, Gongxianosaurus, and Isanosaurus are included within Sauropoda".

**Ruled out.**
- *Jones (2026) as the basis of the placement.* Jones adopts the stem-based definition for the book and places the genus by it. That is a choice of definition, not a phylogenetic result.
- *The skull of Melanorosaurus (Yates, 2007) as the source of the stem-based definition.* Pol and colleagues (2011, 2022) credit it there. The definition is proposed in Yates's paper on *Aliwalia*, published online in 2006, which Rincón and colleagues (2022) cite as "Yates (2006)".

**Open.** Which definition of Sauropoda the dataset adopts. The choice also decides the content of Sauropoda for *Isanosaurus*, *Gongxianosaurus*, the lessemsaurids and *Vulcanodon*.

## Where is the holotype's coordinate printed?

*2026-09-02*

**Conclusion.** In the paper that names *Arcusaurus*, which prints a position for Sauropod Quarry; the *Pulanesaura* papers print none. The two holotypes come from one quarry. Governs `location.coordinates` (-28.4667, 27.825383) on [*Pulanesaura eocollum*](../../../genera/P/Pulanesaura.yml), the same pair as [*Arcusaurus*](../../../genera/A/Arcusaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- yates2011a, p. 611: "Horizon and Locality—Sauropod Quarry, Spion Kop 932, (S28°28.002′ E027°49.523′), Senekal District, Free State, South Africa"; the *Arcusaurus* bones "are much smaller than the basal sauropod that is found in the same quarry".
- mcphee2015a, p. 2, Type locality and horizon: "The much smaller *Arcusaurus pereirabalorum* was recovered from the edge of the same quarry"; Fig. 1 labels "SQ, 'Sauropod [*Pulanesaura*] Quarry'".

**Ruled out.** None.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the Pulanesaura holotype BP/1/6982 or BP/1/6882?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** BP/1/6982. The erecting paper prints BP/1/6882, and the later osteology by the same team states that this was wrong. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [BP/1/6982]` on [*Pulanesaura eocollum*](../../../genera/P/Pulanesaura.yml).

**Evidence.**
- mcphee2017a, p. 831: "it should be noted that the correct catalogue number for the holotypic anterior dorsal vertebra is BP/1/6982, and not BP/1/6882".

**Ruled out.**
- *BP/1/6882.* McPhee and colleagues (2015) print it in the holotype line and diagnosis (mcphee2015a, Holotype), and Jones (2026) repeats it: "**BP/1/6882**—partial vertebra" (jones2026b, p. 375). McPhee and colleagues (2017) correct it.

**Open.** Nothing.

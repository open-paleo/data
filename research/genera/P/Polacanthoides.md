# *Polacanthoides*

## Who named *Polacanthoides ponderosus*, and was the scapula from Bolney?

*2026-10-24*

**Conclusion.** Nopcsa (1928) named the genus, without a species, and Nopcsa (1929) named the species *ponderosus*. Mantell (1841) referred two scapulae from Bolney to *Hylaeosaurus*, and Raven and colleagues (2020) cite that paper for the original referral of the *Polacanthoides* scapula, so the scapula is placed at Bolney by combining the two papers; Mantell gives no register number. This settles the 2026-10-07 entry's Open question. The record lists only the scapula of Nopcsa's two-element type, as a syntype: the humerus comes from the Isle of Wight, not Sussex, so including it would join two localities and the Barremian to a type the 2026-10-07 entry dates from the scapula alone. Governs `erected_in: nopcsa1928a` (genus), species `erected_in: nopcsa1929a`, `type_specimen` and `location.locality: Bolney` on [*Polacanthoides ponderosus*](../../../genera/P/Polacanthoides.yml).

**Evidence.**
- nopcsa1928a, p. 58, footnote 1: "This new genus is based on the scapula 2584, the humerus 1106 and the tibia 1107 of the British Museum (Natural History)"; no species is named.
- nopcsa1929a, p. 31 (translated): the scapula and humerus "together form the type of the animal I had already named Polacanthoides as a genus in an earlier work, for which I now propose the species name ponderosus."
- mantell1841a, pp. 142–143: the second specimen of *Hylaeosaurus* "was found imbedded in a layer of blue clay near Bolney in Sussex", with "two omoplates identical in character".
- raven2020a: the humerus and tibia come "from the Wessex Formation of Brighstone Bay on the Isle of Wight", and Nopcsa "erroneously listed all of the elements as coming from Sussex".

**Ruled out.**
- *Polacanthoides ponderosus Nopcsa, 1928.* The 1928 footnote gives no species name; works citing 1928 for the binomial join the two papers.
- *Listing the humerus (NHMUK PV R 1106) with the scapula.* It is a syntype under Nopcsa (1929), but from a different locality and formation; the record keeps the Sussex element and states the omission in the type-specimen notes.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which stages does the *Polacanthoides* type scapula take?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** The Wealden's range, Berriasian to Aptian. The type scapula is dated no more finely than "the Wealden of Sussex", and the Barremian the record carried belongs to the Isle of Wight elements Nopcsa combined with it. Governs `stage: [Berriasian, Valanginian, Hauterivian, Barremian, Aptian]` and `type_specimen.specimen_id: [NHMUK PV OR 2584]` on [*Polacanthoides ponderosus*](../../../genera/P/Polacanthoides.yml), under the rule that a taxon with no statement about its horizon takes its unit's range.

**Evidence.**
- nopcsa1928a, p. 58: "This new genus is based on the scapula 2584, the humerus 1106 and the tibia 1107 of the British Museum (Natural History) These pieces were all found in the Wealden at Bolney".
- raven2020a: the species was named "based on a chimera consisting of a scapula from the Wealden of Sussex (NHMUK PV OR2584 …) and a humerus and tibia from the Wessex Formation of Brighstone Bay on the Isle of Wight", and Nopcsa "erroneously listed all of the elements as coming from Sussex"; the scapula is "NHMUK PV OR2584", in the OR register.

**Ruled out.**
- *Barremian.* No source read dates the scapula; the Barremian fits the Wessex Formation of the Isle of Wight humerus and tibia, a different animal.
- *NHMUK PV R 2584.* The record's earlier number; Raven and colleagues give the OR register.

**Open.** Whether the scapula is from Bolney: Nopcsa gives Bolney for all three elements, an assignment Raven and colleagues show to be wrong for two of them, and they give the scapula only as "the Wealden of Sussex".

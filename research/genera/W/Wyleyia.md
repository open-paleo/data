# *Wyleyia*

## Is the *Wyleyia* humerus from the Hastings Beds?

*2026-09-09*

**Conclusion.** No. Harrison and Walker (1973) place it in the Weald Clay at Henfield, some 500 to 600 feet below the top of that unit, and Naish and Martill (2007) repeat the Weald Clay. Governs the Weald Clay in `location` on [*Wyleyia valdensis*](../../../genera/W/Wyleyia.yml).

**Evidence.**
- harrison1973a, p. 726, Occurrence: "Weald Clay, Lower Cretaceous, Henfield, Sussex. The ostracods in the Henfield deposits indicate that they lie about 500–600 feet below the top of the Weald Clay."
- naish2007a, p. 503: *Wyleyia valdensis* "is from the Weald Clay Group of Henfield, West Sussex".

**Ruled out.**
- *Hastings Beds, late Berriasian–Valanginian.* The distribution chapter of Weishampel and colleagues (2004) lists "?Avialae indet. (= *Wyleyia valdensis*)" under West Sussex, "1. Hastings Beds (= lower Wealden Group; … Harrison and Walker 1973 …)", "Age: late Berriasian–Valanginian" (weishampel2004b, p. 559). The paper it cites for the row puts the humerus in the Weald Clay, which the same page lists as a separate entry ("2. Weald Clay (= upper Wealden Group …)", "Age: Barremian").

**Open.** Nothing.

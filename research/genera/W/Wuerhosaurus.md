# *Wuerhosaurus*

## Which Wuerho field locality is the *Wuerhosaurus homheni* holotype from?

*2026-09-09*

**Conclusion.** Field locality 64043-5. Dong (1973) does not print a field number in the holotype line itself. He says the description rests mainly on the material from 64043-5 and that the material from the other two ornithischian localities serves as paratypes, so the holotype comes from 64043-5 by elimination. Governs `location.locality: Wuerho, field locality 64043-5` on [*Wuerhosaurus homheni*](../../../genera/W/Wuerhosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dong1973a, Order Ornithischia: "Three ornithischian fossil localities were discovered in the Wuerho Valley fauna: 64043–5, 64043, and 64045. Among these, the material from locality 64043–5 is best preserved. The description below is based primarily on this locality; the material from the other two localities is quite fragmentary and serves as paratypes" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Field localities 64043 and 64045.* The same sentence of Dong (1973) assigns their material to the paratypes; at 64043 these are "three caudal vertebrae" that "are undoubtedly posterior caudal vertebrae" (dong1973a, *Wuerhosaurus*, Caudal vertebrae; translated).
- *Field locality 64043-6.* It is the number of the *Kelmayisaurus* maxilla and mandible, IVPP V4022, in the same memoir: "Field locality number 64043-6; Institute of Vertebrate Paleontology and Paleoanthropology laboratory number V. 4022" (dong1973a, *Kelmayisaurus petrolicus*, Material; translated). [*Kelmayisaurus*](../../../genera/K/Kelmayisaurus.yml) carries it.

**Open.** No source read for this note prints a field number beside IVPP V4006 itself. Dong (1990), the fuller later account of the holotype, gives the site only as "Wuerho, Xinjiang (Sinkiang)" and lists the paratype IVPP V4007 as "three caudal vertebrae", which matches the 64043 material.

## Is *Wuerhosaurus homheni* also from the Jingchuan Formation?

*2026-09-09*

**Conclusion.** No. The holotype, IVPP V4006, comes from the Lianmuqin Formation at Wuerho, in the Junggar Basin. The second formation that Sánchez-Fenollosa and Cobos (2025) give the species appears to come from Ordos Basin material that has been referred to it. Governs `location.formation: Lianmuqin` on [*Wuerhosaurus homheni*](../../../genera/W/Wuerhosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- maidment2008a, p. 379: "The holotype and paratype were recovered from the Lianmuging Formation (Valanginian-Albian: Weishampel *et al.* 2004), Wuerho, Xinjiang, China (Dong 1990). The referred specimen was collected from the Ejinhoro Formation (Lower Cretaceous) of the Ordos Basin, Inner Mongolia, China (Dong 1993)." The referred specimen is "IVPP V6879, a dorsal vertebra", which "was previously referred to *Wuerhosaurus ordosensis*" (same page).
- sánchez-fenollosa2025a, p. 182: "*W. homheni* (late Valanginian–?Barremian, Lianmuqin and Jingchuan Formations, China)".
- sánchez-fenollosa2025a, p. 168, Table 1: the scoring of *W. homheni* draws on "Dong (1973, 1990, 1993), Maidment et al. (2008), Li et al. (2024a)".
- tong2021a, p. 13, Table 1: *Sinemys brevispinus*, "Ordos Basin, Inner Mongolia, China", "Jingchuan Fm."

**Ruled out.**
- *Jingchuan Formation for the holotype.* Sánchez-Fenollosa and Cobos (2025) list the species under two formations and cite no source for either. Maidment and colleagues (2008) put the holotype in the Lianmuqin at Wuerho, and the only Ordos specimen they refer to the species, IVPP V6879, in the Ejinhoro. The Jingchuan is also an Ordos Basin unit. Sánchez-Fenollosa and Cobos score the species partly from Dong (1993), the description of the Ordos material, so their Jingchuan is most likely that material under another unit name. That attribution is an inference: they do not say which specimen the second formation belongs to.

**Open.** Where the Jingchuan comes from. Sánchez-Fenollosa and Cobos (2025) give each unit's distribution in their File S1, section 1.3, which has not been read and would settle it.

# *Leyesaurus*

## Which unit and age is the Leyesaurus holotype from?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** The Balde de Leyes Formation, Early Jurassic, with no stage given by any source. Governs `location.formation`, [Balde de Leyes](../../../stratigraphy/b/balde-de-leyes.yml) (already recorded), and `period` / `stage` on *Leyesaurus marayensis*: Early Jurassic, no stage, instead of Late Triassic, Rhaetian.

**Evidence.**
- martínez2015a, p. 380: "all specimens of *Leyesaurus* found to date were collected from another stratigraphic unit", a new unit that overlies the Quebrada del Barro Formation; p. 382: "The age of the unit is suggested to be Early Jurassic based on the presence of the massospondylid sauropodomorph dinosaur *Leyesaurus*".
- colombi2015a (in-press manuscript, p. 9): "la Formación Balde de Leyes es asignada al Jurásico Inferior" ("the Balde de Leyes Formation is assigned to the Lower Jurassic", translated). The unit's vertebrate association is "monoespecífica formada por … *Leyesaurus marayensis*" ("monospecific, formed by … *Leyesaurus marayensis*", translated; manuscript p. 8).
- martínez2017a, p. 489: "the Quebrada del Barro (upper Norian–Rhaetian) and the Balde de Leyes (Lower Jurassic) formations".

**Ruled out.**
- *Uppermost Quebrada del Barro Formation, Rhaetian.* Apaldetti and colleagues (2011) found the type "in the uppermost level of the Quebrada del Barro Formation … 2 meters below the contact with the Cretaceous unit" (apaldetti2011a, p. 3, Horizon). Those beds were later split off as the Balde de Leyes Formation (above). A Rhaetian age follows only from applying the Quebrada del Barro's "late Norian–Rhaetian" (martínez2017a, p. 491; apaldetti2018a, p. 1227, both from Martínez and colleagues 2015) to the type. No source assigns *Leyesaurus* to the Rhaetian, and Apaldetti and colleagues (2011, p. 4) themselves leaned Jurassic: "the Quebrada del Barro Formation may be of Lower Jurassic age".
- *Placing the type by the Lucianovenator and Ingentia horizons.* *Lucianovenator* is "60 meters below the top of the formation" (martínez2017a, p. 491) and *Ingentia* "160 m below the top" (apaldetti2018a, p. 1227). These are Quebrada del Barro horizons of other taxa and say nothing about a type that lies in the overlying formation.

**Open.** The Early Jurassic age rests on correlation and on *Leyesaurus* itself (Martínez and colleagues 2015, p. 382), which is circular for this record. No stage has been proposed.

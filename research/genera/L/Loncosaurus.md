# *Loncosaurus*

## Which formation is the *Loncosaurus* type from?

*2026-08-29*

**Conclusion.** The Mata Amarilla Formation. Only Varela and colleagues (2012) place Cerro Pari Aike in it, in one sentence that follows Varela's unpublished 2011 work. Ameghino (1899) and Huene (1929) name only the old "Guaranitic" formation. Governs `location.formation: Mata Amarilla` and `location.locality: Cerro Pari Aike` on [*Loncosaurus argentinus*](../../../genera/L/Loncosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ameghino1899a, p. 62 (translated): the remains were found "in the Guarani formation of the Rio Sehuen", "from a layer of red sandstone resting upon a bed of *Ostrea guaranítica*".
- huene1929a, p. 17 (translated): Carlos Ameghino's site lies "about 50 kilometers east of Lake Viedma and 30 kilometers south of Mata Amarilla. Ameghino gives that region the name Par Aik"; the femur and tooth came from "an extremely high level of the dinosaur-bearing layers".
- varela2012a, p. 367: "Ameghino (1899) described at the Cerro Pari Aike (Pari Aike Hill, locality 7, Fig. 3) -currently referred to as the Mata Amarilla Formation, following Varela, 2011-a *Loncosaurus argentinus*".

**Ruled out.**
- *Cardiel Formation.* Weishampel and colleagues (2004) list *Loncosaurus* under "Cardiel Formation (= 'Guaranitic' Formation; Ameghino 1898, 1899a, 1899b; Huene 1929a; …)" (weishampel2004b, p. 602). Neither Ameghino nor Huene uses the name, and Varela and colleagues assign the type locality to the Mata Amarilla. Riccardi and Rolleri's Pari Aike Formation took in outcrops at both Pari Aike and Lago Cardiel (kraemer1997a, Formación Pari Aike, translated), which may be where the Cardiel name came from. That is an inference.
- *Pari Aike Formation.* The name the type locality suggests. Varela and colleagues regard the Pari Aike as "the middle section of the Mata Amarilla Formation" (varela2012a, p. 367), and it is a variant on [Mata Amarilla](../../../stratigraphy/m/mata-amarilla.yml).

**Open.** Two things. First, whether Sickmann and colleagues' Cerro Fortaleza Formation reaches Cerro Pari Aike. It takes in terrestrial deposits "between Lago Viedma and Lago Argentino and east past Tres Lagos", including "rocks that have sometimes been named 'Mata Amarilla Formation'" (sickmann2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 5), and dates them "no older than Campanian". Huene's site, 50 km east of Lake Viedma, may lie in that area, but no source says so. Second, Coria and Salgado's (1996) revision of the type material has not been read.

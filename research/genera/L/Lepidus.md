# *Lepidus*

## Are the Otis Chalk beds Colorado City or Cooper Canyon Formation?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Disputed. Lehman and Chatterjee's (2005) mapping places the Otis Chalk strata in the upper [Cooper Canyon Formation](../../../stratigraphy/c/cooper-canyon.yml); the [Colorado City](../../../stratigraphy/c/colorado-city.yml) assignment rests on Lucas and colleagues' scheme. Governs `location.formation` on *Lepidus praecisio*, which currently follows the Colorado City reading.

**Evidence.**
- lehman2005a, p. 346: "our mapping shows that these strata are actually within the upper part of the Cooper Canyon Formation"; p. 347: "the Otischalkian biochron cannot be supported on the basis of available tetrapod evidence".
- nesbitt2015a, p. 515: Martz (2008) and Stocker (2013) place the beds "near the base of the Cooper Canyon Member"; the paper itself gives only "Otis Chalk area, Dockum Group, Upper Triassic" (p. 514) and calls the correlation unresolved.

**Ruled out.**
- *Colorado City Member as named in 1993.* Lucas and Anderson (1993) named "a new stratigraphic unit, the Iatan Member" (lucas1993c, p. 231); "Colorado City Member is introduced here to replace the preoccupied name Iatan" dates from 1995 (lucas1995a, p. 5). Hunt (1998) credits Colorado City to 1993.
- *A date for the Otis Chalk beds.* The "Rb/Sr isotopic age of approximately 210 Ma" (lehman2005a, p. 347) is on detrital biotite from the upper Cooper Canyon in general, a maximum age relayed from Long and Lehman (1994), not a date for these beds.

**Open.** Which formation. Lehman and Chatterjee describe Lucas and colleagues' older placement as based on the "supposedly 'primitive' elements of the Otis Chalk fauna" (p. 346); Lucas and Anderson also argue from lithology (lucas1993c, p. 233). Later papers using Colorado City include Hunt (1998, hunt1998a, p. 513), Baron and Williams (2018, baron2018c, p. 129) and Marsh and Rowe (2019, marsh2019a, p. 17), and the record also cites Lovelace and colleagues (2025), which was not re-examined for this note. Nesbitt and colleagues' "probably at least older than 223 Ma, or at the youngest, lower Norian" (p. 515) rests on *Paleorhinus* and *Leptosuchus* biostratigraphy.

# *Lexovisaurus*

## Where does *Lexovisaurus* sit?

*2026-10-23*

**Conclusion.** Stegosauridae incertae sedis. Maidment and colleagues (2008) call the holotype an indeterminate member of their clade C, which lies inside Stegosauridae in their tree; later trees break up clade C, so no subfamily is agreed. Governs `parent: Stegosauridae` and `placement: incertae sedis` on [*Lexovisaurus durobrivensis*](../../../genera/L/Lexovisaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- maidment2008a, p. 383: the holotype "is clearly a stegosaur because of the overall morphology of the ilia"; "BMNH R1989-1992 bears no unique characters or character combinations and is regarded as an indeterminate clade C stegosaurs."
- maidment2008a, Fig. 11A, p. 390 (figure reading): clade C (*Loricatosaurus*, *Tuojiangosaurus*, *Paranthodon*, *Stegosaurus*) is nested within Stegosauridae.

**Ruled out.**
- *Stegosaurinae.* Clade C sits inside it in Maidment and colleagues' tree, but Raven and colleagues (2023) place *Loricatosaurus*, the best-known member, between the stegosaurines and the other stegosaurids.

**Open.** Maidment and colleagues give no holotype character that ties it to clade C; the tall-plate synapomorphy they cite comes from referred plates.

## Is the *Lexovisaurus* holotype from the Oxford Clay at Fletton?

*2026-08-29*

**Conclusion.** Yes. Hulke (1887) gave the horizon as the Kimmeridge Clay of Northamptonshire and named no site. Hoffstetter (1957), who erected *Lexovisaurus* on this species, places Leeds's material in the Lower Oxford Clay at Fletton, just south of Peterborough, and Galton (1985) gives Fletton, with the horizon corrected to the Oxford Clay. Governs `location.locality: Fletton` and `location.formation: Oxford Clay` on [*Lexovisaurus durobrivensis*](../../../genera/L/Lexovisaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hulke1887a, p. 699, Fig. 2: "Pelvis of *Omosaurus durobrivensis*, Hulke, from the Kimmeridge Clay of Northamptonshire."
- hoffstetter1957a, p. 541 (translated): "In the vicinity of Peterborough, and notably at Fletton (immediately to the S.), Leeds collected remains of Stegosaurinae in the Lower Oxford Clay. They were described under the name *Omosaurus durobrivensis* Hulke 1887"; the Peterborough stegosaur remains were "first wrongly attributed by Hulke (1887) to the Kimmeridgian".
- galton1985d, p. 211: "*O. durobrivensis* Hulke, 1887, is based on a partial skeleton from Fletton, Northamptonshire. The horizon was originally given as Kimmeridge Clay, but Woodward and Sherborn (1890) noted that it was Oxford Clay (Callovian, Middle Jurassic)."

**Ruled out.**
- *Kimmeridge Clay.* Hulke's horizon, which Hoffstetter (1957, p. 541) calls wrong and Galton (1985, p. 211) reports corrected by Woodward and Sherborn (1890).
- *Eyebury.* Hulke's title places the collector, "A. Leeds, Esq, of Eyebury, Northamptonshire", not the find; Hoffstetter and Galton both give Fletton.

**Open.** The English Wikipedia article on *Lexovisaurus* puts the find at a brick pit at Tanholt, near Eye, and says Fletton was later assumed in error; no paper cited here says so. E. T. Leeds's 1956 catalog of the Leeds collection, cited by Galton (1985), has not been read and may record where the type was dug.

## Is the *Lexovisaurus* holotype Callovian or Oxfordian?

*2026-08-05*

**Conclusion.** Callovian: the Lower Oxford Clay at Fletton. Governs `period.stage: [Callovian]` on [*Lexovisaurus durobrivensis*](../../../genera/L/Lexovisaurus.yml), with the same reading for [*Loricatosaurus priscus*](../../../genera/L/Loricatosaurus.yml) from the same pits.

**Evidence.**
- galton1985d, p. 211: the horizon "was originally given as Kimmeridge Clay, but Woodward and Sherborn (1890) noted that it was Oxford Clay (Callovian, Middle Jurassic)"; the abstract places *Lexovisaurus* in "the Lower Oxford Clay (middle Callovian, Middle Jurassic)".
- maidment2008a, p. 383: BMNH R1989–1992 "was recovered from the Lower Oxford Clay (Callovian) of Fletton, near Peterborough".
- jones2026a, Stegosauria table: "Oxford Clay Fm., United Kingdom *Middle Jurassic, Oxfordian*".

**Ruled out.**
- *Oxfordian.* Jones (2026) gives the stage the formation's name suggests. The Lower Oxford Clay is Callovian in both papers above.

**Open.** Nothing.

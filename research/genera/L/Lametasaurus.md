# *Lametasaurus*

## What does the name *Lametasaurus* cover, and where does it sit?

*2026-10-16*

**Conclusion.** Dinosauria incertae sedis, as a nomen dubium whose application is disputed. Walker (1964) restricted the name to the scutes; later work reads the sacrum, ilia and tibia as abelisaurid, and Carrano and Sampson (2008) treat *Lametasaurus* as the senior name for that theropod. The scutes are read as nodosaurian in older work and as probably titanosaurian since. With elements read as ornithischian, sauropod and theropod, Dinosauria is the level all share. Governs `parent: Dinosauria` and the dispute on [*Lametasaurus*](../../../genera/L/Lametasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- walker1964a: "the endoskeletal elements included by Matley (1923) in the type material of the Indian nodosaur *Lametasaurus* are held to belong to one or other of the two carnosaurs *Indosuchus* and *Indosaurus*, but the numerous scutes are not thought to be carnosaurian and the name *Lametasaurus* is here restricted to them."
- wilson2003a: "The ilia and sacrum described as *Lametasaurus* by Matley (1923) from Bara Simla (now lost) match the holotype of *Rajasaurus*".
- carrano2008a: "*Indosaurus, Lametasaurus* and *Rajasaurus* - are the most likely candidates for synonymy (in which case the name of priority would be *Lametasaurus*)"; "The scutes are probably titanosaurian in origin".
- d'emic2009a: "Lametasaurus indicus has been regarded as a nomen dubium both as an ankylosaur … and as a theropod".
- novas2010b: "*Lametasaurus indicus, Indosuchus raptorius*, and *Indosaurus matleyi* must be considered as nomina dubia."

**Ruled out.**
- *"Recent work identifies ankylosaurian synapomorphies in some osteoderms."* The earlier summary's claim; not found in any paper read. The ankylosaur reading is historical.

**Open.** Whether the type series should be treated as a holotype, as Carrano and Sampson (2008) and Mohabey (2013) call it, rather than syntypes.

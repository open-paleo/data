# *Lambeosaurus*

## Is *Lambeosaurus clavinitialis* a settled junior synonym of *L. lambei*?

*2026-10-29*

**Conclusion.** No; it stays unadded until the question settles. Dodson (1975) made it the female of *L. lambei*, and Evans and Reisz (2007), the specialist revision held, kept the lump only "in lieu of further study" while saying the two may be distinct. Since then the papers held split: Bell (2012, 2014) follow the lump, while Evans's group and others use *L. clavinitialis* as a separate species, Mallon and colleagues (2012) building a biostratigraphic zone on its range. None of the held papers argues the separation; the one said to, Brink and colleagues (2014), has not been read. Governs the absence of an *L. clavinitialis* species block and synonym on [*Lambeosaurus*](../../../genera/L/Lambeosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dodson1975a, p. 51: "L. lambei would obviously be the male … and L. clavinitialis … would represent the female."
- evans2007a, p. 387: the two "may represent distinct, closely related taxa", and *L. clavinitialis* "is on a distinct growth trajectory"; the lump is kept "in lieu of further study".
- bell2014a, p. 582: "*Lambeosaurus lambei* Parks, 1923 In CMN 8703 (= *L.* "*clavinitialis*")".
- mallon2012a, p. 133: one zone's "boundaries mirror the distribution of L. clavinitialis"; the paper follows the taxonomy of Eberth and Evans (2011).

**Ruled out.**
- *L. clavinitialis as a settled junior synonym.* The specialist revision left it open, and later use is split.

**Open.** Brink and colleagues (2014), on crest measurements, and Eberth and Evans (2011) have not been read.

## Is the *Lambeosaurus lambei* holotype from the Dinosaur Park Formation, when its describing paper gives the Belly River?

*2026-08-28*

**Conclusion.** The Dinosaur Park Formation. Gilmore (1924) gives the type locality as a bearing from the mouth of Little Sandhill Creek, "100 feet below contact with the Pierre", and the horizon as the Belly River formation, the name of its time for the whole succession along the Red Deer River. Evans and Reisz (2007) state that all unequivocal *Lambeosaurus* material comes from the Dinosaur Park Formation, and place a *Lambeosaurus lambei* zone in the middle of the formation at Dinosaur Provincial Park. Governs `location.formation: Dinosaur Park` and `location.locality` on [*Lambeosaurus lambei*](../../../genera/L/Lambeosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- gilmore1924b, p. 35: "*Type:* No. 2869, Geol. Surv., Can., consists of a fairly complete skull and right ramus"; "*Locality:* 4 miles southwest of the mouth of Little Sandhill creek, Red Deer river, Alberta, 100 feet below contact with the Pierre"; "*Horizon:* All three specimens from the Belly River formation, Upper Cretaceous."
- evans2007a, p. 373: "All unequivocal *Lambeosaurus* material has been collected from the Dinosaur Park Formation (DPF) of southern Alberta"; pp. 385–386: within "the 60–80 meter thick DPF", a lower association "is succeeded by a *Styracosaurus-Lambeosaurus lambei* association in the next 25 meters of the section".

**Ruled out.**
- *Oldman Formation.* Before the Dinosaur Park Formation was separated from the Oldman, "Belly River" covered both, so Gilmore's horizon is a name of its time and not a rival reading (see [Oldman and Dinosaur Park](../../topics/oldman-and-dinosaur-park.md)); Evans and Reisz place the genus in the Dinosaur Park Formation. Gilmore's "100 feet below contact with the Pierre", taking his Pierre as the marine shale that caps the formation, puts the skull about 30 m below the top of a 60–80 m Dinosaur Park section. That reading of Gilmore's datum and the arithmetic are ours, not either paper's.

**Open.** No paper cited here gives a quarry number for CMN 2869. "Quarry 45" appears in none of them; Sternberg Map Quarry No. 46 is the *L. magnicristatus* holotype's (evans2007a, p. 374). Currie and Russell (2005), which Evans and Reisz cite for the park's quarry numbering, has not been read and may give one.

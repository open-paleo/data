# *Loricosaurus*

## Where does *Loricosaurus* sit, and is it a synonym of *Neuquensaurus*?

*2026-10-16*

**Conclusion.** A nomen dubium at Titanosauria incertae sedis. The plates are titanosaur osteoderms, which every recent source accepts. The saltasaurine placement rested on the synonymy with *Neuquensaurus australis*, which compilations list, two of them with a query, but which no paper read argues from the specimens; keeled titanosaur osteoderms also occur outside Saltasaurinae. The synonymy is not adopted, under the policy of adopting taxonomic changes only on broad consensus. Governs `parent: Titanosauria` and the dispute on [*Loricosaurus*](../../../genera/L/Loricosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huene1929a: "As bearers of such cutaneous ossifications, only acanthophilids (ankylosaurs) and ceratopsids, or rather thyreophorans (Nopesa) can come into question" (translated).
- d'emic2009a: "Keeled osteoderms include a combination of Types 1 and 2 of Huene (1929; Table) and have been found in Argentina and Madagascar."
- olshevsky1991a: the species "could well be a junior synonym of *Neuquensaurus australis*".
- upchurch2004a lists "?Loricosaurus scutatus" under *N. australis*; santucci2022a lists "*Loricosaurus scutatus* Huene 1927" under it without query; molina-pérez2020a counts it a formal synonym.

**Ruled out.**
- *Saltasaurinae.* The earlier parent; it rests only on the unargued synonymy.
- *A junior synonym of Neuquensaurus australis.* Listed by compilations, not argued from the material.

**Open.** A comparison of the plates with *Neuquensaurus* osteoderms would settle the synonymy.

## Which numbers make up the *Loricosaurus* syntype series?

*2026-10-11*

**Conclusion.** The twenty-six plates Huene (1929) lists by number. Three of the five numbers the record also carried, C.S. 1222 to 1224, are foot phalanges Huene describes elsewhere in the same paper; C.S. 1225 appears in no source read, and C.S. 1227 only in a later figure caption that credits it to Huene. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id` (twenty-six ids) on [*Loricosaurus scutatus*](../../../genera/L/Loricosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huene1929a, *Loricosaurus scutatus*: "Twenty-six cutaneous ossifications and skin plates have been found at Cinco Saltos. They are the following numbers", then 1210, 1213–1215, 1218–1221, 1226, 1228–1232, 1235, 1237, 1470–1477, 2006 and 2010 (translated).
- huene1929a, on the titanosaur foot: "there are only three phalanges of the middle digits, C. S. 1222 (plate 12, 14), 1223 (plate 12, 13), 1224 (plate 12, 15)" (translated).
- otero2010a: "Some pedal phalanges (MLP−CS 1202, 1204, 1206, 1222, 1223, 1224) were erroneously drawn as belonging to the manus by Huene (1929: pl. 12: 13–15)".
- d'emic2009a, Figure 7 caption: "V, C.S. 1227 (Huene, 1929)", among the osteoderms.

**Ruled out.**
- *C.S. 1222, 1223 and 1224.* They are limb bones, not armor.
- *C.S. 1225.* No source read gives it.
- *C.S. 1227.* The designating paper does not list it.

**Open.** Which of Huene's plates D'Emic and colleagues figure as C.S. 1227.

## Are the Cinco Saltos syntypes from the Anacleto or the Allen Formation?

*2026-08-29*

**Conclusion.** The Anacleto Formation. Huene (1929) collected the syntypes at Cinco Saltos and names no formation. Later work places the Cinco Saltos dinosaur beds in the Anacleto and expressly not in the Allen. Governs `location.formation: Anacleto` on [*Loricosaurus scutatus*](../../../genera/L/Loricosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huene1929a, *Loricosaurus scutatus* (translated): "Twenty-six cutaneous ossifications and skin plates have been found at Cinco Saltos".
- leanza2004a, p. 80: "Contrary to the view of Powell (1986), the tetrapod taxa found around Cinco Saltos and the Lago Pellegrini area belong to the Anacleto Formation and not to the Allen Formation (see Heredia and Salgado, 1999)".
- santucci2022a, p. 371: "Those 'Dinosaur beds,' where remains of *Neuquensaurus* were found, correspond to the Anacleto Formation ('Senonense inferior,' Huene 1929: 11) (Otero 2010)". The paper lists *Loricosaurus scutatus* as a synonym of *N. australis* (p. 369).

**Ruled out.**
- *Allen Formation.* McIntosh (1990) gives "Rio Colorado Formation or Allen Formation, Rio Negro" for *Saltasaurus australis*, which includes "?Loricosaurus scutatus" (mcintosh1990a, Table 16.1). This undecided reading predates the reassignment Leanza and colleagues report, and it is the view they reject for Cinco Saltos. Upchurch and colleagues (2004) give the Rio Colorado Formation alone for *N. australis*, including ?*L. scutatus* (upchurch2004a, Table 13.1). Their "or Allen Formation" belongs to *S. robustus*.

**Open.** Nothing. The Lago Pellegrini quarries, named in the same sentence by Leanza and colleagues, are a separate dispute; see [Lago Pellegrini quarries](../../topics/lago-pellegrini-quarries.md).

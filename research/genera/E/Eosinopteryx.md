# *Eosinopteryx*

## On what does the Tiaojishan Formation of the *Eosinopteryx* holotype rest?

*2026-08-22*

**Conclusion.** On a dealer's locality and the look of the slab, not on collection in the field. The specimen was bought from a fossil dealer who gave Yaolugou, Jianchang County, as its source. The Tiaojishan is widely exposed there, and Godefroit and colleagues (2013) liken the slab to Tiaojishan specimens of *Anchiornis* and *Xiaotingia*, but they also say that Tiaojishan and Yixian slabs are hard to tell apart by eye. Governs `location.formation: Tiaojishan` and `location.locality: Yaolugou` on [*Eosinopteryx brevipenna*](../../../genera/E/Eosinopteryx.yml).

**Evidence.**
- godefroit2013a, p. 2, Systematic palaeontology: "Locality and horizon. Yaolugou, Jianchang, western Liaoning, China; Middle-Late Jurassic Tiaojishan Formation".
- godefroit2013a, p. 6, Methods, Origin and authenticity of the specimen: the holotype "was acquired by the Yizhou Fossil & Geology Park from a Chinese fossil dealer, according to whom the specimen was collected at the Yaolugou locality"; "The Tiaojishan formation is widely exposed in this area, though it also has limited outcrops of the Early Cretaceous Yixian Formation"; "it is in fact extremely difficult to distinguish between Tiaojishan and Yixian shale slabs on the basis of macro-sedimentary features."

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Whether the slab is Tiaojishan or Yixian. Godefroit and colleagues name the test, "the micro-sedimentary structures and the pollen assemblages within the shale slabs", but report no such study for this specimen. One would settle it.

# *Europasaurus*

## Which bed at Langenberg is the Europasaurus material from?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Bed 83 of the Langenberg quarry section, in the [Süntel Formation](../../../stratigraphy/s/süntel.yml), Kimmeridgian. Governs `location.bed: 83` and `location.formation: Süntel` on *Europasaurus holgeri*.

**Evidence.**
- evers2020a, p. 2, Locality, geology and stratigraphy: the remains "were found in bed 83, not in bed 93 as erroneously stated in some publications (Carballido & Sander, 2013; Marpmann et al., 2014; Sander et al., 2006)"; the age is "'Mittleres Kimmeridge', a northwest-German equivalent to the lower part of the upper Kimmeridgian".
- zuo2017a, p. 9: the Langenberg beds "correspond to the Middle Süntel Formation"; in its Fig. 5 (p. 7) bed 83 falls in the Middle Süntel (our reading of the figure).

**Ruled out.**
- *Bed 93.* Sander and colleagues (2006) give "bed 93 of section at Langenberg quarry" (sander2006a, p. 739), repeated by Carballido and Sander (2013, carballido2013a, p. 339). Evers and Wings (2020) correct it.
- *A Langenberg Member.* No source names one; the bed numbers belong to a quarry section (running from 24 to 153 in Zuo and colleagues' Fig. 5).

**Open.** The blocks were "not found *in situ*", and Evers and Wings (2020, p. 3) consider another bed "possible, although very unlikely".

## Do Zuo and colleagues' Langenberg coordinates, or "Rohstoffbetriebe Oker", locate the *Europasaurus* holotype?

*2026-08-23*

**Conclusion.** Neither is recorded. Zuo and colleagues (2018) print a position for their logged Langenberg section, not for DFMMh/FV 291, and the record holds no coordinate. Rohstoffbetriebe Oker is the company that works the Langenberg Quarry, not the site. Governs `location.locality: Langenberg Quarry` and the empty `location.coordinates` on [*Europasaurus holgeri*](../../../genera/E/Europasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- zuo2017a, p. 3, Materials and methods: "The Langenberg section (51°54′6.74″N, 10°30′27.73″E) is accessible in an active quarry about 5 km east of Goslar"; p. 5, Fig. 3a caption: the section view marks "strata where the dinosaur (*Europasaurus holgeri*) was discovered (yellow star)".
- evers2020a, p. 19, Acknowledgements: thanks to the family "at the Rohstoffbetriebe Oker GmbH & Co. KG for the permission to access the Langenberg Quarry".

**Ruled out.**
- *51.901872, 10.507703 (Zuo and colleagues' pair, our conversion) as the holotype's position.* The pair is the reference point of the logged section. The figure ties the *Europasaurus* strata to the section, not to the point.
- *"Rohstoffbetriebe Oker Quarry" as the locality.* The name is the operating company's.

**Open.** Whether the section's reference point is close enough to the excavation to stand as the quarry's position. Wings and Sander (2012), on the Langenberg vertebrate assemblage, has not been read and may give the site's own position.

## Is the *Europasaurus holgeri* holotype one number or a series?

*2026-07-30*

**Conclusion.** One number, DFMMh/FV 291, which covers the disarticulated skull and vertebral elements of one individual. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [DFMMh/FV 291]` on [*Europasaurus holgeri*](../../../genera/E/Europasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sander2006a, systematic section: "Holotype. DFMMh/FV 291: disarticulated left premaxilla; right maxilla; right quadratojugal; … cervical and sacral vertebrae; and cervical and dorsal ribs of one individual".

**Ruled out.**
- *A "DFMMh/FV 291 series".* Jones (2026) prints "DFMMh/FV 291 series—partial skull and skeleton (Sander et al. 2006)" (jones2026b, p. 379). The paper it cites gives a single catalogue number for the many elements, so no series of numbers stands behind the phrase.

**Open.** Nothing.

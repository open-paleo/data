# *Emausaurus*

## Which unit holds the *Emausaurus* holotype?

*2026-08-22*

**Conclusion.** The Lehmhagen Member of the Posidonia Shale Formation. Ansorge and colleagues (2025) erected the member for the *semicelatum* Subzone interval at the Grimmen clay pit, with its type section in the pit, and name *Emausaurus* among the fossils of its subunit 6c. Governs `location.formation: Posidonia Shale` and `location.member: Lehmhagen` on [*Emausaurus ernsti*](../../../genera/E/Emausaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ansorge2025a, p. 270: Haubold (1990) "published on the skull and some postcranial bones of the ornithischian dinosaur Emausaurus ernsti, which originate from a siemensi concretion … of the semicelatum Subzone."
- ansorge2025a, p. 273: subunit 6c holds concretions with "vertebrate remains (e.g., fish debris, dinosaur Emausaurus ernsti, and ichthyosaur vertebrae)"; p. 275, Fig. 7 caption: "Grimmen clay pit, units 2–7, Posidonia Shale Formation, Lehmhagen Member, lower Toarcian, semicelatum Subzone".
- ansorge2025a, p. 280: "The Lehmhagen Member, named after the village Klein Lehmhagen (Fig. 3), represents the intercalation of typical organo-detrital shales of the Posidonia Shale with coastal heterolithes"; "The type section, located in the northern part of the Grimmen clay pit".

**Ruled out.**
- *"Lehmhagen Member" as the village name with a rank word added.* Read before 2025 the phrase looks like that: Haubold (1990) names only the clay pit near Grimmen and the village east of it, and no unit. Ansorge and colleagues (2025) erect the member formally.
- *The Grimmen Formation.* The pit and the town name the formation, but its base lies higher in the same section: "unit 9, basal Grimmen Formation" (ansorge2025a, p. 280, Fig. 10 caption), above "the brown organic-rich elegantulum clay of the Posidonia Shale Formation" that tops the Lehmhagen Member (p. 281). The *Emausaurus* concretions are in subunit 6c.
- *The Ciechocinek Formation.* Jones (2026) gives "Ciechocinek Fm. (?)", with its own query and no source (jones2026a, Thyreophora specimen table). Ansorge and colleagues map the Ciechocinek as the unit the Grimmen Formation interfingers with "in the east" (ansorge2025a, p. 263, Fig. 2 caption), not at Grimmen.

**Open.** Nothing.

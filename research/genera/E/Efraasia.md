# *Efraasia*

## Which work named *Efraasia minor*?

*2026-11-12*

**Conclusion.** Huene's (1908) monograph on the European Triassic dinosaurs, as *Teratosaurus minor*, with the genus queried. Yates (2003) combined it with *Efraasia*, which Galton (1973) had erected. Governs `erected_in: huene1908a` and `erected_as: Teratosaurus minor` on *E. minor*, on [*Efraasia*](../../../genera/E/Efraasia.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huene1908a, p. 174: the heading "(?) Teratosaurus minor n. sp.", and "die Rückenwirbel weichen von den von Sellosaurus bekannten so sehr ab, daß ich die Art zu (?) Teratosaurus stelle" ("the dorsal vertebrae differ so much from those known of *Sellosaurus* that I place the species in (?) *Teratosaurus*", translated).
- yates2003b: "*Efraasia minor* (Huene, 1908) comb. nov.".

**Ruled out.**
- *Yates (2003) as the species' authority.* The combination is new there, not the species.
- *The query as part of the name.* "(?)" qualifies the generic placement; the binomen is *Teratosaurus minor*, as the figure captions on pp. 175–177 print it.

**Open.** The issue date of p. 174. The title page gives "1907–1908", and the work was issued in six parts; p. 174 is in the third, whose date the volume does not print.

## Is the *Efraasia* holotype from "Burrer Quarry"?

*2026-08-22*

**Conclusion.** No source gives that name for it. Yates (2003) and Sues and Schoch (2025) give the type locality of SMNS 11838 as the Weisser Steinbruch near Pfaffenhofen. Galton (1973) calls the Pfaffenhofen quarry "Burrer'schen Quarry", but for the *Efraasia diagnostica* material, not for SMNS 11838. Governs `location.locality: Weisser Steinbruch, Pfaffenhofen` on [*Efraasia minor*](../../../genera/E/Efraasia.yml).

**Evidence.**
- yates2003b, p. 329: "Holotype. SMNS 11838"; "Type locality. Weisser Steinbruch, Pfaffenhofen, Stromberg region, Germany (Hungerbühler 1998)"; the referred specimens include "12667 (holotype of Palaeosaurus diagnosticus Huene)", and "All specimens are from the type locality except SMNS 17928".
- sues2025a, p. 461: "Holotype. SMNS 11838"; "Type locality. "Weisser Steinbruch" near Pfaffenhofen, Baden-Württemberg"; the referred specimens, including SMNS 12667, are all "from the type locality except SMNS 17928".
- galton1973a, p. 229: the *E. diagnostica* skeletons "were excavated by Professor E. Fraas at the Burrerschen Quarry near Pfaffenhofen"; p. 247: "Locality and horizon: Burrer'schen Quarry near Pfaffenhofen, Nordwürttemberg".
- sues2025a, p. 422, under *Cyamodus rostratus*: "SMNS 17403, incomplete cranium from Burrer Quarry, Gaismühle near Crailsheim, Baden-Württemberg".

**Ruled out.**
- *"Burrer Quarry, Pfaffenhofen".* No source read prints this form. Galton's name is the German genitive, "Burrer'schen", and it is given for the *E. diagnostica* skeletons. The English "Burrer Quarry" in Sues and Schoch (2025) is a different site, at Gaismühle near Crailsheim, and belongs to a placodont.

**Open.** Whether "Burrer'schen Quarry" is another name for the Weisser Steinbruch or a neighboring quarry at Pfaffenhofen. Galton does not use the name Weisser Steinbruch. Yates and Sues and Schoch place SMNS 12667 at the same type locality as SMNS 11838, which makes the two names one quarry, but that is our inference from their wording. Hungerbühler (1998), whom Yates cites for the locality, has not been read.

## Is the *Efraasia* holotype from the Löwenstein Formation or a "Stubensandstein Formation"?

*2026-08-03*

**Conclusion.** The Löwenstein Formation. Stubensandstein is the older name for the same rock, and Sues and Schoch (2025) keep it only for the informal subdivisions S1 to S4 within the Löwenstein. Governs `location.formation: Löwenstein` on [*Efraasia minor*](../../../genera/E/Efraasia.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sues2025a, p. 461: "Type horizon. Middle Stubensandstein (S2), Löwenstein Formation (equivalent of Arnstadt Formation in the basin), Middle Keuper Subgroup".
- yates2003b, p. 317: "the Löwenstein Formation (formerly the Stubensandstein; Bachman *et al*. 1999)".
- sues2025a, p. 469: "the Löwenstein (Stubensandstein) and Trossingen (Knollenmergel) formations".

**Ruled out.**
- *Stubensandstein Formation.* Jones (2026) gives "Stubensandstein Fm., Germany" (jones2026b, p. 372). That is the superseded name with a formation rank attached, not a different unit.

**Open.** Nothing.

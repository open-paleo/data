# *Euoplocephalus*

## At what level of the Dinosaur Park Formation was the *Euoplocephalus* holotype found?

*2026-08-23*

**Conclusion.** The level is not known, so no `part` is recorded. Lambe collected CMN 210 in 1897, and its locality comes from his field notes as Arbour and Currie (2013) report them: the east side of the Red Deer River near the mouth of Berry Creek. The quarry was never mapped. Lambe's 1902 description gives the holotype only "Belly River series, Red Deer river, 1897"; the Berry Creek line on the same page belongs to a tooth collected in 1901 and only provisionally associated with the species. Governs `location.locality: near mouth of Berry Creek`, `location.formation: Dinosaur Park` and the empty `location.part` on [*Euoplocephalus tutus*](../../../genera/E/Euoplocephalus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lambe1902a, p. 57: "Belly River series, Red Deer river, 1897."; the tooth "was collected below Berry creek, on Red Deer river, in 1901".
- vickaryous2002a, p. 159, Type locality: "Dinosaur Park Formation (formerly the Oldman Formation), East side of mouth of Berry Creek at the Red Deer River"; "The actual quarry site was never formally placed onto a map, nor were any specific co-ordinates given, and consequently the exact locality is unclear."
- arbour2013a, p. 21, Stratigraphic Distribution of Ankylosaurid Specimens from Alberta and Montana: "The exact locality for the holotype of Euoplocephalus tutus (CMN 0210) is unknown. Field notes by L. Lambe (18 August 1897; CMN) state that it was collected from the east side of the Red Deer River near the mouth of Berry Creek".
- penkalski2018a, p. 265: "Horizon: Lower middle Dinosaur Park Formation."

**Ruled out.**
- *A lower or middle `part` from Penkalski's horizon line.* Penkalski (2018) gives "Lower middle Dinosaur Park Formation" without stating its basis, while the two papers that discuss the type locality say the quarry was never mapped and its exact place is unknown. A level within the formation needs a located quarry or an elevation.

**Open.** Where in the formation the quarry lay. Lambe's 1897 field notes at the Canadian Museum of Nature, or a relocation of the quarry, would settle it.

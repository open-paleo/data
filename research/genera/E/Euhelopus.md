# *Euhelopus*

## Where was the *Euhelopus* holotype collected, and is it from Yantai County?

*2026-08-23*

**Conclusion.** In Mengyin County, about 1 km west of the village of Ningchiakou. Zdansky's label, as Wiman (1929) gives it, reads "Meng-Yin-Hsien, NW 40 li, Ning Chia Kou, W 2 li": 40 li northwest of Mengyin and 2 li west of Ningchiakou, which Wilson and Upchurch (2009) convert to about 22 km and 1 km. The record's locality names the village, the nearer of the label's two reference points. Governs `location.locality: Ningchiakou` and `location.region: CN-SD` on [*Euhelopus zdanskyi*](../../../genera/E/Euhelopus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- wiman1929a, p. 7 (translated; the page as Wilson and Upchurch cite it): "The specimen a is labeled: Zdansky, April 1923, Shantung, Meng-Yin-Hsien, NW 40 li, Ning Chia Kou, W 2 li."
- wilson2009a, p. 201: Zdansky collected exemplar a "40 *li* northwest of Mengyin and 2 *li* west of 'Ning Chia Kou' (now Ningchiakou) in Yantai County of Shandong Province"; "exemplar a was collected approximately 22 km northeast of Mengyin and approximately 1 km west of Ningchiakou".

**Ruled out.**
- *Yantai County.* Wilson and Upchurch place Ningchiakou "in Yantai County of Shandong Province, which juts into the Bohai and Yellow Seas", but the label puts the site in Meng-Yin-Hsien, Mengyin County, and their own conversion puts it about 22 km from Mengyin.
- *Northeast of Mengyin.* Wilson and Upchurch's "approximately 22 km northeast" contradicts the label's "NW 40 li", which their preceding sentence repeats as northwest.
- *Exemplar b's site.* Specimen b is labeled "Meng-Yin-Hsien, NW 39 li, Ning-Chia-Tung-Kou SE 1 li", "~2-3 km from the location of specimen a" (wiman1929a, p. 7, translated). It is the referred skeleton, not the holotype.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the *Euhelopus* holotype Late Jurassic or earliest Cretaceous?

*2026-08-05*

**Conclusion.** Berriasian–Valanginian, the age of the Mengyin Formation between its youngest detrital zircons and the overlying Qingshan volcanics (see [Mengyin](../../stratigraphy/m/mengyin.md)). No source read dates the type horizon more closely than the formation. Governs `period.stage: [Berriasian, Valanginian]` on [*Euhelopus zdanskyi*](../../../genera/E/Euhelopus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- xu2015b, in-press version, p. 3: "the more precise depositional age range of the Mengyin Formation could be constrained to 145–136 Ma".
- xu2015b, in-press version, p. 7: the four youngest zircons "give a weighted mean 206Pb/238U age of 144.6 ± 4.1 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *Kimmeridgian or early Tithonian.* Upchurch and colleagues (2004) give "Meng-Yin Formation … Kimmeridgian, or early Tithonian" (upchurch2004a, p. 262, Table 13.1). Wilson and Upchurch (2009) trace that age to correlations of the dinosaur fauna and conchostracans and report "a growing consensus that the Mengyin Formation is Early Cretaceous, rather than Late Jurassic" (wilson2009a, p. 203).
- *Barremian–Aptian.* Wilson and Upchurch "ascribe an age range of Barremian-Aptian (ca. 130-112 Ma)" on faunal correlations (wilson2009a, p. 203), which the zircon bracket postdates and excludes.
- *Down to 130.7 Ma.* Moore and colleagues (2020) attribute this figure to Xu and Li (2015), whose paper does not give it (see [Mengyin](../../stratigraphy/m/mengyin.md)).

**Open.** Nothing.

## What is the *Euhelopus zdanskyi* holotype's number?

*2026-08-01*

**Conclusion.** PMU 24705, the current Uppsala number of exemplar a, formerly PMU R 233. Wilson and Upchurch (2009) also count exemplar c, Young's 1935 vertebrae, as part of the holotype; its accession number and whereabouts are unknown, so it has no id in the record. Governs `type_specimen.specimen_id: [PMU 24705]`, its `former_ids` entry and `type_specimen.notes` on [*Euhelopus zdanskyi*](../../../genera/E/Euhelopus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- poropat2013a, p. 3, Historical Background: "The holotype specimen of *Euhelopus zdanskyi*, which includes all of the known skull material, was formerly catalogued as PMU R 233 but is now catalogued as PMU 24705".
- wilson2009a, p. 205: "HOLOTYPE. PMU 233 (exemplar a) and exemplar c (the latter's accession number and whereabouts are unknown".
- wilson2009a, p. 201: "The partial skeleton of exemplar b (PMU 234) was collected approximately 2-3 km from exemplar a".

**Ruled out.**
- *PMU R234.* Molina-Pérez and Larramendi (2020) write "The holotype PMU R234 is the most complete euhelopodid" (molina-pérez2020a, List of Sauropods, Euhelopodidae). PMU 234 is exemplar b, the referred skeleton.

**Open.** Nothing.

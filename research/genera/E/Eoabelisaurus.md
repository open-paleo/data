# *Eoabelisaurus*

## Where does *Eoabelisaurus* sit within Ceratosauria?

*2026-10-09*

**Conclusion.** Ceratosauria incertae sedis. Independent analyses place it inside Abelisauridae, at the base of Abelisauroidea, just outside Abelisauroidea, and inside or next to Ceratosauridae, and the debate is live in both directions as of 2024. In this tree Ceratosauria's children are Ceratosauridae and Abelisauroidea, so Ceratosauria is the least inclusive clade common to every reading. Supersedes the placement at Abelisauridae recorded in the record's dispute history on 2026-06-30. Governs `parent`, `placement` and `dispute` on [*Eoabelisaurus*](../../../genera/E/Eoabelisaurus.yml), and the basal-grade sentence of the [Abelisauridae](../../../clades/Abelisauridae.yml) description.

**Evidence.** Grouped by the data matrix each analysis builds on; a reused matrix is one line of evidence.
- *Pol and Rauhut (2012) lineage.*
  - pol2012a, Fig. 2 and its text: "The analysis places Eoabelisaurus as the most basal member of the Abelisauridae".
  - zaher2020a, p. 111: "Eoabelisaurus is retrieved as the most basal member of this clade".
  - pol2024a, p. 341: "several terminal taxa that form the basal branches of Abelisauridae (Eoabelisaurus, Rugops, Spectrovenator)"; and of the Eoabelisaurus plus Xenotarsosaurus pair, "this relationship is very weakly supported (Bayesian PP = 0.57 and parsimony jackknife = 23%) but in the Bayesian analysis it is placed outside rather than within Abelisauroidea".
  - farke2013a, pp. 15–16, on modified versions of the same two matrices: "the taxon is unambiguously recovered as a non-abelisauroid ceratosaur, sister to a node-based Abelisauroidea"; "An additional two steps were required to force Eoabelisaurus as an abelisaurid".
- *Tortosa and colleagues (2014) lineage.*
  - tortosa2014a, p. 70: "Among Abelisauroidea, Berberosaurus and Eoabelisaurus are found as the basalmost members".
  - filippi2016a, p. 215: "Eoabelisaurus resulted as the basalmost abelisauroid".
  - delcourt2017a, p. 15, Fig. 12: the strict consensus shows Eoabelisaurus as the first branch of the node labeled Abelisauroidea. The text does not name it.
  - cerroni2020a, Fig. 6: the strict consensus shows Eoabelisaurus as the sister taxon of the node labeled Abelisauroidea. The text does not name it.
  - aranciagarolando2020a, Discussion, "Phylogenetic position of Niebla antiqua": "Eoabelisaurus as the sister taxa of the clade comprising Ligabueino + Abelisauroidea".
- *Wang and colleagues (2016).* wang2016b, p. 147, Fig. 4A: Eoabelisaurus is the basalmost member of the node colored as Ceratosauridae, which the figure nests within the node colored as Abelisauridae. In Fig. 4B (juvenile scorings) it falls in Abelisauridae outside Ceratosauridae. The text does not name it.
- *Hendrickx and colleagues (2024).*
  - hendrickx2024a, p. 27, revised Agnolín dataset: "a few basal taxa in a ladder-like position, namely Berberosaurus, Eoabelisaurus, and Ligabueino, the latter being the sister-taxon of the clade Berthasauridae + Abelisauroidea".
  - hendrickx2024a, p. 30, revised Rauhut and Pol dataset: "Etrigansauria (sensu Delcourt 2018), a clade made of Eoabelisaurus and Neoceratosauria (= Ceratosauridae + Abelisauridae; Fig. 16B)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Keep it at Abelisauridae.* Every analysis recovering it there descends from the Pol and Rauhut (2012) matrix, and the newest of them puts it outside Abelisauroidea in its Bayesian run. Farke and Sertich (2013), working from the same matrices, exclude it at two extra steps. Two other lineages and both Hendrickx runs place it outside the family.
- *Abelisauroidea incertae sedis.* Farke and Sertich (2013), Cerroni and colleagues (2020), Aranciaga Rolando and colleagues (2020), and both Hendrickx runs place it outside Abelisauroidea.
- *Ceratosauridae.* Only Wang and colleagues (2016) recover it there. Their tree nests Ceratosauridae inside Abelisauridae, an arrangement none of the other analyses recovers.
- *Citing Novas and colleagues (2013) or Delcourt (2018) for a position.* Both are reviews and run no analysis of their own. novas2013a relays Pol and Rauhut (2012) and questions some of its synapomorphies. delcourt2018c attributes the Ceratosauridae position to Wang and colleagues, which holds for their Fig. 4A only.
- *An "Aranciaga Rolando and colleagues (2021)" analysis.* Hendrickx and colleagues (2025) cite one (hendrickx2025a, p. 438: "an early branching neoceratosaur (Aranciaga Rolando et al., 2021; Hendrickx et al., 2024)"), but their reference list has no entry for it. It is aranciagarolando2020a, released online in 2020 and printed in the January 2021 volume, not a separate paper.

**Open.** Two analyses that Hendrickx and colleagues (2025) cite were not read: an *Aucasaurus* paper by Baiano and colleagues (2023), cited for the abelisaurid reading, and a Cañadón Calcáreo paper by Rauhut and Pol (2021), cited for the non-abelisaurid abelisauroid reading. Neither can move the placement, because the readings already on record span Ceratosauria. The tip-dating tree of Pol and colleagues (2024, Fig. 19a) and the supplementary trees of Wang and colleagues (2016) were also not read.

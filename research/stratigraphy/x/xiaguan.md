# Xiaguan

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Xiaguan?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Turonian to Campanian. D. Y. Wang and colleagues (2013), who formally establish the formation, place the egg-bearing red beds of the Xiaguan-Gaoqiu Basin, in Neixiang and Zhenping Counties of southwestern Henan, in it and date it as middle to late Turonian to middle Campanian on ostracods, bivalves, gastropods, charophytes and plant megafossils from a section measured in 2011, the first fossils other than dinosaurs and eggs found that bear on its age. Their English abstract hedges ("may be" the early to middle Late Cretaceous, "most possibly" Turonian to Campanian), but the Chinese abstract and the conclusions state the same stages without the hedge. Governs `stages: [Turonian, Coniacian, Santonian, Campanian]` on [Xiaguan](../../../stratigraphy/x/xiaguan.yml).

**Evidence.**
- wang2013c, p. 1049, Chinese abstract: "应为土伦（Turonian）中—晚期至坎潘（Campanian）中期" ("should be middle to late Turonian to middle Campanian", translated).
- wang2013c, p. 1058, English abstract: the red beds have "been called Gaogou Formation and Majiacun Formation，Sangping Formation，Xiaguan Formation，but no survey has been conducted on theses stratigraphic sections", because no "invertebrate and plant fossils" had been found "to determine stratigraphic epoch except the dinosaur skeleton (*Nanyangosaurus zhugeii*) and dinosaur egg fossils"; the age "may be the early-middle Late Cretaceous，and most possibly to be the middle-late Turonian to the middle Campanian".
- wang2013a, p. 11, Comparison and Discussion: the age of the red beds "was argued to be Early Cretaceous by Xu et al. [7] based on the evolutionary grade of Nanyangosaurus"; "our results and those of another cladistic analysis [4] suggest a more derived position for Nanyangosaurus"; "regional geological workers insist on an early Late Cretaceous age for the Nanyangosaurus-bearing red beds in Xiaguan Basin".
- xu2000a, p. 189, Summary: "The iguanodontian occurrence from the Sangping Formation supports an Early Cretaceous age of Xiaguan redbed. The systematic position of *Nanyangosaurus* suggests that the Sangpin Formation may represent Albian deposits".

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Cretaceous (Albian), from Xu and colleagues (2000).* They dated the red beds, under the name Sangping Formation, from the evolutionary grade of *Nanyangosaurus*, before the formation was defined and with no invertebrate or plant fossils to go on. R. F. Wang and colleagues (2013) recover *Nanyangosaurus* in a more derived position, removing the basis of the inference, and D. Y. Wang and colleagues (2013) date the formation from its own fossils as Turonian to Campanian and reject the Albian and late Early Cretaceous ages by name.

**Open.** No fossils have been found in the formation's lower strata, which yielded *Nanyangosaurus*; dating them would narrow the range.

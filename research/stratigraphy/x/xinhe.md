# Xinhe

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Xinhe?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Bathonian, Middle Jurassic. Li and colleagues (2025) find *Jinchuanloong* in sandstone of the lower part of the formation and date that horizon late Bathonian on the plant megafossils and palynoflora of the lower part and the lacustrine invertebrates of the upper part. Gao and colleagues (2019) give the same evidence and the same age, in nearly the same words, for their plesiosaur horizon near the bottom of the upper part. Governs `stages: [Bathonian]` and `period: [Middle Jurassic]` on [Xinhe](../../../stratigraphy/x/xinhe.yml).

**Evidence.**
- li2025a, p. 1, abstract: a eusauropod "from the Middle Jurassic Xinhe Formation of Gansu Province, northwestern China".
- li2025a, p. 2, Geological setting: "The dinosaur remains were discovered in the sandstone of the lower part of the Xinhe Formation"; "The megafossil plants, palynoflora from the lower part, as well as the lacustrine invertebrate assemblage from the upper part of the Xinhe Formation … indicate that the dinosaur-bearing horizon is late Bathonian in age".
- li2025a, p. 3, Horizon and locality: "lower part of the Xinhe Formation (late Bathonian)".
- gao2019a, p. 2, Geological background: the same assemblages "indicate that the plesiosaur-bearing horizon is of freshwater origin and late Bathonian in age".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Both papers relay the late Bathonian from earlier studies of the formation's fossils, which have not been read. Li and colleagues apply to the dinosaur horizon in the lower part the sentence Gao and colleagues wrote for the plesiosaur horizon in the upper part; the earlier studies would show whether the age was determined for one horizon or for the formation as a whole.

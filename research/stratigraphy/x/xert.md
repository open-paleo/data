# Xert

## Which stage is the lower Xert under each definition of the base of the Aptian?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Corrects a page in the 2026-09-30 entry: the frau2018a wording it quotes ("separating … from the lower Aptian pro parte") is on p. 148; p. 143 has a near-identical sentence with "distinguishing" and "lowermost Aptian". The entry's conclusion stands, and this one sets out the reasoning behind it. Bover-Arnal and colleagues (2016) date the lower Xert by a Martelites ammonite just above the base of the formation, in the lower M. sarasini Subzone, which they take as late Barremian. On the ammonite zonation, which closes the Barremian with the M. sarasini Zone, that level is latest Barremian. GTS2020 keeps the base of Chron M0r as the working definition of the base of the Aptian, and Frau and colleagues (2017) correlate the lower M. sarasini Subzone with the topmost part of CM0r, so on that definition the same level is earliest Aptian. The upper Xert's strontium value of 0.707425, which Bover-Arnal and colleagues read as latest Barremian to earliest Aptian on GTS2004, falls inside the band Frau and colleagues (2018) find cannot separate the two stages. Bover-Arnal and colleagues place the boundary tentatively just above the base of the overlying Forcall Formation. Either way the formation spans the boundary interval, and both stages stand. Their framework is meant to re-date the vertebrate records of the Morella and Xert formations, Canudo and colleagues' (2008) Tastavinsaurus among them. Governs `stages: [Barremian, Aptian]` on [Xert](../../../stratigraphy/x/xert.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bover-arnal2016a, p. 51: "The ammonite was found above the contact between the Morella and Xert formations"; "Martelites sp. belongs to the Martelites sarasini Zone (Late Barremian), particularly to the lower part of the Martelites sarasini Subzone"; the find "allows a precise age calibration of the lower part of the Xert Formation".
- bover-arnal2016a, p. 51: the 87Sr/86Sr value of 0.707425 from the upper Xert "gives an age of 124.94 Ma", "in accordance with a latest Barremian – earliest Aptian age (GTS2004)".
- bover-arnal2016a, p. 41: "The Barremian–Aptian boundary is tentatively placed close above the base of the succeeding transgressive marls of the Forcall Formation".
- bover-arnal2016a, p. 43: the framework allows "to establish the age of the dinosaur and other vertebrate records found in the Morella and Xert formations", citing Canudo and colleagues (2008) among them.
- frau2017a, p. 311: the events at Gorgo a Cerbara "below, at, and above this magnetochron fall within the Martelites sarasini ammonite Zone"; "the use of the base of magnetozone CM0r as a datum to define the Barremian/Aptian boundary is questioned".
- frau2017a, p. 317: the re-identified ammonites "pinpoint the lower part of the M. sarasini Zone (M. sarasini Subzone)".
- frau2018a, p. 148: "the group of values comprised between 0.707435 and 0.707410 represents a "grey band" in which SIS is unreliable for separating the uppermost Barremian (M. sarasini Zone) from the lower Aptian pro parte".
- frau2018a, p. 139: "Sr-isotope values can neither be used to prove nor to disprove the location of the putative Barremian/Aptian boundary based on biostratigraphy".
- gale2020a, p. 1031: "The GTS2020 scale retains the current Aptian/Barremian usage with its working boundary definition as the base of Chron M0r"; the ammonites "beneath, within, and above magnetochron M0r" have been reidentified "as belonging to the Martelites sarasini Zone, traditionally placed within the Barremian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Barremian alone.* The ammonite makes the lower Xert latest Barremian only on the ammonite zonation; on GTS2020's working definition the same level is earliest Aptian, and the upper Xert's strontium value cannot separate the two stages.

**Open.** Nothing beyond the 2026-09-30 entry's Open: the stage of the boundary interval waits on a ratified base of the Aptian.

## Is the Xert Formation Barremian or Aptian?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Barremian–Aptian. The lower Xert is latest Barremian by ammonites, at the level where the base of the Aptian falls under the magnetostratigraphic definition; the upper Xert cannot be separated by strontium isotopes and may be earliest Aptian. Governs `stages: [Barremian, Aptian]` on Xert and on [*Tastavinsaurus sanzi*](../../../genera/T/Tastavinsaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bover-arnal2016a, p. 51: *Martelites* "in the lower part of the Xert Formation in Torre Miró"; p. 58, Fig. 9: "particularly to the lower part of the Martelites sarasini Subzone"; p. 58: "an earliest Aptian age for the uppermost part of the Xert Formation cannot be ruled out".
- frau2017a, p. 317: lower *sarasini* Subzone ammonites "correlate to the topmost part of magnetozone CM0r and lowermost part of magnetozone C34n"; p. 314: the *sarasini* Zone is "unanimously considered to be the last ammonite zone of the Barremian (Gradstein et al. 2012, Reboulet et al. 2014)".
- gale2020a, pp. 1031–1032: "the base of the Aptian is taken at 121.4 Ma, at the base of Chron M0"; the working group "is likely to recommend taking the maximum negative value of the basal excursion of OAE1a"; p. 1024: "only 6 of the 12 Cretaceous stages had ratified GSSPs".
- bover-arnal2016a, p. 51: "0.707425 ± 0.000013 obtained from the upper part from the Xert Formation"; frau2018a, p. 143: in a band of values "SIS is unreliable for separating the uppermost Barremian (M. sarasini Zone) from the lower Aptian pro parte".

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Aptian.* Canudo and colleagues (2008) give the *Tastavinsaurus* beds as "from the early Aptian of Spain" (canudo2008a, p. 712), following the regional scheme in which these beds were "classically interpreted to be of Early Aptian age", which Bover-Arnal and colleagues ascribe "to the Late Barremian" (bover-arnal2016a, p. 58).

**Open.** Which side of the boundary the upper Xert falls, until the base of the Aptian is ratified. Bover-Arnal and colleagues' 124.94 Ma is on the 2004 timescale (p. 48).

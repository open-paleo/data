# Xert

## Is the Xert Formation Barremian or Aptian?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Barremian–Aptian. The lower Xert is latest Barremian by ammonites, at the level where the base of the Aptian falls under the magnetostratigraphic definition; the upper Xert cannot be separated by strontium isotopes and may be earliest Aptian. Governs `stages: [Barremian, Aptian]` on Xert and on [*Tastavinsaurus sanzi*](../../../genera/T/Tastavinsaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bover-arnal2016a, p. 51: *Martelites* "in the lower part of the Xert Formation in Torre Miró"; p. 58, Fig. 9: "particularly to the lower part of the Martelites sarasini Subzone"; p. 58: "an earliest Aptian age for the uppermost part of the Xert Formation cannot be ruled out".
- frau2017a, p. 317: lower *sarasini* Subzone ammonites "correlate to the topmost part of magnetozone CM0r and lowermost part of magnetozone C34n"; p. 314: the *sarasini* Zone is "unanimously considered to be the last ammonite zone of the Barremian (Gradstein et al. 2012, Reboulet et al. 2014)".
- gale2020a, pp. 1031–1032: "the base of the Aptian is taken at 121.4 Ma, at the base of Chron M0"; the working group "is likely to recommend taking the maximum negative value of the basal excursion of OAE1a"; p. 1024: "only 6 of the 12 Cretaceous stages had ratified GSSPs".
- bover-arnal2016a, p. 51: "0.707425 ± 0.000013 obtained from the upper part from the Xert Formation"; frau2018a, p. 143: in a band of values "SIS is unreliable for separating the uppermost Barremian (M. sarasini Zone) from the lower Aptian pro parte".

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Aptian.* Canudo and colleagues (2008) give the *Tastavinsaurus* beds as "from the early Aptian of Spain" (canudo2008a, p. 712), following the regional scheme in which these beds were "classically interpreted to be of Early Aptian age", which Bover-Arnal and colleagues ascribe "to the Late Barremian" (bover-arnal2016a, p. 58).

**Open.** Which side of the boundary the upper Xert falls, until the base of the Aptian is ratified. Bover-Arnal and colleagues' 124.94 Ma is on the 2004 timescale (p. 48).

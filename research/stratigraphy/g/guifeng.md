# Guifeng

## Are the Hekou, Tangbian and Lianhe successive formations?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** The registry keeps all three as formations of the group, and records the other reading. Xie (2001), working in the Xinjiang Basin, makes them contemporaneous deposits of different depositional systems, though his reproduced sections show them stacked across overlap unconformities; Li and colleagues (2018) follow him and propose dropping the Lianhe as the same sequence as the Hekou, a proposal no later work read adopts. Most works, and the charts of Xi and colleagues (2018), keep them stacked. The registry encodes no order between them, and the [*Gongshuilong*](../../../genera/G/Gongshuilong.yml) and [*Huaxiazhoulong*](../../../genera/H/Huaxiazhoulong.yml) records name the Lianhe and Tangbian, so nothing changes. Answers the Open line of the earlier entry. Governs the children [Tangbian](../../../stratigraphy/t/tangbian.yml) and [Lianhe](../../../stratigraphy/l/lianhe.yml) of [Guifeng](../../../stratigraphy/g/guifeng.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- xie2001a, p. 10: "它们所对应的地层莲荷组、河口组、塘边组是等时的" ["the strata corresponding to them, the Lianhe Formation, the Hekou Formation, and the Tangbian Formation, are isochronous", translated].
- li2018a, p. 1122: "本文建议舍弃莲荷组,将其用河口组代替" ["This paper therefore proposes abandoning the Lianhe Formation and replacing it with the Hekou Formation", translated].

**Ruled out.** - *Merging the Lianhe into the Hekou.* One proposal, not adopted.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Should Gale and colleagues (2020) stay cited on the Guifeng?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No. GTS2020 was cited only for the base ages of the Coniacian and Santonian, used to place the 89–86 Ma basalt ages that Xi and colleagues (2018) relay; ages are now placed on the ICS chart v2024/12 in the research note, as the 2026-11-23 entry does, and the chart is not a source on the unit. Governs the removal of gale2020a from [Guifeng](../../../stratigraphy/g/guifeng.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- xi2018a, p. 275: "two isotopic ages of 89–86 Ma from the basalt of the Hekou Formation imply that the age of the Guifeng Group might be early to mid-late Cretaceous (Wu et al., 2002)".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the basis of the Guifeng Group's age readings and its equation with the Nanxiong?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Neither early Late Cretaceous reading rests on dates measured in the paper that gives it. Xi and colleagues' (2018) 89–86 Ma basalt ages for the Hekou Formation, and the age reading drawn from them, are relayed from Wu and colleagues (2002); on the ICS chart v2024/12, whose Coniacian begins at 89.8 Ma and Santonian at 85.7 Ma, 89 to 86 Ma is Coniacian. Li and colleagues (2018) place the group in the early Late Cretaceous from its stratigraphic and basin-tectonic relationship to the Ganzhou and Huobashan groups, not from new dates. Jin and colleagues (2022) equate the group with the Nanxiong Formation of the Ganzhou Basin, crediting Liu (1997), list *Banji long*, *Ganzhousaurus nankangensis*, *Jiangxisaurus ganzhouensis* and *Nankangia jiangxiensis* among its vertebrates, and give their own specimens as Maastrichtian Hekou Formation. Those four taxa are placed in the Nanxiong Formation by their describing papers, which were not re-read for this entry. Governs `stages: [Coniacian, Santonian, Campanian, Maastrichtian]` and the dispute on [Guifeng](../../../stratigraphy/g/guifeng.yml), unchanged. It adds to the 2026-10-01 entry and supersedes none.

**Evidence.**
- xi2018a, p. 275: "The Guifeng Group (Hekou, Tangbian and Lianhe Formations), consisting primarily of red beds, yields late Cretaceous ostracodes and dinosaur eggs", with the basalt ages and the age reading credited to "(Wu et al., 2002)".
- xi2021a, p. 387: the group "consists mainly of red clastic sediments and yields fossils such as Late Cretaceous dinosaur eggs and ostracods" (translated).
- li2018a, p. 1121, 5.4 Guifeng Group: "Although the precise age of the Guifeng Group remains to be worked out, considering its relationship with the Ganzhou Group and the Huobashan Group in stratigraphy and in basin tectonic evolution, this paper inclines to place it in the early Late Cretaceous" (translated).
- li2018a, p. 1130 (the authors' English): the "Hekou and Tangbian formations are contemporaneous heterotopic facies in different environment, likely with lateral superimposition".
- jin2022a, p. 2, Geological setting: "Vertebrate fossils were also reported from the Nanxiong Formation (= Guifeng Group) that is exposed in the vicinity of the Gannan Basin", among them *Banji long*, *Ganzhousaurus nankangensis*, *Jiangxisaurus ganzhouensis* and *Nankangia jiangxiensis*.
- jin2022a, p. 7, Locality and Horizon: "Ganzhou railway station (GM30516) and Third High School of Ganxian County (GM30496); Hekou Formation (Upper Cretaceous, Maastrichtian), Guifeng Group".

**Ruled out.**
Nothing beyond the readings the 2026-10-01 entry rules out.

**Open.** Nothing beyond the points the 2026-10-01 entry leaves open.

## What is the age of the Guifeng Group?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Coniacian–Maastrichtian, with a dispute over the basal age. Governs `stages` (Coniacian–Maastrichtian) and the dispute on Guifeng, and the stages of its formations, such as [Tangbian](../../../stratigraphy/t/tangbian.yml) and [Lianhe](../../../stratigraphy/l/lianhe.yml).

**Evidence.**
- li2018a, p. 1130 (the authors' English): "the Guifeng Gr might start in early Late Cretaceous (after~100Ma)".
- xi2018a, p. 275: "two isotopic ages of 89–86 Ma from the basalt of the Hekou Formation imply that the age of the Guifeng Group might be early to mid-late Cretaceous (Wu et al., 2002)"; xi2021a, p. 387, gives "晚白垩世中晚期" ("middle to late Late Cretaceous", translated), also citing Wu and colleagues (2002). The two Xi papers are therefore one source, not two.

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian–Danian only, from palaeomagnetism.* Xing and colleagues give "Palaeomagnetic studies have dated the Guifeng Group to 71.4–65.0 Ma (Gu 1991; Zuo et al. 1991)" (xing2020b, p. 1), relaying work from the Jitai Basin, not the Ganzhou Basin.

**Open.** Two points. Li and colleagues treat "Hekou and Tangbian formations" as "contemporaneous heterotopic facies in different environment" and the Hekou and Lianhe as "probably the same sequences but named differently" (li2018a, p. 1130, following Xie 2001), which conflicts with the registry's stacked Campanian Tangbian and Maastrichtian Lianhe. And Xi and colleagues' Fig. 4 (xi2021a, p. 386) draws the Lianhe on into the Danian, which the registry does not record.

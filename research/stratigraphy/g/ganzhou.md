# Ganzhou

## Does Ling's 85.9 Ma K-Ar date on the Maodian Formation put the Ganzhou Group in the Coniacian?

*2026-10-22*

**Conclusion.** No. The date is a whole-rock K-Ar age on an altered basalt in the lowest bed of the Maodian Formation, and Ling (1996), who obtained it, dates the formation only to the epoch, "late Early Cretaceous to early Late Cretaceous", weighing it against charophytes of late Early Cretaceous age and an older K-Ar age of 101.8 Ma from another locality. Read at its number, it would put the base of the group in the Coniacian and the Zhoutian Formation above it younger still, against the Cenomanian–Turonian the 2026-10-03 entry rests on. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian]` on [Ganzhou](../../../stratigraphy/g/ganzhou.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- ling1996a, p. 56: "dark-green iddingsitized olivine basalt, for which a whole-rock K-Ar isotopic age of 85.9Ma was obtained" (translated), bed 1 of the Maodian stratotype; p. 57: "Li Kunying (1989) obtained a whole-rock K-Ar isotopic age of 101.8±3.2Ma from iddingsitized basalt in the lower part of this formation at Qingxi, Qianshan" (translated).
- ling1996a, p. 57: "The age of this formation is therefore late Early Cretaceous to early Late Cretaceous" (translated).
- li2018a, p. 1108: whole-rock isotopic ages of the 1980s and 1990s in the region were "mostly not very accurate or even wrong" (translated), their localities and horizons poorly documented.

**Ruled out.**
- *Coniacian from 85.9 Ma.* The number falls in the Coniacian on the ICS chart v2024/12 (85.7–89.8 Ma), but Ling himself draws only an epoch from it, the two K-Ar ages from the same formation differ by about 16 Myr, and whole-rock K-Ar on an altered basalt is the kind of date Li and colleagues (2018) set aside.

**Open.** No Maodian unit is in the registry. A modern zircon date from the Maodian would settle the base of the group.

## Are the Ganzhou Group and its Zhoutian Formation Turonian–Coniacian, Albian–Cenomanian, or Cenomanian–Turonian?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian–Turonian, on palynology and on the 96–90 Ma figure later papers give, which falls in the Cenomanian and Turonian on every chart. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian]` on Ganzhou and on the [Zhoutian Formation](../../../stratigraphy/z/zhoutian.yml).

**Evidence.**
- li2018a, p. 1121: "如果周田组位于茅店组之上，则其沉积时间可能晚于95~90Ma" ("if the Zhoutian Formation lies above the Maodian Formation, its deposition may be later than 95–90 Ma", translated), a conditional floor.
- han2024a, pp. 5, 7: Cenomanian–Turonian palynology, relayed from Li (2017).
- xing2024a, p. 114: "Turonian–early Coniacian; Upper Cretaceous; 96–90 Ma", credited on p. 115 to "Li et al. 2018; Wu et al. 2020". On the current chart 96 Ma is Cenomanian and 90 Ma Turonian.

**Ruled out.**
- *Turonian–early Coniacian.* Xing and colleagues' stage label does not match their own numbers: 90 Ma is Turonian on every chart from GTS 2004 to ICS 2024/12, and 96 Ma is Cenomanian. Reading the range as an age, rather than Li and colleagues' conditional floor, is itself a step beyond the source (our inference).
- *Late Albian–early Cenomanian.* Xi and colleagues give "late Albian–early Cenomanian" (xi2021a, p. 387), and xi2018a (online-first, p. 20) relays Li and colleagues as "late early Cretaceous", which misreads Li's floor.

**Open.** The palynology rests on Li (2017), which has not been read. No Maodian unit is in the registry, so the Zhoutian's position above it is unrecorded.

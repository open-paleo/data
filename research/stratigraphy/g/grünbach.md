# Grünbach

## On what does Suess (1881) rest a pre-Turonian age for the Grünbach?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** On one correlation, not on the fossils of the bone-bearing beds. Suess (1881) names neither the formation nor the Muthmannsdorf workings; he identifies the horizon by its position in the Gosau succession of the "Neue Welt", south of Vienna and west of Wiener Neustadt: a basal calcareous breccia, then freshwater sandstones, marls and a few coal seams with *Melanopsis*, *Unio* and a flora that includes a true palm, the horizon that "has yielded the reptilian bones"; brackish and marine beds above; then a rudist-coral limestone that he takes for "the true French Turonian zone of *Hippurites cornu-vaccinum*". The bones, lying below that limestone, are therefore "older than the true Turonian deposits". The age is relative to that zone and stands or falls with its identification as Turonian, while the bone-bearing beds themselves are dated to the early Campanian on foraminifera and nannoplankton (Csiki-Sava and colleagues 2015). The order of beds Suess gives is the Gosau succession in which Csiki-Sava and colleagues (2015) and Augustin and colleagues (2023) still place the formation. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` on [Grünbach](../../../stratigraphy/g/grünbach.yml).

**Evidence.**
- suess1881a, p. 702: "in the valley of the "Neue Welt," the spot where the bones were gathered which I sent to you, several shafts pass twice through one and the same seam of coal".
- suess1881a, p. 703: "the "Neue Welt" (south of Vienna, west of Wiener Neustadt)"; "The base of the Gosau beds is formed by a calcareous breccia"; "Then follows a series of freshwater beds, sandstones, marls and a few seams of coal, accompanied by freshwater Mollusca such as *Melanopsis* ... and *Unio*, and the remains of a highly heterogeneous flora, comprising a true Palm"; "It is this horizon which has yielded the reptilian bones."
- suess1881a, p. 703: "This is the true French Turonian zone of *Hippurites cornu-vaccinum*"; "it is certain that they are older than the true Turonian deposits, and especially older than the zone of *Hippurites cornu-vaccinum*".
- csiki-sava2015a, p. 36: "Although Suess (1881) suggested a pre-Turonian age, more recent studies"; p. 37: "clearly demonstrated that the vertebrate-bearing beds are Campanian in age"; "the Grünbach Formation was dated as early Campanian on the basis of foraminiferan (*Globotruncana elevata* Zone) and nannoplankton data (Zone UC 15)".
- augustin2023a, p. 173: "the lower Campanian Grünbach Formation of the Gosau Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *A pre-Turonian age, from Suess (1881).* It is deduced from the bones' position below a limestone that Suess takes for the French Turonian zone of *Hippurites cornu-vaccinum*, not from any fossil of the bone-bearing beds; foraminifera and nannoplankton from the formation date it to the early Campanian.

**Open.** Nothing.

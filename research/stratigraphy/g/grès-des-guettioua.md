# Grès des Guettioua

## What do the cited works say about the extent and age of the Grès des Guettioua?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The formation is the sandstone and conglomerate at the base of the Couches rouges, below the Iouaridène and Jbel Sidal Formations, and it is Middle Jurassic. Jenny, Le Marrec and Monbaron (1981) keep the name E. Roch introduced in 1933 for the whole red-bed sequence of the Guettioua basin, but narrow it to the sandstones by moving the overlying red pelites into their new Iouaridène Formation. They designate no reference section, because the sandstones are lenticular, and describe a typical one north of Amougguez instead. In the Taguelft area they equate the formation with the Tagalft Formation. They date it Bathonian, taking the sauropod skeleton found in it in the Tilougguit basin as the decisive argument. Allain and Aquesbi (2008) call it Bathonian in their text, but their stratigraphic chart (Fig. 2) draws the Grès de Guettioua box from the upper Bathonian into the Callovian, sharing the Bathonian part with the El Mers Formation along a diagonal. Governs `stages: [Bathonian, Callovian]` and the variant Tagalft on [Grès des Guettioua](../../../stratigraphy/g/grès-des-guettioua.yml).

**Evidence.**
- jenny1981a, p. 629: "E. Roch introduced as early as 1933 ... the term *Guettioua Sandstones* for the entire sequence of red beds in this basin"; "we shall therefore retain it, but excluding from the 'Guettioua Sandstone Formation' the red pelites that overlie these sandstones"; "These pelites belong to the Iouaridène Formation" (translated).
- jenny1981a, p. 629: "a precise reference section would not be of interest, but a typical lithology can be described by observing, for example, the well-exposed section north of Amougguez" (translated).
- jenny1981a, p. 631: "brick-red silts and sandy levels with oblique stratifications, which are assigned essentially to the Guettioua Sandstone Formation (= Tagalft Formation, Monbaron [1980])" (translated).
- jenny1981a, p. 632: "The Red Beds, which rest above the 'Calcaires corniches' or the Tilougguit Formation, are divided into three lithostratigraphic units: — the Grès des Guettioua Formation, with or without interstratified volcanic flows; — the Iouaridène Formation; — the Jbel Sidal Formation" (translated).
- jenny1981a, p. 634: the skeleton "in the Grès des Guettioua of the Tilougguit basin ... is an important argument for definitively assigning this unit to the Bathonian"; "The paleontological arguments stated therefore argue in favor of a Middle Jurassic age, and not Lower Cretaceous, for the Grès des Guettioua Formation and that of the Iouaridène" (translated).
- allain2008a, p. 347: "The Bathonian Guettioua Formation has yielded the skeleton of the sauropod *Atlasaurus imelakei*", "and its lateral equivalent in the Middle Atlas, the El Mers Formation"; the Red beds complex "is constituted by three successive lithologic formations ...: the Djebel Sidal, the Iouaridene and the Guettioua formations".
- allain2008a, p. 349, Fig. 2: "Grès de Guettioua F." spans the CALL row and the upper part of the BATHONIAN row, above a diagonal with "El Mers F." below it.

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Cretaceous.* The red beds were placed in the Lower Cretaceous "without paleontological arguments beginning in 1956" (jenny1981a, p. 627, translated). Jenny and colleagues (1981) reassign them to the Middle Jurassic on vertebrates, palynomorphs, lithostratigraphy and geochronology.

**Open.** Whether the formation reaches the Callovian. Jenny and colleagues (1981) give the Bathonian alone; the Callovian rests on Monbaron and colleagues' (1999) "Bathonian-Callovian" for the lower Couches rouges and on Allain and Aquesbi's (2008) chart, which gives no source for the extension.

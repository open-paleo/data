# Grinstead Clay

## What do the cited works say about the age and position of the Grinstead Clay?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Valanginian, unchanged. Norman (2013) dates the Whiteman's Green outcrops at Cuckfield "upper-middle Valanginian", and Austen and Batten (2018) draw the unit within the Valanginian; Drew (1861), who names the clay, gives it no age. Norman's "~137 Ma" beside the stage is an approximate figure, not a measured age, so the stated stage is the reading; on the ICS chart v2024/12 137 Ma lies only 0.05 Myr above the base of the Valanginian at 137.05 Ma. The rank and parent stay as the 2026-09-30 and 2026-10-08 entries in [Wealden units](../../topics/wealden-units.md) set them; Austen and Batten's column agrees with them, drawing a Grinstead Clay formation of the Hastings group between the Lower and Upper Tunbridge Wells Sand. Governs `stages: [Valanginian]` on [Grinstead Clay](../../../stratigraphy/g/grinstead-clay.yml).

**Evidence.**
- norman2013a, p. 390: the Whiteman's Green quarries "exposed Hastings Group Beds of the Grinstead Clay Formation (Fig. 2: GC Fm), which is a mappable lithological unit that is exposed in the more westerly parts of the Weald"; "These outcrops are regarded as being of upper-middle Valanginian in age (~137 Ma)".
- norman2013a, p. 389: some of Mantell's specimens "most likely came from the quarries located near Whiteman's Green, Cuckfield", and the lectotype of *Iguanodon anglicus* retains "the calcareous, gritty sandstone (Cuckfield Stone)"; pp. 389–390: the *Kukufeldia tilgatensis* dentary "was also collected from one of the Whiteman's Green quarries".
- norman2013a, p. 391, Fig. 3: "Cuckfield Stone (Grinstead Clay Member, upper middle Valanginian)"; p. 392, Fig. 4: "Probably collected from the Grinstead Clay Member (upper middle Valanginian)".
- drew1861a, pp. 283–284: "The bed of clay that appears in the Tunbridge Wells Sand, and thickens to 50 ft. to the westward, may well be called Grinstead Clay, from its occurring on the north side of the town of East Grinstead"; "This clay of course divides the sand into Upper and Lower Tunbridge Wells Sand".
- drew1861a, p. 281: in Penshurst Park "I measured a thickness of 17 feet of clay, and I saw below it a bed of white rock-sand; while the Wadhurst Clay is not far beneath"; at East Grinstead "At the very top of it there is red clay, which is a very useful mark by which to recognize this stratum; lower down it is brown clay, or brown shale, or blue shale".
- drew1861a, p. 282: at the Balcombe Tunnel cuttings "More than 40 ft. of this Grinstead Clay can be measured on the north side; and there is probably about 50 ft. of it on the whole".
- austen2018a, corrected proof, p. 4, Fig. 3: the column for the Wealden Supergroup places "Grinstead Clay" in the Formation column of the Hastings group, between "Lower Tunbridge Wells Sand" and "Upper Tunbridge Wells Sand", with "L Grinstead Clay", "Cuckfield Stone" and "U Grinstead C" as its beds, within the Valanginian; caption: "L, U, C, lower, upper (Grinstead) Clay".

**Ruled out.**
- *A stage read from Norman's "~137 Ma" instead of his stated stage.* The number is approximate and comes with the stage; it is not a measured age, so it is not re-placed on the current chart. Re-placed, it would fall 0.05 Myr inside the Valanginian and change nothing.
- *Member rank on Norman's evidence.* Norman's text and Fig. 2 call the unit the Grinstead Clay Formation, and both figure captions call it the Grinstead Clay Member, so the paper supports neither rank over the other. The rank follows the 2026-10-08 entry in [Wealden units](../../topics/wealden-units.md).

**Open.** What the Valanginian rests on. None of the passages quoted gives the evidence for the age; Galton (2009) credits it to Rawson and colleagues (1978), which would show whether it is a biostratigraphic determination for the Grinstead Clay itself.

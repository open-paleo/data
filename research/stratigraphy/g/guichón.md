# Guichón

## Does a clade's range give the Guichón the Maastrichtian?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No; the Maastrichtian is dropped. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry on the stages. Soto Núñez and colleagues (2026) take the Coniacian–Maastrichtian range of Aeolosaurini, in which their new sauropod nests, as evidence that "suggests this age" for the formation, and their Spanish abstract draws only a Late Cretaceous age from it. For the formation itself they give "perhaps late Santonian–early Campanian" and "Santonian or early Campanian". A clade's range offered as a suggestion is not a reading of the unit's age, and the paper's own reading stops in the Campanian. Its other Maastrichtian statements are an eggshell range relayed from Casadío and colleagues (2002) and the age of the overlying Mercedes Formation, a different unit. The stages between Goso and Perea's (2003) Aptian and the Campanian stay, since stages are contiguous. Governs `stages` on [Guichón](../../../stratigraphy/g/guichón.yml), Aptian to Campanian, and `stage: [Santonian, Campanian]` on [*Mesetasaurus*](../../../genera/M/Mesetasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sotonúñez2026a, p. 279, Abstract: "Given that Aeolosaurini is a Coniacian–Maastrichtian clade, this new sauropod provides additional evidence that suggests this age for the Guichón Formation."
- sotonúñez2026a, p. 279, Resumen: "Dado que Aeolosaurini es un clado del Coniaciano–Maastrichtiano, este nuevo saurópodo es otra evidencia de la edad Cretácica Tardía de la Formación Guichón."
- sotonúñez2026a, p. 295: "most lines of evidence seem to point to a Late Cretaceous age for the Guichón/Puerto Yeruá Formation, perhaps late Santonian–early Campanian, given that the overlying Mercedes Formation has been considered Campanian–Maastrichtian (Goso & Perea, 2003)".
- sotonúñez2026a, p. 298: "Evidence favors a younger age for the unit (i.e., Santonian or early Campanian)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian from the range of Aeolosaurini.* A clade's range, offered as a suggestion, which the paper narrows for the formation.
- *Maastrichtian from the Mercedes Formation.* The age of the unit above.

**Open.** Unchanged: a numerical age from the formation would settle the range.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Guichón?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Unresolved between the Aptian and the Maastrichtian. No work cited gives a numerical date; the readings rest on stratigraphic position, facies and fauna. The early end is Goso and Perea's (2003) Aptian, which Soto and colleagues (2012) trace to the unconformity below the Mercedes Formation and the facies likeness to the Albian Migues Formation, and Rusconi's (1933) "Cenomanian or older" as Soto Núñez and colleagues (2026) relay it. The late end is Mones's (1997) Santonian–?Campanian, which Soto and colleagues (2012) give as the youngest proposed age, and the Coniacian–Maastrichtian that Soto Núñez and colleagues (2026) draw from an aeolosaurin sauropod, narrowed in their discussion to "perhaps late Santonian-early Campanian". Soto and colleagues (2012) allocate the formation to the Late Cretaceous from a derived lithostrotian found with *Sphaerovum* eggshells. The stages are the union of these readings, and the conflict is the record's dispute. Governs `period`, `stages` and `dispute` on [Guichón](../../../stratigraphy/g/guichón.yml).

**Evidence.**
- soto2012a, p. 70, 2. Geological setting: "The Argentinean Puerto Yeruá Formation is correlated with both the Guichón (Goso and Perea, 2004) and Mercedes formations. The age of the Guichón Formation is not clear. Proposed ages range from Aptian (Goso and Perea, 2004) to Santonian–?Campanian (Mones, 1997)"; "the geological evidence such as the unconformity with the overlying Mercedes Formation (Campanian–Maastrichtian ...) and the facies similarity with the Migues Formation (Albian ...) from the southern Santa Lucía Basin, led some authors (Goso, 1999; Goso and Perea, 2004) to suggest an Early Cretaceous age for this unit".
- soto2012a, p. 76, 5. Discussion: the purported ornithomimid and iguanodontid teeth "do not support a Late Cretaceous age for the Guichón Formation"; "within Lithostrotia, a biconvex first caudal centrum has only been reported in taxa from late Late Cretaceous units of Argentina", and *Sphaerovum* "remains have been reported so far from several Campanian–Maastrichtian units".
- soto2012a, p. 76, 6. Conclusions: "the simultaneous occurrence of a derived lithostrotian and Sphaerovum provides an important biostratigraphic criterion to allocate the Guichón Formation (and the correlated Puerto Yeruá Formation) to the Late Cretaceous".
- sotonúñez2026a, p. 279, Abstract: "The Upper Cretaceous Guichón Formation"; "A phylogenetic analysis retrieves the new taxon deeply nested within Aeolosaurini. Given that Aeolosaurini is a Coniacian–Maastrichtian clade, this new sauropod provides additional evidence that suggests this age for the Guichón Formation".
- sotonúñez2026a, p. 280, Geological setting: "The unit overlies the Hauterivian basaltic flows of the Arapey Formation and, in turn, is overlain by the coarse sandstones and conglomerates of the Campanian–Maastrichtian Mercedes Formation"; p. 282: "Originally dated as 'Cenomanian or older' (Rusconi, 1933) or even Aptian (Goso & Perea, 2003), recent findings ... suggest the Guichón Formation could be considerably younger".
- sotonúñez2026a, p. 295, On the age of the Guichón Formation: "Overall, most lines of evidence seem to point to a latest Cretaceous age for the Guichón/Puerto Yeruá Formation, perhaps late Santonian-early Campanian, given that the overlying Mercedes Formation has been considered Campanian-Maastrichtian (Goso & Perea, 2003)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Hauterivian or Barremian.* The Hauterivian basalts of the Arapey Formation lie below the formation, and a lava flow below is a limit, not an age, so they give no stage to the Guichón.

**Open.** A numerical age from the formation itself would settle the range. Rusconi (1933) and Mones (1997) are known here only through the papers that relay them.

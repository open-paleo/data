# Gruhalde

## Does the Gruhalde keep the Rhaetian?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Yes. Answers the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Jordan and colleagues' (2016) definition gives "Carnian to Rhaetian (?)" after the German stratigraphic scheme and Kozur and Bachmann (2008), whose correlation chart puts the "Trossingen Fm. (Knollenmergel)" wholly in the Rhaetian. Jordan and colleagues take the former «Knollenmergel» into the member and write that the uppermost part of its type section "may correlate with the German Trossingen Formation", so the queried Rhaetian has a published basis; a queried stage in a considered dating counts, as with Felton's "?Late Barremian" for the [Eumeralla](../e/eumeralla.md). Zahner and Brinkmann's (2019) "latest Norian" for the uppermost member at Frick is relayed from Jordan and colleagues, whose text gives "Late Norian" only for the *Plateosaurus* levels below it, and a narrower dating adds to the range rather than replacing it. Governs `stages: [Carnian, Norian, Rhaetian]` on [Gruhalde](../../../stratigraphy/g/gruhalde.yml), unchanged, and the note on kozur2008a.

**Evidence.**
- jordan2016a, p. 278, Gruhalde Member: "Chronostratigr. Age Carnian to Rhaetian (?) (DSK 2002, 2005; Kozur and Bachmann 2008)."
- jordan2016a, p. 277: "The presence of Plateosaurus remains points to a Late Norian age (C.A. Meyer, pers. comm. 2015)"; "The uppermost part of the section may correlate with the German Trossingen Formation".
- kozur2008a, p. 56, Fig. 3: "Trossingen Fm. (Knollenmergel)" lies within the stage column's "Rhaetian", whose base the caption gives among the "Calculated numeric ages for the base of the Carnian, Norian and Rhaetian stages".
- zahner2019a, p. ?, Horizon and locality: "New upper dinosaur layer, 1 m beneath the Triassic–Jurassic boundary, uppermost Gruhalde Member, Klettgau Formation, latest Norian", with the age credited to Jordan and colleagues (2016).

**Ruled out.**
- *Dropping the Rhaetian for the query.* The query marks the authors' doubt about a reading they make, and the scheme they cite places the correlative beds in the Rhaetian.

**Open.** Unchanged: a paper dating the top of the member where the Belchen Member does not overlie it would settle the Rhaetian.

## What does the definition of the Gruhalde Member say about its extent and age?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Jordan and colleagues (2016) formally define the member in the Klettgau Formation, which the same paper introduces, with its type locality at the Gruhalde clay pit in Frick AG, and make it take in all the strata formerly called «Bunte Mergel», «Obere Bunte Mergel» and «Knollenmergel». Its definition gives the age as "Carnian to Rhaetian (?)", after the German stratigraphic scheme of the DSK (2002, 2005) and Kozur and Bachmann (2008). In the type section they correlate the coarse-grained sand of the middle part with the German Löwenstein Formation and take its *Plateosaurus* remains to point to a Late Norian age; that age rests on a personal communication, so it adds nothing beyond the Norian already in the range (see [methods](../../methods.md#a-personal-communication-is-not-a-published-determination)). Governs `parent: Klettgau`, `stages: [Carnian, Norian, Rhaetian]` and `lithology` on [Gruhalde](../../../stratigraphy/g/gruhalde.yml).

**Evidence.**
- jordan2016a, p. 278, §4.4.6 Formal definition of the Gruhalde member: "Names previously in use «Obere Bunte Mergel», «Knollenmergel»"; "Type locality Gruhalde clay pit" "in Frick AG"; "Chronostratigr. Age Carnian to Rhaetian (?) (DSK 2002, 2005; Kozur and Bachmann 2008)"; "Heterogeneous succession of variegated to greyish dolomitic marl with dolomite interlayers".
- jordan2016a, p. 277: "The Gruhalde Member therefore encompasses all strata formerly called «Bunte Mergel», «Obere Bunte Mergel», and «Knollenmergel»".
- jordan2016a, p. 277: "The middle (third) part can be correlated, due to the presence of coarse-grained sand, with the German Löwenstein Formation"; "The presence of Plateosaurus remains points to a Late Norian age (C.A. Meyer, pers. comm. 2015)".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Whether the Rhaetian belongs in the range. The definition marks it with a query, and Zahner and Brinkmann (2019) place a layer in the uppermost member at Frick, 1 m below the Triassic–Jurassic boundary, in the latest Norian. A paper dating the top of the member where the Belchen Member does not overlie it would settle it.

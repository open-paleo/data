# Gugyedong

## What rock makes up the Gugyedong, and what lies beneath it?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The member is mainly purple mudstone and siltstone, and it makes up most of the Hupyeongdong Formation (Choi and Kwon 2019). The light gray paraconglomerate that yielded *Ultrasaurus tabriensis* is one bed, 0 to 1 m thick, at the top of Kim's lower member, which Kim (1983) calls an exceptional intercalation in the red beds; it is not the member's general rock. Beneath the Gugyedong, Kim's section at Tabri has the Iljig Formation and then the Gumidong Conglomerate (龜尾洞礫岩), which carries the same place name (구미동) as the Gumidong Member that Choi and Kwon place below the Gugyedong in the Hupyeongdong. That the two are one unit is our reading from the name and the position; neither paper equates them. Governs `lithology` on [Gugyedong](../../../stratigraphy/g/gugyedong.yml). It adds to the 2026-09-30 entry and supersedes none.

**Evidence.**
- choi2019a, p. 552: "반면 구계동층원은 후평동층의 대부분을 차지하며 주로 자색의 이암과 실트암으로 구성된다" ("The Gugyedong Member, in contrast, makes up most of the Hupyeongdong Formation and consists mainly of purple mudstone and siltstone", translated); the lower member, "구미동층원은 역암과 이암이 호층을 이루며" ("the Gumidong Member consists of alternating conglomerate and mudstone", translated).
- kim1983a, p. 115, Abstract: the bones came from "the light gray granite cobble-bearing paraconglomerate with very coarse lime concretion pebbles in the Gugyedong Formation of the Hayang Group. The paraconglomerate is an exceptional intercalation in the red beds of the Cretaceous System in Korea."
- kim1983a, p. 117: the section at Tabri runs "아래에서 위로 一直層, 龜尾洞礫岩 및 龜溪洞層" ("from bottom to top, the Iljig Formation, the Gumidong Conglomerate and the Gugyedong Formation", translated); the lower member "주로 灰赤色셰일로 구성되어" ("consists mainly of grayish red shale", translated), and the dinosaur bed is "두께 0~1m인 淡灰綠色準礫岩層" ("a pale grayish green paraconglomerate bed 0–1 m thick", translated).
- kim1983a, p. 116, Fig. 1 caption: "B: Gumidong Conglomerate, C: Gugyedong Formation (CL: Lower part, CU: Upper part)".

**Ruled out.**
- *A gray paraconglomerate as the member's lithology.* It is the dinosaur bed, which Kim (1983) describes as an exceptional intercalation 0 to 1 m thick; the lower member beneath it is mainly grayish red shale, and Choi and Kwon (2019) describe the member as mainly purple mudstone and siltstone.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does Choi and Kwon's zircon work bracket the Gugyedong at 106–100 Ma?

*2026-10-09*

**Conclusion.** No. The member stays Albian on the basis given for its formation in the [Hupyeongdong](../h/hupyeongdong.md) entry of 2026-10-03, which rules the bracket out: the ages from the units above are maximum depositional ages and do not bound the Hupyeongdong from above. Governs the `choi2019a` note on Gugyedong and the location and `choi2019a` notes on [*Ultrasaurus tabriensis*](../../../genera/U/Ultrasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- choi2019a, as quoted in the Hupyeongdong entry: maximum depositional ages of 104.9, 99.8 and 96 Ma for the Jeomgok, Sagok and Banyawol formations, and none from the Hupyeongdong.

**Ruled out.**
- *About 106 to 100 Ma, late Albian.* As in the Hupyeongdong entry.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the Gugyedong Aptian, and is it a formation?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Neither. It is an Albian member of the Hupyeongdong Formation in the [Hayang Group](../../../stratigraphy/h/hayang.yml); its Aptian came from a compilation table with no dating behind it. Governs `rank: member`, `parent: Hupyeongdong` and `stages: [Albian]` on Gugyedong, and the absence of the Aptian from Hayang.

**Evidence.**
- choi2019a, p. 552: "후평동층은 … 하부의 구미동층원과 상부의 구계동층원으로 구성되는데" ("the Hupyeongdong Formation consists of the lower Gumidong Member and the upper Gugyedong Member", translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian Gugyedong Formation.* Wilson and Upchurch (2005) list "Ultrasaurus tabriensis" from the Gugyedong as Aptian in a reference table (wilson2005a, p. 314, Table 2), citing Kim (1983) and others; Kim (1983) gives only the horizon, "the Gugyedong Formation of the Hayang Group" (kim1983a, p. 115), and no stage.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Ziliujing

## What do Franceschi and colleagues (2022) and Xu and colleagues (2017) give for the ends of the Ziliujing?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Sinemurian at the base and Toarcian at the top, now from the primary papers. Answers the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Franceschi and colleagues (2022) place the Sinemurian–Pliensbachian boundary within the Dongyuemiao Member from a carbon-isotope excursion, which makes the member's lower part late Sinemurian, and refer the Ma'anshan to the Pliensbachian–Toarcian on palynology. Xu and colleagues (2017) date the Da'anzhai Member's black shales at 180.3 ± 3.2 Ma by Re–Os, Toarcian on the ICS chart v2024/12, and tie them to the Toarcian oceanic anoxic event. Franceschi and colleagues' palynological "Hettangian–Sinemurian" covers the Dongyuemiao and the Zhenzhuchong together, and the registry treats the Zhenzhuchong as a separate formation below. Governs `stages: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on [Ziliujing](../../../stratigraphy/z/ziliujing.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- franceschi2022a, p. 13: "its recognition allows us to place the Sinemurian-Pliensbachian boundary within the Dongyuemiao Member (Fig. 4)".
- franceschi2022a, p. 12: the palynology "confirms attribution of the Ma’anshan Member to the Pliensbachian–Toarcian, while the underlying Dongyuemiao and Zhenzhuchong Members can be referred to the Hettangian–Sinemurian".
- xu2017c, p. 3: "The Re–Os isochron from the upper Lower Jurassic lacustrine Da’anzhai Member (Core A) gives an age of 180.3 ± 3.2 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *Hettangian, from the palynology.* The age is given to two members together, one of which the registry treats as the separate [Zhenzhuchong](../../../stratigraphy/z/zhenzhuchong.yml) Formation, and the same paper places the upper Dongyuemiao in the Sinemurian–Pliensbachian transition.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Ziliujing Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Sinemurian to Toarcian. Yao and colleagues (2025) list the formation's three members from the base up as the Dongyuemiao, Ma'anshan and Da'anzhai, between the Lower Jurassic Zhenzhuchong Formation below and the Middle Jurassic Xintiangou Formation above. They give the Dongyuemiao Member as generally accepted to be Pliensbachian, with geochemical and palynological data suggesting late Sinemurian to early Pliensbachian, and the Da'anzhai Member as Toarcian. The late Sinemurian sets the formation's lowest stage and the Toarcian its highest. Both are relayed from other works, Franceschi and colleagues (2022) for the Dongyuemiao and Xu and colleagues (2017) for the Da'anzhai. Governs `stages: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` and `period: [Early Jurassic]` on [Ziliujing](../../../stratigraphy/z/ziliujing.yml).

**Evidence.**
- yao2025a, p. 1, Systematic paleontology: "Dongyuemiao Member of the Ziliujing Formation, Early Jurassic, late Sinemurian-Pliensbachian".
- yao2025a, p. 7, Discussion: the Dongyuemiao Member "is generally accepted to be of Pliensbachian age based on regional stratigraphy and geological context"; "Geochemical and palynological data even suggest a late Sinemurian to early Pliensbachian age for this member."
- yao2025a, p. 7, Discussion: "The uppermost layer of the Ziliujing Formation, the Da'anzhai Member, has been solidly dated to the Toarcian stage through various lines of evidence such as palynostratigraphy, Re-Os chronology, and chemostratigraphy."
- yao2025a, p. e1, Geological setting and locality information: the formation "is positioned between the underlying Lower Jurassic Zhenzhuchong Formation and the overlying Middle Jurassic Xintiangou Formation" and "comprises three continuous members, arranged in ascending order: Dongyuemiao Member, Ma'anshan Member, and Da'anzhai Member".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** The two end stages rest on works Yao and colleagues cite rather than on their own data: the late Sinemurian on Franceschi and colleagues (2022), and the Toarcian on Xu and colleagues (2017). Either paper would confirm or narrow the stage it supports.

# Zhoutian

## What do the cited works say about the Zhoutian's relation to the Hekou Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Xing and colleagues (2024) write that the Zhoutian may represent "roughly contemporaneous but allogenic deposition with the overlying Hekou Formation", the lowest formation of the Guifeng Group, crediting Li and colleagues (2018). In their account the two formed at about the same time in different settings, the Zhoutian in a shallow lake and the Hekou in an alluvial fan, and the Zhoutian sandstones at Mazhou are much finer-grained than the Hekou's. The text of Li and colleagues (2018) does not discuss the two units together; their stratigraphic framework for eastern Jiangxi (Fig. 8b) draws the Zhoutian of the Ganzhou Basin and the Guifeng Group of the Xinjiang Basin across the same interval of the lower Upper Cretaceous, which is the likely basis of the attribution (read from the figure). Governs no value on [Zhoutian](../../../stratigraphy/z/zhoutian.yml) directly: the Jiangxi Hekou Formation is not in the registry, and the Zhoutian's stages rest on the [Ganzhou](../g/ganzhou.md) entries.

**Evidence.**
- xing2024a, p. 115, Type locality and horizon: "the Guifeng Group consists of the Hekou, Tangbian, and Lianhe formations"; "It is possible that the Zhoutian Formation represents roughly contemporaneous but allogenic deposition with the overlying Hekou Formation (Li et al. 2018). The Zhoutian Formation is generally interpreted as a shallow lacustrine assemblage, whereas the Hekou Formation was likely deposited in a mesoalluvian fan".
- xing2024a, p. 115, Type locality and horizon: the Mazhou fossils lie "in the upper part of the Zhoutian Formation, and the clastic grain size is significantly finer than that of the Hekou Formation".
- li2018a, p. 1120, Fig. 8b: the "stratigraphic framework suggested by this work" for eastern Jiangxi draws the Zhoutian Formation above the Maodian Formation in the Ganzhou Basin column, and the Guifeng Group with its Hekou and Tangbian formations in the Xinjiang Basin column, over the same part of the Upper Cretaceous.

**Ruled out.** Nothing new. The readings of the paper's age label, "Turonian–early Coniacian; Upper Cretaceous; 96–90 Ma", are ruled out in the [Ganzhou](../g/ganzhou.md) entries of 2026-10-03 and 2026-11-23.

**Open.** Nothing.

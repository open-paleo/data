# Zuunbayan

## Does the Zuunbayan entry cover Khand and colleagues' Tamtsag Zuunbayan?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No; that unit is a homonym no record names. The records in the entry, [*Shamosaurus*](../../../genera/S/Shamosaurus.yml) and [*Mongolostegus*](../../../genera/M/Mongolostegus.yml), come from Khamaryn Us in the East Gobi. Governs nothing on [Zuunbayan](../../../stratigraphy/z/zuunbayan.yml).

**Evidence.** None.

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Khosbayar (1996) has not been read.

## Does the Tamtsag basin's Zuunbayan Formation change the age of Zuunbayan?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** No. Khand and colleagues (2000) record a second use of the name: Khosbayar (1996) established a Zuunbayan Formation in the Tamtsag basin of eastern Mongolia, whose description was not published, and Khand and colleagues correlate it with the Shinekhudag and Khukhteeg Formations of the Dornogobi basins. Those are the two formations that Khand and colleagues equate with the lower and upper Zuunbayan of the East Gobi (the 2026-09-30 entry), so the Tamtsag usage spans the same Hauterivian–Albian interval. Governs `stages: [Hauterivian, Barremian, Aptian, Albian]` on [Zuunbayan](../../../stratigraphy/z/zuunbayan.yml).

**Evidence.**
- khand2000a, p. 63: "Khosbayar [6] established in the Tamtsag basin the Tsagaantsav, Zuunbayan, Sainshand and Bayanshiree Formations but the description of them was not published."
- khand2000a, pp. 63–64: "The Zuunbayan Formation is correlative with the Shinekhudag and Khukhteeg Formations in the basins of Dornogobi."
- khand2000a, p. 76: reference 6 is "P. Khosbayar, In: D. Bat-Erdene (ed.), Problems of Geology and Mineral Resources in Mongolia, Special Issue, Ulaanbaatar, (1996) 5."

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Whether the Tamtsag Zuunbayan is the same body of rock as the East Gobi unit or a separate unit that shares its name. Khand and colleagues correlate the two by age only; Khosbayar (1996), which has not been read, would show what the Tamtsag formation includes.

## What is the age of the Dzun Bayn (Zuunbayan) unit?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Hauterivian–Albian, as the sum of the two formations it was later split into. Governs `stages: [Hauterivian, Barremian, Aptian, Albian]` on Zuunbayan.

**Evidence.**
- khand2000a, p. 62: the Shinekhudag Formation is "Hauterivian-Barremian. According to the Vasilev et al. [19], it was the lower Zuunbayan Formation"; p. 63: the "Khukhteeg Formation is considered to be Aptian-Albian in age. According to Vasilev et al. [19], it was the upper Zuunbayan Formation."
- vanitterbeeck2004a, p. 403: "the Dzun Bayn suite, Mongolia, which according to Khand et al. (2000) ranges in age from Hauterivian to Albian".
- benton2000c, p. xxiv: "Dzun Bayan (= Züünbayan)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Ostracod ranges as the unit's age.* Van Itterbeeck and colleagues print "Stratigraphic distribution. Hauterivian–Albian" (p. 398) for individual ostracod species, not for the unit.

**Open.** Narrower readings recorded on the registry entry (Aptian–Albian only; Barremian) were not re-examined for this note.

# Zhangjiawa

## Does Huang and colleagues' (2005) polarity correlation reach the Zhangjiawa Member?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Yes, for all but the member's base. Their Table 2 puts sampling sites 55–151 of the Chuanjie section in the member, and they correlate sites 99–71 with the middle and lower Toarcian and the part above site 103 with the upper Toarcian. Only the interval from the base of the member down through the Shawan Member is too unstable magnetically to correlate; the text sets that base at site 71, the table at site 55. Like the rest of their Lower Jurassic correlation, the Toarcian is "roughly" and has no numerical anchor, and it disagrees with the Sinemurian that Luo and Wu (1994) give the upper faunal assemblage of the Dark Red Beds. Governs the reading of Huang and colleagues (2005) behind `stages: [Hettangian, Sinemurian]` on [Zhangjiawa](../../../stratigraphy/z/zhangjiawa.yml), which this entry leaves unchanged (see Open).

**Evidence.**
- huang2005a, p. 324, Table 2: sampling sites 1–54 are in the Shawan Member and 55–151 in the Zhangjiaao Member.
- huang2005a, p. 325: "The upper part of the Lufeng Formation, above site 103, can roughly be correlated with the alternating-polarity zone of the upper Toarcian, and the horizons of sites 99–71 may correspond to the middle and lower Toarcian"; "The horizons of sites 71–14, that is, from the base of the Zhangjiaao Member through the Shawan Member, have unstable rock magnetism and disordered directions" (translated).
- huang2005a, p. 328: the Lufeng Formation "belongs roughly to the upper Sinemurian through the Toarcian" (translated).
- luo1994a, p. 268: "the upper faunal assemblage is Early Jurassic in age, most probably Sinemurian, and definitely no older than Hettangian".

**Ruled out.**
- *The member lies wholly in Huang and colleagues' uncorrelated interval.* Their unstable interval runs from the base of the member downward, not through it. The sites above it are in the member by their own Table 2, and they correlate those sites with the Toarcian.

**Open.** Whether the Toarcian belongs in the member's stages. Huang and colleagues' polarity correlation puts most of the member in the Toarcian, and Luo and Wu's faunal correlation puts its upper assemblage most probably in the Sinemurian; neither rests on a date from within the member. The Pliensbachian and Toarcian of the parent formation (the 2026-11-22 entry of the [Lufeng](../l/lufeng.md) note) can only come from this member, since the Shawan Member below it stops at the Sinemurian (our inference from the two members' stages). A dated horizon in the member would settle it.

# Zhengyang Formation

## What stages do the Zhengyang and *Qianjiangsaurus* take?

*2026-10-13*

**Conclusion.** Santonian to Maastrichtian, the union of the two papers' readings of the formation's top. Dai and colleagues (2024) find the formation's absolute age unknown and estimate the *Qianjiangsaurus* horizon from the ranges of its relatives, Santonian to early Maastrichtian; Ma and colleagues (2026) put a comparable level in the late Santonian to early Campanian, beside an unpublished date of about 84 Ma that they relay. These date a level inside the formation, so they date the formation too. Governs `stages` on the [Zhengyang Formation](../../../stratigraphy/z/zhengyang.yml) and `stage` on [*Qianjiangsaurus changshengi*](../../../genera/Q/Qianjiangsaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dai2024a, p. 16: "the absolute age of the formation is still unknown"; "The eight taxa, four of which are found in Asia, display an age assemblage spanning the Santonian–early Maastrichtian time interval (Fig. 10). Consequently, the horizon of *Qianjiangsaurus* at the top of the Upper Cretaceous Zhengyang Formation is geochronologically estimated to be within the late half of the Late Cretaceous."
- ma2026a, p. 6: "from the uppermost part of the Zhengyang Formation (see Supplementary Materials); late stage of the Late Cretaceous (~84 Ma: unpublished data by J. Wang)"; elsewhere "during the same time interval (~late Santonian to early Campanian)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Campanian to Maastrichtian.* No paper gives it; it matches only Dai and colleagues' faunal comparison with the Djadokhta and Baruungoyot, which they offer for faunal interchange, not as the horizon's age.
- *Late Santonian to early Campanian alone.* It would drop Dai and colleagues' reading of the same level.

**Open.** A measured date for the formation.

# Foremost

## What do the cited works say about the makeup and age of the Foremost?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian. Eberth (2005) gives the Belly River Group at Dinosaur Provincial Park as the Foremost (170 m), Oldman and Dinosaur Park formations, the Foremost lowest. It is paralic to non-marine and gradationally overlies the marine shales of the Pakowki (Claggett) Formation, with the McKay and Taber coal zones at its bottom and top and the Herronton Sandstone Zone above the Taber. He includes the Herronton in the Foremost, moving the formation's upper boundary up-section from where Eberth and Hamblin (1993) drew it. The base of the formation lies in the *Baculites asperiformis* ammonite biozone, with an estimated age of 80 Ma, which he says concurs with 79.14 ± 0.15 Ma for an altered volcanic ash 30 m above the base. The ash age is credited to a personal communication (A. Deino), so under the rule in [methods](../../methods.md) it adds no reading of its own; both figures fall in the Campanian. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` and `parent: Belly River` on [Foremost](../../../stratigraphy/f/foremost.yml).

**Evidence.**
- eberth2005a, p. 59: the group "is approximately 280 m thick and consists of the Foremost (170 m), Oldman (40 m), and Dinosaur Park (70 m) formations"; "The paralic to non-marine Foremost Formation, which gradationally overlies marine shales of the Pakowki (Claggett) Formation, includes the McKay and Taber coal zones at the bottom and top, respectively, and an extensive sandy zone overlying the Taber Coal Zone referred to here as the Herronton Sandstone Zone".
- eberth2005a, p. 55, Fig. 3.1 caption: "The upper boundary of the Foremost Formation has been adjusted up-section compared to Eberth and Hamblin 1993. Here, the Foremost Formation includes the Herronton Sandstone Zone".
- eberth2005a, pp. 64–65: "The base occurs within the Baculites asperiformis ammonite biozone and has an estimated age of 80 Ma", "which concurs with an age of 79.14 (±0.15) Ma (A. Deino, pers. comm., 1993) for an altered volcanic ash collected 30 m above the base of the Foremost Formation".

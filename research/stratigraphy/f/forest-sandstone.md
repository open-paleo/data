# Forest Sandstone

## Does any stage reach the Forest Sandstone by its own evidence?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No; the formation keeps the Early Jurassic only. Supersedes the 2026-11-25 entry on the stages. Viglietti and colleagues' (2018) "most likely" Sinemurian–Pliensbachian for the *Vulcanodon* stratum rests on their correlation of the upper formation with the Clarens and on the Toarcian lava above it, a limit; their Rhaetian/Hettangian for the lower part is a maximum from fauna shared with the upper Elliot. Raath (1969) dates the deposits "Upper Triassic" from Bond's (1955) correlation with the South African Red Beds, and the Hettangian of Rauhut (2003) and Bristowe and Raath (2004) is the range of a taxon. Cooper (1984) gives *Vulcanodon* an "early Jurassic age" in his own voice, which supports the epoch. [*Vulcanodon karibaensis*](../../../genera/V/Vulcanodon.yml) and [*Megapnosaurus rhodesiensis*](../../../genera/M/Megapnosaurus.yml) take the formation's range. Governs the absent `stages` on [Forest Sandstone](../../../stratigraphy/f/forest-sandstone.yml).

**Evidence.**
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 10: "A Sinemurian–Pliensbachian age seems most likely for this taxon, given its stratigraphic proximity to the Toarcian-aged Lower Basalt".
- raath1969a, p. 1: "on the basis of its fossil fauna Bond (op. cit.) has correlated the Forest Sandstone with the Upper part of the South African Red Beds. The deposits are thus Upper Karroo (Upper Triassic) in age."
- cooper1984a, p. 203: "in view of its early Jurassic age, its characters have considerable phylogenetic significance".

**Ruled out.**
- *Sinemurian and Pliensbachian.* The age of a correlative unit, bounded by the lava above.
- *Hettangian.* A maximum, and a taxon's range.

**Open.** Raath (1972), the *Vulcanodon* type description, has not been read.

## Does the Forest Sandstone take the Rhaetian and the Late Triassic?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** No; both are dropped. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry's Rhaetian to Pliensbachian. Viglietti and colleagues (2018) give the Rhaetian twice, neither time as an age: the fauna of the lower part provides "a maximum age" of Rhaetian/Hettangian for the lower layers, and the *Vulcanodon* stratum "could be as old as the Rhaetian", a possibility resting on that maximum and on Visser's (1984) suggested unconformity, while they judge Sinemurian–Pliensbachian "most likely". A limit adds no stage, and a possibility is not a reading. Raath's (1969) "Upper Triassic" is an early faunal reading made through Bond's (1955) correlation with the upper Red Beds; Viglietti and colleagues make the same faunal correlation, with the Upper Elliot, and reach only a Rhaetian/Hettangian maximum, so Raath's epoch is superseded, as the Kayenta's early faunal Late Triassic was (see [Kayenta](../k/kayenta.md)). Visser's (1984) "Late Triassic" is a label for the Stormberg basins as a whole. The Hettangian rests on Rauhut (2003) and Bristowe and Raath (2004). Governs `period: [Early Jurassic]` and `stages: [Hettangian, Sinemurian, Pliensbachian]` on [Forest Sandstone](../../../stratigraphy/f/forest-sandstone.yml).

**Evidence.**
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 9, 4.3 Revised Stratigraphy for *Vulcanodon karibaensis*: "the presence of the theropod genus *Coelophysis*, and the sauropodomorph species *Massospondylus carinatus* in the lower part of the Forest Sandstone in the Mid-Zambezi, Mana Pools, and Tuli basins (Cooper, 1981) provides some evidence for a maximum age for the lower layers of the Forest Sandstone of Rhaetian/Hettangian".
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 10, same section: "the *Vulcanodon* stratum, which is situated only a few metres below the contact with the Batoka Basalt Formation, could be as old as the Rhaetian or as young as the Pliensbachian. A Sinemurian–Pliensbachian age seems most likely for this taxon".
- raath1969a, p. 1: "on the basis of its fossil fauna Bond (op. cit.) has correlated the Forest Sandstone with the Upper part of the South African Red Beds. The deposits are thus Upper Karroo (Upper Triassic) in age."
- visser1984a, p. 5: "Four Late Triassic depositional basins which were tectonically controlled are recognised."

**Ruled out.**
- *Rhaetian from the maximum age of the lower layers.* A limit.
- *Rhaetian from the possible age of the* Vulcanodon *stratum.* A possibility resting on the same limit, against the authors' own most likely reading.
- *Late Triassic from Raath (1969).* Superseded by the same faunal correlation made since.
- *Late Triassic from Visser (1984).* A label for the Stormberg basins, not a dating of this unit.

**Open.** Unchanged from the 2026-11-23 entry: a radiometric date from the formation or the basal Batoka Basalt would narrow the age.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Forest Sandstone?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Rhaetian to Pliensbachian. Viglietti and colleagues (2018) place the *Vulcanodon* type horizon in the uppermost Forest Sandstone, below the Batoka Basalt, and correlate the upper part of the formation with the Clarens Formation, so that *Vulcanodon* is likely Sinemurian–Pliensbachian. *Coelophysis* and *Massospondylus carinatus* in the lower part give the lower layers a maximum age of Rhaetian/Hettangian. Because Visser (1984) suggested that the northern basins could be older than their main Karoo equivalents and that an unconformity may lie beneath the basalt in the Mid-Zambezi Basin, they allow the *Vulcanodon* stratum to be anywhere from Rhaetian to Pliensbachian, and that span sets the formation's range. Visser (1984) treats the Forest Sandstone as a northern correlative of the Cave Sandstone, which he works in throughout, and places the Stormberg basins in the Late Triassic. Governs `stages` and `period` on [Forest Sandstone](../../../stratigraphy/f/forest-sandstone.yml).

**Evidence.**
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 3, Abstract: "*Vulcanodon* is from lower in the stratigraphy than previously documented, lying within the uppermost Forest Sandstone rather than the interbedded sandstones of the Batoka Basalt Formation. Sedimentological data suggest that the upper part of the Forest Sandstone correlates with the Clarens Formation of the main Karoo Basin, implying that *Vulcanodon* is likely Sinemurian–Pliensbachian in age".
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 9, 4.3 Revised Stratigraphy for *Vulcanodon karibaensis*: *Coelophysis* and *Massospondylus carinatus* in the lower part of the formation provide "some evidence for a maximum age for the lower layers of the Forest Sandstone of Rhaetian/Hettangian".
- viglietti2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 10, 4.3 Revised Stratigraphy for *Vulcanodon karibaensis*: "Visser (1984) postulated that the Stormberg-equivalent units in more northerly basins could be older than their main Karoo equivalents", and "suggested that in the Mid-Zambezi basin, there may be an uncomformity between the Forest Sandstone and the overlying Batoka Basalt formations. Therefore, the *Vulcanodon* stratum ... could be as old as the Rhaetian or as young as the Pliensbachian. A Sinemurian–Pliensbachian age seems most likely for this taxon, given its stratigraphic proximity to the Toarcian-aged Lower Basalt".
- visser1984a, p. 5, Abstract: "The Drakensberg Volcanics with radiometric ages of 114 My to 194 My cap the sedimentary sequence"; "Four Late Triassic depositional basins which were tectonically controlled are recognised".
- visser1984a, p. 16: in Zimbabwe, "Cave Sandstone. (Millet Seed Sandstone, Forest Sandstone".
- visser1984a, p. 19: "Fitch and Miller (1971, p. 64-84) use age determinations to correlate the lavas of the Lebombo and Stormberg regions".
- jourdan2008a, p. 2, 1. Introduction: among the dated events of the Karoo province, "the 1.9 km-thick Lesotho lava pile (181.6 ± 0.7 Ma)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Visser's (1984) 114 to 194 My for the capping lavas as a bound on the formation.* The span comes from the radiometric work of its day, and Visser cites the potassium-argon ages of Fitch and Miller (1971) for the lavas. Jourdan and colleagues (2008) give the Lesotho lava pile of the same Drakensberg Volcanics an argon-argon age of 181.6 ± 0.7 Ma, so Visser's eighty-million-year bracket is superseded, not an open range for the lavas above the sandstone.

**Open.** Viglietti and colleagues (2018, accepted manuscript, p. 9) write that "the lack of detailed faunal correlations and radiometric age assessments preclude the identification of a more precise age for the *Vulcanodon* layer"; a radiometric date from the formation or the basal Batoka Basalt would narrow it.

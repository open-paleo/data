# Fort Crittenden

## Does the Fort Crittenden take a Sonoita group as parent?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No. Stoyanow (1949) named the Sonoita group, with the Fort Buchanan and Fort Crittenden formations, but the later works read, Ratkevich (1998) and D'Emic and colleagues (2016), treat the Fort Crittenden as a formation with no group. **Evidence.** None.

**Ruled out.** - *Sonoita group.* Not taken up by later work.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Fort Crittenden?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian. Sullivan and Lucas (2006) date the formation from K/Ar biotite ages on the volcanics around it: 70–74 Ma on the Salero Formation, which conformably overlies it, and about 75 Ma on volcanics beneath it in the Canelo Hills. They read these as "a Campanian age" of "about 74 Ma", and add a late Campanian age by correlation with the Ringbone Formation of southwestern New Mexico, whose overlying Hidalgo andesites give Ar–Ar ages of about 70.5 and 71.4 Ma. Stoyanow (1949), who divides the Sonoita group into the Fort Buchanan and Fort Crittenden formations, describes the group as fresh-water Upper Cretaceous deposits and gives no stage. He had first taken the Fort Crittenden strata for Tertiary, and dinosaur teeth and bones led him to a "pre-Eocene" age. He compares the Sonoita group with the Cabullona group of northeastern Sonora, without stating a correlation. Governs `stages` and `period` on [Fort Crittenden](../../../stratigraphy/f/fort-crittenden.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sullivan2006c, p. 15: "The Salero Formation volcanic rocks that conformably overlie the Fort Crittenden Formation have yielded K/Ar ages on biotite of 70-74 Ma"; "a biotite K/Ar age of ~ 75 Ma for volcanics beneath the Fort Crittenden Formation in the Canelo Hills. These ages indicate a Campanian age for the Fort Crittenden Formation, and suggest it is about 74 Ma, within the Kirtlandian time interval."
- sullivan2006c, p. 15: the Fort Crittenden "is an obvious correlative" of the Ringbone Formation; ages on the overlying Hidalgo andesites "support a late Campanian age for the Ringbone Formation", "and thus, by correlation, the Fort Crittenden Formation".
- sullivan2006c, p. 14: Stoyanow "reported fossils of fish, turtles, and 'Gorgosaurus' from Upper Cretaceous strata (Fort Crittenden Formation) in Adobe Canyon in the Santa Rita Mountains near Tucson".
- stoyanow1949a, p. 59: "the sequence of fresh-water Upper Cretaceous deposits exposed along the eastern front of the Santa Rita Mountains"; "The Sonoita group readily admits of two divisions: the lower—Fort Buchanan formation, and the upper—Fort Crittenden formation, both names adopted from the near-by ruins of two old forts."
- stoyanow1949a, p. 59: "The Fort Crittenden formation is separated from the Fort Buchanan formation by a comparatively thin conglomeratic bed"; "Like Schrader, I was inclined to regard these strata as Tertiary. However, a find of dinosaurian teeth and bones clearly indicates a pre-Eocene age for this unit."
- stoyanow1949a, p. 59: Barnum Brown, by personal communication, finds that two teeth "are from a carnivorous dinosaur skull of about the size of a large specimen of Gorgosaurus libratus".
- stoyanow1949a, p. 60: "The Sonoita group strongly resembles the Cabullona group" of northeastern Sonora, "in which also occur the freshwater mollusks and dinosaurs".

**Ruled out.**
- *A* Gorgosaurus *record from the formation.* Stoyanow's (1949) teeth were compared, in a personal communication, with a skull "of about the size of a large specimen of Gorgosaurus libratus": a statement about size, not an identification. Sullivan and Lucas (2006) recall the name in quotation marks.
- *Tertiary.* Stoyanow (1949) held this view at first and set it aside on the dinosaur find.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Farmington

## What do Hunt and Lucas (1992) say about the name, type section and origin of the Farmington Member?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Bauer (1916) named the unit the Farmington Sandstone Member, the only one of the Kirtland's four members he named, with its type section on the San Juan River, where it is 139 m thick. Hunt and Lucas (1992) shorten the name to Farmington Member because the unit also holds conglomerate, mudstone and siltstone. They describe it as a clastic wedge of fluvial origin, built from the northwest, whose sandstone lenses thin and become fewer to the southeast, and they say it interfingers with the Hunter Wash Member. Governs `name` and `lithology` on [Farmington](../../../stratigraphy/f/farmington.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hunt1992a, p. 220: "The Farmington Sandstone Member was the only one of the four members of the Kirtland Formation named by Bauer (1916). Since the Farmington Sandstone Member also includes conglomerate, mudstone and siltstone, we refer to it as the Farmington Member."
- hunt1992a, p. 220: "The type section of the Farmington is along the San Juan River ..., where the unit is 139 m thick"; "The Farmington is a clastic wedge originating northwest of the present San Juan Basin"; "Individual sandstone lenses of the Farmington become thinner and less numerous to the southeast".
- hunt1992a, p. 222: "It is clear that the Farmington Member is of fluvial origin".
- hunt1992a, p. 234: "the Farmington Member interfingers with the Hunter Wash Member".

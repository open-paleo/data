# Fleming Fjord

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Fleming Fjord Group?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Norian and Rhaetian, unchanged. Clemmensen and colleagues (2020), who raise the unit to a group, date it "most likely Norian – early Rhaetian". The Norian comes from the vertebrates and from Kent and Clemmensen's (1996) magnetostratigraphy, whose preferred correlation with the Newark sequence puts the Malmros Klint and the lowest 80 m of the Carlsberg Fjord beds at about 211 to 207.5 Ma, "in the late Norian". The Rhaetian comes from the uppermost Tait Bjerg Member, whose ostracods, pelecypods and palynomorphs Kent and Clemmensen report as Rhaetian and Clemmensen and colleagues as "Probably early Rhaetian". Clemmensen and colleagues place the group at about 41° N on northern Pangaea. Governs `stages: [Norian, Rhaetian]` and `period: [Late Triassic]` on [Fleming Fjord](../../../stratigraphy/f/fleming-fjord.yml).

**Evidence.**
- clemmensen2020a, p. 95, Abstract: the new group "is subdivided into three new formations, the Edderfugledal, Malmros Klint and Ørsted Dal Formations (all elevated from members)"; "Most vertebrate bones have been found in uppermost Malmros Klint Formation, and in the Carlsberg Fjord and Tait Bjerg Members. The Norian–early Rhaetian, lacustrine Fleming Fjord Group was deposited at about 41° N on the northern part of the supercontinent Pangaea."
- clemmensen2020a, p. 101: "The Fleming Fjord Group has been dated to the Late Triassic (most likely Norian – early Rhaetian) based on sparse invertebrate fossils, land-derived palynomorphs, vertebrate remains, and palaeomagnetic data".
- clemmensen2020a, p. 112: "The Fleming Group was given a Late Triassic (late Carnian – early Rhaetian) age by Clemmensen (1980b) and Müller et al. (2005). Kent and Clemmensen (1996) placed the main part of the group in the late Norian – early Rhaetian".
- clemmensen2020a, p. 121: the Ørsted Dal Formation records "a relatively short time interval from about 209.5 to about 207 Ma, which according to the most recent views places the unit in the latest Norian (Kent et al. 2017)".
- clemmensen2020a, p. 127: the Tait Bjerg Member is "Probably early Rhaetian as indicated by agediagnostic ostracods and terrestrial palynomorphs".
- clemmensen2020a, p. 130: "from faunal evidence it is not possible to determine whether the uppermost part of the Ørsted Dal Formation is of Rhaetian age and whether the lowermost part of the group, including the Edderfugledal Formation, extends into the Carnian"; "Magnetochronological work supports that the vertebrate bearing red beds of the Fleming Fjord Group are of late Norian age (Kent & Clemmensen 1996)".
- kent1996a, p. 121, Abstract: "A 210 m section of Late Triassic Fleming Fjord Formation (the Malmros Klint Member and the lowermost 80m of the overlying Carlsberg Fjord beds of the Ørsted Dal Member)"; the preferred correlation "suggests that the sampled interval represents about a 3.5 m.y. interval of the late Norian".
- kent1996a, p. 123: "Conchostracans in the Edderfugledal and Malmros Klint Members of the Fleming Fjord Formation suggest a Late Triassic (?Carnian to ?Norian) age, while ostracods, pelecypods, and palynomorphs in the Tait Bjerg Beds indicate that the uppermost part of the Fleming Fjord Formation is Rhaetian in age".
- kent1996a, p. 133: the first option "would imply that the Fleming Fjord section extends from the just within magnetochron E16n (ca. 207.5 Ma) to magnetochron E19n (ca. 211 Ma), representing a duration of about 3.5 m.y. in the late Norian"; the second, "from just within magnetochron E13n (ca. 218 Ma) to just within magnetochron E16n (ca. 212.5 Ma)", in the early Norian; "the first correlation option is preferred".

**Ruled out.**
- *A Carnian base.* Clemmensen and colleagues (2020) report "late Carnian – early Rhaetian" as the age Clemmensen (1980b) and Müller and colleagues (2005) gave the group, then give their own as "most likely Norian – early Rhaetian" and write that faunal evidence cannot show whether the Edderfugledal reaches the Carnian. Clemmensen's later dating narrows his earlier one. Kent and Clemmensen's (1996) conchostracan "?Carnian to ?Norian" is queried at both ends, a hedge rather than a reading.
- *Norian only, from the magnetochronology.* Kent and Clemmensen (1996) sampled the Malmros Klint and the lowest 80 m of the Carlsberg Fjord beds, not the Tait Bjerg Member, so their late Norian and the Ørsted Dal's "latest Norian" that Clemmensen and colleagues (2020) derive from it say nothing against the Tait Bjerg's Rhaetian, which rests on its own ostracods and palynomorphs.

**Open.** Whether the Edderfugledal Formation reaches the Carnian. Clemmensen and colleagues (2020) relay a "late Carnian – early Norian" for it from Müller and colleagues (2005), not read; that paper would show what the Carnian rests on.

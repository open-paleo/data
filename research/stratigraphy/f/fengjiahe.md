# Fengjiahe

## Is Sánchez-Fenollosa and Cobos's (2025) age for the Fengjiahe independent of Yao and colleagues'?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** No. Their "late Sinemurian–Toarcian" for the formation repeats Yao and colleagues (2022) word for word, and their supplement credits Yao and colleagues for it, so it is one reading, not two. Chen and colleagues (1982), the direct source for the formation's whole Lower Jurassic, gain a note on their correlation table. Governs the notes on sánchez-fenollosa2025a and chen1982a on [Fengjiahe](../../../stratigraphy/f/fengjiahe.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- yao2022a, p. 5: "Upper part of the Fengjiahe Formation, near Jiaojiadian village, Yimen County, Yuxi Prefecture, Yunnan Province, China; late Sinemurian–Toarcian (Huang et al., 2005; Figure 1C)."
- sánchez-fenollosa2025a, p. 180: "Y. kopchicki (Fengjiahe Formation, late Sinemurian–Toarcian, China)".
- sánchez-fenollosa2025a, File S1, p. 4: "Chronostratigraphic distribution: upper Sinemurian–Toarcian (Fengjiahe Formation) (Yao et al. 2022)."
- chen1982a, p. 1230, Table 1: one "Fengjiahe Form." cell spans both Lower Jurassic rows, "J₁¹ Hett.-Plies." and "J₁² Dom.-Toar.".
- chen1982a, p. 1232: "The Fengjiahe Formation (or Lower Lufeng Formation) (740—1600 m) now referred to Lower Jurassic".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing beyond the 2026-11-23 entry's.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Fengjiahe Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Hettangian to Toarcian, unchanged: the whole Lower Jurassic. Chen and colleagues (1982) set the formation across both Lower Jurassic rows of their correlation table, "Hett.-Plies." and "Dom.-Toar.", and refer it to the Lower Jurassic in their text. Zhen and colleagues (1985) relay that work as "Hettangian-Pliensbachian", the lower row only. Huang and colleagues (2005) list the Fengjiahe beside the Lufeng Formation under the Lower Jurassic but sample no Fengjiahe beds; their "upper Sinemurian through the Toarcian" is a reading of the Lufeng Formation in the Chuanjie section, which Yao and colleagues (2022) carry to the upper Fengjiahe at Jiaojiadian through the two formations' lateral equivalence. That span lies inside the range and removes nothing from it. Governs `stages: [Hettangian, Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` and `period: [Early Jurassic]` on [Fengjiahe](../../../stratigraphy/f/fengjiahe.yml).

**Evidence.**
- chen1982a, p. 1230, Table 1: in the Kang-Dian column the Fengjiahe Formation fills both Lower Jurassic rows, J₁¹ "Hett.-Plies." and J₁² "Dom.-Toar."; p. 1232: "The Fengjiahe Formation (or Lower Lufeng Formation) (740—1600 m) now referred to Lower Jurassic".
- zhen1985a, p. 144: the fauna of Young's "lower Lufeng series", "now termed the Fengjiahe Formation", was "Long considered ... to be of Late Triassic (Rhaetic) age" and "is now considered to be of Early Jurassic age by most Chinese workers"; "on the basis of non-marine invertebrate fossils (Chen et al., 1982a), Chen et al. (1982b) assigned a Hettangian-Pliensbachian age to the Fengjiahe Formation".
- huang2005a, p. 324, Table 1: the Fengjiahe Formation (J₁f) of the Yipinglang–Yubacun–Mimalong section beside the Lufeng Formation (J₁l) of the Chuanjie section, with its two members, under the Lower Jurassic (translated). The Fengjiahe appears nowhere else in the paper, and Table 2 lists no sampling sites in it.
- huang2005a, p. 325: "The upper part of the Lufeng Formation, above site 103, can roughly be correlated with the alternating-polarity zone of the upper Toarcian"; the sites "from the base of the Zhangjiaao Member through the Shawan Member, have unstable rock magnetism and disordered directions"; "The horizons of sites 21–14 roughly correspond to the middle Sinemurian" (translated).
- huang2005a, p. 326: sites 734–711 of the Yubacun Formation "correspond to the normal polarity zone spanning the Early Jurassic Hettangian to the Late Triassic Rhaetian", and the Yubacun, "the variegated transitional beds between the Upper Triassic Shezi Formation and the Lower Jurassic Lufeng Formation, should be assigned to the Jurassic" (translated).
- huang2005a, p. 328: "the overlying Lufeng Formation then belongs roughly to the upper Sinemurian through the Toarcian" (translated).
- yao2022a, p. 3, Geological setting: "The Fengjiahe Formation is currently thought to be a lateral equivalent of the Lufeng Formation"; p. 4: the Fengjiahe's invertebrate and micropaleontological correlations are "consistent with" a Hettangian–Sinemurian Lufeng, but "more recent magnetostratigraphic evidence posits a younger age for the Lufeng Formation, namely late Sinemurian–Toarcian (Huang et al., 2005)".
- yao2022a, p. 5, Horizon and locality: "Upper part of the Fengjiahe Formation, near Jiaojiadian village, Yimen County, Yuxi Prefecture, Yunnan Province, China; late Sinemurian–Toarcian (Huang et al., 2005; Figure 1C)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Hettangian to Pliensbachian, ending below the Toarcian.* Zhen and colleagues (1985) relay it from Chen and colleagues (1982), whose Table 1 carries the formation through the "Dom.-Toar." row as well; the relay gives only the lower half of the source's range.
- *Late Sinemurian to Toarcian as the formation's span.* Huang and colleagues (2005) give it for the Lufeng Formation of the Chuanjie section and date no Fengjiahe beds, and they could not correlate the Lufeng's lower part, from the base of its upper member down, at all. Yao and colleagues (2022) apply it to the upper Fengjiahe at one locality through the lateral equivalence, which says nothing about the formation's lower beds. The 2026-10-06 entry rules out the same span as a dating of the Yimen beds.
- *A Late Triassic part.* Young (1951) took the lower Lufeng fauna as Rhaetic, a reading that Zhen and colleagues (1985) report most Chinese workers have replaced with the Early Jurassic. Huang and colleagues (2005) name no Norian, and their one Rhaetian is a polarity zone in the Yubacun Formation, below the Fengjiahe.

**Open.** None of the works cited dates the formation's own beds by magnetostratigraphy or radiometric age; the range rests on Chen and colleagues' correlation table.

## Which stages do the Yimen sauropodomorphs take?

*2026-10-06*

**Conclusion.** The formation's full range, Hettangian to Toarcian. Neither describing paper names a stage, and the Pliensbachian the records carried comes from reference works that cite no source for it. Governs `stage: [Hettangian, Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on [*Irisosaurus yimenensis*](../../../genera/I/Irisosaurus.yml) and [*Yimenosaurus youngi*](../../../genera/Y/Yimenosaurus.yml), replacing `[Pliensbachian]` on both, under the rule that a taxon with no statement about its horizon takes its unit's range.

**Evidence.**
- peyredefabrègues2020a, Horizon and locality: "Upper member of the Fengjiahe Formation, Lower Jurassic", at Jiaojiadian area B near Zhanmatian village, Yimen County; no stage appears in the paper.
- bai1990a, Locality and stratigraphic position: "Early Jurassic Fengjiahe Fm. at Jiaojiadian, Yimen Co., Yunnan Province"; no stage.

**Ruled out.**
- *Pliensbachian.* Jones (2026) gives it for *Yimenosaurus* in a table (jones2026b) and "Hettangian/Sinemurian" for *Irisosaurus* from the same locality; Paul (2024) gives it for *Yimenosaurus* (paul2024a). Neither cites a source.
- *Late Sinemurian–Toarcian from the Yuxisaurus site.* Yao and colleagues (2022) give the upper Fengjiahe at Jiaojiadian as "late Sinemurian–Toarcian (Huang et al., 2005)" (yao2022a), relaying a magnetostratigraphic age for the Lufeng Formation, not a dating of these beds.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Farak

## Does the Farak take the Albian?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** No; the Albian and the Early Cretaceous are dropped. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry on the stages and answers its Open line. The Albian rested only on Hunt and colleagues' (1994) appendix, which gives "Alb/Cen" for two sauropods from the "Farak Fm.". The appendix names its sources only for the table as a whole, gives no basis for these rows, and lists both species again as "Alb" in the Continental intercalaire of Niger. A table label is not a reading, and a reading a compilation reports adds a stage only once its original is traced. Sereno and colleagues (2026) date the formation Early to Middle Cenomanian on its vertebrate fauna, below the Cenomanian–Turonian limestone, and give the Albian to the Tégama Group formations beneath it, after Taquet (1976). Governs `period: [Late Cretaceous]`, `stages: [Cenomanian]` and the note on hunt1994a on [Farak](../../../stratigraphy/f/farak.yml); [*Rugops primus*](../../../genera/R/Rugops.yml), [*Spinosaurus mirabilis*](../../../genera/S/Spinosaurus.yml) and [*Carcharodontosaurus iguidensis*](../../../genera/C/Carcharodontosaurus.yml), all Cenomanian, are unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- hunt1994a, p. ?, appendix table under "EARLY CRETACEOUS" (no folio printed): "Alb/Cen | Dicr | Rebbachisaurus tamesnensis | Farak Fm. | Niger" and "Alb/Cen | Titan | Aegyptosaurus baharijensis | Farak Fm. | Niger".
- hunt1994a, p. ?, same table: both species again as "Alb", the first in the "CI" of Algeria, Niger and Tunisia, the second in the "CI" of Niger.
- sereno2026a, supplementary materials, p. 8: "Following Taquet (15), the Tégama Group was divided into three formations (Tazolé, Elrhaz, Echkar) ending in the Albian and followed by a fourth formation (Farak) underlying the marine platform and regarded as Cenomanian."
- sereno2026a, supplementary materials, p. 10: "Thus, vertebrate biostratigraphic correlation suggests an Early to Middle Cenomanian age for both the Kem Kem Group in Morocco and the Iguidi and Jenguebi fossil areas in Niger."

**Ruled out.**
- *Albian from Hunt and colleagues (1994).* A compilation table's label, with no source for the row.

**Open.** Faure (1966), who defined the formation, has not been read; it would show the age the formation was first given.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Farak?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian, from Sereno and colleagues (2026). They take the formation from Faure's section at the Farak falaise north of Tanout, directly below the Cenomanian–Turonian limestone, assign the Jenguebi and Iguidi (In Abangharit) fossil areas to it, and date its vertebrate fauna Early to Middle Cenomanian by comparison with the Kem Kem Group of Morocco. Their "~95 million years ago" is an estimate from position, not a measured age: they note that no terrestrial Cretaceous formation in Niger or Morocco has been dated radioisotopically, and place the beds at "~95 Mya or older" because they lie below the limestone, whose base they tie to the Cenomanian–Turonian boundary at 93.9 Ma and the transgression peak at about 94.5 Ma. The Albian in `stages` rests on the "Alb/Cen" of Hunt and colleagues' (1994) table alone (see Open). Governs the Cenomanian in `stages` on [Farak](../../../stratigraphy/f/farak.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sereno2026a, p. 782, Research Article Summary, Rationale: the Jenguebi area "was discovered in beds equivalent in age [Farak Formation; Cenomanian ~95 million years ago (Mya)] to those yielding fossil remains of *S. aegyptiacus*".
- sereno2026a, p. 1 of 10, Type locality and horizon: the holotype "was found in Sirig Taghat ..., Jenguebi area, Agadez Region, Republic of the Niger, Farak Formation"; the Mesozoic sediments south of the Aïr Massif in the Iullemeden Basin, "initially informally described as "Continentale intercalaire"", include "four successive terrestrial formations of Cretaceous age, Tazolé, Elrhaz, Echkar, and Farak", "the last underlying the well-dated Cenomanian-Turonian limestone" (continued on p. 2 of 10).
- sereno2026a, p. 2 of 10, Type locality and horizon: "We conclude that both the Jenguebi and Iguidi (In Abangharit) fossil areas represent exposures of the Farak Formation"; their fauna "compares closely with the Kem Kem Group of Morocco", "Early to Middle Cenomanian, 100 to 95 million years ago (Mya)".
- sereno2026a, supplementary materials, p. 6: the Farak "is the fossil-bearing unit preserving the remains of *S. mirabilis* and its associated Early-Middle Cenomanian vertebrate fauna".
- sereno2026a, supplementary materials, p. 8: "Following Taquet (15), the Tégama Group was divided into three formations (Tazolé, Elrhaz, Echkar) ending in the Albian and followed by a fourth formation (Farak) underlying the marine platform and regarded as Cenomanian."
- sereno2026a, supplementary materials, p. 10: "None of the terrestrial Cretaceous formations in either Morocco or Niger have been radioisotopically dated"; the beds below the platform "would be estimated to be ~95 Mya or older"; "vertebrate biostratigraphic correlation suggests an Early to Middle Cenomanian age for both the Kem Kem Group in Morocco and the Iguidi and Jenguebi fossil areas in Niger".
- sereno2026a, supplementary materials, p. 11: "we moved downsection from the Cenomanian-Turonian limestone to the Farak falaise"; Faure's sections there "did not yield vertebrate fossils but did preserve abundant fossil wood".
- hunt1994a, p. 274, Early Cretaceous table: *Rebbachisaurus tamesnensis* and *Aegyptosaurus baharijensis*, "Farak Fm.", Niger, stage "Alb/Cen".

**Ruled out.** Nothing beyond the Echkar for the Iguidi beds, which the 2026-10-23 entry in [Echkar](../e/echkar.md) rules out.

**Open.** Whether the Albian stands. Hunt and colleagues (1994) give "Alb/Cen" in a compilation table without an argument, and no other work cited gives the formation an Albian; Sereno and colleagues' Albian belongs to the Tégama formations below it. Faure (1966), who defined the formation, would show the age it was first given.

# Frontier

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Frontier?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian to Coniacian. Merewether and colleagues (1979), the specialist treatment of the formation in eastern Wyoming, resolve it into three members, the Belle Fourche (Cenomanian), an unnamed member (early and middle Turonian) and the Wall Creek (late Turonian and early Coniacian); their Wall Creek is the source of the Coniacian. Merewether and colleagues (1984) give the same range in southwestern Wyoming: Cenomanian, Turonian and early Coniacian along the overthrust belt, and middle Turonian to early Coniacian for the upper part of the formation on the Moxa arch, with the basal Chalk Creek Member "largely of Cenomanian and earliest Turonian age". The base stays in the Cenomanian for the reason given in the 2026-09-30 entry on [Belle Fourche](../b/belle-fourche.md): the Albian–Cenomanian boundary lies below the Mowry Shale. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian, Coniacian]` and `period: [Late Cretaceous]` on [Frontier](../../../stratigraphy/f/frontier.yml).

**Evidence.**
- merewether1979a, p. 67, Abstract: "Most of the lower Upper Cretaceous rocks in eastern Wyoming are of marine origin and contain invertebrate fossils of Cenomanian, Turonian, and Coniacian age"; "The Frontier Formation in eastern Wyoming is divided into as many as three members, which are, from older to younger, the Belle Fourche Member of Cenomanian age, the unnamed member of early and middle Turonian age, and the Wall Creek Member of late Turonian and early Coniacian age".
- merewether1984a, p. 1, Abstract: "At north-trending outcrops along the eastern edge of the overthrust belt, the Frontier is of Cenomanian, Turonian, and early Coniacian age"; there it "conformably overlies the Lower Cretaceous Aspen Shale", while on the north flank of the Uinta Mountains "These strata disconformably overlie the Lower Cretaceous Mowry Shale"; on the Moxa arch "the upper part of the Frontier is of middle Turonian to early Coniacian age", and the formation "conformably overlies the Mowry".
- merewether1984a, p. 5, Stratigraphy, Overthrust belt: "The Chalk Creek is largely of Cenomanian and earliest Turonian age and apparently is correlative with the Belle Fourche Shale and part of the Greenhorn Formation of northeastern Wyoming"; "the basal part of the Chalk Creek in some areas could be Early Cretaceous, as proposed by Ryer (1977b) for the member in northeastern Utah. Strata typical of the lower part of the Chalk Creek seemingly intertongue with the upper part of the Aspen Shale at outcrops on the east side of the overthrust belt (J. W. M'Gonigle and T. A. Ryer, oral commun., 1980) and in the subsurface along the Moxa arch".
- singer2020a, p. 1670: "Only recently ... has the placement of the Albian–Cenomanian boundary below the Mowry Shale/Fish Scales Formation been widely accepted".

**Ruled out.**
- *An Early Cretaceous base, from the Chalk Creek Member.* Merewether and colleagues (1984) give it as a possibility ("could be"), after Ryer (1977), resting on the lower Chalk Creek's apparent intertonguing with the upper Aspen Shale, reported partly from an oral communication. "Lower Cretaceous" for the Aspen and the Mowry follows the former placement of the Albian–Cenomanian boundary at the top of the Mowry; with the boundary below the Mowry, as Singer and colleagues (2020) record, beds at the Frontier's base are Cenomanian. That the Aspen is the western equivalent of the Mowry, so that the same holds for it, is an inference from the two units' parallel positions beneath the Frontier in Merewether and colleagues (1984); no work cited states it. This extends the 2026-09-30 Belle Fourche entry's ruling on the same reading.

**Open.** Ryer (1977), which Merewether and colleagues cite as 1977b, has not been read; it would show what evidence the Early Cretaceous possibility rests on.

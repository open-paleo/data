# Santa Maria

## Which faunal and radiometric arguments give the Santa Maria its Ladinian and its Carnian?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The two 2026-10-01 entries stand: the upper, *Hyperodapedon*, part is Carnian by U-Pb, and the lower, *Dinodontosaurus*, part is Ladinian to early Carnian by inference. This entry records the arguments behind each. Colbert (1970) framed the question for the fauna as a whole: its cynodonts and pseudosuchians point to the Ladinian and its rhynchosaurs and dinosaurs to the Carnian, and he chose the Carnian. Langer and colleagues (2018) date the top of the formation at Cerro da Alemoa, the type section of the Alemoa Member. For the lower zone, Martinelli and colleagues (2017) infer a late Ladinian to early Carnian age from the Chañares correlation and a date on the overlying Santa Cruz Sequence, and Novas and colleagues (2021) give the reason the zone may span both stages: the Chañares holds an earliest Carnian zone and an older zone inferred to reach the Ladinian, and the Brazilian zone has taxa close to both. Governs `stages: [Ladinian, Carnian]` on [Santa Maria](../../../stratigraphy/s/santa-maria.yml).

**Evidence.**
- colbert1970a, p. 28: "The Santa Maria Formation has been regarded either as of Middle Triassic or Late Triassic age".
- colbert1970a, p. 29: Caster (1952) was "more equivocal about the Santa Maria Formation, indicating it covered a considerable time sequence, from the final stages of the Early Triassic through the Late Triassic"; Cox (1965) "suggested upon the basis of his review of the evidence, that the Santa Maria Formation might be of Ladinian age, antedating slightly the Carnian Ischigualasto Formation".
- colbert1970a, p. 34: the choice is "between the evidence of the cynodonts and the pseudosuchians on the one hand, which favors a possible late Middle Triassic or Ladinian age, as against the evidence of the rhynchosaurs and the dinosaurs on the other hand, which favors a possible Upper Triassic or Carnian age".
- colbert1970a, p. 36, Conclusions: the Santa Maria and Ischigualasto faunas "can very logically be placed within the lower or Carnian phase of the Upper Triassic", which is "more appropriate than an assignment of the Santa Maria assemblage to the late Middle Triassic on the basis of genera representing evolutionary lines persisting from earlier stages of Triassic history".
- langer2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 1, Abstract: "a weighted mean 206Pb/238U date of 233.23 ± 0.73 Ma (2σ complete uncertainty) from the Santa Maria Formation".
- langer2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 3: Cerro da Alemoa "is the typesection of the Alemoa Member of the Santa Maria Formation".
- langer2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 8: "Better age constraints on the Dinodontosaurus Assemblage-Zone in Brazil, which is supposed to be coeval with the Chañares fauna, but older than the Santacruzodon AZ (Abdala and Ribeiro, 2010), will shed more light at these unexpected temporal relationships".
- martinelli2017a, p. 527: "The age of the Dinodontosaurus AZ is inferred on the basis of biostratigraphic correlations with the Chañares Formation of the Ischigualasto-Villa Unión Basin, western Argentina (Fiorelli et al. 2013; Marsicano et al. 2016) and radiometric dating of the overlying Santa Cruz Sequence (Philipp et al. 2013), both suggesting a late Ladinian–early Carnian age".
- novas2021a, accepted manuscript, pp. 11–12: "Radioisotopic ages constrained the Massetognathus-Chanaresuchus AZ to 236.1 ± 0.6–233.7 ± 0.4 Ma (earliest Carnian; Marsicano et al., 2016; Ezcurra et al., 2017) and, as a consequence, the older Tarjadia AZ is inferred to extend into the Ladinian".
- novas2021a, accepted manuscript, p. 12: the Brazilian assemblage "also includes species very closely related to those restricted to the Tarjadia AZ in the Chañares Formation"; "The taxonomic similarities of both AZs of the Chañares Formation with the whole Dinodontosaurus AZ of Brazil suggest that the latter may also include two different faunal associations".

**Ruled out.**
- *Carnian only, from Colbert (1970).* Colbert treats the Santa Maria fauna as one assemblage and places it in the Carnian on its dinosaurs and rhynchosaurs. The works that divide the formation into assemblage zones date only the upper zone directly, and give the lower one a Ladinian to early Carnian age by correlation with the Chañares; his reading does not address that lower zone.

**Open.** Nothing beyond the 2026-10-01 entries: a direct date for the *Dinodontosaurus* zone, which Langer and colleagues (2018) also call for.

## Should the Santa Maria Formation keep the Ladinian?

*2026-10-01*

Answers the open point of the entry below.

**Conclusion.** Yes, as an inference: the lower, *Dinodontosaurus*, part has no direct date, but its own-voice correlations give a Ladinian–early Carnian age. Governs the Ladinian in `stages: [Ladinian, Carnian]` on Santa Maria.

**Evidence.**
- martinelli2017a, p. 527: "The age of the Dinodontosaurus AZ is inferred … both suggesting a late Ladinian–early Carnian age", from the Chañares correlation and a dated level.
- pavanatto2018a, pp. 179–180: "(Pinheiros-Chiniquá Sequence, Ladinian–early Carnian)"; the zone "might have been deposited during Ladinian to early Carnian".
- novas2021a, accepted manuscript, p. 12: the zone "may also include two different faunal associations", and "the older Tarjadia AZ is inferred to extend into the Ladinian (Ezcurra et a., 2017, 2021b)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Carnian only for the lower part.* Sues and Schoch's "Carnian-age Dinodontosaurus and Santacruzodon assemblage zones" (sues2025a, p. 469) is weighed against the three own-voice Ladinian–early Carnian readings above.

**Open.** A direct date for the *Dinodontosaurus* zone.

## What is the age of the Santa Maria Formation?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** The upper part (*Hyperodapedon* Assemblage Zone) is late Carnian by U-Pb; the lower part (*Dinodontosaurus* Assemblage Zone) is traditionally Ladinian and not re-examined here. Governs `stages: [Ladinian, Carnian]` on Santa Maria, `stages: [Carnian]` on its [Alemoa Member](../../../stratigraphy/a/alemoa.yml), and `stage: [Carnian]` on [*Saturnalia tupiniquim*](../../../genera/S/Saturnalia.yml), [*Bagualosaurus agudoensis*](../../../genera/B/Bagualosaurus.yml) and [*Pampadromaeus barberenai*](../../../genera/P/Pampadromaeus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- langer2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 6: 233.23 ± 0.73 Ma "serves as the best maximum estimate for the depositional age of the bone bed near the top of the Santa Maria Formation".
- langer2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 7: the bone bed is at "Cerro da Alemoa (type-locality of Saturnalia tupiniquim)", and the date "is consistent with a late Carnian (Tuvalian) age for their associated fauna". The date "supports the correlation of the Hyperodapedon Assemblage-Zone (AZ) of the Santa Maria Formation to the Scaphonyx-Exaeretodon-Herrerasaurus biozone" of the Ischigualasto Formation.
- langer2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 7: 225.42 ± 0.37 Ma is "the maximum age of the Caturrita Formation", which is "early Norian (Lacian)". The Norian lies in the overlying unit, not in the Santa Maria.

**Ruled out.**
- *Upper Anisian or lower Ladinian for the whole formation.* Reig (1963) assigns the Santa Maria fauna "tentativamente, al Anisiano superior o al Ladiniano inferior" ("tentatively, to the upper Anisian or lower Ladinian", translated; reig1963a, p. 19), by faunal correlation. It predates radiometric dating and does not address the *Hyperodapedon* beds dated above.
- *"Probably Middle Triassic."* Cox (1965) gives "the Santa Maria Formation, of probably Middle Triassic age" (cox1965a, p. 477), with no dating of its own.
- *Early Triassic.* This reading does not come from Caster (1952), who puts "the Santa Maria episode … more nearly equivalent to the Middle Triassic hiatus and the Molteno" (caster1952a, p. 143). It comes from a reading of the wide spread his correlation chart gives the Santa Maria (fig. 15; "an enormous spread on the correlation table", p. 142), relayed in Colbert (1970).

**Open.** The age of the lower, *Dinodontosaurus*, part. Sues and Schoch (2025) call it "the Carnian-age Dinodontosaurus and Santacruzodon assemblage zones of the Santa Maria Supersequence" (sues2025a, p. 469), writing of the supersequence rather than the formation, and Langer and colleagues (2018, accepted manuscript, p. 8) say the *Dinodontosaurus* zone "is supposed to be coeval with the Chañares fauna". Whether the Ladinian should go was not examined for this note.

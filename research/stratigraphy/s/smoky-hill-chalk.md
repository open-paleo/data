# Smoky Hill Chalk

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Smoky Hill Chalk?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Late Coniacian to early Campanian, which gives Coniacian, Santonian and Campanian. Hattin (1982) dates the member in its Kansas type area from the Western Interior inoceramid and ammonite zones, each correlated with a stage: the base lies close to the Lower/Upper Coniacian boundary and the top is Lower Campanian. Clarke (2004), following Stewart and colleagues (1990), gives the same span. Governs `stages: [Coniacian, Santonian, Campanian]` on [Smoky Hill Chalk](../../../stratigraphy/s/smoky-hill-chalk.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hattin1982a, p. 1, Abstract: "Strata of the Smoky Hill Chalk Member (Coniacian-Campanian) are exposed extensively in the type area, where 12 key sections are the basis for a composite section that is 181.8 m (596.3 ft) thick."
- hattin1982a, p. 27, Chronologic Comparison of Pueblo, Colorado, and Kansas Sections: "in the Smoky Hill composite section this contact lies approximately at the base of the I. (Volviceramus) grandis Range Zone"; "This places the contact close to the Lower/Upper Coniacian boundary"; the uppermost chalk of southeastern Colorado and "equivalent parts of the Smoky Hill Member in Kansas" have not yielded diagnostic fossils, "but these beds are also assignable to the Lower Campanian because in both areas Lower Campanian fossils are known in slightly younger strata of the Pierre".
- hattin1982a, p. 27, Table 2: "Standard Western Interior biostratigraphic zones", each assigned to a stage and substage, from the Upper Turonian to the Lower Campanian.
- clarke2004a, p. 30: "The entire Smoky Hill Chalk Member of the Niobrara Formation is upper Coniacian–lower Campanian in age (Stewart et al., 1990)"; "Stewart (1990) estimated the interval containing Hattin's (1982) Marker Units 8–10 to be upper Santonian in age. Marker Units 15–20 must therefore be, at the earliest, from later in the upper Santonian through the early Campanian."
- clarke2004a, p. 30: "Bennett (1990) concluded that, historically, nearly all of the fossil vertebrates, including the YPM material, were collected in the upper part of Smoky Hill Chalk Member from between Marker Unit 15 and 20 of Hattin (1982)."
- clarke2004a, p. 47: "The top of the Smoky Hill Chalk Member, close to Marker Unit 23 of Hattin (1982), is in the early Campanian (Stewart et al., 1990)."

**Ruled out.**
- *A late Santonian to early Campanian age for the member.* That is Clarke's (2004) estimate for Marker Units 15 to 20 of Hattin (1982), the interval that yielded nearly all the fossil vertebrates according to Bennett (1990). It dates part of the member, which reaches down to near the Lower/Upper Coniacian boundary, and is not a dating of the whole.

**Open.** Nothing.

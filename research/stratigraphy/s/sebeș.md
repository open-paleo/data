## Does the Sebeș begin in the latest Campanian?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Possibly, at Petrești-Arini. Answers the first point of the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Vremir and colleagues (2014) date the marine Bozeș Formation below to the mid late Campanian by nannofossils and, from continuous sedimentation through the brackish beds at its top, conclude that the Sebeș "began in this area sometime during the latest Campanian–earliest Maastrichtian". They give the formation no date from within it. Their "perhaps continues into the Paleocene" for the top is a possibility and adds no epoch. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Sebeș](../../../stratigraphy/s/sebeș.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- vremir2014a, p. 31: the Bozeș Formation at the site "falls within the CC22/UC15 … Zone, which is mid late Campanian in age (Ogg and Hinnov, 2012)".
- vremir2014a, p. 31: "it can be most parsimoniously concluded that deposition of the continental vertebrate-bearing Sebeş Formation began in this area sometime during the latest Campanian–earliest Maastrichtian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Paleocene.* Raised as a possibility only.

**Open.** Whether the top reaches the end of the Maastrichtian, as before.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Sebeș?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Maastrichtian, possibly from the latest Campanian. Csiki-Sava and colleagues (2016) date the formation by its position: its lower, transitional beds lie conformably on marine deposits whose nannoplankton show that marine deposition ended before the end of the Campanian, which makes the lower part early Maastrichtian, and palynology from the lower part of the continental succession agrees. They allow that deposition "already started during the latest Campanian" in the Petrești area, citing Vremir and colleagues (2014). No magnetostratigraphic or radiometric age is available. The top is poorly constrained: from the thickness of the formation they judge it "possible that most of the Maastrichtian is represented", but the upper part is cut by erosional unconformities beneath Priabonian red beds and Langhian marine beds. Díez Díaz and colleagues (2025) give "uppermost Campanian–upper Maastrichtian", citing Vremir (2010) and Vremir and colleagues (2015); that range repeats the same group's work rather than adding a dating. The Campanian in `stages` rests on the latest-Campanian start at Petrești, which Csiki-Sava and colleagues give only as "conceivable". Governs `stages` on [Sebeș](../../../stratigraphy/s/sebeș.yml).

**Evidence.**
- csiki-sava2016a, p. 676: "age constraints for the Sebeş Formation rely mainly on their conformable position atop of fossiliferous marine deposits; neither magnetostratigraphic nor radiometric ages are as yet available".
- csiki-sava2016a, p. 676: "recent detailed nannoplankton data suggest that fully marine deposition ended at least locally before the end of the Campanian".
- csiki-sava2016a, p. 676: "the early Maastrichtian age of the transitional and continental beds from the lower Sebeş Formation, sitting on top of and conformably overlying these upper Campanian deposits, is rather well established. It is also conceivable that in the Petreşti area their deposition already started during the latest Campanian"; "The Maastrichtian age of the Sebeş Formation is also supported by palynological data reported from the lower part of the continental succession".
- csiki-sava2016a, p. 677: from the thickness ("over 450 m on the left side of the Mureş River, and close to 1000 m on the right side") "it is possible that most of the Maastrichtian is represented. Unfortunately, upper age constraints are poor, as the coarser-grained upper part of the Sebeş Formation is covered along erosional unconformities by upper Eocene (Priabonian) continental red beds in the Alba Iulia area, and by middle Miocene (Langhian …) marine deposits in the Sebeş area".
- díezdíaz2025a, p. 5, Geological setting: "the major fossil-bearing unit is the Sebeş Formation (uppermost Campanian–upper Maastrichtian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Miocene.* The beds were first "formalized as the lower Miocene Sebeş"; "the Miocene age of the Sebeş Formation was reconsidered based on definitively in situ vertebrate (including dinosaur) fossils" (csiki-sava2016a, p. 676).

**Open.** Whether the formation begins in the latest Campanian. Vremir and colleagues (2014), on the Petrești-Arini section, would settle it; it has not been read. Whether the top reaches the end of the Maastrichtian is unresolved in the works cited.

## Is the Șard Formation another name for the Sebeș?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** Yes. Csiki-Sava and colleagues (2015) equate the two in their own captions, crediting a photograph to Vlad Codrea. Their bibliography lists a 2012 paper on "the Şard Formation" of the same south-western Transylvanian Basin. Governs `variants: [Șard]` on [Sebeș](../../../stratigraphy/s/sebeș.yml).

**Evidence.**
- csiki-sava2015a, p. 61, Fig. 10C: *Allodaposuchus precedens* "(Sebeş = Şard Formation, Oarda de Jos, southwestern Transylvanian Basin; photo by Vlad Codrea/Massimo Delfino)"; Fig. 10H: *Balaur bondoc* "(Sebeş = Şard Formation, Sebeş-Glod, southewestern Transylvanian Basin …)".
- csiki-sava2015a, p. 138: Mariş (2012), "The sedimentology of the Upper Cretaceous continental deposits from the south-western part of the Transylvanian Basin, the Şard Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Şard and Sebeş, with cedillas, as further variants.* The cedilla forms are the usual substitution for the comma-below letters in Romanian typesetting, not another name. The registry keeps the comma-below forms, as in its file name.

**Open.** Nothing.

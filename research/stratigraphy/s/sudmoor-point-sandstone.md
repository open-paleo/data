# Sudmoor Point Sandstone

## Where does the Hauterivian–Barremian boundary fall relative to the Sudmoor Point Sandstone?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Just above it, on the two works that place the boundary in the section, so the unit takes the Hauterivian as well as the Barremian, with the question held in the dispute. Jacobs and colleagues (2023) report unpublished paleomagnetic results that put the boundary "a short distance above the Sudmoor Point Sandstone". Robinson and Hesselbo (2004) begin their carbon-isotope section at Sudmoor Point, call its base "possibly late Hauterivian or early Barremian", and draw the boundary on their Fig. 7 at a composite depth of about −130 m, above their lowest Sudmoor Point samples at about −155 m. That the Sudmoor Point Sandstone lies among those lowest samples is our inference from Stewart's (1981) placing of the member near the base of the exposed Wealden. Jacobs and colleagues' U–Pb date of 127.3 ± 2.7 Ma falls in the Hauterivian at its central value both on the ICS chart v2024/12 (base of the Barremian 125.77 Ma) and at the 126.5 Ma that Lockwood and colleagues (2021) give for the boundary, and its error spans both stages; it comes from a turtle found loose at Brook Bay, not from this unit. Governs `stages: [Hauterivian, Barremian]` and the `dispute` on [Sudmoor Point Sandstone](../../../stratigraphy/s/sudmoor-point-sandstone.yml).

**Evidence.**
- jacobs2023a, p. 14, 5.2. Implications of calcite U–Pb geochronology for the Wessex Formation: "Recent palaeomagnetic studies demonstrate that the boundary between chrons CM3n and CM5r, coincident with the Hauterivian–Barremian boundary, fall a short distance above the Sudmoor Point Sandstone (Conall Mc Niocaill pers comm. 2021)"; "The yielded U–Pb age of 127.3 ± 2.7 Ma suggests that the turtle specimen is of youngest Hauterivian or oldest in Barremian age"; "This confirms that the Hauterivian–Barremian boundary falls below the Hanover Point Sandstone and above the Sudmoor Point Sandstone."
- jacobs2023a, p. 1, Abstract: "The specimen was found ex-situ"; "the first radiometric date for the Wessex Formation of 127.3 ± 2.7 Ma".
- jacobs2023a, p. 3, 2. Locality and geological context: the specimen "was collected from the foreshore at Brook Bay".
- robinson2004a, pp. 136–137: "The section from Sudmoor Point to Barnes High exposes c. 180 m of the Wessex Formation, the base of which is possibly late Hauterivian or early Barremian in age (Fig. 4)".
- robinson2004a, p. 140, Fig. 7: the composite Isle of Wight curve is correlated with the Cismon core, with the Hauterivian–Barremian line at about −130 m and the Isle of Wight data reaching to about −155 m.

**Ruled out.**
- *Robinson and Hesselbo's boundary as about 70 m above the base of the exposed Wessex.* Jacobs and colleagues (2023, p. 14) relay it so, as a "tentative" placement "higher" than the palynological one. Robinson and Hesselbo's text puts it "close to the base of the exposed Wessex Formation" (pp. 141–142, quoted in the 2026-11-23 entry in [Wessex](../w/wessex.md)), and their Fig. 7 puts it some 25 m above their lowest sample.

**Open.** The paleomagnetic placement is a personal communication; a published magnetostratigraphy of the Sudmoor Point section would settle the stage. Whether the lowest Sudmoor Point samples of Robinson and Hesselbo come from the Sudmoor Point Sandstone itself is not stated in their text.

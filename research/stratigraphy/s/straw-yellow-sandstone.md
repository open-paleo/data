## Does Zhang and colleagues' (2009) Baojishan–Honghui paper date the member?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** No. Answers the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Zhang and colleagues (2009), which Li and colleagues (2019, 2024) cite for the late Bathonian, name the member once, as the lower part of the Wangjiashan Formation, about 240 m thick, and give it no age; their Fig. 2 dates the formation as a whole, Bajocian to early Callovian (see [Wangjiashan](../w/wangjiashan.md)). No original cited for the late Bathonian names that stage. Li and colleagues' (2024) Bathonian, on the member's own plants and palynoflora and the invertebrates of the overlying member, is a published reading and stands. Governs `stages: [Bathonian]` on [Straw-yellow Sandstone](../../../stratigraphy/s/straw-yellow-sandstone.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- zhang2009c, p. 624: "the Wangjiashan Formation is divided into a lower straw-yellow sandstone member and an upper oil shale member. The former is relatively stable in thickness, generally 240 m" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Bajocian to Callovian for the member, from the formation's span in Fig. 2.* The figure does not divide the members, and a parent's age does not date its child.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the definition and age of the Straw-yellow Sandstone?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Li and colleagues (1982) proposed the member as the lower part of the Wangjiashan Formation, a name they credit to the No. 133 Coalfield Exploration Team (1976): straw-yellow quartz-feldspar sandstone with interbedded purple sandy shale, 476.75 m thick in the Shuidonggou–Kushuixia section, with a 20–60 m grayish-white fine conglomerate at its base that rests disconformably on the Longfengshan Formation. They date it only as Middle Jurassic. The late Bathonian comes from Li and colleagues (2019) and Li and colleagues (2024), who give it on the plants and palynoflora of the member and the invertebrates of the overlying Oil Shale Member, and cite it to Li and colleagues (1982), Du (1985) and Zhang (2009). Of the two of those works that were checked, Li and colleagues (1982) give Middle Jurassic, and Du (1985), on the member's spores and pollen, gives "late Middle Jurassic"; neither names the Bathonian. The Zhang (2009) that both papers cite is Zhang's paper on the sedimentary environments of the Baojishan–Honghui Basin, not Zhang's (2009) lexicon of Chinese formation names. The Bathonian therefore rests on the two papers of Li and colleagues, whose sources give a broader late Middle Jurassic. Governs `stages: [Bathonian]` and `lithology` on [Straw-yellow Sandstone](../../../stratigraphy/s/straw-yellow-sandstone.yml).

**Evidence.**
- li1982a, p. 38: "It was first named by the No. 133 Coalfield Exploration Team (1976), with its type locality at Shuidonggou"; "in this paper it is proposed that the lower part be named the Straw-yellow Sandstone Member and the upper part the Oil Shale Member" (translated).
- li1982a, p. 38: "a thick sequence of straw-yellow quartz-feldspar sandstone"; "Its base is bounded by a 20–60 m thick layer of grayish-white fine conglomerate, which is in disconformable contact with the underlying Longfengshan Formation" (translated).
- li1982a, p. 37, unnumbered composite column: "Wangjiashan Formation, Straw-yellow Sandstone Member", thickness "476.75" m, age "Middle Jurassic" (translated).
- li1982a, p. 39: "In summary, the Wangjiashan Formation is of Middle Jurassic age" (translated).
- du1985a, p. 135: "its age can likewise be assigned to the Middle Jurassic; but it should be said to have reached the late Middle Jurassic" (translated).
- du1985a, p. 140, Abstract: "these assemblages should be assigned to Middle Jurassic age and may correspond to those of the Yanan Formation and the Zhiluo Formation".
- li2019b, p. 2: "The 607-m-thick Wangjiashan Formation was informally divided into two members"; "The lower member is composed of straw-yellow sandstones (477 m thick) which were deposited mainly in a lake deltaic setting"; "the underlying straw-yellow sandstone member is thought to have been formed in the late Bathonian (Li et al. 1982; Du 1985; Zhang 2009)".
- li2019b, p. 11, References: "Zhang, H. 2009. Sedimentary environments and coal accumulation of the Baojishan-Honghui Basin, eastern Qilian Mountains. Acta Sedimentologica Sinica 27 (4): 622–631".
- li2024a, p. 2, Geological setting: "the Straw-yellow Sandstone Member of the Wangjiashan Formation is considered to be late Bathonian in age".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Zhang (2009) that Li and colleagues (2019) cite for the late Bathonian is the lexicon of Chinese formation names.* Their reference list gives Zhang's paper in Acta Sedimentologica Sinica on the Baojishan–Honghui Basin. The lexicon's Wangjiashan Formation entry gives only "Mid Jurassic" and has no entry for the member.
- *Li and colleagues (1982) or Du (1985) as a source of a Bathonian date.* Neither names a stage: the first gives Middle Jurassic, the second late Middle Jurassic.

**Open.** No work checked here states the Bathonian except the two papers of Li and colleagues, so whether the member is Bathonian rather than, more broadly, late Middle Jurassic is unsettled. Zhang's (2009) paper on the Baojishan–Honghui Basin is the remaining cited source for the late Bathonian and would settle whether any original names the stage.

# Sao Khua

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Sao Khua?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Early Cretaceous. The Jurassic readings rest on comparisons of the vertebrate fauna and on magnetostratigraphy, and Racey and colleagues (1996) set them aside on palynology of the underlying Phra Wihan Formation. The Cretaceous brackets of Racey and colleagues (1996), Buffetaut and Suteethorn (1999) and Jia and colleagues (2022) contain the late Valanginian–early Hauterivian of Tucker and colleagues (2022), adopted in the 2026-09-30 and 2026-10-07 entries, without narrowing it. Buffetaut and Suteethorn's two limits are both exclusive, which leaves Valanginian to Barremian under a "probably"; Buffetaut and colleagues (2009) state the upper limit the same way. Even at its error limit the middle Sao Khua maximum of 133.8 ± 1.8 Ma, 135.6 Ma, is younger than the base of the Valanginian (137.05 Ma on the ICS chart v2024/12), so it excludes the Berriasian for that part of the formation. Tumpeesuwan and colleagues' (2010) late Barremian, ruled out in the 2026-09-30 entry, correlates one bivalve subgenus with formations in Japan and Korea, a different class of evidence from the zircon ages of Tucker and colleagues and Jia and colleagues. Governs `period: [Early Cretaceous]` and `stages: [Valanginian, Hauterivian]` on [Sao Khua](../../../stratigraphy/s/sao-khua.yml).

**Evidence.**
- tucker2022a, p. 5: the middle Sao Khua maximum is "Y3Z at 133.8 (±1.8) Ma", the weighted average "of the youngest three (3) concordant grains", "placing it in the Late Valanginian".
- tucker2022a, p. 11: the new age is set against earlier estimates of "Hauterivian–late Barremian (Meesook, 2000; Nulay and Arjwech, 2021; Tumpeesawan et al., 2010; Samathi et al., 2019a, 2019b, 2021)".
- buffetaut1999a, p. 14: "The Sao Khua Formation can therefore safely be placed in the Lower Cretaceous. Its exact age is still somewhat uncertain: it is probably younger than Berriasian and older than Aptian."
- buffetaut2009a, p. 229: the formation "can thus be considered as later than earliest Cretaceous and earlier than Aptian".
- tumpeesuwan2010a, p. 93, Abstract: "The temporal distribution of this subgenus is limited to the Late Barremian Formations of Japan and Korea, thus we suggest a Late Barremian age for the Sao Khua Formation", the subgenus being *Pseudohyria* (*Matsumotoina*). Samathi and colleagues (2019) relay the work as "Tumpeesawan et al. 2010" (samathi2019a, p. 242), as do Tucker and colleagues (tucker2022a, p. 11).
- buffetaut1986b, p. 170: the Phu Wiang assemblage is "very similar to assemblages from well-known late Jurassic localities in western North America (Morrison Formation) and China (Shangshaximiao Formation of Sichuan)"; "Its vertebrate fauna thus indicates a late Jurassic age for the Sao Khua Formation, which is in agreement with other palaeontological and magnetostratigraphical evidence".
- racey1996a, p. 8: "Maranate and Vella (1986) suggested a Middle Jurassic (Bathonian-Callovian) age for the Sao Khua Fm on the basis of palaeomagnetic data".
- racey1996a, p. 24: the formation "has previously been dated as Late Jurassic on the basis of its very rich vertebrate fauna".
- racey1996a, p. 25: the sauropods "would indicate a Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous age"; "the published vertebrate data suggests that the Sao Khua Formation is probably Early Cretaceous in age rather than Late Jurassic"; "Previous palynological studies (Hahn, 1982) indicate a Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous age"; palynomorphs from the underlying Phra Wihan Formation show that the formation "is no older than Early Cretaceous in age"; with "the Aptian age of the Khok Kruat Formation", it "must fall within the age range Berriasian-Aptian".
- martin1994a, p. 1087: "The Sao Khua Formation was usually thought to be Late Jurassic in age on the basis of its vertebrate fauna"; "Magnetostratigraphic data suggest a somewhat older, Middle Jurassic, age (Maranate and Vella, 1982)". The reference list gives Maranate and Vella's study as 1986 (p. 1092).
- martin1994a, p. 1087: "Pending a clarification of its geological age, the Sao Khua Formation will be considered here as Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous in age."
- jia2022a, p. 3481, Abstract: zircon ages give "approximately 140 Ma" for the Sao Khua, "indicating that the Phra Wihan Formation and the overlying Sao Khua Formation are Early Cretaceous deposits" (translated).
- jia2022a, p. 3485: the 140 Ma peak is read as "constraining the lower limit of its depositional age to the Early Cretaceous" (translated); the paleomagnetic samples from the formation's three sites "did not yield meaningful paleomagnetic results" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Jurassic.* Buffetaut and Ingavat (1986) draw it from the resemblance of the vertebrates to Morrison and Shangshaximiao faunas and name no stage; Racey and colleagues (1996) relay the same dating from Buffetaut and colleagues (1993). Racey and colleagues find that many of the taxa range into the Early Cretaceous, and the Phra Wihan palynology puts the formation no older than Early Cretaceous, as do the zircon ages of Jia and colleagues (2022) and Tucker and colleagues (2022).
- *Middle Jurassic (Bathonian–Callovian).* Maranate and Vella's (1986) paleomagnetic reading, as Racey and colleagues (1996) and Martin and colleagues (1994) relay it. Martin and colleagues date the study 1982 in their text and 1986 in their reference list. It is older still than the Late Jurassic that the Phra Wihan palynology excludes. The constraint of Jia and colleagues (2022) is geochronological, not magnetostratigraphic: their samples from the formation gave no paleomagnetic result.
- *Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous.* Hahn's (1982) palynology as Racey and colleagues relay it, the sauropods in Racey and colleagues' review, and Martin and colleagues' (1994) span, which they adopt only until the age is clarified. The Phra Wihan palynology and both zircon studies exclude the Jurassic part.
- *Berriasian.* The Berriasian end of Racey and colleagues' range is the age of the underlying formation, a bound rather than a dating. Jia and colleagues' (2022) Berriasian to Barremian is the paleontological reading they compare their result with; their own result is a floor of about 140 Ma, a maximum age that adds no stage. Tucker and colleagues' middle Sao Khua maximum excludes the Berriasian for that part of the formation even at its error limit.

**Open.** As in the 2026-09-30 entry: how young the formation can be.

## Does the Sao Khua Formation take the Barremian?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** No. The 2026-09-30 entry's conclusion governs the unit as well as its taxa: Tucker and colleagues' late Valanginian–early Hauterivian, with the late Barremian ruled out. That a maximum depositional age leaves a younger age open is a possibility, not a reading, and adds no stage. Governs `stages: [Valanginian, Hauterivian]` on [Sao Khua](../../../stratigraphy/s/sao-khua.yml), replacing `[Valanginian, Hauterivian, Barremian]`, and matches the stages of its six taxa.

**Evidence.**
- tucker2022a, p. 15: "a tightly constrained late Valanginian–early Hauterivian age of 133.6–132.1 Ma for Sao Khua Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Barremian from the open upper bound.* A maximum age does not exclude younger stages, but no source read gives the formation a Barremian on evidence other than the bivalve correlation the 2026-09-30 entry rules out.

**Open.** As in the 2026-09-30 entry: how young the formation can be.

## What is the age of the Sao Khua Formation?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Late Valanginian to early Hauterivian on Tucker and colleagues' (2022) detrital-zircon reading, which assumes the youngest zircons are near-depositional; a younger age is not excluded by maximum ages alone. Governs `stages` on Sao Khua and on its taxa, such as [*Phuwiangosaurus sirindhornae*](../../../genera/P/Phuwiangosaurus.yml) and [*Siamotyrannus isanensis*](../../../genera/S/Siamotyrannus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- tucker2022a, p. 15: "a tightly constrained late Valanginian–early Hauterivian age of 133.6–132.1 Ma for Sao Khua Formation"; p. 11: the zircon populations are "near-contemporaneous … near-synchronous with sedimentation". The middle Sao Khua maximum is 133.8 ± 1.8 Ma (p. 1, Table 1) and the overlying Phu Phan's 132.4 ± 2.0 Ma (p. 1; ±2.6 on p. 11).
- gale2020a, p. 1029: "The base of the Hauterivian is taken at 132.6 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Barremian.* Tumpeesuwan and colleagues (2010) "roughly" assign the formation "to a Late Barremian age based on the geological range of the subgenus" of one non-marine bivalve (tumpeesuwan2010a, p. 104); the same species elsewhere comes from a formation "assigned to Hauterivian-Barremian" (p. 103). Later late-Barremian statements trace to them (for example samathi2024a, p. 2, which also lists Buffetaut and Suteethorn 1999 among them in error).
- *Berriasian–Aptian, Berriasian–Barremian.* These are outer brackets: "the Sao Khua Formation must fall within the age range Berriasian-Aptian" from the neighboring formations (racey1996a, p. 25); "probably younger than Berriasian and older than Aptian" (buffetaut1999a, p. 14); and an earlier palaeontological "Berriasian to Barremian" that Jia and colleagues (2022) quote while giving their own reading, "早白垩世瓦兰今期之后的沉积" ("deposited after the Early Cretaceous Valanginian", translated; jia2022a, p. 3493).

**Open.** How young the formation can be. A maximum depositional age does not by itself rule out the Barremian, and Tucker and colleagues' closed bracket depends on their near-syndepositional assumption. Their abstract's "no later than 133.8" and "no earlier than 132.4" do not follow from maximum ages alone.

# Sainte-Barbe Clays

## Does the age in Robaszynski and colleagues' (2001) lexicon apply to the Sainte-Barbe Clays?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** No. The lexicon gives one age for the Hainaut Group, under the group's heading and after its list of four formations (the Sainte-Barbe Clays, Hautrage Clays, Baudour Clays and Saint-Pierre Gravel), so the statement covers the Wealden-facies set as a whole rather than this formation. The Barremian does not appear in it. The stages follow Yans and colleagues (2012), who date the formation in the Iguanodon Sinkhole at Bernissart from its angiosperm pollen and carbon-isotope trend. Governs `stages: [Barremian, Aptian]` on [Sainte-Barbe Clays](../../../stratigraphy/s/sainte-barbe-clays.yml).

**Evidence.**
- robaszynski2001a, p. 123: under the Hainaut Group, "Several formations may be recognised", the first being the "Sainte-Barbe Clays Formation (SBA): grey, sandy, lignitic and highly fossiliferous clays, filling a sinkhole".
- robaszynski2001a, p. 124: the group's "Age: Late Jurassic ("Purbeckian") to Early Cretaceous (Aptian) and possibly even extending into Early Cenomanian".
- yans2012a, p. 79: "Robaszynski et al. (2001) concluded that this formation was Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous in age"; "These studies suggest that this formation is late Late Barremian to earliest Aptian in age."

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Jurassic to Aptian, possibly into the Early Cenomanian.* Robaszynski and colleagues (2001) give this range for the Hainaut Group, not for the Sainte-Barbe Clays alone, and Yans and colleagues (2012), who read it as the formation's age, replace it with the late Barremian to earliest Aptian.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Shishugou

## Does the Shishugou keep the Bathonian?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No; the formation is Callovian to Oxfordian. Supersedes the 2026-11-25 entry on the stages and answers its Open line. The Bathonian's only source is Eberth and colleagues' (2001) "is regarded as Bathonian to Oxfordian", relayed from four works: Hendrix and colleagues (1992) and Vincent and Allen (2001), both read, name no stage, and Chen (1985) and Chen (1996) have not been read. A reading another work reports adds a stage only once its original is read. The Callovian and Oxfordian rest on Moore and colleagues (2018, 2020). [*Fushanosaurus*](../../../genera/F/Fushanosaurus.yml), [*Tienshanosaurus*](../../../genera/T/Tienshanosaurus.yml) and [*Monolophosaurus*](../../../genera/M/Monolophosaurus.yml), which have no horizon reading of their own, take the formation's range. Governs `stages: [Callovian, Oxfordian]` and the note on eberth2001a on [Shishugou](../../../stratigraphy/s/shishugou.yml).

**Evidence.**
- eberth2001a, p. 1639: the megasequence "is regarded as Bathonian to Oxfordian in age (Chen 1985; Hendrix et al. 1992; Chen 1996a; Vincent and Allen in press)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Bathonian.* A relay whose read originals name no stage.

**Open.** Chen (1985) and Chen (1996) have not been read.

## Do Vincent and Allen (2001) and Hendrix and colleagues (1992) support the Bathonian?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** They name no stage. Answers part of the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Two of the four works Eberth and colleagues (2001) cite for the Bathonian to Oxfordian are now read: Vincent and Allen (2001) give the Shishugou Group as "Middle to Upper Jurassic" throughout, from Chinese regional compilations, and Hendrix and colleagues (1992) name no stage for it, placing it across the Middle and Upper Jurassic only in a figure. Eberth and colleagues' Bathonian is a published reading and stays under the union rule. Governs `stages: [Bathonian, Callovian, Oxfordian]` on [Shishugou](../../../stratigraphy/s/shishugou.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- eberth2001a, p. 1639: the megasequence "is regarded as Bathonian to Oxfordian in age (Chen 1985; Hendrix et al. 1992; Chen 1996a; Vincent and Allen in press)".
- vincent2001a, p. 343: "Mesozoic strata within the Junggar Basin are nonmarine, and the resolution of age dating is relatively poor."
- vincent2001a, p. 354: "the Middle to Upper Jurassic Shishugou Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *Dropping the Bathonian because two of the cited works name no stage.* Their silence does not contradict it; a newer, narrower dating would be needed to bound it, and a narrower one would add rather than replace.

**Open.** The other two works Eberth and colleagues cite, Chen (1985) and Chen (1996), are unread; either may be where the Bathonian comes from.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Shishugou?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Bathonian to Oxfordian. Eberth and colleagues (2001) give the Shishugou Megasequence, which at Huoxiaoshan is the 450 m formation alone, a Bathonian to Oxfordian age, as a reading they relay from earlier work ("is regarded as"); they did not see the base of the formation at Wucaiwan, and they divide its fauna into Middle and Late Jurassic assemblages after Dong (1992). Moore and colleagues (2018) date the *Bellusaurus* quarry and "much of the Shishugou Formation" to the earliest Oxfordian from two tuffs at Wucaiwan. That stage is stated, so it is carried over; on the current chart the lower tuff falls in the late Callovian, a stage the formation already has. Governs `stages` and `period` on [Shishugou](../../../stratigraphy/s/shishugou.yml).

**Evidence.**
- eberth2001a, p. 1639: the Shishugou Megasequence, "450 m thick at Huoxiaoshan", "is regarded as Bathonian to Oxfordian in age (Chen 1985; Hendrix et al. 1992; Chen 1996a; Vincent and Allen in press)".
- eberth2001a, p. 1636: "The Shishugou Megasequence consists of the 450-m-thick Shishugou Formation"; at Wucaiwan "we were unable to locate the basal contact", and the base just to the north is described as "unconformable on Carboniferous strata".
- eberth2001a, p. 1636: "The Middle Jurassic vertebrate assemblage from the Shishugou Formation in northeastern Junggar consists of fish, lungfish, turtles, crocodilians, amphibians, small sauropods, theropods, hypsilophodontids, ankylosaurs, therapsids, and lizards (Dong 1992). The Late Jurassic assemblage from the Shishugou Formation is less diverse".
- moore2018a, p. 6, Systematic Paleontology: "Radiometric dating at Wucaiwan provides ages of 162.2 ± 0.2 and 159.7 ± 0.3 Ma for the T-1 and T-BW tuffs", "placing the Bellusaurus quarry, and much of the Shishugou Formation, within the earliest Oxfordian (early Late Jurassic)".
- moore2018a, p. 61, Phylogenetic affinities of Bellusaurus: the dating implies a smaller extension of the age of Neosauropoda "than was inferred by earlier studies that considered the Shishugou Formation to be as old as Aalenian in age (Upchurch & Barrett, 2005)".
- upchurch2005a, p. 117: "the 'Wucaiwan' or 'Shishigou' Formation, in which Bellusaurus is found, has been suggested to be somewhere within the Aalenian–Callovian (see chapter 4, appendix 4.2)".
- hesselbo2020a, p. 1004, Table 26.4: base of the Oxfordian 163.5 Ma in GTS 2012 and 161.5 Ma in GTS 2020; base of the Callovian 165.3 Ma in GTS 2020.

**Ruled out.**
- *An Aalenian or Bajocian lower part.* Upchurch and Barrett (2005) report that the formation "has been suggested" to fall within the Aalenian–Callovian, citing another chapter: a reported spread, not their own dating (see [methods](../../methods.md)). Moore and colleagues (2018) set their radiometric dates against it, and Eberth and colleagues (2001) begin the megasequence in the Bathonian.
- *Re-placing the "earliest Oxfordian" of Moore and colleagues (2018) on the current chart.* The label fits GTS 2012, whose Oxfordian begins at 163.5 Ma (the paper does not name its chart); on GTS 2020 and the ICS chart v2024/12 it begins at 161.5 Ma, so the T-1 tuff (162.2 Ma) in the middle beds is late Callovian and the beds between the two tuffs straddle the Callovian–Oxfordian boundary. A stated stage is carried over rather than re-placed (see [methods](../../methods.md)), and the re-placed reading would add no stage: the Callovian is already in the formation's range.

**Open.** The Bathonian rests on the age Eberth and colleagues (2001) relay from four earlier works, none of them in the works cited, and the base of the formation is not dated directly. Reading those works, or a date from the lower beds, would settle whether the formation reaches the Bathonian.

# Santana Group

## Does the Santana Group get an entry, and what does it date?

*2026-10-14*

**Conclusion.** Yes, at group rank, Aptian; the Romualdo Formation sits in it. The group scheme is the one [Rank that moves with the parent](../../topics/rank-that-moves-with-the-parent.md) adopts, and it left the Romualdo without a parent only because the registry had no Santana entry. The group's age comes from the one statement about the group itself, Arai and Assine's (2020) palynology. The Romualdo's own readings reach the Albian, outside its parent; that is a finding to resolve, not a reason to widen the group. Supersedes the Open line of the rank note on the Romualdo's missing parent. Governs the [Santana Group](../../../stratigraphy/s/santana.yml) and `parent: Santana` on the [Romualdo](../../../stratigraphy/r/romualdo.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sayão2020a, p. 2: "Nowadays, there is a consensus to consider the former Santana Formation as the Santana Group, that is further divided from base to top, into the Barbalha, Crato, Ipubi and Romualdo formations".
- arai2020a, abstract: "the Romualdo Formation is almost entirely Aptian in age"; "the entire Santana Group is of Aptian age because of the conspicuous presence of Sergipea variverrucata"; the Albian appears there only as earlier work, the unit having "been extended into the Albian".
- kellner2011b, p. 104: "the Albian Romualdo Formation of the Santana Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian–Albian for the group.* A child is checked against its parent, not used to widen it ([CONTRIBUTING](../../../CONTRIBUTING.md#age-period-and-stages)).
- *Arai and Assine extending the Romualdo into the Albian.* The record's earlier note had this backwards; they report the extension and conclude Aptian.

**Open.** The Romualdo's Albian against its group's Aptian: Kellner and colleagues (2011) and the Romualdo's older literature date it Albian, while Arai and Assine find the whole group Aptian on palynology.

# Scollard

## Does Sankey's 65–60 Ma add the Selandian?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No; the stages stand, Maastrichtian and Danian. Sankey (2001) gives the Scollard "(65–60 Ma)" after Eberth and Ryan (1992), not read, and no work read dates the top of the formation. Governs `stages` on [Scollard](../../../stratigraphy/s/scollard.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- sankey2001a, p. 214: "Scollard (65–60 Ma) formations span 20 mys (Eberth and Ryan, 1992)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Selandian.* Relayed from an unread work.

**Open.** Eberth and Ryan (1992) have not been read.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Scollard, and about its lower and upper parts?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Maastrichtian and Danian. In the Red Deer River valley the formation lies conformably on the Battle Formation, whose bentonite 75 cm below the contact is dated 66.936 ± 0.047 Ma. Eberth and Kamo (2019) take about 66.88 Ma for the base, and place the Cretaceous–Paleogene boundary about 40 m above the base at Knudsen's Farm. Eberth and Kamo (2020) accordingly divide the formation into a Cretaceous portion and a Paleogene portion. The lower part falls in the Maastrichtian, and the beds above the boundary fall in the Danian, the first stage of the Paleocene. Eberth and Braman (2012) separate, on palynology, a lower Scollard with fewer gymnosperms and more angiosperms from a coal-rich upper Scollard judged wetter, and Sankey (2001) reads the lower Scollard dinosaur fauna, like that of the upper Aguja, as one of drier, open habitat. Governs `stages: [Maastrichtian, Danian]` and `period: [Late Cretaceous, Paleocene]` on [Scollard](../../../stratigraphy/s/scollard.yml).

**Evidence.**
- eberth2019a, accepted manuscript, Abstract: "Our data also provide a maximum age of ~66.88 Ma for the base of the overlying Scollard Formation".
- eberth2019a, accepted manuscript, Discussion, Scollard Formation: the contact on the Battle Formation "is conformable in the Drumheller region" and "lies only 75 cm above the Battle bentonite"; "The K-Pg boundary is present in the Scollard Formation exposures at Knudsen Farm ~40 meters above the base of the formation"; "the K-ScF has an estimated age-range of ~66.88–66.04 Ma".
- eberth2020a, accepted manuscript, Table 3: "Base of Scollard Fm; base of R. collaris-P. reductus biozone", 66.88 Ma, "Interpolated by Eberth and Kamo (2019)"; "BB (TMP2017.033.0001); 75 cm below base of Scollard Fm", 66.936 ± 0.047 Ma.
- eberth2020a, accepted manuscript, Fig. 3 caption, the lithostratigraphy of the Edmonton Group: "K-Sc, Cretaceous portion of the Scollard Formation"; "Pg-Sc, Paleogene portion of the Scollard Formation".
- eberth2012a, p. 1069, Fig. 16 caption, Tolman Bridge area: "The Whitemud Member is draped by sediments of the Battle Formation. Note the basal sandstones (ss) of the Scollard Formation (SFm) at the top of the section".
- eberth2012a, p. 1058: the palynostratigraphic data "were drawn from 572 samples collected from outcrop sections and the CPOG Strathmore core".
- eberth2012a, p. 1085, Appendix D: high gymnosperm abundances in the "upper Scollard Formation where coals are abundant and conditions were judged independently to be wetter"; the Morrin and Tolman members "and lower Scollard Formation have reduced numbers of gymnosperms with greater numbers of angiosperms"; Wodehouseia spinata "becomes more common in the Battle and Scollard formations"; Scollardia trapaformis, of the Carbon Member, "has been rarely seen in the base of the Scollard Formation".
- sankey2001a, p. 214: microvertebrate collections "from the Milk River (84–82.5 Ma), Oldman and Dinosaur Park (79.5–74 Ma), Horseshoe Canyon (72–67 Ma), and Scollard (65–60 Ma) formations span 20 mys (Eberth and Ryan, 1992)".
- sankey2001a, p. 214: the Talley Mountain microsites "are more similar to the lower Scollard Formation", given as about 66 Ma, "than to more contemporaneous units"; the shared traits are "1) R. isosceles is more abundant than R. gilmorei; 2) Troodon and Dromaeosaurus are absent or rare; and 3) pachycephalosaurids are present to common"; R. isosceles is more abundant "in the geologic units that represent open, drier habitats".
- sankey2001a, p. 211: Saurornitholestes cf. langstoni "is the most abundant theropod in the Milk River and Horseshoe Canyon formations, but is slightly less abundant in the Dinosaur Park and Scollard formations".

**Ruled out.**
- *A wholly Paleocene Scollard, on Sankey's 65–60 Ma.* The span is relayed from Eberth and Ryan (1992), and its 65 Ma start is the Cretaceous–Paleogene boundary of an older time scale. Sankey (2001) also calls the lower Scollard about 66 Ma and latest Cretaceous (p. 214), and Eberth and Kamo (2019) date the base at about 66.88 Ma, in the Maastrichtian.

**Open.** No work cited dates the top of the formation. Sankey's 65–60 Ma, if placed on the ICS chart v2024/12 as a bare number, would reach the Selandian (base 61.66 Ma). It is a rounded span relayed from Eberth and Ryan (1992), which has not been read, and its base is already wrong for the current scale, so it is not counted here; a dated level in the upper Scollard would settle whether the formation reaches the Selandian.

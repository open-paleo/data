# Shengjinkou

## Which succession do Wang and colleagues (2021) give for the Shengjinkou in the Turpan-Hami Basin?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** In the Turpan-Hami Basin, Wang and colleagues (2021) give the Tugulu Group as the Sanshilidadun, Shengjinkou and Lianmuqin formations in ascending order, so the Shengjinkou there lies on the Sanshilidadun. In the southern Junggar Basin, Zheng and colleagues (2023) give the group as the Qingshuihe, Hutubihe, Shengjinkou and Lianmuqin, so the Shengjinkou lies on the Hutubihe. The Lianmuqin overlies it in both basins. At Hami the formation is a lacustrine sequence mainly of gray-white sandstones with tempestite interlayers. Wang and colleagues call it Early or Lower Cretaceous throughout, and the paper gives no stage and no numeric age, although later work cites it for the unit's age (see the 2026-09-21 entry). Governs `parent: Tugulu` on [Shengjinkou](../../../stratigraphy/s/shengjinkou.yml), and bears on its `stages` as the Open line says.

**Evidence.**
- wang2021a, p. 2, Geological settings: "In Turpan-Hami Basin, this stratigraphic unit includes, from bottom to top, the Sanshilidadun, Shengjinkou, and Lianmuqin formations"; "the Shengjinkou Formation whose lacustrine sedimentary sequence is mainly composed by the gray-white sandstones, within some tempestite interlayers made up of brown mudstone breccias".
- wang2021a, p. 2, Introduction: "the Early Cretaceous deposits of the Hami Gobi, more specifically from the Shengjinkou Formation of the Tugulu Group".
- zheng2023a, p. 766: "Within the southern Junggar Basin proper, the Tugulu Group consists (in ascending order) of the Qingshuihe, Hutubihe, Shengjinkou, and Lianmuqin Formations".
- zheng2023a, pp. 768–769: the Shengjinkou's clam shrimp and pollen "indicate an Early Cretaceous biostratigraphic age. Our ca. 135.2 Ma U-Pb age places the upper Shengjinkou Formation within the Valanginian Stage."

**Ruled out.** The clam shrimp as the source of the Valanginian, as the 2026-09-21 entry has it. In Zheng and colleagues (2023) the fossils give only an Early Cretaceous age, and the Valanginian comes from their U-Pb date; the subject of the sentence falls on p. 768 and its verb on p. 769, across a figure. This supersedes that part of the 2026-09-21 entry.

**Open.** Whether the Valanginian holds for the Shengjinkou of the Turpan-Hami Basin. The 135.2 Ma U-Pb age that places the upper Shengjinkou in the Valanginian (zheng2023a, pp. 768–769) comes from a tuff in the Junggar Basin, and none of the works cited on the unit dates the Hami beds or correlates them with the Junggar succession other than by the shared name. A dating of the Hami beds, or a published correlation between the two basins, would settle it.

## Is the Shengjinkou Aptian?

*2026-09-21*

**Conclusion.** No. The only Aptian for the formation is that of Averianov and colleagues (2025), who credit it to Wang and colleagues (2021), and Wang and colleagues give no stage. The stage comes from the clam shrimp of Zheng and colleagues (2023). Governs `stages: [Valanginian]` on [Shengjinkou](../../../stratigraphy/s/shengjinkou.yml).

**Evidence.**
- averianov2025b, p. 8: *Hamititan* "is based on a series of articulated strongly procoelous caudal vertebrae from the Aptian Shengjinkou Formation of Xinjiang, China (Wang et al., 2021)".
- wang2021a, p. 2: "the Shengjinkou Formation of the Tugulu Group, which is distributed in the Turpan-Hami Basin, south of the Tian Shan Mountains in Xinjiang, China".
- zheng2023a, p. 769: the clam shrimp evidence "places the upper Shengjinkou Formation within the Valanginian Stage".

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian.* Wang and colleagues write "Early Cretaceous" throughout and name no stage. The one Aptian in their paper is in the title of a cited *Moabosaurus* paper. See [Averianov and colleagues (2025)](../../references/a/averianov2025b.md) on how that paper cites.
- *A Shengjinkou in another basin, to which the Aptian might belong.* Wang and colleagues place the formation in the Tugulu Group of the Turpan-Hami Basin, the parent the record gives. Averianov and colleagues cite Wang for the same taxon, so both mean this unit.

**Open.** Nothing.

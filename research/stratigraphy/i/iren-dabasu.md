# Iren Dabasu

## Which stages does the Iren Dabasu Formation span?

*2026-10-06*

**Conclusion.** Coniacian to Maastrichtian, the union of the two live datings, with the disagreement between them recorded as a dispute. Governs `stages: [Coniacian, Santonian, Campanian, Maastrichtian]` and `dispute` on [Iren Dabasu](../../../stratigraphy/i/iren-dabasu.yml). The 2026-09-30 entry's `stage: [Campanian]` for the formation's species stands.

**Evidence.**
- currie1993d, p. 140: "younger than Cenomanian and is best considered early Senonian in age. Preliminary data suggest that it may ultimately prove to be as young as Campanian".
- vanitterbeeck2005b, p. 714: "the age of the Iren Dabasu Formation can probably be refined to the latest Campanian–Early Maastrichtian", from charophytes and ostracods.

**Ruled out.**
- *Cenomanian and Turonian.* The early Late Cretaceous reading rested on primitive hadrosaurs; Currie and Eberth (1993) overturn it, and the later microfossil dating is younger still.
- *Campanian–Maastrichtian alone.* Van Itterbeeck and colleagues write that the microfossil age calls the vertebrate-based estimates into question, but neither they nor a later source states that Currie and Eberth's reading is superseded.

**Open.** Which reading holds. Zanno (2010) reports that the formation's dates "have not yet been firmly established" (zanno2010a, p. 531).

## What is the age of the Iren Dabasu Formation?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Campanian is the only stage both primary datings allow: post-Cenomanian ("early Senonian", possibly Campanian) from vertebrates, and latest Campanian–early Maastrichtian from microfossils. Governs `stage: [Campanian]` on the Iren Dabasu species, such as [*Alectrosaurus olseni*](../../../genera/A/Alectrosaurus.yml), [*Bactrosaurus johnsoni*](../../../genera/B/Bactrosaurus.yml) and [*Gigantoraptor erlianensis*](../../../genera/G/Gigantoraptor.yml). The formation's own range remains disputed.

**Evidence.**
- currie1993d, p. 140: "younger than Cenomanian and is best considered early Senonian in age. Preliminary data suggest that it may ultimately prove to be as young as Campanian"; abstract, p. 127: "the generally accepted Cenomanian designation may be too old".
- vanitterbeeck2005b, p. 714: "the age of the Iren Dabasu Formation can probably be refined to the latest Campanian–Early Maastrichtian", from charophytes and ostracods; the narrower range rests on correlation with the Nemegt.
- bonnetti2014a, accepted manuscript, Biostratigraphic information: "we propose a post-late Campanian age which is consistent with ages proposed in Van Itterbeeck (2005, 2007)", from palynology of Erlian Formation drill cores, not the Iren Nor outcrops.

**Ruled out.**
- *Cenomanian.* The pre-1993 view that Currie and Eberth (1993) overturned. Jones (2026) still gives "Late Cretaceous, Cenomanian" for *Erliansaurus* in a specimen table (jones2026c, p. 505), while giving Campanian or Maastrichtian for the other Iren Dabasu taxa.
- *Campanian as Currie and Eberth's result.* Brochu (2003) writes that "more recent work indicates a Campanian age for the Iren Dabasu beds … (Eberth and Currie, 1993)" (brochu2003a, p. 2). Currie and Eberth say only that it "may ultimately prove to be as young as Campanian".
- *Coniacian–Campanian.* Carr (2023) gives "Coniacian–Campanian, or 89.8–83.6 million years in age (Gilmore, 1933; Currie and Eberth, 1993)" (carr2023a, p. 1). The figures span Coniacian–Santonian, since 83.6 Ma is the base of the Campanian (our reading).

**Open.** The formation's full range. Zanno (2010) reports micropaleontology giving Campanian–Maastrichtian and macrovertebrates giving "Cenomanian-early Campanian"; these were not re-examined for this note.

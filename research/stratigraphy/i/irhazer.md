# Irhazer

## Is "Argiles de l'Irhazer" a variant of the Irhazer Group?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No. Current usage gives the name to the unranked lower unit of the group, below the Tiourarén Formation (Remes and colleagues 2009; Serrano-Martínez and colleagues 2014); only older usage, which Rauhut and López-Arbarello (2009) relay, applied it to the whole group. No record needs the lower unit as an entry; [*Spinophorosaurus*](../../../genera/S/Spinophorosaurus.yml) stays filed in the group, with its horizon in a location note. Governs the absent `variants` on [Irhazer](../../../stratigraphy/i/irhazer.yml) and the location note on *Spinophorosaurus nigerensis*.

**Evidence.**
- remes2009a, p. 2: "allow the localization of the site at the base of the Irhazer Group (‘‘Argiles de l’Irhazer’’ below the Tiourarén Formation)".

**Ruled out.** - *A variant.* The name now means a lower unit.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Irhazer Group?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Bajocian to Oxfordian, the union of a reading for each end of the group. Its base takes Remes and colleagues' (2009) "presumably Middle Jurassic (Bajocian-Bathonian)" for the Spinophorosaurus site, which they place at the base of the group below the Tiourarén Formation. Its top is the Tiourarén, "the uppermost unit of the Irhazer Group" in Rauhut and López-Arbarello (2009), whose Bathonian to Oxfordian rests on the reasoning in the [Tiourarén](../t/tiourarén.md) entries; with the uppermost unit in the Jurassic, the group carries no stage after the Oxfordian. Governs `period: [Middle Jurassic, Late Jurassic]` and `stages: [Bajocian, Bathonian, Callovian, Oxfordian]` on [Irhazer](../../../stratigraphy/i/irhazer.yml).

**Evidence.**
- remes2009a, p. 2, Locality and horizon: the site lies "at the base of the Irhazer Group ('Argiles de l'Irhazer' below the Tiourarén Formation)"; "The Irhazer Group has traditionally been considered Jurassic to 'Neocomian' in age"; "The stratigraphical and phylogenetic position of Spinophorosaurus is consistent with an even earlier age, presumably Middle Jurassic (Bajocian-Bathonian)".
- remes2009a, pp. 2–3, Locality and horizon: "since it is currently not possible to date the strata of the Irhazer Group directly, it cannot be excluded that the Argiles de l'Irhazer are even older (Lower Jurassic)"; the lower limit is the Teloua sandstones of the underlying Agadez Group, "regarded as Upper Triassic".
- rauhut2009a, p. 260: "The Tiouaren Formation represents the uppermost unit of the Irhazer Group"; "Originally included in the upper part of the 'argiles de l'Ihrazer'"; Greigert and Pougnet (1967) "also assumed an Early Cretaceous age for the 'argiles de l'Ihrazer', based on conchostracans" and bivalves; "the Irhazer Group ('argiles de l'Irhazer') is Jurassic to 'Neocomian' in age", after Taquet (1976).
- rauhut2009a, p. 261: the invertebrates come "most probably from beds now included in the Tiouaren Formation"; although cited for an Early Cretaceous age, the conchostracans are reported only as "consistent with an age ranging between Middle Jurassic and Late Cretaceous"; "the biostratigraphic utility of the invertebrates from the Tiouaren Formation is questionable at best".
- rauhut2009a, p. 263: "Accepting a Jurassic age of the Tiouaren Formation, the fine-grained deposits of the Irhazer Group, including this formation ..., are thus consistent with a low rate of subsidence and deposition during the Jurassic".

**Ruled out.**
- *Lower Jurassic.* Remes and colleagues (2009) say only that it "cannot be excluded" because the group cannot be dated directly. That is a possibility left open, not a reading, and gives the group no Early Jurassic (see [Methods](../../methods.md)).
- *Jurassic to "Neocomian".* Both papers report it as the traditional range, after Taquet (1976). Its Cretaceous part was the Early Cretaceous age then given to the Tiourarén at the top of the group, which Rauhut and López-Arbarello (2009) replaced with a pre-Kimmeridgian, probably late Middle Jurassic age.
- *Early Cretaceous, after Greigert and Pougnet (1967).* It rests on conchostracans and unionid bivalves. Rauhut and López-Arbarello (2009) find the conchostracans consistent with anything from Middle Jurassic to Late Cretaceous and the invertebrates' biostratigraphic utility "questionable at best", and the beds that yielded them are most probably in the Tiourarén.

**Open.** A date from the rock. The base rests on the phylogenetic position of Spinophorosaurus, which Remes and colleagues give only as "presumably" Bajocian–Bathonian, and the top on the relationships of the Tiourarén's dinosaurs; neither end is dated directly.

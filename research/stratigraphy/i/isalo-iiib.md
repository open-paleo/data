# Isalo IIIb

## Were Isalo III and Isalo IIIb established as formal units?

*2026-10-09*

**Conclusion.** No. Both are lettered divisions of Besairie's Isalo series, carried as facies labels. Every source that discusses the scheme treats them as subdivisions, and none points to a formal definition. The papers that print "Isalo III Formation" or "Isalo IIIb Formation" use the label in passing, as a horizon or a comparison fauna, without discussing rank. Governs `informal` on [Isalo III](../../../stratigraphy/i/isalo-iii.yml) and [Isalo IIIb](../../../stratigraphy/i/isalo-iiib.yml).

**Evidence.**
- geiger2004a, p. 369: Isalo I, II and IIIa–c "are all cross-bedded and yet they are traditionally combined together as the Isalo group, or 'Isalo Series'"; "The Isalo III Sandstones are Jurassic in age and should not be included in the Formation as defined herein."
- raveloson2019a, p. 2: the outcrops "are equivalent to the upper levels of the Isalo 'Group' (W Madagascar) and named 'Isalo III-Facies Continental' by Besairie"; "The Isalo III subgroup is divided into two subunits: Isalo IIIa and Isalo IIIb."
- maganuco2005a, p. 166: "a single Subunit referable to the Isalo IIIb"; its Fig. 3 (p. 169) labels the "Isalo I Unit & Isalo II Unit" and the "Isalo IIIa Subunit" and "Isalo IIIb Subunit" within the "Isalo Group".
- bindellini2019a, p. 137: "the stratigraphic subunit known as Isalo IIIb".
- Passing uses of the formation label: buffetaut2005a, p. 468 ("the Isalo IIIb Formation crop out"); mannion2010a, p. 291; rauhut2009a, p. 264; royo-torres2020a, p. 22. None discusses rank or who established the unit.

**Ruled out.**
- *Treating the formation label as evidence of formal status.* The four papers that print it do so only as a horizon or comparison, and Mannion and colleagues (2010) take "Isalo IIIb Formation" from Buffetaut (2005).

**Open.** Besairie's own works, which set up the lettered scheme, and Collignon (1953), whom Buffetaut (2005) cites for the age, were not read. The `rank` of both entries was not changed here. The sources disagree: Isalo III is a "Formation" in passing uses and a "subgroup" for Raveloson and colleagues (2019), and Isalo IIIb a "Formation" or a "subunit". As recorded, a formation (Isalo IIIb) sits inside another formation (Isalo III).

## Is the Sakaraha Formation another name for Isalo IIIb?

*2026-10-09*

**Conclusion.** No. As its authors place it, the Sakaraha Formation is a unit of the southern Morondava Basin. The equation of the Mahajanga Basin's Isalo IIIb with the Sakaraha rests on Bindellini and Dal Sasso (2019), who credit it to Geiger and colleagues (2004), and Geiger and colleagues do not make it. Sakaraha is not added to `variants`, and the entry keeps its name. Governs `name` and `variants` on [Isalo IIIb](../../../stratigraphy/i/isalo-iiib.yml).

**Evidence.**
- maganuco2005a, p. 166: the Ambondromamy, Port-Bergé, Tsinjorano and Kamoro River material "pertains without doubt to a single Subunit referable to the Isalo IIIb". The paper never uses the name Sakaraha.
- geiger2004a, p. 372: "The Sakaraha Formation is only found today in the southern Morondava Basin where it occurs at outcrop and in boreholes."
- geiger2004a, p. 375: "The area around Sakaraha is the type locality for the Sakaraha Formation".
- geiger2004a, pp. 372–373: "the Sakaraha Formation ranges from Bajocian to Early Bathonian, making it time equivalent to the Bemaraha Formation".
- geiger2004a, p. 369: the term Isalo III covers sandstones of which "Some ... clearly belong to the Andafia Formation ..., but others can be attributed to younger formations such as the Sakaraha, Ankazoabo and Sakanavaka Formations". The paper never assigns Isalo IIIb itself to any of them, and its only Mahajanga Basin formation is the Beronono, a lateral equivalent of the Andafia.
- bindellini2019a, p. 138: "the Isalo IIIb subunit outcropping in the Mahajanga Basin has been included in the Sakaraha Formation. Geiger et al. (2004) demonstrated that this formation has a Bathonian age". Its Fig. 1 legend pairs "Sakaraha Fm / Isalo IIIb (Bathonian)".
- hendrickx2025a, p. 439: Isalo IIIb is "currently referred to as the Sakaraha Formation, well-dated to the Bathonian", citing Geiger and colleagues (2004) first.

**Ruled out.**
- *Sakaraha as a variant of Isalo IIIb.* Bindellini and Dal Sasso (2019) and Hendrickx and colleagues (2025) print the name for this horizon, but both trace it to Geiger and colleagues (2004), whose Sakaraha is a southern Morondava formation "only found today" there. Geiger and colleagues also date it Bajocian to Early Bathonian, not the Bathonian that Bindellini and Dal Sasso say they demonstrated. Listing it would assert an identity of rock that the paper defining the unit's extent does not support.
- *Sakaraha as the current formal name, with Isalo IIIb informal.* The same chain of citations; no paper read here formally extends the Sakaraha into the Mahajanga Basin.

**Open.** Two further works that Hendrickx and colleagues (2025, p. 439) cite for the name were not read. A paper that formally extends the Sakaraha Formation into the Mahajanga Basin would reopen the question. Separately, the entry's `rank: formation` sits uneasily with Maganuco and colleagues (2005), who call Isalo IIIb a "Subunit", and with Geiger and colleagues (2004), who treat the Isalo III divisions as informal; that rank was not reviewed here.

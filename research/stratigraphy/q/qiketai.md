# Qiketai

## Does the Qiketai take a stage, and does it reach the Upper Jurassic?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** It takes the Bathonian, and stays Middle Jurassic. Answers the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Wang and colleagues (1998), from well palynology in the Turpan Basin, date the Sanjianfang and Qiketai together as "mainly late Bathonian", and their English abstract assigns the two assemblages to the early and late Bathonian "respectively", giving the Qiketai its own late Bathonian. Greene and colleagues (2000) give the formation only as Middle Jurassic, marking "the end of the Middle Jurassic" below the Qigu. Maisch and Matzke's (2019) "Middle to Upper Jurassic" is the label of a chart row they caption as controversial, and their Callovian or Oxfordian belongs to *Xinjiangtitan*, whose bed is assigned to the Qigu; neither carries the formation into the Upper Jurassic. Governs `stages: [Bathonian]`, `period: [Middle Jurassic]` and the pointers to wang1998b and greene2000a on [Qiketai](../../../stratigraphy/q/qiketai.yml).

**Evidence.**
- wang1998b, p. 975: "三间房组和七克台组的孢粉组合特征面貌相似,所代表的地质时代也大体相当,即为中侏罗世晚期;考虑到其中的 Classopollis 属含量尚未达到很高,所以其时代似应以巴通期晚期为主。" ["the palynological assemblages of the Sanjianfang and Qiketai formations are similar … their age should probably be mainly late Bathonian", translated].
- wang1998b, p. ?, English abstract: "the CCP and GCP assemblages are considered to be at late Middle Jurassic (corresponding to early to late Bathonian age, respectively)".
- greene2000a, p. 147: "The Qiketai Formation marks the end of the Middle Jurassic and is overlain by hundreds of meters of Upper Jurassic massive red beds".
- maisch2019a, p. 113, Fig. 3: the Qiketai Formation in a row labeled "Middle to Upper Jurassic", the caption adding that its "stratigraphic position … remains controversial".

**Ruled out.**
- *Upper Jurassic from Maisch and Matzke (2019).* A chart row and an age for a bed assigned to the Qigu.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do Maisch and Matzke (2019) say about the age of the Qiketai Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** They call its position controversial, and the one age their text prints for it runs against their own comparison. The text makes the formation "probably coeval to the Toutunhe Formation of the Junggar Basin" and concludes that it "may therefore be lower Early Jurassic rather than Middle Jurassic in age", citing Fang and colleagues (2015). Fang and colleagues name no Qiketai Formation, and they place the Toutunhe among "the Upper Jurassic Toutunhe, Qigu and Kalazha Formations", deposited after 161.2 Ma. Maisch and Matzke themselves call *Xinjiangtitan*, which they attribute to the Qiketai, an "Upper Jurassic mamenchisaurid" and date it Callovian or Oxfordian; set the Qiketai beside the Toutunhe in the "Middle to Upper Jurassic" row of a figure of the Upper Jurassic succession; and say the Toutunhe "has recently been regarded as Upper Jurassic". The comparison therefore points to a younger age, not to the Early Jurassic; "Early" reads as a slip for "Late" (our reading). The Middle Jurassic in `period` follows Wu and colleagues (2013), and the dispute records the Toutunhe comparison with the Upper Jurassic it implies. Their attribution of *Xinjiangtitan* to the Qiketai, against Xing and colleagues' (2015) Qigu, is weighed in the [*Xinjiangtitan*](../../genera/X/Xinjiangtitan.md) note. Governs `period: [Middle Jurassic]` and `dispute` on [Qiketai](../../../stratigraphy/q/qiketai.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- maisch2019a, p. 112: "Another very large Upper Jurassic mamenchisaurid, *Xinjiangtitan shanshanensis*"; "It is from strata attributed to the Qiketai Formation, which is probably coeval to the Toutunhe Formation of the Junggar Basin and may therefore be lower Early Jurassic rather than Middle Jurassic in age (see Fang et al. 2015 and the discussion on dating of these sediments below)"; "Xing et al. (2015) erroneously stated that *X. shanshanensis* was recovered from the Qigu Formation".
- maisch2019a, p. 113, Fig. 3: a "Simplified scheme of the Upper Jurassic succession in the Junggar and Turpan-Hami basins", with the Qiketai Formation and *Xinjiangtitan* beside the Toutunhe Formation in the row labeled "Middle to Upper Jurassic"; caption: "The Toutunhe Formation has recently been regarded as Upper Jurassic (Fang et al. 2015)"; "The stratigraphic position of the Qiketai Formation remains controversial."
- maisch2019a, p. 113: the dates of Fang and colleagues "range between 151 and 161 Ma for syndepositional zircons from the Toutunhe, Qigu and Kalaza formations and indicate that these three formations span most of the Upper Jurassic from the Oxfordian to the Tithonian".
- maisch2019a, p. 115: "*Xinjiangtitan* appears to be the oldest form (Callovian or Oxfordian)".
- fang2015a, p. 50: the southern Junggar Basin Jurassic comprises "the Lower Jurassic Badaowan Formation, the Middle Jurassic Sangonghe and Xishanyao Foramtions, and the Upper Jurassic Toutunhe, Qigu and Kalazha Formations"; p. 59: "the sedimentation age of the Toutunhe Formation (younger than 161.2 Ma)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Jurassic.* Maisch and Matzke's "lower Early Jurassic" is contradicted by their own figure, by their "Upper Jurassic mamenchisaurid", and by the Upper Jurassic Toutunhe of the work they cite for it. A slip that its own document contradicts is not a reading (see [methods](../../methods.md)).

**Open.** Whether the formation reaches the Upper Jurassic. Maisch and Matzke's Toutunhe comparison and their Callovian or Oxfordian for *Xinjiangtitan* would carry it there, but both depend on beds that the *Xinjiangtitan* note assigns to the Qigu, while Wu and colleagues (2013) list the Qiketai among the Middle Jurassic strata below the Qigu. Greene and colleagues (2000) and Wang and colleagues (1998), who treat the Qiketai as Middle Jurassic (see the [Qigu](../q/qigu.md) note), are the works on the formation itself; a dating of the Turpan Basin beds would settle it.

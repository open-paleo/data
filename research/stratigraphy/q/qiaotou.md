# Qiaotou

## Does Ji and colleagues' (2005) Late Jurassic or Early Cretaceous add an epoch to the Qiaotou?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** No. Ji and colleagues (2005) call the age of the unit, which they rank as a formation, "equivocal" and report that some have suggested a Late Jurassic age and others an Early Cretaceous one, citing an earlier work for both, with their own isotopic dating still in process. They take neither side, so the statement is a reported spread of others' readings and adds no epoch. They place the *Jinfengopteryx* holotype in the middle part of the unit, stratigraphically below the *Sinosauropteryx*-bearing beds of the Yixian Formation, among fossils that are common elements of the Jehol Biota. Governs `period: [Early Cretaceous]` on the [Qiaotou](../../../stratigraphy/q/qiaotou.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ji2005a, p. 198: "The holotype is from the middle part of the Qiaotou Formation, which is stratigraphically lower than the *Sinosauropteryx*-bearing beds of the Yixian Formation".
- ji2005a, p. 198: "The age of the Qiaotou Formation is equivocal. It has been suggested to be Late Jurassic by some paleontologists, or Early Cretaceous by others"; p. 199: "The work of the isotopic dating is in process."
- ji2005a, p. 198: "The associated fossils are some common elements of the Jehol Biota".

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Jurassic in `period`.* Ji and colleagues (2005) relay the Late Jurassic as one of two earlier opinions and adopt neither; under the rule on reported spreads in methods.md, an opinion relayed this way adds a stage or epoch only once traced to its original.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Qigu Formation

## What stages does the Qigu take?

*2026-10-14*

**Conclusion.** Bathonian to Kimmeridgian, the union of the readings that rest on more than a maximum depositional age. All are Junggar Basin readings; no Turpan Basin bed is dated by its own evidence, and Wang and colleagues' (1998) late Bathonian for the Turpan Qiketai only bounds the beds above it. Governs `stages` on the [Qigu Formation](../../../stratigraphy/q/qigu.yml).

**Evidence.**
- wang2012a: a SHRIMP zircon U-Pb age of 164.6 ± 1.4 Ma on a tuff; "the main age of the Qigu Fromation is Middle Jurassic Callovian period. The lower part of the formation might have entered late Late Bathonian period".
- averianov2005a: "the Qigu Formation as Callovian (Wang 2001)", on palynology.
- maisch2019a: "the middle Qigu Formation (Late Jurassic, ?Oxfordian-Kimmeridgian)".
- upchurch2021a: the formation "has been regarded as Late Jurassic (Oxfordian–early Kimmeridgian) by more recent studies".

**Ruled out.**
- *Kimmeridgian–Tithonian, and the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary inside the upper Qigu.* Zhang and colleagues (2022) and others place the boundary there on the 151 Ma youngest detrital zircons of Fang and colleagues (2015) (zhang2022a, fang2015a), a maximum depositional age; a limit is not an age.
- *Upchurch and colleagues' citation of Fang and colleagues (2016) for the Oxfordian–early Kimmeridgian.* That paper gives no stage for the Qigu; the reading stands on Maisch and Matzke (2019) and the other works they cite.

**Open.** A dating of the Turpan Basin beds themselves.

## Are the Turpan Basin beds the Qigu Formation?

*2026-10-14*

**Conclusion.** They stay under the Qigu, one entry for both basins, with the doubt recorded in the dispute. Every paper describing a Turpan Basin fossil from these beds calls them Qigu, and no source has erected another name for them: Maisch and Matzke (2019) recommend one but propose none. The one stated basis for the name there is lithological: Greene and colleagues (2000) treat the thick Upper Jurassic red beds above the Qiketai as a boundary marker across northwest China. Against the name, Wings and colleagues (2012) find no stated basis for carrying it from the Junggar Basin, and Upchurch and colleagues (2021) follow them. For it, Wang and colleagues (1998) correlate the Turpan Sanjianfang and Qiketai with the Junggar Toutunhe on palynology, which puts the overlying Turpan beds where the Junggar Qigu lies in the succession; that supports the name by position, not as proof that the beds are continuous. Governs the single [Qigu](../../../stratigraphy/q/qigu.yml) entry, its dispute, and `location.formation: Qigu` on [*Xinjiangtitan shanshanesis*](../../../genera/X/Xinjiangtitan.yml).

**Evidence.**
- wings2012a, p. 928: "The Qigu Formation is well known in the Junggar Basin and has been also identified in the Turpan Basin, but no explicit justification has been given for this correlation (Shao et al. 1999) and the assignment of units to this formation is not transparent."
- wang1998b: the Sanjianfang and Qiketai assemblages are "roughly equivalent, namely the late Middle Jurassic … their age should probably be mainly late Bathonian", and "most similar to those of the Toutunhe Formation of the Junggar Basin".
- wu2013a: the *Xinjiangtitan* horizon "is the Middle Jurassic Qigu Formation", in a Shanshan succession of "Xishancun, Sanjianfang, Qiketai, Qigu and Kalazha formations".
- greene2000a: "The Qiketai Formation marks the end of the Middle Jurassic and is overlain by hundreds of meters of Upper Jurassic massive red beds of the Qigu Formation (J3q; see Figure 3). This is a common boundary marker throughout north-west Chinese basins", the one stated basis for the name in the Turpan Basin, a lithological one.
- upchurch2021a: "there is currently no explicit evidence that the putative outcrops of the Qigu Formation in the Turpan Basin correlate with those in the Junggar Basin".

**Ruled out.**
- *A separate entry for the Turpan beds.* No published name exists for one.
- *Dropping the formation from Turpan records.* Every describing paper names it, and the doubt belongs in the dispute.
- *The Qiketai for Xinjiangtitan.* Maisch and Matzke (2019) say Wu and colleagues place it there; Wu and colleagues say Qigu.

**Open.** Whether the *Xinjiangtitan* bed is Qigu or Qiketai. Wu and colleagues' site coordinates, 42°57′47″N, 90°34′26″E, fall within the minute that Greene and colleagues (2000) give for a complete Qiketai Formation section "At Qiketai (N42° 57', E90° 34')" (greene2000a); a minute is about 2 km, so this is a lead for a measured section, not a reassignment.

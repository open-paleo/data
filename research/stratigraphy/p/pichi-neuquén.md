# Pichi Neuquén

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Pichi Neuquén?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Aptian and Albian, disputed between the two. Canudo and colleagues (2018) report that no direct date exists and set an Aptian reading, which they credit to Musacchio and Vallati (2000), against Leanza's (2003) younger one. Leanza assigns the member to the Albian from its position in the Rayoso Formation, whose most probable age Leanza gives as late Aptian to Albian. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` and the `dispute` on [Pichi Neuquén](../../../stratigraphy/p/pichi-neuquén.yml).

**Evidence.**
- canudo2018a, p. 680: "from the oldest to youngest these are the Rincón, Quili Malal, Pichi Neuquén, and Cañadón de la Zorra members"; the bones "were found in the middle part of the Pichi Neuquén Member, approximately 44 metres from its base".
- canudo2018a, p. 680: "There is no consensus regarding the age of this member, for no direct datings have been taken. Some authors place it in the Aptian (Musacchio and Vallati 2000), whereas Leanza (2003) considers it to be rather more modern (lower Albian). In this paper, we ascribe an age range from Aptian to lower Albian to the new rebbachisaurid."
- leanza2003a, p. 4: the Bajada del Agrio Group comprises the Huitrín Formation and "the Rayoso Formation (Rincón, Quili Malal, Pichi Neuquén and Cañadón de la Zorra Members)" (translated).
- leanza2003a, p. 17: the unit was "defined by Weaver (1931: 79) as 'Pichi Neuquén Member'"; it "lies conformably above the Quili Malal Member and is similarly overlain by the Cañadón de la Zorra Member"; its thickness "in the Cerro La Visera profile reaches 41.50 meters"; the sandstones "were deposited in a continental fluvial environment" (translated).
- leanza2003a, p. 17, Edad y correlaciones: "Following the analysis carried out in considering the age of the Rayoso Formation ..., it is maintained that the Member in question corresponds to the Albian" (translated).
- leanza2003a, p. 14: "the most probable age of the Rayoso Formation is estimated to correspond to the upper Aptian - Albian" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian to early Albian as Canudo and colleagues' own dating of the member.* They ascribe that range "to the new rebbachisaurid", in the sentence after reporting no consensus on the member's age, and it spans the two readings they relay. It dates the taxon, not the member, and adds no reading of its own.

**Open.** No direct date for the member. Musacchio and Vallati (2000), the source of the Aptian reading, has not been read; it would show what the Aptian rests on and whether it applies to this member or to the formation.

# Purbeck

## Does the Durlston Formation need an entry?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No. The records that mention Durlston Bay, [*Owenodon*](../../../genera/O/Owenodon.yml) and [*Echinodon*](../../../genera/E/Echinodon.yml), are filed in the Lulworth, the *Owenodon* dentary coming from the top of the Lulworth below the Durlston's basal Cinder Member; no record names the Durlston. Governs nothing on [Purbeck](../../../stratigraphy/p/purbeck.yml).

**Evidence.** None.

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the rank and age of the Purbeck?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** A group of two formations, the Durlston above and the Lulworth below, spanning the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary: Tithonian and Berriasian. Barker and colleagues (2023) give the group "Tithonian–Berriasian", and Hopson and colleagues (2008) place the base of the Berriasian in the Lulworth Formation near the base of the group, so the beds below it are Tithonian. Clements (1993) ranks the strata a group after Townson (1975) and leaves the boundary's position in the sequence open. Governs `rank: group` and `stages: [Tithonian, Berriasian]` on [Purbeck](../../../stratigraphy/p/purbeck.yml).

**Evidence.**
- clements1993a, p. 181: Durlston Bay "has long been regarded as the type-section of the Purbeck Beds (Purbeck Limestone Group)" and "is the thickest well-exposed section of the Purbeck Group in the country".
- clements1993a, p. 182: "Following Townson (1975 ...) the strata considered here, are regarded as a lithostratigraphic Group - the Purbeck Limestone Group, although other authors ... have treated them as a Formation."
- clements1993a, p. 182: Townson "divide the Group into the Durlston Formation above and the Lulworth Formation below. This is a formalisation of the terms proposed by Casey (1963)."
- clements1993a, p. 182: the traditional Lower, Middle and Upper divisions "are not simple lithostratigraphic units, since they rely in part at least on biostratigraphical considerations for their definition", but "retain a useful informal role".
- clements1993a, p. 182: "Casey's divisions reflected his views as to the position of the Jurassic/Cretaceous boundary in the sequence. The palaeontological evidence for this has been challenged ..., and indeed the position of the System boundary in the sequence remains very uncertain"; "the boundary between these Formations does not even correspond to any change in the general petrological and biological character of the strata."
- barker2023a, pp. 3–4, Introduction: the Purbeck Group is "a succession that underlies the Wealden Supergroup and spans the Jurassic-Cretaceous boundary (Tithonian–Berriasian".
- barker2023a, pp. 5–6, Geological context and provenance of HASMG G369a: "Three fault-bounded inliers result in surface exposures of the Purbeck Group within the Weald sub-basin, located north and northwest of Battle in East Sussex", "surrounded by the overlying Hastings Group"; "These are the oldest exposed rocks in the region"; the exposures "are represented by both of its constituent Lulworth and Durlston Formations ..., which are principally Berriasian in age".
- hopson2008a, p. 1, Introduction: "Traditionally in southern England the base of the Cretaceous ... has been defined at the base of the Cinder Bed that marks the lower boundary of the Durlston Formation"; "In Dorset the base of the Berriasian Stage is judged to fall (although not formally defined) at the base of the Cypris Freestone within the Mupe Member of the Lulworth Formation near the base of the Purbeck Group."

**Ruled out.**
- *Formation rank for the Purbeck.* Clements (1993) notes that other authors have treated the strata as a formation, and follows Townson (1975) in ranking them a group of two formations. Barker and colleagues (2023) also write Purbeck Group, with "constituent Lulworth and Durlston Formations".
- *Lower, Middle and Upper Purbeck as formal units.* Clements (1993) keeps them only informally, because they rest partly on biostratigraphy and the Upper/Middle boundary is hard to recognize from the rock alone.
- *The Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary at the Lulworth–Durlston contact.* Casey's (1963) division of the beds reflected his placement of the boundary, which Hopson and colleagues (2008) describe as the traditional one at the base of the Cinder Bed. Clements (1993) reports that its paleontological basis has been challenged and that the boundary's position "remains very uncertain", and Hopson and colleagues (2008) place the base of the Berriasian lower, in the Lulworth Formation. The Lulworth is therefore not wholly Jurassic, and the formation boundary does not divide the group's two stages.

**Open.** No work cited fixes the base of the Berriasian within the group; Hopson and colleagues (2008) call their placement in Dorset "not formally defined".

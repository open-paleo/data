# Portezuelo

## What do the works behind the Portezuelo's datings give for the formation itself?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Late Turonian to early Coniacian, as the 2026-10-01 entry concludes; this entry checks the sources behind it and behind the readings it rules out. Leanza (1999), Leanza and Hugo (2001) and Leanza and colleagues (2004) give the formation itself late Turonian to early Coniacian, and Garrido (2010) adopts that. Hugo and Leanza (2001) give the formation alone the upper Turonian, and the Turonian–Coniacian span to the Río Neuquén Subgroup and to the mapped Portezuelo+Plottier pair. Legarreta and Gulisano's (1989) 90 to 86 Ma and Cruz and colleagues' (1989) 90.5 to 88.5 Ma, as Garrido relays them, are for the Portezuelo together with the other upper units of the subgroup. No work cited dates the formation radiometrically or biostratigraphically. Governs `stages: [Turonian, Coniacian]` on [Portezuelo](../../../stratigraphy/p/portezuelo.yml).

**Evidence.**
- leanza1999a, p. 26: "The age of the Portezuelo Formation can be regarded as Late Turonian - Early Coniacian"; for the Río Neuquén Subgroup, "the age of this subgroup can be regarded as Late Turonian - Coniacian".
- leanza2001a, p. 120: "The age of the Portezuelo Formation can be regarded as late Turonian-early Coniacian".
- leanza2004a, p. 69: "The age of the Portezuelo Formation is Late Turonian–Early Coniacian (Hugo and Leanza, 2001a)".
- hugo2001a, p. 2, Abstract: "the upper Turonian-Coniacian Río Neuquén Subgroup including the Portezuelo and Plottier Formations"; the group's age "ranges from early Cenomanian (97 ± 3 Ma) to early Campanian (74 ± 3 Ma)"; the Spanish Resumen on p. 1 gives the same subgroup ages.
- hugo2001a, p. 16: "The units that comprise the Río Neuquén Subgroup are estimated to have been deposited during the Upper Turonian - Coniacian" (translated).
- hugo2001a, p. 17: the Portezuelo+Plottier pair "is assigned to the upper Turonian-Coniacian" (translated); "the Portezuelo Formation is attributed to the Upper Turonian" (translated).
- garrido2010a, p. 143: "This unit does not yet have absolute datings or biostratigraphic studies that would allow a reliable age to be established for it" (translated).
- garrido2010a, pp. 143–144: Legarreta and Gulisano (1989) place "these deposits, together with the rest of the upper units of the Río Neuquén Subgroup, between 90 and 86 Ma, corresponding to the Middle Turonian – Late Coniacian interval (International Stratigraphic Chart, 2009)" (translated); "Cruz et al. (1989) assign the same set an age between 90.5 and 88.5 Ma, spanning from the Middle Turonian to the basal Early Coniacian" (translated); Hugo and Leanza (2001) and Leanza and colleagues (2004) "tentatively place this unit between Late Turonian – Early Coniacian, a criterion also adopted in this work" (translated).
- garrido2010a, p. 144: the Los Bastos Formation, above the Portezuelo, "was recognized in the original schemes of Herrero Ducloux (1938, 1939) under the designation 'Portezuelo Medio', within his 'Estratos del Portezuelo'" (translated); p. 146: its age is inferred "between the Early Coniacian and the Middle Coniacian" (translated).
- garrido2010a, p. 146: the Sierra Barrosa Formation "was recognized in the original schemes of Herrero Ducloux (1938, 1939) under the designation 'Portezuelo Superior'" (translated), and is assigned "to the Middle-Late Coniacian" (translated).
- calvo2004b, p. 566: "The age of the Portezuelo Formation has been interpreted as Turonian through Santonian on the basis of sequence stratigraphy (Leanza, 1999; Leanza and Hugo, 2001)"; "Fossils from the Futalognko quarry come from red claystones corresponding to the top Portezuelo Formation".
- cruz1989a, pp. 708, 710: "The Portezuelo (sandstones) and Plottier (pelites) members of the Río Neuquén Formation" are "interpreted as low base-level and high base-level deposits respectively of Sequences IV and V" (translated); p. 709, Fig. 9: the Río Neuquén Formation spans 90.5 to 88.5 Ma, with the two members drawn as interfingering facies and no boundary age between them.

**Ruled out.**
- *A Santonian reach from Calvo and colleagues' (2004) sources.* The two works they cite for "Turonian through Santonian on the basis of sequence stratigraphy", Leanza (1999) and Leanza and Hugo (2001), both give the Portezuelo late Turonian to early Coniacian, and Leanza's (1999) chart keeps the formation within the Turonian and Coniacian. The Santonian has no support in the works Calvo and colleagues cite.
- *Middle Turonian, or late Coniacian, from Legarreta and Gulisano (1989) or Cruz and colleagues (1989).* Both spans are for the Portezuelo together with the other upper units of the Río Neuquén Subgroup (Garrido 2010, p. 144: "the same set"); in Cruz and colleagues' chart the Portezuelo and Plottier are interfingering members of one formation with no separate ages.
- *Cruz and colleagues' (1989) member rank as a rival rank.* It is the older scheme of Cazau and Uliana (1973), in which the Río Neuquén was a formation with members, as the [Neuquén Group subgroups](../../topics/neuquén-group-subgroups.md) note records for the Candeleros.

**Open.** Hugo and Leanza (2001) give the formation the upper Turonian only (p. 17), while Leanza and colleagues (2004) and Garrido (2010) relay them as late Turonian to early Coniacian. The early Coniacian rests on Leanza (1999), Leanza and Hugo (2001) and Leanza and colleagues (2004), which state it without giving grounds, and on Garrido's (2010) adoption; none of the works cited gives evidence that fixes the top of the formation. Garrido's two new formations take in Herrero Ducloux's "Portezuelo Medio" and "Portezuelo Superior", so a reading of the Portezuelo in the older, wider sense may include Coniacian beds now in the Los Bastos and Sierra Barrosa; which works used the wider sense was not checked.

## What is the age of the Portezuelo Formation?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Late Turonian–early Coniacian, following Garrido (2010). Governs `stages: [Turonian, Coniacian]` on Portezuelo and on the species whose type horizon is in it.

**Evidence.**
- garrido2010a, p. 144: "entre el Turoniano Tardío – Coniaciano Temprano, criterio también adoptado en este trabajo" ("between Late Turonian – Early Coniacian, a criterion also adopted in this work", translated). Garrido adopts the age of Hugo and Leanza (2001) and Leanza and colleagues (2004).
- garrido2010a, p. 144: "Formación Los Bastos (nom. nov.)", placed "por encima de la Fm. Portezuelo" ("above the Portezuelo Fm.", translated). Sierra Barrosa follows on p. 146. Strata that older work could include in a broadly drawn Portezuelo are now separate formations; that the new formations account for the older Santonian readings is an inference, not a statement in the paper.
- calvo2004b, p. 565, abstract: "Portezuelo Formation (Upper Turonian-Lower Coniacian)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Turonian–Santonian.* Calvo and colleagues (2004) report it from sequence stratigraphy, "interpreted as Turonian through Santonian on the basis of sequence stratigraphy" (calvo2004b, p. 566), while their own abstract gives upper Turonian–lower Coniacian (p. 565). Benson and colleagues (2010) repeat "Portezuelo Formation (Turonian–Santonian)" (benson2010d, p. 72) and cite Calvo and colleagues. Both predate Garrido (2010).
- *Upper Turonian–lower Santonian?* Salgado and colleagues (2009) give "Portezuelo (upper Turonian – lower Santonian?)" (salgado2009b, p. 768), with the Santonian queried. It predates Garrido (2010) and gives no new evidence.

**Open.** Nothing. No radiometric date exists for the formation; Garrido (2010, p. 143) says so outright.

# Piedra Parada

## Does the Piedra Parada keep both the Barremian and the Aptian?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** Yes. Answers the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Leanza and Hugo's own charts set the member in the upper third of the Barremian (1995, 1997), and Leanza (2003) draws it in the Aptian, correlating it with the Salina Member, which he dates early Aptian; he does not say he is revising the charts, so the later reading adds to them. Salgado and colleagues' (2006) "Barremiano-Aptiano inferior" is not tied by its wording to the member rather than the formation, and adds nothing of the member's own. Leanza (2003) is now cited; the age sentence under his Piedra Parada heading names the Puesto Quiroga Member, a slip. Governs `stages: [Barremian, Aptian]`, unchanged, and the pointers to leanza2003a, leanza1997a, leanza1995a and salgado2006b on [Piedra Parada](../../../stratigraphy/p/piedra-parada.yml); [*Zapalasaurus bonapartei*](../../../genera/Z/Zapalasaurus.yml) is unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- leanza1995a, p. 31: the chart sets "Fm. La Amarga" in the Barremiano row, with Piedra Parada in its upper third.
- leanza1997a, p. 38, Fig. 5: the formation and its three members in the "BARREMIANO" row, Piedra Parada in the upper third.
- leanza2003a, p. 23: "El Miembro Piedra Parada de la Formación La Amarga es correlacionable, en consecuencia, con el Miembro Salina de la Formación Huitrín" ["The Piedra Parada Member of the La Amarga Formation is therefore correlatable with the Salina Member of the Huitrín Formation", translated]; p. 13: "la edad del Miembro Salina puede estimarse que corresponde al Aptiano inferior" ["the age of the Salina Member can be estimated to correspond to the lower Aptian", translated]; p. 19, Fig. 7: "APTIANO" beside "Mb. Piedra Parada".
- leanza2003a, p. 23: "por su posición en secuencia el Miembro Puesto Quiroga puede ser asignado en sentido amplio al Aptiano" ["by its position in sequence the Puesto Quiroga Member can be broadly assigned to the Aptian", translated].
- salgado2006b, p. 695, Resumen: "el Miembro Piedra Parada de la Formación La Amarga, cuya edad es considerada como Barremiano-Aptiano inferior".

**Ruled out.**
- *Leanza (2003) as a supersession of the Barremian charts.* It states no revision.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Piedra Parada Member, and whose dating is it?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Barremian to early Aptian. Salgado and colleagues (2006) give the member "Barremian-lower Aptian" in their abstract and "upper Barremian–lower Aptian" in the type horizon line, where they credit the range to Leanza and Hugo (1997). Leanza and Hugo (1997) do not give that range: they assign the whole La Amarga Formation to the upper Hauterivian and Barremian and give the member no stage, and Salgado and colleagues' own review credits the Barremian–early Aptian to Leanza (2003, which they cite as 2002) and Leanza and colleagues (2004). Leanza (2003) assigns the member broadly to the Aptian by its position below the intra-Aptian unconformity at the base of the Lohan Cura Formation, and correlates it with the Salina Member of the Huitrín Formation. The section's age sentence names the "Puesto Quiroga Member", but it sits under the Piedra Parada heading and describes a unit overlain by the Lohan Cura, whose basal member is the Puesto Quiroga, so the name is a slip for the Piedra Parada (our reading). Salgado and colleagues also describe the member as a fluvial sequence 110 to 190 m thick, from which no fossils had been reported before the *Zapalasaurus* holotype. Governs `stages: [Barremian, Aptian]` on [Piedra Parada](../../../stratigraphy/p/piedra-parada.yml). The formation-level reasoning is in the 2026-11-23 and 2026-10-01 entries of [Puesto Antigual](puesto-antigual.md), whose Open already quotes the two Salgado and colleagues phrases for this member.

**Evidence.**
- salgado2006b, p. 695, abstract: "the Piedra Parada Member of the La Amarga Formation, whose age is regarded as Barremian-lower Aptian".
- salgado2006b, p. 696: Leanza and Hugo (1995, 1997) "assigned to these units an age ranging from the late Hauterivian to the Barremian", the units "finally being assigned to the Barremian–early Aptian interval (Leanza, 2002; Leanza et al., 2004)" (translated).
- salgado2006b, p. 698: "Its deposits form a monotonous fluvial sequence composed of sandstones, conglomerates, sandy mudstones, and siltstones. With thicknesses ranging from 110 to 190 m, paleontological records from these levels had not been previously documented" (translated).
- salgado2006b, p. 699, Horizon and locality: "Piedra Parada Member of the La Amarga Formation (upper Barremian–lower Aptian, Leanza and Hugo, 1997)" (translated).
- leanza1997a, p. 57: the La Amarga Formation "se asigna al Hauteriviano superior y Barremiano" ["is assigned to the late Hauterivian and Barremian", translated].
- leanza2003a, p. 23, Piedra Parada Member, Age and correlations: "In the absence of paleontological records, by its position in sequence the Puesto Quiroga Member can be assigned broadly to the Aptian"; "The Piedra Parada Member of the La Amarga Formation is correlatable, consequently, with the Salina Member of the Huitrín Formation" (translated).
- leanza2003a, p. 7, Fig. 3: the Piedra Parada Member drawn in the Aptian row, beside the Salina Member, below the Intermediate Miranican unconformity "(ca 117 Ma)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Upper Barremian–lower Aptian as Leanza and Hugo's (1997) dating.* Salgado and colleagues (2006) credit it to them in the type horizon line, but Leanza and Hugo (1997) date the formation upper Hauterivian and Barremian and give the member no stage (leanza1997a, p. 57); the wording matches the formation range of Leanza (2003).

**Open.** Whether Salgado and colleagues' "Barremian-lower Aptian" dates the member or repeats the formation range, which would make it the parent's statement under the parent-range rule in methods.md. Leanza (2003) gives the member an Aptian age of its own by position, and Leanza and Hugo's (1995) chart sets all three members in the Barremian (see [Puesto Antigual](puesto-antigual.md)), so the Barremian–Aptian span stands on two readings either way.

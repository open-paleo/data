# Point Loma

## Does the Point Loma regain the Maastrichtian?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** Yes; the formation is Campanian to Maastrichtian. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry on the stages. Sliter (1968), now read, places the uppermost siltstone, siltstone–sandstone and sandstone members of the La Jolla section in his *Rugoglobigerina rugosa* Zone, which his own foraminifera date early Maastrichtian; he refers the beds to the Rosario Formation, and Kennedy and Moore (1971), who name the Point Loma Formation, place them in it, with "Lower Maestrichtian forams … about 30 m below the base of the Cabrillo Formation". The upper beds at Point Loma itself Sliter puts in the latest Campanian. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` and the notes on kennedy1971a and sliter1968a on [Point Loma](../../../stratigraphy/p/point-loma.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sliter1968a, p. 14: "The Rugoglobigerina rugosa Zone is restricted to the La Jolla section where it occurs in the uppermost part of the siltstone member and throughout the siltstone-sandstone member and sandstone member."
- sliter1968a, p. 9: "Rugoglobigerina rugosa Zone—early Maastrichtian".
- kennedy1971a, p. 713: "Lower Maestrichtian forams have been found about 30 m below the base of the Cabrillo Formation in siltstone of the Point Loma Formation (Sliter, 1968, p. 14)".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does Nilsen and Abbott's late Campanian–early Maestrichtian age date the Point Loma Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Not on its own. Nilsen and Abbott (1981) give "a late Campanian–early Maestrichtian age" from megafossils of the San Diego area (Sundberg, 1979), an age for the Upper Cretaceous turbidites of the Point Loma and the overlying Cabrillo together rather than for this formation alone. They place the Point Loma in the Rosario Group, which Kennedy and Moore (1971) divided in ascending order into the Lusardi, Point Loma and Cabrillo formations, with an 83 m type section at the southern end of the Point Loma Peninsula. Governs the reading of Nilsen and Abbott's age for `stages` on [Point Loma](../../../stratigraphy/p/point-loma.yml).

**Evidence.**
- nilsen1981a, p. 1256: Kennedy and Moore "subdivided the group in ascending order into the Lusardi, Point Loma, and Cabrillo Formations".
- nilsen1981a, p. 1259: "the Point Loma Formation, 83 m thick in its type section along the cliff at the southern end of the Point Loma Peninsula".
- nilsen1981a, p. 1270: "Megafossils from the San Diego area indicate a late Campanian–early Maestrichtian age (Sundberg, 1979)".
- nilsen1981a, p. 1272: "Scattered megafossils from the thick turbidite sandstone sequences of the Point Loma Formation (Sundberg, 1979)".
- nilsen1981a, p. 1272: "Foraminifers from the Point Loma Formation near La Jolla range in age from late Campanian (Globotruncana elevata zone) to early Maestrichtian (Globotruncana tricarinata zone)".
- kennedy1971a, p. 712: "Near the top of the formation at Bird Rock in La Jolla, he found lower Maestrichtian forams."
- kennedy1971a, p. 713: "Lower Maestrichtian forams have been found about 30 m below the base of the Cabrillo Formation in siltstone of the Point Loma Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Maastrichtian for the Point Loma from the megafossil age.* The sentence dates the San Diego area's Upper Cretaceous, which takes in both formations, so it does not by itself put the Point Loma in the Maastrichtian.

**Open.** Whether the formation reaches the Maastrichtian. The argument above answers only the megafossil age. Nilsen and Abbott also date foraminifera from the Point Loma itself near La Jolla from the late Campanian to the early Maestrichtian, and Kennedy and Moore (1971), who named the formation, report lower Maestrichtian foraminifera near its top at Bird Rock, about 30 m below the base of the Cabrillo. Both statements rest on Sliter (1968) and count as one reading, but it is a reading about this formation alone. Nilsen and Abbott also credit Sundberg (1979) with megafossils from the Point Loma turbidites, so the San Diego megafossil age may itself draw on this formation. Sliter (1968, p. 14) has not been read; it would settle the zone assignment.

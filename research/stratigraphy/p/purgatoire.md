# Purgatoire Formation

## Is the Purgatoire part of a Dakota Group?

*2026-10-14*

**Conclusion.** No parent is recorded, and the Dakota stays a formation. "Dakota Group" is the Colorado and New Mexico usage for a larger unit than the Dakota Formation of Kansas and Utah, and its make-up is contested: Brill and Carpenter (2007) put the Purgatoire in it, while Mateer (1987), reviewing the group, rejects the two-formation scheme with the Purgatoire, and Noblett and colleagues (1987), Brill and Carpenter's source for the Colorado Springs section, treat the Purgatoire and the Dakota as separate formations. The registry's Dakota is the formation, which overlies the Purgatoire, so it cannot be the Purgatoire's parent. Governs the absent `parent` on [Purgatoire](../../../stratigraphy/p/purgatoire.yml) and `rank: formation` on [Dakota](../../../stratigraphy/d/dakota.yml).

**Evidence.**
- brill2007a: "the lower Lytle Member of the Purgatoire Formation (Dakota Group), which is of Aptian-Albian age".
- noblett1987a: "In the Colorado Springs region, the Purgatoire Formation separates the Morrison and Dakota Formations".
- mateer1987a: "The twofold division of the Dakota Group (Purgatoire and Dakota formations) is rejected in favor of a fourfold division comprising the Lytle, Plainview".
- sprinkel2012a: the Dakota "was first named as the Dakota Group by Meek and Hayden (1862)"; "We use Dakota Formation for the strata that overlie the Cedar Mountain Formation".
- brenner2000a: clayrocks "in the Dakota Formation" in a Kansas core "rest unconformably on fine-grained sandstone of the Purgatoire Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *A Dakota Group entry as the Purgatoire's parent.* It would be a second, differently scoped Dakota, on a scheme its own regional reviewer rejects.

**Open.** Nothing.

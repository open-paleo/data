# Portland

## What does Olsen and Galton's Stormberg review say about the age of the Portland?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Early Jurassic, Sinemurian to Toarcian at its widest. Olsen and Galton (1984) note that the Portland's protosuchian crocodiles and anchisaurid prosauropods, the families it shares with the upper Stormberg assemblage, occur with Pliensbachian to Toarcian palynoflorules, and that K/Ar dates of 194–185 Ma from the basalt flows beneath the formation show an Early Jurassic age for the basalts and the beds above them. Their Figure 4 captions date the formation "Sineumurian – Toarcian", a misprint for Sinemurian, for the type of *Grallator cursorius*, and "Toarcian" for the type of "*Anchisauripus sillimani*". The record's Hettangian does not come from this paper. Governs `period: [Early Jurassic]` and the Sinemurian, Pliensbachian and Toarcian in `stages` on [Portland](../../../stratigraphy/p/portland.yml).

**Evidence.**
- olsen1984a, p. 92: "the Portland Formation of the Hartford Basin of Connecticut and Massachusetts shares protosuchian crocodiles and anchisaurid prosauropods. These remains are associated with palynoflorules ranging in age from Pliensbachian to Toarcian"; "K/Ar dates from the basalt flows underlying the Portland Formation range from 194-185 Ma", "indicating an Early Jurassic age for the basalts" "and overlying beds".
- olsen1984a, p. 98, Fig. 4B caption: "type of *Grallator cursorius* of Hitchcock 1858 from the Portland Formation (Sineumurian – Toarcian) of the Hartford Basin, Massachusetts"; "type of "*Anchisauripus sillimani*" of Lull 1915" "from Portland Formation (Toarcian) of Massachusetts".

**Ruled out.**
- *The 194–185 Ma K/Ar dates as the formation's lower limit.* On the current chart they would fall in the Sinemurian and Pliensbachian and so exclude the Hettangian. They are K/Ar dates from before recalibration, relayed by Olsen and Galton (1984) from earlier work, and they date the basalts beneath the formation, which bound its age without dating it (CONTRIBUTING.md, "Limits are not ages" and "Recalibrated dates"). They do not set its lower limit.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Are the Tithonian and the Late Triassic readings for the Portland?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** No. The Portland of this entry is the Early Jurassic formation of the Hartford Basin. Governs `period: [Early Jurassic]` and `stages: [Hettangian, Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on [Portland](../../../stratigraphy/p/portland.yml).

**Evidence.**
- weishampel2004b, p. 532, under 3.20, Massachusetts: "2. Portland Formation (Olsen 1980a; … = Turner Falls Formation)", "Age: Pliensbachian-Toarcian".
- weishampel2004b, p. 546: the English entries "Portland Stone (Phillips 1871; Delair 1993; Benton and Spencer 1995)", "Age: early Tithonian (= early Portlandian; Cope et al. 1980b)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Tithonian.* The Tithonian in *The Dinosauria* distribution chapter belongs to the Portland Stone of southern England, listed under English counties. That is a different unit with a similar name.
- *Late Triassic.* White (1973) gives *Ammosaurus* and *Yaleosaurus* from the "Upper Triassic, Newark Series, Portland Sandstone" (white1973a, catalogue entries for those genera). A catalogue alone does not carry an epoch (see [methods](../../methods.md#a-reference-work-corroborates-but-almost-never-carries-a-value-alone)), and the record's Early Jurassic rests on Olsen and Galton (1984), who give palynoflorules and K/Ar dates.

**Open.** Nothing.

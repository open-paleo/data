# Prince Creek

## What do Fiorillo and colleagues (2010) say about the age of the Prince Creek Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** They call the formation Campanian–Maastrichtian and date two of its fossil sites within that span. Pollen work by Frederiksen (1990) puts the Ocean Point vertebrate deposits close to the Campanian–Maastrichtian boundary, and their own pollen analyses of beds about 50 km up the Colville River, near Kikak Creek, give an earliest Maastrichtian age for the Kikak-Tegoseak Quarry bonebed. That is a stage read from overlapping palynomorph ranges, so it is carried over as given. The tuff dates at Ocean Point that they relay are potassium-argon and argon-argon dates. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Prince Creek](../../../stratigraphy/p/prince-creek.yml), unchanged; the restriction of the formation by Mull and colleagues (2003) and the Ocean Point tuff ages are in the 2026-10-01 entry.

**Evidence.**
- fiorillo2010a, p. 456: the Prince Creek Formation "(Campanian to Maastrichtian)"; "Overlapping age ranges for some of the palynomorphs indicate an earliest Maastrichtian age for the site".
- fiorillo2010a, p. 457: "The Late Cretaceous (Campanian-Maastrichtian) Prince Creek Formation"; "Mull and others (2003) have recently revised the Cretaceous and Tertiary stratigraphic nomenclature in the Colville Basin of the North Slope of Alaska".
- fiorillo2010a, p. 458: "Conrad and others (1990) have published potassium-argon and argon-argon dates from tuffs at Ocean Point"; "In the Ocean Point area pollen work suggests that the age of the vertebrate fossil deposits are close to the Campanian-Maastrichtian boundary (Frederiksen 1990). Our pollen analyses ... of Prince Creek Formation sediments approximately 50 km up river (south) of Ocean Point, in the area of Kikak Creek, suggest an earliest Maastrichtian age for the mass accumulation of horned dinosaur bones".

**Ruled out.** Nothing new; the Coniacian of the formation before its restriction is treated in the 2026-10-01 entry.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does the Prince Creek Formation take the Danian?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** No. Its span runs into the Paleocene, but no source read for this note places the Cretaceous–Paleogene boundary within the formation, so the period includes the Paleocene and the stages stop at the Maastrichtian. Governs the absence of `Danian` from Prince Creek's stages.

**Evidence.**
- fiorillo2012a, p. 561; fiorillo2013a, p. 2; fiorillo2014a, pp. 2–3: the formation was deposited from the late or latest Campanian "to Paleocene".

**Ruled out.**
- *Danian from the stated span.* A span ending "to Paleocene" gives the period only; a unit takes the Danian when a source places the boundary within it, as for the [Scollard](../../../stratigraphy/s/scollard.yml) (eberth2020a, accepted manuscript, Fig. 3 caption: "K-Sc, Cretaceous portion of the Scollard Formation … Pg-Sc, Paleogene portion").

**Open.** Nothing.

## What stages does the Prince Creek Formation span?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Campanian–Maastrichtian (reaching the Paleocene) since Mull and colleagues (2003) restricted the formation; the Coniacian and Turonian readings belong to the older, broader unit. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on Prince Creek and the stage of [*Pachyrhinosaurus perotorum*](../../../genera/P/Pachyrhinosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- fiorillo2010a, p. 457: Mull and colleagues (2003) restricted "the Prince Creek Formation to units overlying, and interfingering with, the marine Schrader Bluff Formation"; p. 458: Ocean Point tuffs "range from approximately 68 to 71 Ma, with a best age estimate of 69.1 +/– 0.3 Ma", relaying Conrad and colleagues (1990).
- loewen2024a, p. 64, Table 2: *P. perotorum* from the Prince Creek Formation at 73.4–70.0 Ma.

**Ruled out.**
- *Coniacian.* Brett-Surman (1989) lists "Prince Creek Fm, Coniacian ?" (brett-surman1989a, p. 145), under the older scheme, in which the formation included "the Tuluvak Tongue, a lower, coarser-grained tongue generally below and interfingering with the Schrader Bluff Formation" (fiorillo2010a, p. 457).
- *Turonian.* Frederiksen's (1990) Turonian is for another unit: "The Seabee Formation is at least partly of Turonian age" (frederiksen1990a, p. E3).

**Open.** Nothing for the stages. Mull and colleagues (2003) have not been read.

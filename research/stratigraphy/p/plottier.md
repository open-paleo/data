# Plottier

## Do the southern Mendoza beds belong to the Plottier?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** They are placed in it in print, so the Santonian stays. Answers the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Musacchio and Vallati (2007) report their Zampal assemblage "from the Plottier Formation" by name, citing the regional mapping, and assign it to the Coniacian–Santonian; Musacchio (2006) assigns the same beds by their likeness to the Plottier elsewhere, with the name in quotation marks; González Riga and colleagues (2016) put the *Notocolossus* beds at Cerro Guillermo in "the basal-most bed of the Upper Cretaceous (upper Coniacian–lower Santonian, ~86 Ma) Plottier Formation"; and Garrido (2010) has the unit's outcrops continue "hacia el sector mendocino". The distance from the type area, within the same basin, is not grounds to set aside a published assignment. Garrido's own late Coniacian, "pudiendo llegar extenderse hasta el Santoniano Temprano", is a possibility and adds nothing. Governs the Santonian in `stages` on [Plottier](../../../stratigraphy/p/plottier.yml), unchanged; [*Notocolossus gonzalezparejasi*](../../../genera/N/Notocolossus.yml) is unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- musacchio2007a, p. 273: "a non marine microfossil assemblage … is reported from the Plottier Formation Herrero-Ducloux. The same one was recovered next to Zampal and the Calmu-Có mine of evaporites, in the Malargüe Department, south of Mendoza"; p. 277: "the assignation of this assemblage to the interval Coniacian-Santonian within the Cretaceous".
- musacchio2006a, p. 94: the brick-red mudstones at El Zampal are "muy parecidas a las que son conspicuas para la Formación Plottier en otras partes de la cuenca" ["very similar to those that are conspicuous for the Plottier Formation in other parts of the basin", translated].
- gonzálezriga2016a, p. 2: "the basal-most bed of the Upper Cretaceous (upper Coniacian–lower Santonian, ~86 Ma) Plottier Formation".
- garrido2010a, p. 148: "se considera a esta última edad como la más probable, pudiendo llegar extenderse hasta el Santoniano Temprano" ["this latter age is considered the most probable, potentially extending to the Early Santonian", translated].

**Ruled out.**
- *Dropping the Santonian for distance.* The beds are assigned to the formation in print.

**Open.** No age from the type area itself.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Plottier, and where does its Santonian come from?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Coniacian and Santonian, as the 2026-10-01 entry concludes. The Santonian comes from microfossils in southern Mendoza, at El Zampal and Calmu-Có, dated by correlation with the Adamantina Formation of Brazil: Musacchio (2006) labels the shared charophyte and ostracod association Turonian–Santonian, and Musacchio and Vallati (2007) assign it to the Coniacian–Santonian, attributing that assignment to the 2006 paper. González Riga and colleagues (2016) give the basal bed at Cerro Guillermo, also in southern Mendoza, upper Coniacian–lower Santonian. In the type region, Hugo and Leanza (2001) assign the formation to the Coniacian, within a Río Neuquén Subgroup of late Turonian to Coniacian age, and place the Santonian in the Río Colorado Subgroup above; Garrido (2010) weighs these readings and adopts the Late Coniacian, possibly reaching the early Santonian. Filippi and colleagues (2011), with Garrido among the authors, relay Musacchio and Vallati. Salgado and colleagues' (2009) "middle to upper? Santonian" at El Anfiteatro is given without a source, for a Plottier that Garrido's scheme would divide among the Plottier, Los Bastos and Sierra Barrosa Formations. Governs `stages: [Coniacian, Santonian]` on [Plottier](../../../stratigraphy/p/plottier.yml).

**Evidence.**
- musacchio2006a, p. 93: charophyte assemblages are known "in the Portezuelo (a), "Plottier" (b), and Anacleto (c) formations" (translated).
- musacchio2006a, p. 94: "The brick-red mudstones at El Zampal, Mendoza Province, very similar to those that are conspicuous for the Plottier Formation in other parts of the basin (Cazau & Uliana, 1973), contain an association of Lychnothamnus barbosai nov. comb. – Ilyocypris riograndensis" (translated); the *I. riograndensis* subzone at El Zampal "was correlated with the entire set of the Plottier, Bajo de la Carpa, and Anacleto formations" and assigned "to the Senonian, pre-Maastrichtian" (translated).
- musacchio2006a, p. 95, Fig. 3 caption: the "Lychnothamnus barbosai - Ilyocypris cf. riograndensis (Turoniano-Santoniano) Association, from Adamantina Formation in Brasil (A-C) and Plottier Formation in Argentina (D-F)"; the gyrogonites come from "the "Plottier" section" (translated).
- musacchio2007a, p. 273: "For the first time, a non marine microfossil assemblage containing charophytes, megaspores, seedremains and ostracods is reported from the Plottier Formation", from "next to Zampal and the Calmu-Có mine of evaporites, in the Malargüe Department, south of Mendoza (70º 45´ W, 36º 48´ S)".
- musacchio2007a, p. 277: the ostracods confirm "the correlation previously postulated by one of the authors (Musacchio, 2006) between the Plottier Formation and the Adamantine Formation", and "the assignation of this assemblage to the interval Coniacian-Santonian".
- hugo2001a, p. 2, Abstract: "the upper Turonian-Coniacian Río Neuquén Subgroup including the Portezuelo and Plottier Formations; and the Santonian-lower Campanian Río Colorado Subgroup comprising the Bajo de la Carpa and Anacleto Formations".
- hugo2001a, p. 5, Cuadro 1: the Plottier stands against the Coniacian and the Portezuelo against the Turonian.
- hugo2001a, p. 18: "la Formación Plottier se asigna al Coniaciano" ("the Plottier Formation is assigned to the Coniacian", translated).
- garrido2010a, p. 147: the type locality is "in the vicinity of the city of Plottier", at "S 38° 56' – W 68° 19' 30"" (translated).
- garrido2010a, p. 148: Musacchio (2006) and Musacchio and Vallati (2007) "attribute to the Plottier Fm. an age within the Coniacian–Santonian interval"; Hugo and Leanza (2001) "place the Plottier Fm. within the Late Coniacian" (translated); Salgado and colleagues (2009) describe the Plottier of the Meseta de Rentería area, "which would also include, according to this new scheme, the Los Bastos and Sierra Barrosa Fms." (translated).
- filippi2011a, p. 2: the quarry "is located in the mid-high part of the Plottier Formation (Fig. 2), dated by Musacchio and Vallati (2007) on the basis of their non-marine microfossil assemblage to the Coniacian-Santonian interval"; the byline includes A. Garrido.
- salgado2009b, p. 768: at El Anfiteatro, the "Cerro Lisandro (lower Turonian), Portezuelo (upper Turonian – lower Santonian?), and Plottier (middle to upper? Santonian) formations", with no source cited for the ages.
- gonzálezriga2016a, p. 2, Type locality and horizon: "Cerro Guillermo, Malargüe Department, southern-most Mendoza Province"; "The holotype and referred specimen were collected 403m apart in the basal-most bed".

**Ruled out.**
- *Turonian from Musacchio (2006).* The Turonian–Santonian is the label of the association as known from the Adamantina Formation in Brazil, and Musacchio and Vallati (2007), with Musacchio as first author, give the Plottier assemblage the Coniacian–Santonian. Hugo and Leanza's (2001) late Turonian belongs to the Río Neuquén Subgroup as a whole; they give the Plottier itself the Coniacian.
- *Musacchio's (2006) "firm correlation criteria" hedge as a hedge on the Plottier.* The words "have not provided, on the basis of available material, firm correlation criteria" (p. 94, translated) concern the charophytes and ostracods of the Portezuelo Formation, tentatively assigned to the Turonian–Santonian by Musacchio (2000); the Plottier association follows in the next paragraph.
- *No Santonian, on Hugo and Leanza's (2001) placement of the Santonian in the Río Colorado Subgroup.* The microfossil dating from southern Mendoza, González Riga and colleagues' basal bed, and Garrido's (2010) early Santonian each allow the Santonian, and the stages hold the union of what the sources publish.

**Open.** The Santonian rests on localities in southern Mendoza (El Zampal, Calmu-Có, Cerro Guillermo), about 300 km from the type locality near Plottier (our arithmetic from the coordinates in musacchio2007a, p. 273, and garrido2010a, p. 147), and Musacchio (2006) puts the El Zampal unit name in quotation marks. Whether the Mendoza beds are the Plottier of the type area is a correlation question that none of the works cited settles. Garrido and Leanza and colleagues (2004) relay Hugo and Leanza as "Late Coniacian", where Hugo and Leanza print "Coniacian"; the difference does not change the stages.

## What is the age of the Plottier Formation?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Late Coniacian, possibly reaching the early Santonian. Governs `stages: [Coniacian, Santonian]` on Plottier.

**Evidence.**
- garrido2010a, p. 148: "se considera a esta última edad como la más probable, pudiendo llegar extenderse hasta el Santoniano Temprano" ("this latter age is considered the most probable, potentially extending to the Early Santonian", translated). "This latter age" is Hugo and Leanza's (2001) "dentro del Coniaciano Tardío" ("within the Late Coniacian"), weighed against Musacchio's (2006) and Musacchio and Vallati's (2007) "Coniaciano – Santoniano" from microfossils.
- gonzálezriga2016a, p. 2, Type locality and horizon: "basal-most bed of the Upper Cretaceous (upper Coniacian–lower Santonian, ~86 Ma) Plottier Formation".
- filippi2011a, p. 2: "from the late part of the Coniacian to the early part of the Santonian; Legarreta and Gulisano, 1989; Cruz et al., 1989".

**Ruled out.**
- *Santonian only.* Simón and Salgado (2023) give "Notocolossus gonzalezparejasi (Plottier Formation, Santonian)" (simón2023a, p. 719), and Filippi and colleagues (2011) say just "Santonian" in their locality section (filippi2011a, p. 4). Both repeat the sources above and do not date the formation themselves.
- *Middle to upper Santonian.* Salgado and colleagues (2009) print "Plottier (middle to upper? Santonian)" (salgado2009b, p. 768) with no supporting evidence. An outlier.
- *~86 Ma as a Santonian date.* González Riga and colleagues' (2016) ~86 Ma sits at the Coniacian–Santonian boundary of the 2012 timescale (base Santonian 86.3 ± 0.5 Ma) but in the latest Coniacian of the 2020 timescale (85.7 ± 0.2 Ma; gale2020a, p. 1066, Table 27.4; radioisotopic date 85.66 ± 0.19 Ma in Table 27.3, p. 1064). It does not push the formation into the Santonian.

**Open.** No radiometric date exists for the formation. Garrido's Plottier section mentions none, while his sections on the Portezuelo and Bajo de la Carpa say outright that none exists; that the Plottier has none is an inference from that silence.

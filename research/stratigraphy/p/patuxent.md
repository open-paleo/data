# Patuxent

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Patuxent and its Arundel Clay?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Aptian. Stanford and colleagues (2011) date the formation Aptian on palynology, "evidently in the younger end" of the stage, but give the Propanoplosaurus holotype as early Aptian; the two statements differ only within the stage. D'Emic and colleagues (2012) name no stage in their text, but their Fig. 1 plots the specimens from the "Arundel clay of the Patuxent Formation" in the upper Aptian, with ages taken from earlier works. Oreska and colleagues (2013) give the formation no stage. D'Emic and colleagues and Oreska and colleagues treat the Arundel Clay as a clay or facies of the Patuxent, while Stanford and colleagues describe it as a lignitic facies of the Potomac Group that occurs as lenses at various levels within the group, not at one horizon. The registry carries the Arundel as a formation of its own, so the Arundel material these works discuss (USNM 3049, USNM 3121, Oreska and colleagues' fauna) bears on that unit as much as on this one. Governs `stages: [Aptian]` on [Patuxent](../../../stratigraphy/p/patuxent.yml).

**Evidence.**
- stanford2011a, p. 917: "The Patuxent Formation is the oldest stratigraphic unit exposed in the Atlantic Coastal Plain of Maryland, based on palynologic assemblages … which indicate an Aptian age, evidently in the younger end of this interval"; it "outcrops in a narrow belt trending south-southwest from the Delaware-Maryland border to Fredericksburg, Virginia".
- stanford2011a, p. 917: the Potomac Group "also includes a light-gray and lignitic facies called the Arundel Clay", "from which all previous Lower Cretaceous dinosaur finds have been made"; "The Arundel Clay occurs as lenses of diverse sizes at various stratigraphic levels within the Potomac Group"; Patuxent lithology "varies markedly within the outcrop belt in Maryland, dominated by medium to coarse sands, sandstones, and pebble conglomerates that are interbedded with large volumes of siderite-cemented sandstones, siltstones (both gray and red), lignite-bearing light gray clays, and red clays", which "apparently accumulated in distal deltaic fans and sluggish river floodplains".
- stanford2011a, p. 918: "Occurrence.—Patuxent Formation, Potomac Group (early Aptian in age)".
- d'emic2012a, p. 14, Fig. 1: "Arun, Arundel clay of the Patuxent Formation specimens", plotted in the Aptian below the 112 Ma base of the Albian, the ages following earlier works.
- d'emic2012a, p. 18: large-bodied theropod remains other than Acrocanthosaurus "are limited to a few teeth and bones from the Cedar Mountain Formation of Utah, Arundel clay of the Patuxent Formation of Maryland, and Cloverly Formation of Wyoming and Montana".
- d'emic2012a, p. 20: Harris compared USNM 3049, a caudal centrum "from the Arundel clay of the Patuxent Formation", with Acrocanthosaurus and "suggested that they represent different theropod taxa", but "the centrum does not possess any features unique to theropods, so it could pertain to an ornithischian or a sauropod rather than a theropod"; "Some isolated teeth from the Arundel clay of the Patuxent Formation of Maryland are similar to Acrocanthosaurus in proportions, denticulation, and the presence of weak banding (e.g., USNM 3121 …)".
- oreska2013a, p. 268: Sørensen Index values were calculated "between several Lower Cretaceous units: the Cloverly, Antlers, Patuxent (Arundel Clay facies), and Cedar Mountain (Mussentuchit Member) formations".
- oreska2013a, p. 284: "there are 10 formally described genera in the Patuxent but at least 46 in the Mussentuchit and 28 in the Antlers"; "the Cloverly, Antlers, and Mussentuchit faunas are more similar to one another than any are to the Patuxent".
- oreska2013a, Table 3: under the broader definitions of taxonomic similarity, the Patuxent scores 0.353 against the Cloverly, 0.373 against the Antlers and 0.253 against the Mussentuchit.
- oreska2013a, p. 286: "The lack of faunal similarity between the Cloverly and Patuxent faunas may be due to geographic (and/or temporal) separation, but Sørensen indices can also reflect sampling bias"; "In addition to being poorly sampled for vertebrates, the Patuxent Formation yields multiple taxa not identified below the family level, which were therefore omitted from the analysis".

**Ruled out.**
- *No stage from D'Emic and colleagues (2012).* Their text names none, but Fig. 1 places the Arundel clay specimens in the Aptian. The figure relays earlier works and adds no stage beyond the Aptian.
- *An age from Oreska and colleagues' (2013) faunal comparison.* They allow that the Patuxent's dissimilarity may reflect "temporal" separation but weigh it against poor sampling, and date neither the formation nor its fauna.

**Open.** Whether the Patuxent's Aptian is early or late: Stanford and colleagues (2011) say both, and the palynological works they cite for it have not been read. Whether the Arundel Clay is a facies of the Patuxent, as D'Emic and colleagues and Oreska and colleagues treat it, or a separate unit of the Potomac Group occurring at several levels, as Stanford and colleagues describe it.

# Poison Strip Sandstone

## What do Kirkland and Madsen (2007) and Joeckel and colleagues (2019) say about the age of the Poison Strip Sandstone?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Neither determines an age for the member in its own text. Kirkland and Madsen (2007) say only that its fossils "suggest persistence of the Yellow Cat fauna"; their two charts place it differently: the correlation chart, modified from Kirkland and colleagues (1999), in the upper Aptian (Fig. 4), and the generalized column against the time scale near the Barremian–Aptian boundary (Fig. 19). Joeckel and colleagues (2019) relay that the member's dinosaur fauna is "considered to be early Aptian", with a "potential correlation" to the faunas of the Lakota Formation and the upper Wealden. They set aside the 146 Ma detrital zircon maximum depositional age that Ludvigson and colleagues (2010) report from the member, because the sampled sandstone overlies a carbonate dated at 119.4 ± 2.6 Ma, so the zircons were recycled from older Mesozoic strata. Ludvigson and colleagues place that carbonate in the lower Poison Strip Sandstone at Ruby Ranch Road and read the two dates together as early Aptian for the basal strata there. The readings from these works fall in the Barremian and Aptian. Governs `stages: [Hauterivian, Barremian, Aptian, Albian]` on [Poison Strip Sandstone](../../../stratigraphy/p/poison-strip-sandstone.yml).

**Evidence.**
- kirkland2007a, p. 6, Fig. 4: "Correlation chart of important middle Cretaceous stratigraphic sequences. Modified after Kirkland and others (1999)"; the chart plots "Poison Strip SS" in the upper Aptian.
- kirkland2007a, p. 12: "Overall, the fossils in the Poison Strip Sandstone suggest persistence of the Yellow Cat fauna."
- kirkland2007a, p. 25, Fig. 19: "A generalized column of Utah's late Mesozoic strata plotted against the geological time scale"; the column plots "Poison Strip Ss." at about the Barremian–Aptian boundary.
- kirkland2007a, p. 72: "the UGS Geologic Mapping Program maps the basal conglomerate as Buckhorn west of the Green River and as Poison Strip east of the river".
- joeckel2019a, p. ?, Dinosaur faunas (accepted manuscript): "The youngest of these faunas, considered to be early Aptian, is preserved in the Poison Strip Member"; "indicates a potential correlation between the Poison Strip Member fauna and that of the Lakota Formation (South Dakota, USA; 700 km distant), as well as that of the upper Wealden fauna in Europe".
- joeckel2019a, p. ?, Discussion, U-Pb geochronology (accepted manuscript): "Ludvigson et al. (2010, p. 968) reported a detrital zircon U-Pb Maximum Depositional Age (MDA) of 146±0.49 Ma from the Poison Strip Sandstone Member"; "That detrital zircon population was produced from a sandstone bed overlying a carbonate bed that produced a U-Pb age of 119.4±2.6 Ma, and thus those zircons were recycled from older Mesozoic strata".
- ludvigson2010b, p. 960: "In the lower Poison Strip Sandstone, at the position of samples RRR-7 to RRR-8 (Fig. 8), is a 1.1-meter-thick brecciated laminar and chalcedony-replaced micritic limestone"; "This unit yields the early Aptian U-Pb date".
- ludvigson2010b, p. 968: "detrital zircon sample RRR-12 occurs a few meters above sample RRR-8"; the two dates "show conclusively that the basal sampled strata at Ruby Ranch Road are early Aptian in age".

**Ruled out.**
- *The 146 Ma maximum depositional age as the member's age.* The zircons come from a sandstone a few meters above a carbonate dated at 119.4 ± 2.6 Ma, so they are recycled grains; Joeckel and colleagues (2019) say so, and Ludvigson and colleagues (2010) themselves read the pair of dates as early Aptian.

**Open.** Kirkland and Madsen's (2007) two charts disagree, upper Aptian against the Barremian–Aptian boundary, and the text gives no reason for either placement.

# Peruc-Korycany

## Why do the cited works use the combined name Peruc-Korycany Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Fejfar and colleagues (2005) join the Peruc and Korycany Beds "of the local stratigraphic column" into one formation because the two cannot be separated cleanly: the continental Peruc Beds broadly underlie the marine Korycany Beds, but the boundary between them interfingers, with repeated marine incursions, and some deposits of the two are of the same age. They credit the combined usage to Houša (1987). Later works keep the formation and treat the Peruc and Korycany as its members (see the 2026-10-07 entry in [*Burianosaurus*](../../genera/B/Burianosaurus.md)). Governs the name and `rank: formation` on [Peruc-Korycany](../../../stratigraphy/p/peruc-korycany.yml).

**Evidence.**
- fejfar2005a, p. 296: sedimentation "started in the early Cenomanian and continued during the middle Cenomanian as a typical characteristic depositional regression sequence (Peruc Beds of the local stratigraphic column", and "The typical late Cenomanian marine facies are predominantly sandstones" (Korycany Beds).
- fejfar2005a, p. 296: "The boundary between the Peruc and the Korycany Beds is irregular, with paralic development of several marine incursions into terrestrial areas."
- fejfar2005a, p. 296: "Therefore, we use the term Peruc-Korycany Formation to characterize the mixed lithologies deposited in this area", and "Some marine deposits of the Korycany Beds and continental sediments of the Peruc Beds are considered to be isochronous".

**Ruled out.**
- *A facies relationship with no superposition.* An earlier reference note read the combined name as marking "a facies relationship rather than superposition". Fejfar and colleagues describe a regression in the early and middle Cenomanian (Peruc Beds) followed by a late Cenomanian marine transgression (Korycany Beds), so the two are in broad succession; only the boundary interval interfingers and is in part isochronous.

**Open.** Nothing.

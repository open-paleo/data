# Phu Kradung

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Phu Kradung Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous, with no stage fixed by a dated horizon. Racey and colleagues (1996) give only a hedged choice of epochs. Racey (2009) reports palynology placing most of the formation in the Early Cretaceous and vertebrate dating that suggests the Tithonian or the Early Cretaceous. Tong and colleagues (2021) read the turtles as Late Jurassic low in the formation and Early Cretaceous high in it. Jia and colleagues (2022) set a floor with a detrital zircon maximum depositional age of about 154 Ma, which excludes the Late Triassic and Early Jurassic readings. Governs `period: [Late Jurassic, Early Cretaceous]` and the Tithonian and Berriasian in `stages` on [Phu Kradung](../../../stratigraphy/p/phu-kradung.yml). The Kimmeridgian and Valanginian are not settled here; see Open.

**Evidence.**
- racey1996a, p. 5, Abstract: the Nam Phong Formation "is dated accurately for the first time as late Norian to Rhaetian", and the Phra Wihan, Sao Khua, Phu Phan and Khok Kruat Formations "are reassigned to the Early Cretaceous (Berriasian to Aptian) on the basis of new palynological data"; the Phu Kradung, "which is conventionally assigned to the Lower Jurassic, is probably Late Jurassic or Early Cretaceous, although this cannot be determined with confidence".
- racey2009a, p. 47: Racey and Goodall's (2009) palynological data "also indicate that most of the Phu Kradung Formation is Early Cretaceous", "whereas the vertebrate dating suggests that the Phu Kradung Formation is Late Jurassic (Tithonian) or Early Cretaceous in age"; p. 52: "A Late Jurassic age for the lowermost part of the Phu Kradung Formation cannot be completely ruled out".
- racey2009a, p. 53: from a personal communication that the gap between the Phu Kradung and Sao Khua vertebrate assemblages is wider than that between the Sao Khua and Khok Kruat, "if the Khok Kruat Formation is Aptian then it is more likely that the Sao Khua Formation is Barremian (the inference being that the Phu Kradung Formation would be earliest Cretaceous (Berriasian) or Latest Jurassic (Tithonian)".
- tong2021a, p. 2, Geological Setting: "studies on palynology [25] and detrital zircon [26] indicated that the Phu Kradung Formation ranges from Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous"; p. 13, section 5: "the age of the Phu Kradung Formation is still uncertain".
- tong2021a, p. 14, section 5: "The evidence from the turtle assemblages seems to support a Late Jurassic age for the lower part of the formation and an Early Cretaceous age for its upper part"; "the upper part of the Phu Kradung Formation is basal Cretaceous (Berriasian–?Valanginian [77]) in age", reference 77 being Racey (2009); p. 14, Conclusions: the macrobaenid "provides further support for the Early Cretaceous age of the upper part of the Phu Kradung Formation".
- jia2022a, p. 3481, Abstract: detrital zircons give "maximum possible depositional ages of approximately 208, approximately 154, and approximately 140 Ma" for the Nam Phong, Phu Kradung and Sao Khua Formations, and the poles for the Nam Phong and "the Late Jurassic Phu Kradung Formation" "passed the fold test" (translated).
- jia2022a, p. 3483: Carter and Bristow (2003), "through fission track and U-Pb detrital zircon studies, suggest a depositional age of Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous" (translated).
- jia2022a, p. 3485: in sample Nt17 "the youngest apparent ages obtained are 154±6 Ma and 161±3 Ma", and the youngest peak, about 154 Ma, is taken as the maximum possible depositional age (translated); the Sao Khua samples (Nt34–36) gave no meaningful paleomagnetic result.
- jia2022a, p. 3493: the Phu Kradung's "youngest detrital zircon peak age of about 154 Ma (Late Jurassic Kimmeridgian)" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Triassic to Early Jurassic, or the conventional Early Jurassic.* Jia and colleagues' maximum depositional age of about 154 Ma puts the whole formation in the Late Jurassic or later.
- *A stage from Racey and colleagues (1996).* The paper offers two epochs as alternatives and disclaims the choice in the same sentence; it names no stage for this formation, though it dates the units above and below it to stages.
- *Tithonian or Berriasian from Racey's (2009) inference on the vertebrate gap.* It rests on a personal communication and on an "if" about the Khok Kruat; under the methods rule on personal communications it adds no stage of its own.

**Open.** Two stages lack a reading. The Kimmeridgian rests on Ren and colleagues (2024), who cite Jia and colleagues (2022), and Jia and colleagues attach "Kimmeridgian" to the 154 Ma maximum depositional age, which is a limit, not an age. The Valanginian rests on Tong and colleagues' "Berriasian–?Valanginian", which they credit to Racey (2009); Racey's chapter names no Valanginian, and gives the formation's top as Berriasian or Tithonian (p. 53). The nearest source for a Valanginian is Racey and Goodall (2009), who put the Phu Kradung, Phra Wihan and Sao Khua together in "the Early Cretaceous, with a maximum age-range of Berriasian – early Barremian", perhaps "late Valanginian–early Barremian" (racey2009b, p. 81). Whether that range dates the Phu Kradung alone, and so whether it adds stages up to the Barremian, needs a reading of Racey and Goodall's Phu Kradung samples.

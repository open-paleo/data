# Oxford Clay

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Oxford Clay?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Callovian to Oxfordian. Cope and colleagues (1980) correlate the Lower and Middle Oxford Clay with the Callovian, up to the *lamberti* Zone, and the Upper Oxford Clay with the Lower Oxfordian *mariae* and lower *cordatum* Zones. The base lies at different levels from area to area: near the top of the *calloviense* Subzone in Dorset, at the base of the *enodatum* Subzone at Peterborough, and in the *jason* Zone in Kent, where the 'Ornatus Beds' stand in for the Lower Oxford Clay. They use the Lower, Middle and Upper divisions without giving the unit a rank or a parent. Governs `stages: [Callovian, Oxfordian]` and `period: [Middle Jurassic, Late Jurassic]` on [Oxford Clay](../../../stratigraphy/o/oxford-clay.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cope1980a, p. 57: "the Callovian Oxford Clay may be divided, on lithological grounds, into Lower and Middle units".
- cope1980a, p. 48: in Dorset the Lower Oxford Clay–Kellaways Rock junction "is placed at about the top of the *calloviense* Subzone".
- cope1980a, p. 55: "At Peterborough, the Kellaways Beds–Oxford Clay junction is placed at the base of the *enodatum* Subzone".
- cope1980a, p. 52: in Kent "the 'Ornatus Beds' are broadly equivalent to the Lower Oxford Clay of central England, appearing to occupy the *jason* Zone, *coronatum* Zone, and the lower part of the *athleta* Zone"; *K. jason* was recorded "from the base of the Oxford Clay at Guilford and Tilmanstone".
- cope1980a, p. 67: at Purton the Oxford Clay is exposed "from the *cordatum* Zone, *costicardia* Subzone (Red Nodule Beds) down to the *mariae* Zone, *scarburgense* Subzone".
- cope1980a, Fig. 8: the Middle Oxford Clay columns reach the *lamberti* Zone; Fig. 11a: the Upper Oxford Clay of column O1 lies in the Lower Oxfordian (our reading of the figures).

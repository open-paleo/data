# On Gong

## Does any work date the On Gong to a stage?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No; the Aptian and Albian are dropped, leaving the Early Cretaceous. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry. Every stage-level age is the Ondai Sair's carried over by correlation: Dong (1993) makes the On Gong "approximately equivalent" in age to the Ondai Sair and names no stage; Weishampel and colleagues (2004) give "Aptian-Albian (i.e., approximately equivalent with the Ondai Sair Formation; Dong 1993c)" and credit their Ondai Sair age to Shuvalov (1974), whose held text never names the Ondai Sair; Mannion (2011) reaches "Aptian-Albian (?)" the same way, and Averianov and colleagues (2025) repeat it in passing. The age of a correlative does not date a unit, and the Andakhuduk's dating would be the same. Gilmore (1933) gives the On Gong as Lower Cretaceous. Governs `period: [Early Cretaceous]` and the absent `stages` on [On Gong](../../../stratigraphy/o/on-gong.yml), and the absent `stage` on [*Mongolosaurus haplodon*](../../../genera/M/Mongolosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dong1993c, p. 2098: "In age, this formation is approximately equivalent to the Guyang Group of Inner Mongolia and the Ondai Sair Formation of Mongolia."
- weishampel2004b, p. 567: "5. On Gong Formation (Gilmore 1933b; …) … Age: Aptian-Albian (i.e., approximately equivalent with the Ondai Sair Formation; Dong 1993c)".
- mannion2011b, p. 358: "Aptian-Albian (?), Early Cretaceous, On Gong Formation (Hu Khung Ulan locality)".
- gilmore1933b, p. 11: "HORIZON:—On Gong, Lower Cretaceous."

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian–Albian from the Ondai Sair.* The age of a correlative.

**Open.** No date or index fossil from the On Gong itself.

## How is the On Gong Formation dated?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Aptian–Albian, by correlation only: none of the works cited dates the On Gong from its own rocks or fossils. Dong (1993) makes it "approximately equivalent" in age to the Guyang Group and the Ondai Sair Formation of Mongolia, and Weishampel and colleagues (2004) and Mannion (2011) give it the Aptian–Albian they assign to the Ondai Sair, which Weishampel and colleagues credit to Shuvalov (1974). Mannion marks the age with a query. The *Mongolosaurus haplodon* holotype, the formation's best-known fossil, was collected at Hu Khung Ulan by Walter Granger in 1928 on the Central Asiatic Expeditions; Gilmore (1933) called it the only dinosaur then found in the formation, and Dong (1993) later lists a small theropod from it as well. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on [On Gong](../../../stratigraphy/o/on-gong.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dong1993c, p. 2098: "In age, this formation is approximately equivalent to the Guyang Group of Inner Mongolia and the Ondai Sair Formation of Mongolia. The fossils obtained include a few fragments of turtle, a small theropod dinosaur, and the sauropod Mongolosaurus hoplodon".
- weishampel2004b, p. 564: "Hühteeg Svita (= Ondai Sair and Oshih formations ...)", "Age: Aptian-Albian (Shuvalov 1974)".
- weishampel2004b, p. 567: On Gong Formation, "Age: Aptian-Albian (i.e., approximately equivalent with the Ondai Sair Formation; Dong 1993c)".
- mannion2011b, p. 356: "collected in 1928 by Walter Granger, as part of the Central Asiatic Expeditions"; "The On Gong Formation has been suggested to be approximately equivalent to the Ondai Sair Formation (included within the Hühteeg Svita Formation) of Mongolia (Dong 1993), which has been dated as Aptian-Albian (Shuvalov 1974; Weishampel et al. 2004)".
- mannion2011b, p. 358: "Aptian-Albian (?), Early Cretaceous, On Gong Formation (Hu Khung Ulan locality)".
- gilmore1933b, p. 11: "this is the only dinosaur so far found in the On Gong formation".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Whether the Ondai Sair, and so the On Gong, reaches the Albian. Shuvalov (1974), the source Weishampel and colleagues give for the Aptian–Albian, dates the deposits at Khobur and Khuren-Dukh by correlation with the Khulsyngol Formation, and refers to the conchostracans of the Anda-Khuduk as Hauterivian–Barremian. The [Andakhuduk](../../../stratigraphy/a/andakhuduk.yml), under which the Ondai Sair is a variant, is dated Hauterivian to Aptian on its own sources. A decision on whether the On Gong keeps its Albian, or takes the Andakhuduk's range, would settle the stages.

# Valle de la Luna

## What do the cited works say about the definition and age of the Valle de la Luna Member?

*2026-11-23*

Supersedes in part the 2026-10-21 entry, which has Ezcurra (2010) combine two of Shipman's (2004) figures into 217.0 ± 1.7 Ma: the figure is printed a year earlier, credited to Shipman, by Currie and colleagues (2009), whose authors include Shipman, for a bentonite about 70 m from the top of the formation in Section 1, and Ezcurra places the ash "following Currie et al. 2009". That entry's conclusion, that the thesis itself does not print the date, stands.

**Conclusion.** Carnian and Norian, with the dispute. Currie and colleagues (2009) designate the member, 250–470 m of mudstone and sandstone with its type section in their Section 1 and its base at the top of the uppermost calcareous paleosol of the Cancha de Bochas Member, and give all four members a Carnian age of deposition, reading their dates, among them 217.0 ± 1.7 Ma near the top, on the Triassic time scale of Gradstein and colleagues (1995). Ezcurra (2010) recalculates that date to about 219.4–220.4 Ma, which puts the top of the member in the mid-Norian and the Carnian–Norian boundary probably within the member. Martínez and colleagues (2011) date a bentonite near the top of the formation at 225.9 ± 0.9 Ma. Sciscio and colleagues (2017) list the 217.0 and 225.9 Ma dates for the upper member as the only radiometric control on the overlying Los Colorados Formation, and attribute the mismatch to the dating method or to the sedimentology. Both upper dates are Norian on the ICS chart v2024/12 (Norian base 227.3 Ma), as the 2026-10-21 entry notes for the second. Governs `stages: [Carnian, Norian]` and the `dispute` on [Valle de la Luna](../../../stratigraphy/v/valle-de-la-luna.yml).

**Evidence.**
- currie2009a, p. 84: "The newly designated units are, in ascending order, the La Peña Member, the Cancha de Bochas Member, the Valle de la Luna Member, and the Quebrada de la Sal Member. Vertebrate fossils and isotopic ages of volcanic rocks in the Ischigualasto Formation indicate a Carnian age of deposition for all of the lithostratigraphic members described below".
- currie2009a, p. 85: "The Valle de la Luna Member of the Ischigualasto Formation consists of 250–470 m of mudstone and sandstone"; its type section lies "within Section 1 of the study area"; "The base of the member is placed at the top of the upper-most calcareous paleosol in the Cancha de Bochas Member".
- currie2009a, p. 76: plagioclase from a bentonite about 70 m from the top of the formation "in Section 1 (Fig. 3) provide a date of 217.0 ± 1.7 Ma (Shipman, 2004)"; "Collectively, these ages support a Carnian age of deposition based on the Triassic time-scale of Gradstein et al. (1995)".
- ezcurra2010a, p. 372: the ash lies "approximately 610 m from the base of the formation (following Currie et al. 2009, fig. 3) (top of the Valle de la Luna Member, Section 1 of Currie et al. 2009)"; "the Carnian–Norian boundary should be located close to the middle of the formation probably within the Valle the la Luna Member" (sic, for "Valle de la Luna").
- martínez2011a, p. 209: "radioisotopic ages that bracket the formation between 231.4 and 225.9 million years ago (Ma)".
- sciscio2017a, accepted manuscript pp. 26–27: "The sole radiometric controls for the Los Colorados Formation come from 40Ar/39Ar dating of the underlying beds of the upper Valle de la Luna Member ... and these provide a discrepancy in ages ... despite being obtained from the same unit"; the discrepancy "is likely to indicate either the low-precision methods of 40Ar/39Ar dating, the complexity in underpinning a central age-date plateau, or other sedimentological complications such as a strongly diachronous Formation with many paraconformities".
- sciscio2017a, Fig. 8 caption: "(ii) 40Ar/39Ar date of the Valle de la Luna Member (ISCH-6-611): 225.9±0.9 Ma by Martínez et al. (2011)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Carnian only, on Currie and colleagues (2009).* Their Carnian covers all four members, but the two dates from the upper member, theirs and that of Martínez and colleagues (2011), are both Norian on the current chart, and Ezcurra (2010) puts the Carnian–Norian boundary within the member.
- *217.0 ± 1.7 Ma as a figure Ezcurra (2010) or Sciscio and colleagues (2017) produced.* Both relay it; Currie and colleagues (2009, p. 76) print it first, credited to Shipman (2004).

**Open.** Whether Currie and colleagues' Carnian for the member is a stated stage, carried over as a reading, or a reading of 217.0 ± 1.7 Ma on the Gradstein and colleagues (1995) scale, which the current chart would place in the Norian, is not settled here; if the latter, the Carnian side of the dispute rests on an old-chart label. Currie and colleagues (2001), the meeting abstract credited with the 223 Ma date, and the source of the 217.0 ± 1.7 Ma figure that Currie and colleagues (2009) credit to a thesis that does not print it, are unread.

## Does Shipman's thesis give the 217.0 ± 1.7 Ma date credited to it for the top of the member?

*2026-10-21*

**Conclusion.** No. Shipman (2004) prints a 223 Ma date at 611 m in its section 1, credited to Currie and colleagues (2001), and its own 218.5 ± 1.7 Ma at 352 m in its section 3. Ezcurra's (2010) "217.0 ± 1.7 Ma" on plagioclase at about 610 m pairs the first date's height with the second's uncertainty and a value that appears in neither. Sciscio and colleagues (2017) repeat the figure. The member's Norian does not depend on it: the 225.9 ± 0.9 Ma date of Martínez and colleagues (2012), 630 m above the base, is Norian on the ICS chart v2024/12 (Norian base 227.3 Ma). Governs the `shipman2004a` and `ezcurra2010a` notes on [Valle de la Luna](../../../stratigraphy/v/valle-de-la-luna.yml) and the `ezcurra2010a` note on [Ischigualasto](../../../stratigraphy/i/ischigualasto.yml); `stages: [Carnian, Norian]` is unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- shipman2004a, p. 103, Fig. B-8: "611m, 223 Ma (Currie et al., 2001)" beside section 1; "352m, 218.5± 1.7 Ma" beside section 3; "90m, 228.06± 0.78 Ma (Rogers et al., 1993)" beside section 2. Fig. B-9, p. 104, repeats the three labels.
- shipman2004a, p. 77: "The date we did obtain was from section 3 at 352 m and produced a date of 218 ± 1.7Ma"; p. 126: "Two ashes near the top of the Ischigualasto Formation have been dated at 223 and 218.5±1.5 Ma (this study)".
- ezcurra2010a, p. 372: "Another age dating has been performed by Shipman (2004) on plagioclase crystals from a bentonite ash, approximately 610 m from the base of the formation … This dating yielded an 40Ar/39Ar date of 217.0 ± 1.7 Ma (Shipman 2004)."
- sciscio2017a, Fig. 8 caption: "40Ar/39Ar date of the Valle de la Luna Member (feldspars): 217.0±1.7 Ma by Shipman (2004)".
- martínez2012b, p. 17: bentonite ISCH-6-611, "630 m above the base and 70 m below the top of the formation", at 225.9 ± 0.9 Ma.

**Ruled out.**
- *217.0 ± 1.7 Ma as Shipman's date for the top of the member.* The thesis text and its measured-section figures give no such value. The value at the 611 m height is 223 Ma and is credited to Currie and colleagues (2001), not to the thesis.
- *218.5 ± 1.7 Ma as a date for the top of the Valle de la Luna Member.* It comes from 352 m in Shipman's section 3, one of the two ashes the thesis places "near the top of the Ischigualasto Formation" (p. 126). The thesis names no members, so placing that ash in Currie and colleagues' (2009) scheme would be our inference.

**Open.** Currie and colleagues (2001) is a meeting abstract that has not been read, so the method and uncertainty of the 223 Ma date are unknown. The thesis text says only one of its own samples gave a reliable age (p. 77), but elsewhere calls both near-top dates "this study" (p. 126).

## Does "Valle de la Luna" in the Eoraptor literature mean the member?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** No. Sereno and colleagues (2013) use it for the valley in Ischigualasto Provincial Park where the lower members crop out. *Eoraptor* comes from the Cancha de Bochas Member. Governs `member: Cancha de Bochas` on [*Eoraptor*](../../../genera/E/Eoraptor.yml), and which sources date `stages: [Carnian, Norian]` on [Valle de la Luna](../../../stratigraphy/v/valle-de-la-luna.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sereno2013a, p. 83: "the lower portion of the Ischigualasto Formation in the Valle de la Luna ('Valley of the Moon') of Ischigualasto Provincial Park"; p. 86: the lower two members "are well exposed along the southern edge of the basin in a region called the Valle de la Luna. All of the remains of *Eoraptor lunensis* come from the Cancha de Bochas Member in the Valle de la Luna".

**Ruled out.**
- *Sereno and colleagues' "mid-Carnian" as the member's age.* It dates the Cancha de Bochas Member (sereno2013a, p. 86).
- *Peyre de Fabrègues and colleagues (2015) as naming the member.* Their Table 2 gives *Eoraptor* as "Ischigualasto Formation | Valle de la Luna, Provincia de San Juan", which is the valley in the locality column (peyredefabrègues2015a, Table 2).

**Open.** Nothing.

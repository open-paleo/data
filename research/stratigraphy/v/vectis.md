# Vectis

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Vectis Formation, and where in it does the Aptian begin?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Barremian and Aptian, unchanged. The Aptian rests on Kerth and Hailwood's (1988) magnetostratigraphy, which correlates the reverse-polarity Vectis Magnetozone in the Shepherd's Chine Member with the lowermost Aptian chron CM-0 and so puts the base of the Aptian at least 25–30 m below the top of that member. Lockwood and colleagues (2021, 2024) and Bonsor and colleagues (2023) repeat the placement, citing Kerth and Hailwood, and the Lockwood papers also Robinson and Hesselbo (2004). The Barremian rests on Barker and colleagues (2020), "Barremian to lower Aptian", and on Robinson and Hesselbo's mid-Barremian at the formation's base; the boundary inside the youngest member does not fix the base, as the 2026-10-05 rule in [methods](../../methods.md) on a stage boundary inside a unit sets out with this formation as its case. Kerth and Hailwood give the formation as "formerly the Wealden Shales", and Bonsor and colleagues repeat it. The works differ on thickness: 50–60 m in Kerth and Hailwood, "40 m or more" in Lockwood and colleagues (2021) after Radley and Barker (1998), and "approximately 40 m" in Lockwood and colleagues (2024), above about 180 m of Wessex Formation. Governs `stages: [Barremian, Aptian]` and `variants: [Wealden Shales]` on [Vectis](../../../stratigraphy/v/vectis.yml).

**Evidence.**
- kerth1988a, p. 351: the Vectis magnetozone "may be correlated either with Lower Aptian reverse polarity Chron CM-0 or with Lower Barremian Chron CM-1, with the former being the more likely", so that "the base of the Aptian stage ... must lie within the Vectis Formation, at least 25-30m beneath the top of the Shepherd's Chine Member, rather than at the top of this member as hitherto commonly accepted".
- kerth1988a, p. 352: "the Vectis Formation (formerly the Wealden Shales) which comprises 50-60 m of sediments", deposited under "increasing marine influence", in terminology the paper adopts from earlier authors.
- kerth1988a, p. 359: the CM-1 reading, "of Lower to Middle Barremian age", "would imply the occurrence of a large stratigraphic gap in these sections, spanning the Upper Barremian and the lowermost Aptian"; the CM-0 reading, "we believe more plausible", puts the base of the Aptian "within the Shepherd's Chine Member of the Vectis Formation, at or below the base of the Vectis reverse polarity magnetozone".
- robinson2004a, p. 142: "fossil-wood carbon-isotope values from the mid-Barremian (at the base of the Vectis Formation) onwards".
- lockwood2021a, p. 848: "represented by argillaceous deposits and occasional interbedded sandstone and shelly limestones"; p. 849: "deposited predominantly in a shallow coastal lagoon of fluctuating salinity"; "the Wessex Formation on the Isle of Wight is approximately 180 m thick (Stewart 1978) and the Vectis Formation is 40 m or more (Radley & Barker 1998)"; "the Barremian–Aptian boundary lies within the Shepherds Chine Member, which forms the youngest unit of the formation (Kerth & Hailwood 1988; Robinson & Hesselbo 2004)".
- bonsor2023a, p. 3: "The Vectis Formation comprises the Cowleaze Chine Member and the younger Shepherd's Chine Member, both dominated by grey dysaerobic mudstones, intercalated by a 2–6 m thick arenaceous unit, the Barnes High Sandstone Member, deposited at the mouth-bar of a distributary river system as it entered a brackish lagoon"; "The Shepherd's Chine Member is younger than magnetostratigraphic cron CM-0, signifying that the base of the Aptian passes through the unit (Kerth & Hailwood 1988)"; "the Vectis Formation (formerly known as the Wealden Shales)".
- lockwood2024a, p. 2, Geological setting: the Wealden Group of the Isle of Wight "is further divided into the older Wessex Formation and the younger Vectis Formation"; the Vectis "is represented by approximately 40 m of clays and shales, with occasional interbedded sandstones and shelly limestones containing the bivalve Filosina gregaria and small oysters, deposited predominantly in a shallow coastal lagoon of varying salinity".
- lockwood2024a, p. 3, Geological setting: "The overlying Vectis Formation has been shown to contain the Barremian–Aptian boundary within the Shepherds Chine Member, which forms the youngest unit of the formation (Kerth & Hailwood, 1988; Robinson & Hesselbo, 2004)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Entirely Barremian, with the base of the Aptian at the top of the Shepherd's Chine Member.* The placement Kerth and Hailwood (1988) call "hitherto commonly accepted"; their magnetostratigraphy moves the boundary at least 25–30 m down into the member, and every later work cited follows them.
- *The Vectis Magnetozone as Lower to Middle Barremian chron CM-1.* Kerth and Hailwood's own alternative, which they judge less plausible because it needs a gap spanning the Upper Barremian and lowermost Aptian; it would leave the formation without the Aptian.

**Open.** Nothing.

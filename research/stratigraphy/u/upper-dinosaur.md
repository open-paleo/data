# Upper Dinosaur

## What do the uranium-lead date and the palynology say about the age of the Upper Dinosaur Member?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Neither fixes the member's stage, and Bussert and colleagues (2009) leave open whether it is wholly Late Jurassic or in part Early Cretaceous. Romer (2001) dated an ilium of *Brachiosaurus brancai* from the Upper Saurian Bed at Tendaguru Hill by U-Pb and found no unique solution: the method gives a minimum age for the fossil, the most consistent solution lies between 140 and 150 Ma, unrecognized additions of radiogenic lead make the age too old, and the solution was chosen to fit the biostratigraphic correlations. Bussert and colleagues place 140 to 150 Ma "approximately" in the Tithonian and the Berriasian on the time scale of Gradstein and colleagues (2004); on the ICS chart v2024/12 the span runs from the last 0.8 Myr of the Kimmeridgian (base Tithonian 149.2 Ma) through the Tithonian into the Berriasian (base 143.1 Ma). As a minimum and not a measured age, the figure permits a Berriasian without asserting one. Bussert and colleagues set against it Schrank's (2005) report that *Cicatricosisporites* is absent from the member, which "could indicate a Tithonian age". Governs `stages: [Kimmeridgian, Tithonian, Berriasian]` on [Upper Dinosaur](../../../stratigraphy/u/upper-dinosaur.yml), unchanged; the Berriasian is the possibility the 2026-09-30 entry on [Tendaguru](../t/tendaguru.md) gives.

**Evidence.**
- bussert2009a, p. 165, Age: "U-Pb dating of a pelvic bone from Brachiosaurus brancai of the Upper Dinosaur Member provided an age range from ca. 140 to 150 Ma (Romer 2001) that falls approximately into the Tithonian ... and the Berriasian"; the absence of *Cicatricosisporites* in the member and the one below "could indicate a Tithonian age of these units"; "More work is needed to decide unambiguously whether the Upper Dinosaur Member is wholly Late Jurassic or, in part, Early Cretaceous."
- bussert2009a, p. 165, Age: earlier views "positioned the boundary at the base of ... or within the Upper Dinosaur Member", against most workers, who "considered the Upper Dinosaur Member as Late Jurassic".
- romer2001a, p. 4202: "The dated material originates from the Upper Saurian Bed at Tendaguru Hill, Tanzania".
- romer2001a, p. 4201, Abstract: "Diagenetic incorporation of uranium into bone material establishes a U-Pb system that provides a minimum age for the fossil"; "No precise age can be determined from the data, although the most consistent solution falls in the range of 140 to 150 Ma"; "The effect of unrecognized diagenetic additions of radiogenic Pb is an overestimation of the age".
- romer2001a, p. 4209, 5.4 Age Constraints: "The best age estimate for the investigated bone (1) should be compatible with constraints from biostratigraphic correlations".

**Ruled out.**
- *A Berriasian age for the member from the U-Pb date.* The 140 to 150 Ma span reaches the Berriasian, but Romer gives it as the most consistent of several solutions and as a minimum age, warns that undetected radiogenic lead makes it too old, and fits it to the biostratigraphic correlations, so it is not an independent measured age. Bussert and colleagues, Schrank (2005) and Rauhut (2011) all trace their Berriasian or earliest Cretaceous to this one figure, which counts once.

**Open.** Whether the member reaches the Cretaceous, as Bussert and colleagues leave it. A dinoflagellate, ammonite or ash date from the member itself would settle it; the Tendaguru entries leave the same question open for the formation's Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary.

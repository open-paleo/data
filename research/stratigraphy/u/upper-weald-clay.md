# Upper Weald Clay

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Upper Weald Clay?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Barremian. Norman (2013) ends the Weald Sub-basin succession at "the top of the Barremian (Upper Weald Clay Formation)" and, after Batten (2011), dates the formation's exposures at Smokejacks Brickworks near Ockley, Surrey, and at Henfield, West Sussex, as "middle Barremian (~128 Ma)". Barker and colleagues (2023) and Raven and colleagues (2020) also give the formation as Barremian. The stated stage is the reading, and it is carried over (see [methods](../../methods.md), "A stated stage is carried over; a bare number is placed on the current chart"). Governs `stages` on [Upper Weald Clay](../../../stratigraphy/u/upper-weald-clay.yml).

**Evidence.**
- norman2013a, p. 389, Fig. 2 caption: "The Weald Sub-basin exposes an almost complete stratigraphic succession from the upper Berriasian (Ashdown Beds Formation) to the top of the Barremian (Upper Weald Clay Formation)."
- norman2013a, p. 390: material "has been collected from the Upper Weald Clay Formation (Fig. 2) exposures in quarries such as Smokejacks Brickworks, near Ockley in Surrey (Fig. 1) and Henfield in West Sussex. These exposures have been dated as middle Barremian (~128 Ma) (Batten, 2011)."

**Ruled out.**
- *Hauterivian, from Norman's "~128 Ma".* On ICS chart v2024/12 the Hauterivian ends and the Barremian begins at 125.77 Ma, so 128 Ma placed on that chart falls in the Hauterivian. The number is an approximate figure that Norman attaches to a stated stage, "middle Barremian", not a measured date, so the stage governs and the number is not re-placed.

**Open.** Nothing.

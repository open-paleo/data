# Chalk

## Does the Chalk Group include the Albian Red Chalk?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** No. Hopson (2005) dates the group "Upper Cretaceous, Cenomanian to Maastrichtian" and puts its base on the Upper Greensand and Gault in the Southern Province and on the Hunstanton Formation in the Northern Province. The Hunstanton Formation is the Red Chalk, which earlier Northern Province schemes counted as the base of the Ferriby Chalk; it is Albian, and the November 1999 BGS meeting that Hopson reports agreed it is a formation outside the group, older than the hiatus at the base of the Cenomanian. The group is divided into the Grey Chalk and White Chalk subgroups, and the Grey Chalk Subgroup, whose base is the erosion surface at the base of the Cenomanian, is divided in the Southern Province into the West Melbury Marly Chalk and Zig Zag Chalk formations. Governs `period: [Late Cretaceous]` and `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian, Coniacian, Santonian, Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Chalk](../../../stratigraphy/c/chalk.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hopson2005a, p. 15: "The group is formally divided into two subgroups, the Grey Chalk and White Chalk subgroups"; lower boundary "Generally unconformable (at a burrowed surface) on the underlying Lower Cretaceous strata (Upper Greensand and Gault formations in the Southern Province and on the Hunstanton Formation in the Northern Province)"; "Upper Cretaceous, Cenomanian to Maastrichtian."
- hopson2005a, p. 15, Grey Chalk Subgroup: "Divided in the Southern Province into the West Melbury Marly Chalk Formation and the Zig Zag Chalk Formation"; lower boundary "Unconformable, set at the burrowed erosion surface marking the base of the Cenomanian".
- hopson2005a, p. 31: the basal part of the Ferriby Formation "is treated as a separate unit named the Hunstanton Formation (about 3 m thick) and excluded from the Chalk Group"; "The Hunstanton Formation, commonly referred to as the Red Chalk, is of Lower Cretaceous (Albian) age".
- hopson2005a, p. 33: "The consensus in the meeting held at BGS Keyworth in November 1999 was that it was a formation, but not part of the newly defined Chalk Group, and as defined it is older than the major hiatus at the base of the Cenomanian".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Red Chalk as the base of the group, carrying it into the Albian.* Earlier Northern Province schemes placed it in the Ferriby Chalk Formation, at the base of the Chalk (hopson2005a, p. 31). The framework Hopson sets out excludes it, and the group's age begins at the Cenomanian (p. 15, p. 33).

**Open.** Nothing.

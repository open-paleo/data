# Chubut

## Do the Bajo Barreal and Lago Colhué Huapi belong to the Chubut Group?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** Yes; both take `parent: Chubut`. Suárez and colleagues (2014) place the Bajo Barreal in the group's Golfo San Jorge succession above the Castillo Formation, and Casal and colleagues (2015) erect the Lago Colhué Huapi Formation within the group as its youngest deposits. Both formations' stages fall within the group's. The Laguna Palacios Formation, which papers differ on as the group's top unit, gets no entry while no record names it. Governs `parent` on [Bajo Barreal](../../../stratigraphy/b/bajo-barreal.yml) and [Lago Colhué Huapi](../../../stratigraphy/l/lago-colhué-huapi.yml).

**Evidence.**
- suárez2014a, pp. 68–69: the Castillo Formation, "in turn, conformably underlying fluvial deposits with tuff intercalations of the Bajo Barreal and Laguna Palacios formations".
- casal2015a, p. 77: "permiten proponer una nueva unidad litoestratigráfica dentro del grupo, la Formación Lago Colhué Huapi (nov. nom.)" ["allow us to propose a new lithostratigraphic unit within the group, the Lago Colhué Huapi Formation (nov. nom.)", translated].

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does the Chubut Group keep the Hauterivian and Barremian, and what dates its top?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** The base moves to the Aptian; the Coniacian–Maastrichtian top stands. Supersedes the 2026-11-25 entries on the stages. No work read gives any unit of the group a Hauterivian. The Barremian appears only relayed: Codignotto and colleagues' (1978) "possibly Barremian" in Nullo (1983), and Chebli and colleagues' (1976) Barremian–Aptian in Bonaparte and Gasparini (1979); Nullo's own reading puts the whole Los Adobes in the Lower Cretaceous. The Aptian rests on Musacchio and Chebli's (1975) own microfossils from the Cerro Barcino (see [Cerro Barcino](../c/cerro-barcino.md)). At the top, Casal and colleagues (2015) date the Lago Colhué Huapi Formation Coniacian–Maastrichtian from its dinosaurs, and Ibiricu and colleagues (2025) give it the same range and a late Maastrichtian level near its top. Governs `stages`, Aptian to Maastrichtian, and the notes on nullo1983a and rauhut2004a on [Chubut](../../../stratigraphy/c/chubut.yml); [*Campylodoniscus ameghinoi*](../../../genera/C/Campylodoniscus.yml) is unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- nullo1983a, p. 60: "se ubica a toda la Formación Los Adobes en el Cretácico inferior, mientras que si bien los niveles más bajos de la Formación Cerro Barcino son de edad Cretácica inferior (Aptiano?)" ["the entire Los Adobes Formation is placed in the Lower Cretaceous, while although the lowest levels of the Cerro Barcino Formation are of Lower Cretaceous age (Aptian?)", translated].
- nullo1983a, p. 59: Codignotto and colleagues "asignan una edad posiblemente barremiana para los términos inferiores del Miembro Puesto La Paloma o superiores del Miembro Bardas Coloradas" ["assign a possibly Barremian age to the lower portions of the Puesto La Paloma Member or the upper portions of the Bardas Coloradas Member", translated].
- bonaparte1979c, p. 405: "Chebli et al. (1976) interpreted the age of the Chubut Group, to the east of Paso de Indios, as spanning the Barremian-Aptian interval (in part)".
- casal2015a, p. 77: "El contenido paleontológico de restos de dinosaurios permitió definir una edad comprendida entre el Coniaciano-Maastrichtiano" ["The paleontological content of dinosaur remains allowed us to define an age ranging between the Coniacian-Maastrichtian", translated].
- ibiricu2025a, p. 2: "the Upper Cretaceous (Coniacian–Maastrichtian) Lago Colhué Huapi Formation"; the new taxon comes from a horizon a few meters below the top, "latest Cretaceous (Maastrichtian, probably late Maastrichtian) in age".

**Ruled out.**
- *Hauterivian.* No work read gives it.
- *Barremian.* A relayed possibility and a relay of an unread work.

**Open.** Chebli and colleagues (1976) and Lizuaín and colleagues (1995) have not been read.

## Why does the Chubut Group not take Lesta and Ferello's Valanginian?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Their Valanginian is known only through relays, its authors called it provisional, and later work modified it. Bonaparte and Gasparini (1979) report that Lesta and Ferello's (1972) scheme, "which they expressly considered provisional", placed the Sierra San Bernardo units in the Valanginian to lower Senonian, and that Chebli and colleagues (1976) "by extension modified" it; Martínez (1998) lists it among "older ages". Lesta and Ferello (1972) has not been read, a relayed statement adds nothing to the reading it relays, and the reading as relayed is provisional. Answers the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line on the Valanginian. Governs `stages` on [Chubut](../../../stratigraphy/c/chubut.yml), which start at the Hauterivian, unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- bonaparte1979c, p. 405: "The stratigraphic scheme of these authors, which they expressly considered provisional, indicates that the Cretaceous units outcropping in the region of Sierra San Bernardo occupying the Valenginian-lower Senonian interval".
- bonaparte1979c, p. 405: "Chebli et al. (1976) interpreted the age of the Chubut Group, to the east of Paso de Indios, as spanning the Barremian-Aptian interval (in part), an interpretation that by extension modified the opinion of Lesta and Ferello (1972)."
- martínez1998a, p. 120: "Older ages have been suggested by Lesta and Ferello (Valanginian - lower Senonian, 1972)" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Valanginian from Lesta and Ferello (1972).* Relayed, provisional, and modified.

**Open.** Lesta and Ferello (1972) itself, which would show the basis of the scheme.

## What does Suárez and colleagues' (2014) U–Pb dating give for the top of the Chubut Group?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** A Cenomanian, possibly early Turonian, age for the group's youngest units, against Coniacian to Maastrichtian readings from other work; the disagreement is live and is recorded as a dispute. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry's Ruled out item on a Cenomanian minimum age and the part of its Open line about the U–Pb source, now read. Suárez and colleagues (2014) date tuffs in the Laguna Palacios Formation and the Manuel Arce Member of the Cerro Barcino Formation, which they treat as the youngest units, to 97.6 to 96.0 Ma, and the Bajo Barreal to 99.3 Ma; a few younger grains near 93 Ma allow a late Cenomanian–early Turonian reading, which they favor less. Casal and colleagues (2015) reject the minimum age on the stratigraphic position of their Lago Colhué Huapi Formation. Under the union rule the stages stay. Governs the `dispute` on [Chubut](../../../stratigraphy/c/chubut.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- suárez2014a, p. 72: "Thirteen grains of sample CH-8409 of the Laguna Palacios Formation gave a Cenomanian age of 97.6 ± 0.9 Ma".
- suárez2014a, p. 73: "constraining the Chubut Group to an older age than previously thought, not younger than the Cenomanian or the late Cenomanian-early Turonian".
- casal2015a, p. 90: "the paleontological content of the new unit and its stratigraphic relationship with the Laguna Palacios Formation rule out the Cenomanian age as the minimum age of the Chubut Group" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Dropping the Coniacian to Maastrichtian.* Suárez and colleagues date the units they sampled; Casal and colleagues publish a Coniacian to Maastrichtian formation inside the group, and a newer dating adds to the older readings rather than replacing them.

**Open.** Where the Lago Colhué Huapi lies relative to the dated Laguna Palacios tuffs. A date from the Lago Colhué Huapi itself would settle the dispute.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Chubut Group?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The group's age differs by region, and the readings run from the Hauterivian to the Maastrichtian. In the west its lowest units are Early Cretaceous: Rauhut (2004), after the provincial map of Lizuaín and colleagues (1995) and Nullo (1983), gives the group near Paso de Indios as Hauterivian–Cenomanian, with the Los Adobes Formation Hauterivian–Barremian, and Musacchio and Chebli (1975) date the Cerro Barcino Member Aptian. In the Sierra San Bernardo, Bonaparte and Gasparini (1979) read the sauropods of the Castillo, Bajo Barreal and Laguna Palacios formations as Senonian before the Maastrichtian, and find the group east of Paso de Indios older. Martínez (1998) lists Senonian and Maastrichtian readings, and Casal and colleagues (2015) have the group developing from the Aptian to the Campanian and reject a Cenomanian minimum age. Governs `stages` and `period` on [Chubut](../../../stratigraphy/c/chubut.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bonaparte1979c, p. 393, Abstract: the sauropods of the Castillo, Bajo Barreal and Laguna Palacios formations "indicate that the components of both lithologic groups cropping out in the Sierra San Bernardo are contemporaneous" with the Neuquén Group, dated "Senonian premaastrichtian".
- bonaparte1979c, p. 404: "diverse references in the literature coincide in assigning to the Upper Cretaceous the formational units of this typical region of the Chubut Group" (translated).
- bonaparte1979c, p. 405: the scheme of Lesta and Ferello (1972), "which they expressly considered provisional", places the units of the Sierra San Bernardo in the "Valenginian-lower Senonian interval"; "Chebli et al. (1976) interpreted the age of the Chubut Group, to the east of Paso de Indios, as spanning the Barremian-Aptian interval (in part)"; the sauropods "essentially confirm the Senonian age suggested by Huene (1929) for the taxa from the zone of Sierra San Bernardo"; Musacchio and Chebli (1975) "obtained microfossils of Aptian age" from the Cerro Barcino Member; "the Chubut Group outcropping in the region to the east of Paso de Indios is somewhat older than the section of the Chubut Group outcropping in the Sierra San Bernardo" (translated).
- martínez1998a, p. 120: "The age of the Chubut Group is controversial although there is major agreement in assigning it to the Upper Cretaceous: Senonian (Huene, 1929; Sciutto and Martínez, 1994), Maastrichtian (Menéndez, 1959); Senonian-pre-Maastrichtian (Bonaparte and Gasparini, 1979)"; "Older ages have been suggested by Lesta and Ferello (Valanginian - lower Senonian, 1972) and Lesta et al. (Barremian-Campanian, 1980)"; the fauna of the Bajo Barreal Formation, "especially the sauropods, indicates a possible Cenomanian age"; the group is made up, "from older to more modern", of "the Matasiete (Aptian), Castillo, Bajo Barreal and Laguna Palacios (Upper Cretaceous) formations" (translated).
- rauhut2004a, p. 894: according to the provincial map of Lizuaín and colleagues (1995), "the only Mesozoic sedimentary unit cropping out in this area is the Chubut Group (Hauterivian-Cenomanian; Fig. 1), and no uppermost Cretaceous units are found in this part of the province".
- rauhut2004a, p. 895: northwest of the Cañadón Grande, "the outcropping units of the Chubut Group include the lowermost Los Adobes Formation (Hauterivian-Barremian) and the lower two units of the Cerro Barcino Formation, the Puesto la Paloma (Barremian-Aptian) and Cerro Castaño (Aptian-Albian) members (Nullo, 1983)".
- casal2015a, p. 78: the group's lacustrine and fluvio-lacustrine systems "developed from the Aptian (Hechem et al. 1987) to the Campanian (Hechem et al. 1990)" (translated).
- casal2015a, p. 77, Abstract: "a new lithostratigraphic unit in the Group: Lago Colhué Huapi Formation", which "overlies the Bajo Barreal Formation (Upper Member) and it is covered by the Laguna Palacios or Salamanca Formation".
- casal2015a, p. 90: "the age of the proposed formation is comprised between the Coniacian-Maastrichtian"; U-Pb ages "of 97.6 and 96 Ma (Cenomanian) for the Laguna Palacios Formation" were reported, but "the paleontological content of the new unit and its stratigraphic relationship with the Laguna Palacios Formation rule out the Cenomanian age as the minimum age of the Chubut Group" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *A Cenomanian minimum age for the group, from U–Pb ages of 97.6 and 96 Ma on the Laguna Palacios Formation.* Casal and colleagues (2015) reject it: the Lago Colhué Huapi Formation, whose dinosaurs they date Coniacian to Maastrichtian, lies below the Laguna Palacios or the Salamanca, so the top of the group cannot be Cenomanian.

**Open.** Lesta and Ferello's (1972) Valanginian to early Senonian is relayed by Bonaparte and Gasparini (1979), who call it provisional and report that the sauropods support the earlier Upper Cretaceous ages, and by Martínez (1998), as one of the "older ages"; neither the Valanginian nor a reason for omitting it is recorded, and Lesta and Ferello (1972) has not been read. The U–Pb ages on the Laguna Palacios that Casal and colleagues (2015) reject have not been read in their source either, and they conflict with the Ar–Ar dates of 95.8 to 91.0 Ma from the Bajo Barreal below it that Martínez and colleagues (2016) report; reading that source would show whether the conflict is live.

# Cerro Barcino

## Does any work read give a lithology for the whole Cerro Barcino?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No; the field stays empty. Nullo (1983) describes only the Puesto La Paloma and Cerro Castaño members, the two that crop out on his map sheet, and Page and colleagues (1999) do not describe the formation's rock as a whole; a lithology comes from a work describing the whole unit. Governs the absent `lithology` on [Cerro Barcino](../../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-barcino.yml).

**Evidence.** None.

**Ruled out.** - *Nullo's member descriptions.* Two members on one sheet.

**Open.** A work describing the whole formation, such as one setting out the member scheme of Codignotto and colleagues (1978).

## Which of the Cerro Barcino's stages survive the rules?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** Aptian to Turonian; the Hauterivian, Barremian and Coniacian–Campanian are dropped. Supersedes the 2026-11-25 entry on the stages. Musacchio and Chebli (1975) date the member, as the Cerro Barcino Member of the Gorro Frigio Formation, by their own ostracods and charophytes at Paso de Indios, "near to or just Aptian", with "Aptiano?" on each new species; the query qualifies their own reading. Suárez and colleagues (2014) date the Puesto Manuel Arce Member themselves at 97.4 ± 0.8 Ma, Cenomanian, and allow it to be "as young as late Cenomanian-early Turonian", an undecided dating that gives both. The Barremian is Codignotto and colleagues' "possibly Barremian", relayed by Nullo (1983); the Hauterivian is Canale and colleagues' (2014) citation of Page and colleagues (1999), who give the formation none; and the Coniacian–Campanian rests on Page and colleagues' synthesis chapter, which relays the Senonian and Turonian–Coniacian readings and infers the Campanian of the Puesto Manuel Arce from its passage into the Salamanca Formation above, a bound. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian, Cenomanian, Turonian]` and the notes on musacchio1975a, page1999a and canale2014a on [Cerro Barcino](../../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-barcino.yml); its genera, Aptian to Cenomanian, are unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- musacchio1975a, p. 70: "The microfossils of Paso de Indios studied suggest a Lower Cretaceous age (near to or just Aptian)."
- musacchio1975a, p. 76: "EDAD: Cretácico Inferior (Aptiano?)." ["AGE: Lower Cretaceous (Aptian?).", translated].
- suárez2014a, p. 72: "15 gave a Cenomanian age of 97.4 ± 0.8 Ma (MSWD = 0.92)".
- canale2014a, p. 3: the formation's "age has been regarded as Hauterivian to Senonian (Campanian?) in age (Page et al. 1999)".
- page1999a, p. 473: "el Miembro Puesto Manuel Arce alcanzaría una edad campaniana-maastrichtiana inferior ya que pasa transicional y concordantemente a la Formación Salamanca" ["the Puesto Manuel Arce Member would reach a Campanian–early Maastrichtian age since it passes transitionally and concordantly into the Salamanca Formation", our translation; the corpus translation places "inferior" on the Campanian].

**Ruled out.**
- *Hauterivian.* A misrelay of Page and colleagues (1999).
- *Barremian.* A relayed possibility.
- *Coniacian–Campanian.* A synthesis chapter's relays and an inference from the overlying unit.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What ages does Nullo (1983) give the Cerro Barcino's members?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Only relayed, hedged ones. Nullo (1983) reports Codignotto and colleagues' (1978) "possibly Barremian" age for the lower Puesto La Paloma Member or the upper Bardas Coloradas Member below it, and places the Cerro Castaño Member in the Lower Cretaceous "with the possibility of being Aptian" on material that Musacchio and Chebli (1975) studied. Neither is a reading of his own, and a possibility adds no stage. Governs the note on nullo1983a on [Cerro Barcino](../../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-barcino.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- nullo1983a, p. 59: "Codignotto et al. (1978), analyzing together the paleontological data presented in Chebli et al. (1976), assign a possibly Barremian age to the lower portions of the Puesto La Paloma Member or the upper portions of the Bardas Coloradas Member" (translated).
- nullo1983a, p. 59: the levels of the Cerro Castaño Member "are placed in the Lower Cretaceous, with the possibility of being Aptian" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Barremian or Aptian member ages from Nullo.* Both are relayed possibilities.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What does Nullo (1983) record of the Cerro Barcino's members on the Pampa de Agnia sheet?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Only two members of the formation crop out on the sheet, the Puesto La Paloma and above it the Cerro Castaño, which lies on the Puesto La Paloma in lithological continuity, so that the boundary is in places hard to draw. The Puesto La Paloma is mainly rhyodacitic tuffs, sandy tuffs of the same composition and rhyolitic tuffs, light green to grayish green; in the cañadón La Primavera section, the thickest measured on the sheet, it is 40.5 m thick. The Cerro Castaño is tuffites, tuffaceous sandstones, claystones and a few tuff beds; the 54 m measured in the same section is partial. The Cerro Castaño is overlain in some localities by the Bajo El Pajarito Formation and, where that unit is absent, by Tertiary basalts. Governs the `nullo1983a` note on [Cerro Barcino](../../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-barcino.yml), and `parent: Cerro Barcino` and `lithology` on [Cerro Castaño](../../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-castaño.yml).

**Evidence.**
- nullo1983a, p. 56: the highest units cropping out "belong to the Cerro Barcino Formation, of which only two Members crop out, the Puesto La Paloma and the Cerro Castaño" (translated).
- nullo1983a, p. 58: the Puesto La Paloma's "predominant lithology consists of rhyodacitic tuffs, sandy tuffs of similar composition and rhyolitic tuffs", "light green to grayish green"; in the cañadón La Primavera, "Total thickness 40.50 m", "the thickest profile measured in the area" (translated).
- nullo1983a, p. 58: the Cerro Castaño "consists of tuffites, tuffaceous sandstones, claystones and, in smaller amounts, some tuff levels"; "They lie above the preceding Member in lithological continuity, which in some cases makes it difficult to mark the separation correctly" (translated).
- nullo1983a, p. 59: "Its upper contact is marked in some localities by the Bajo El Pajarito Formation, while in other sectors, given the absence of this unit, the top is formed by the Tertiary basalts"; "Total thickness 54 m"; "The thickness of this Member is partial" (translated).

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

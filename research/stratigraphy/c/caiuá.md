# Caiuá

## What do the cited works say about the makeup of the Caiuá Group and the source of its Aptian–Albian?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Among the works cited, the Aptian–Albian has two original statements: Arai and Dias-Brito's (2023), and the earlier suggestion of Fúlfaro and colleagues (1999) and Dias-Brito and colleagues (2001), which Batezelli (2015) relays "Based on stratigraphic relationship with Bauru Group, facies analysis and evidences of climatic variations". Dias and colleagues (2020) relay Batezelli's two-sequence scheme, in which the Caiuá Group is the Early Cretaceous Sequence 1 below Unconformity K-1, so their Early Cretaceous counts with Batezelli and adds no reading. Fernandes and Coimbra (2000) credit the division of the suprabasaltic Upper Cretaceous sequence into the Bauru and Caiuá groups to Fernandes and Coimbra (1994), adopt the three-formation subdivision of the Caiuá from Fernandes and Coimbra (1996), and state no stage for the group. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian, Cenomanian]` on [Caiuá](../../../stratigraphy/c/caiuá.yml), unchanged. It adds to the 2026-10-09 entry and supersedes none.

**Evidence.**
- fernandes2000a, p. 717: "The suprabasaltic Upper Cretaceous sequence is formed by the Bauru and Caiuá groups (Fernandes & Coimbra 1994)"; "For the Caiuá Group, the subdivision into the Santo Anastácio, Rio Paraná and Goio Erê formations of Fernandes & Coimbra (1996) was adopted" (translated).
- fernandes2000a, p. 720: the Santo Anastácio Formation's depositional setting is sand sheets on desert plains "marginal to the great dune complexes of the sand sea (Caiuá Desert)" (translated).
- batezelli2015a, p. 4, Chronostratigraphy: "The fossil content of the Caiuá Group is composed by pterosaurs bone fragments occurring in three horizons of reddish sandstone of the Goio Erê Formation"; "Preliminary analysis suggests these pterosaurs fossils belong to the Tapejaridae group"; "Based on stratigraphic relationship with Bauru Group, facies analysis and evidences of climatic variations, some authors also suggests Aptian-Albian ages to the Caiuá Group (e.g. Fulfaro et al., 1999; Dias-Brito et al., 2001)".
- dias2020a, p. 2, 2. Geologic context of the Bauru Basin in Triângulo Mineiro: Batezelli divides the basin into "2 s-order [sic] sequences, an Early Cretaceous Sequence 1 and a Late Cretaceous Sequence 2, separated by a regional unconformity (Unconformity K-1). Sequence 1 is represented by eolian and fluvial deposits of the Caiuá Group units, whereas Sequence 2 is represented by lacustrine, fluvial, and alluvial deposits of the Araçatuba, Adamantina, Uberaba, and Marília formations".

**Ruled out.**
Nothing beyond the readings the 2026-10-09 entry rules out.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What Late Cretaceous stage does the Caiuá take?

*2026-10-09*

**Conclusion.** The Cenomanian, from Menegazzo and colleagues (2016), added under the union rule to the Aptian–Albian of Arai and Dias-Brito (2023). Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian, Cenomanian]` and the Late Cretaceous in `period` on [Caiuá](../../../stratigraphy/c/caiuá.yml).

**Evidence.**
- menegazzo2016a, p. 138: the fauna "is suggestive of Cenomanian age for the Caiua Formation", and, without the hedge, "As demonstrated by the paleontological data, the sedimentation of the Bauru Basin began in the Cenomanian with the deposition of the Caiuá and Pirapozinho formations".
- menegazzo2016a, p. 146: sedimentation "began in the Cenomanian with the Caiuá and Pirapozinho formations".
- fernandes2000a, Abstract: "The Upper Cretaceous sequence is now divided in two groups, partially contemporaneous: Caiuá Group … and Bauru Group", an epoch without a stage.

**Ruled out.**
- *Turonian.* Menegazzo and colleagues' "Cenomanian-Turonian stratigraphic interval" (Fig. 4C) is shared with the Pirapozinho Formation, whose age they give as "Cenomanian–early Turonian?"; nothing in the paper dates the Caiuá to the Turonian.
- *Coniacian–Maastrichtian.* Fernandes and Coimbra (2000) give it for the Bauru Basin as a whole, not for the group.
- *Coniacian–Campanian.* Langer and colleagues (2019) write that "a younger (Late Cretaceous) age can be suggested" from inferred synchronicity with the Adamantina Formation; a suggested correlation is not a reading.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Clarens

## Is the Bushveld Sandstone the Clarens Formation?

*2026-10-14*

**Conclusion.** Yes; the Bushveld Sandstone is an older name for the Clarens, and the registry carries it as a variant. Bordy and Head (2018), the committee lithostratigraphy of the Clarens, list it among the formation's former names in the northern basins. That settles the correlation question of the entry moved below from the retired Bushveld Sandstone note: Smith and colleagues' (1993) Clarens reading holds, and Van Heerden's (1979) Elliot correlation does not. Haughton (1924) used "Bushveld Series" for the whole Transvaal Stormberg, a wider scope than the sandstone name Bordy and Head record. Supersedes the Open line of the entry below. Governs `variants: [Cave Sandstone, Bushveld Sandstone]` on [Clarens](../../../stratigraphy/c/clarens.yml), and the retirement of the separate Bushveld Sandstone entry.

**Evidence.**
- bordy2018a: the Clarens "has also been referred … as the Bushveld Sandstone in the Lebombo, Springbok Flats, Tshipise, Tuli and Ellisras/Lephalale Basins (e.g., Kynaston, 1906a, b, c; 1907; du Toit, 1926; Truter, 1945; van Eeden et al., 1955)".
- bordy2018a: "Its inferred Sinemurian to Pliensbachian (Early Jurassic) age is derived from the vertebrate biostratigraphy of the conformably underlying Elliot Formation and isotope geochronology of the conformably overlying Drakensberg and Lebombo Groups".
- smith1993a, p. 160: "equivalents to the Clarens sandstone are present in … northern Transvaal (Bushveld sandstone)".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Elliot correlation.* Van Heerden (1979) correlates by age; the formation's defining paper names the Bushveld Sandstone as the Clarens.
- *A separate entry.* It would be a second entry for one unit.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which correlations bear on the age of the Bushveld Sandstone?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** Two published correlations, to the Elliot and to the Clarens, which lie one above the other, so both cannot hold. Neither states a stage, so the unit has none. Governs the `dispute` and the absent `stages` on [Clarens](../../../stratigraphy/c/clarens.yml), then the separate Bushveld Sandstone entry.

**Evidence.**
- vanheerden1979a, p. 68: "the Bushveld Sandstone is more or less the same age as the Elliot Formation farther south".
- smith1993a, p. 160: "Biostratigraphic and lithofacies equivalents to the Clarens sandstone are present in … northern Transvaal (Bushveld sandstone) …, and a general Late Triassic to Early Jurassic age is inferred for these deposits as a whole."
- haughton1924a, p. 450: "The Stormberg Series in the Transvaal is preserved as a number of outliers, forming the Bushveld Series, of which the most important occur on the Springbok Flats".

**Ruled out.**
- *Bordy and Eriksson (2015) as a third correlation.* The paper does not name the Bushveld Sandstone. It reassigns "the upper part of the Irrigasie Formation (Worthing Member – Springbok Flats Basin)" to the Elliot Formation (bordy2015a, p. 315). Reaching the Bushveld from there needs Haughton's Springbok Flats outliers and an assumption that they are the Irrigasie's upper part, which neither paper states.
- *The ages in Galton and Upchurch's Table 12.1.* The Bushveld Sandstone appears in the rows for *Euskelosaurus browni*, "late Carnian or early Norian" across four units, and *Massospondylus carinatus*, "Hettangian-Pliensbachian" across the units of three countries (galton2004a, Table 12.1). Each is the taxon's range, not a dating of this unit ([methods](../../methods.md), on counting an edited book and its chapters).
- *The Elliot's or the Clarens's stages.* The age of a correlative does not date a unit ([CONTRIBUTING](../../../CONTRIBUTING.md#age-period-and-stages)).
- *Late Triassic from White (1973).* A catalogue entry, "Upper Triassic, Stormberg Series, Bushveld Sandstone", under *Gigantoscelus*; the methods rule on reference works uses this unit as its case.

**Open.** Which correlation holds, and whether either can be put at stage resolution. Smith and colleagues' Late Triassic to Early Jurassic is stated for the Clarens equivalents "as a whole", so it is a range for several units rather than a dating of this one.

## What is the age of the Clarens Formation?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Mainly Pliensbachian, reaching from the late Sinemurian to the early Toarcian, where its top interfingers with the Drakensberg lavas. Governs `stages: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on Clarens, and `stage: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian]` on lower Clarens taxa such as [*Heterodontosaurus tucki*](../../../genera/H/Heterodontosaurus.yml) and [*Geranosaurus atavus*](../../../genera/G/Geranosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bordy2020a, p. 1: "A mainly Pliensbachian age is obtained for the Clarens Formation"; p. 13: the upper Stormberg records "a sedimentation record from the middle Norian to early Toarcian". The upper Clarens maximum age is 187.5 ± 1.6 Ma (p. 10, Table 1).
- sciscio2017a, accepted manuscript, p. 7: "the intercalation of the uppermost Clarens Formation with the lowermost lava flows", whose ages "fall into the range 183±1.0 Ma (Jourdan et al., 2007)".
- hesselbo2020a, p. 1001: the base of the Toarcian is 184.2 Ma on GTS 2020, against "182.7 Ma (revised as 183.7 Ma in GTS2016)" on GTS 2012, so lavas at 183 Ma are Toarcian.
- sereno2012a, p. 11: "*Heterodontosaurus* and the fragmentary *Geranosaurus* were collected in lower levels of the overlying … Clarens Formation"; the referred skull SAM-PK-K1332 is "in the Upper Elliot Formation" (p. 16).

**Ruled out.**
- *Rhaetian, Upper Keuper.* Haughton (1924) concluded "this would denote a Rhaetic age for the Cave Sandstone" (haughton1924a, p. 491), and Crompton and Charig (1962) "probably of Upper Keuper age" (crompton1962a, p. 1075); both predate the radiometric work.
- *81.6 Ma for the lavas.* Jourdan and colleagues print "81.6 ± 0.7 Ma" on one page (jourdan2008a, p. 16) and "181.6 ± 0.7 Ma" elsewhere in the same paper; the 81.6 is the paper's own slip.

**Open.** The *Heterodontosaurus* holotype's exact level: Sereno's sentence concerns the genus, and Crompton and Charig place the holotype "In the transitional beds of the Cave Sandstone, below the basalt" (p. 1075). Olsen and Galton's "Early Jurassic (Hettangian-Pliensbachian)" (olsen1984a, p. 87) covers the whole upper Stormberg assemblage. Jourdan and colleagues (2007), the source of the 183 Ma lava ages, have not been read.

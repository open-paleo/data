# Clarens

## Which of the Clarens's stages rest on a reading?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** The Pliensbachian only; the Sinemurian and Toarcian are dropped. Supersedes the 2026-09-30 and 2026-11-23 entries on the stages. Bordy and colleagues (2020), from their own detrital-zircon dates combined with the litho-, magneto- and biostratigraphy, conclude that the Clarens depositional episode "was mostly Pliensbachian", a reading in their own voice like the [Winton](../w/winton.md)'s. The Sinemurian has no reading: Sereno (2012) gives "ca. 195 Ma, Sinemurian" after Knoll (2005), not read; Norman and colleagues (2011) cite Jourdan and colleagues' lava ages for "latest Sinemurian-Pliensbachian", and a lava age above the formation is a limit; Bordy and colleagues' own earlier "inferred to be Sinemurian–Pliensbachian", from the fauna it shares with the upper Elliot, gives way to their synthesis; and Bordy and colleagues (2018) infer the same range from the Elliot's vertebrates below and the lavas above. The Toarcian rests on Sciscio and colleagues' (2017) argument that the top of the formation, interbedded with the lowest lavas, is as old as they are, at 183 ± 1 Ma after Jourdan and colleagues (2007), not read; Bordy and colleagues' early Toarcian closes the upper Stormberg as a whole and takes its end from relayed lava ages. [*Heterodontosaurus tucki*](../../../genera/H/Heterodontosaurus.yml), [*Geranosaurus atavus*](../../../genera/G/Geranosaurus.yml) and [*Gigantoscelus molengraaffi*](../../../genera/G/Gigantoscelus.yml), with no stage reading of their own, take the formation's Pliensbachian. Governs `stages: [Pliensbachian]` on [Clarens](../../../stratigraphy/c/clarens.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bordy2020a, p. 4: "this synthesis allows us to infer that the duration of the Elliot depositional episode was middle Norian–Sinemurian, whereas the Clarens depositional episode was mostly Pliensbachian".
- sereno2012a, p. 11: the Clarens Formation, "the age of which is regarded as Early Jurassic (ca. 195 Ma, Sinemurian) (Knoll 2005; Gradstein and Ogg 2009)".
- sciscio2017a, accepted manuscript, p. 7: "it is conservative to assume that at least the topmost section of the Clarens Formation is contemporaneous with the lowermost KLIP age".

**Ruled out.**
- *Sinemurian.* Relayed from an unread work, or inferred from the age of the unit below and the lavas above.
- *Toarcian, for now.* Sciscio and colleagues' argument rests on a lava age that has not been read at its source.

**Open.** Jourdan and colleagues (2007) and Knoll (2005) have not been read; the first would decide the Toarcian.

## Do Norman and colleagues (2011) misrelay Olsen and Galton (1984)?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Against the text, yes; against the figure, no. Olsen and Galton (1984) date the upper Stormberg assemblage, the upper Elliot and Clarens together, "Early Jurassic (Hettangian-Pliensbachian)", and their text gives no Sinemurian range for the Stormberg. Norman and colleagues (2011) cite them for "Hettangian-Sinemurian", which matches Olsen and Galton's Fig. 2, where the upper Elliot sits level with the Hettangian and the Clarens in the lower Sinemurian; Norman and colleagues do not say which they used. Olsen and Galton's range is for the two formations together and does not date the Clarens alone. Governs nothing in `stages` on [Clarens](../../../stratigraphy/c/clarens.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- olsen1984a, p. 87: "the upper Stormberg assemblage is Early Jurassic (Hettangian-Pliensbachian) in age".
- norman2011a, p. 189: "Clarens Formation (formerly 'Cave Sandstone', Stormberg Series): Hettangian-Sinemurian (Olsen & Galton, 1984)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Hettangian–Sinemurian as Olsen and Galton's stated range.* Their text says Hettangian–Pliensbachian.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Do the upper Elliot dates and the Lesotho lavas narrow the Clarens's Sinemurian–Toarcian range?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** No; both ends of the 2026-09-30 range stand. Bordy and colleagues (2020) date the Clarens younger than Sereno (2012) does, mainly Pliensbachian, and close the upper Stormberg record in the early Toarcian; since the Clarens is the youngest unit of that record and lies beneath the Toarcian basalts, the early Toarcian end can only be its top. The Sinemurian is the weaker end. Their sample Q6, from the uppermost upper Elliot, has a maximum depositional age of 191.9 ± 1.5 Ma, which is Sinemurian on the GTS2012 boundary of 190.8 Ma but Pliensbachian on the GTS2020 boundary of 192.9 Ma, the one the current ICS chart (v2024/12) carries; their Fig. 2 is drawn on the ICS chart v2018/08. So the Sinemurian in the earlier inference that the Clarens is "Sinemurian–Pliensbachian" was framed on stage labels from before 2020. It is kept because Q6 is a single sample, which the paper itself finds too few to test the upper Elliot's correlation; its uncertainty reaches 193.4 Ma, inside the Sinemurian (this arithmetic is ours); and the paper expects lithostratigraphic boundaries to be diachronous across the basin, so the base of the Clarens need not be younger everywhere than the top of the upper Elliot at the Q6 site. The Lesotho lava pile above the formation, which Jourdan and colleagues (2008) give as 181.6 ± 0.7 Ma, falls inside the Toarcian (184.2 to 174.7 Ma on GTS2020), so it bounds the Clarens from above without excluding a Toarcian top. Olsen and Galton's (1984) "Early Jurassic (Hettangian-Pliensbachian)" is the age of the upper Elliot and Clarens assemblage taken together, which they correlate with the upper Newark Supergroup, the Glen Canyon and the lower Lufeng Series, so it does not put the Clarens in the Hettangian. Of the three Karoo papers by Jourdan and colleagues cited (2005, 2007, 2008), only the 2008 paper names the Clarens. Governs `stages: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on [Clarens](../../../stratigraphy/c/clarens.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bordy2020a, p. 1, Abstract: "a Hettangian–Sinemurian age for the upper Elliot Formation".
- bordy2020a, p. 4, Introduction: the Clarens "is inferred to be Sinemurian–Pliensbachian. The age of the upper boundary of the Stormberg Group succession is provided by the conformably overlying, Toarcian-aged continental flood basalts".
- bordy2020a, p. 4, Fig. 2: "Geological time scale based on the International Chronostratigraphic Chart (v2018/08; ICS, 2018)"; "most lithostratigraphic boundaries are likely to be diachronous, i.e., they are timetransgressive laterally across the basin".
- bordy2020a, p. 10, Table 1: sample Q6, "tsa um uEF", 191.9 ± 1.5 Ma.
- bordy2020a, p. 17, 5.3: the new maximum depositional ages do not test the upper Elliot correlation "because of the limited number of productive detrital zircon-bearing samples in the uEF (e.g., Q6: <191.9 Ma".
- hesselbo2020a, p. 1004, Table 26.4: base of the Pliensbachian 190.8 Ma in GTS2012 and 192.9 Ma in GTS2020; base of the Toarcian 184.2 Ma and of the Aalenian 174.7 Ma in GTS2020.
- olsen1984a, p. 87, Abstract: "the upper Stormberg assemblage is Early Jurassic (Hettangian-Pliensbachian) in age"; "the upper Stormberg (upper Elliot and Clarens formations) assemblage broadly correlates with the upper Newark Supergroup of eastern North America, the Glen Canyon of the southwestern United States, and the lower Lufeng Series of China".
- jourdan2008a, p. 2, Introduction: "the 1.9 km-thick Lesotho lava pile (181.6 ± 0.7 Ma)"; p. 5, 2.1: "dated at 181.6 ± 0.7 Ma".
- jourdan2008a, p. 16, 5.2.3: some intrusive events "occurred early, before the Lesotho lava-pile emplacement", "as attested by pipe-related deposits in the Clarens formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *No Sinemurian, because the upper Elliot below already reaches the Pliensbachian.* It rests on one sample whose uncertainty reaches into the Sinemurian, and on a boundary that Bordy and colleagues expect to be diachronous.
- *Hettangian.* Olsen and Galton's Hettangian–Pliensbachian covers the upper Elliot and the Clarens together, and the upper Elliot lies below the Clarens.
- *No Toarcian, the lavas closing the formation earlier.* The 181.6 Ma lava pile is Toarcian on the current chart.

**Open.** A second dated sample from the top of the upper Elliot, or one from the base of the Clarens, would settle whether the Clarens reaches the Sinemurian.

## Is the Bushveld Sandstone the Clarens Formation?

*2026-10-14*

**Conclusion.** Yes; the Bushveld Sandstone is an older name for the Clarens, and the registry carries it as a variant. Bordy and Head (2018), the committee lithostratigraphy of the Clarens, list it among the formation's former names in the northern basins. That settles the correlation question of the entry moved below from the retired Bushveld Sandstone note: Smith and colleagues' (1993) Clarens reading holds, and Van Heerden's (1979) Elliot correlation does not. Haughton (1924) used "Bushveld Series" for the whole Transvaal Stormberg, a wider scope than the sandstone name Bordy and Head record. Supersedes the Open line of the entry below. Governs `variants: [Cave Sandstone, Bushveld Sandstone]` on [Clarens](../../../stratigraphy/c/clarens.yml), and the retirement of the separate Bushveld Sandstone entry.

**Evidence.**
- bordy2018a: the Clarens "has also been referred … as the Bushveld Sandstone in the Lebombo, Springbok Flats, Tshipise, Tuli and Ellisras/Lephalale Basins (e.g., Kynaston, 1906a, b, c; 1907; du Toit, 1926; Truter, 1945; van Eeden et al., 1955)".
- bordy2018a: "Its inferred Sinemurian to Pliensbachian (Early Jurassic) age is derived from the vertebrate biostratigraphy of the conformably underlying Elliot Formation and isotope geochronology of the conformably overlying Drakensberg and Lebombo Groups".
- smith1993a, p. 160: "equivalents to the Clarens sandstone are present in … northern Transvaal (Bushveld sandstone)".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Elliot correlation.* Van Heerden (1979) correlates by age; the formation's defining paper names the Bushveld Sandstone as the Clarens.
- *A separate entry.* It would be a second entry for one unit.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which correlations bear on the age of the Bushveld Sandstone?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** Two published correlations, to the Elliot and to the Clarens, which lie one above the other, so both cannot hold. Neither states a stage, so the unit has none. Governs the `dispute` and the absent `stages` on [Clarens](../../../stratigraphy/c/clarens.yml), then the separate Bushveld Sandstone entry.

**Evidence.**
- vanheerden1979a, p. 68: "the Bushveld Sandstone is more or less the same age as the Elliot Formation farther south".
- smith1993a, p. 160: "Biostratigraphic and lithofacies equivalents to the Clarens sandstone are present in … northern Transvaal (Bushveld sandstone) …, and a general Late Triassic to Early Jurassic age is inferred for these deposits as a whole."
- haughton1924a, p. 450: "The Stormberg Series in the Transvaal is preserved as a number of outliers, forming the Bushveld Series, of which the most important occur on the Springbok Flats".

**Ruled out.**
- *Bordy and Eriksson (2015) as a third correlation.* The paper does not name the Bushveld Sandstone. It reassigns "the upper part of the Irrigasie Formation (Worthing Member – Springbok Flats Basin)" to the Elliot Formation (bordy2015a, p. 315). Reaching the Bushveld from there needs Haughton's Springbok Flats outliers and an assumption that they are the Irrigasie's upper part, which neither paper states.
- *The ages in Galton and Upchurch's Table 12.1.* The Bushveld Sandstone appears in the rows for *Euskelosaurus browni*, "late Carnian or early Norian" across four units, and *Massospondylus carinatus*, "Hettangian-Pliensbachian" across the units of three countries (galton2004a, Table 12.1). Each is the taxon's range, not a dating of this unit ([methods](../../methods.md), on counting an edited book and its chapters).
- *The Elliot's or the Clarens's stages.* The age of a correlative does not date a unit ([CONTRIBUTING](../../../CONTRIBUTING.md#age-period-and-stages)).
- *Late Triassic from White (1973).* A catalogue entry, "Upper Triassic, Stormberg Series, Bushveld Sandstone", under *Gigantoscelus*; the methods rule on reference works uses this unit as its case.

**Open.** Which correlation holds, and whether either can be put at stage resolution. Smith and colleagues' Late Triassic to Early Jurassic is stated for the Clarens equivalents "as a whole", so it is a range for several units rather than a dating of this one.

## What is the age of the Clarens Formation?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Mainly Pliensbachian, reaching from the late Sinemurian to the early Toarcian, where its top interfingers with the Drakensberg lavas. Governs `stages: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on Clarens, and `stage: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian]` on lower Clarens taxa such as [*Heterodontosaurus tucki*](../../../genera/H/Heterodontosaurus.yml) and [*Geranosaurus atavus*](../../../genera/G/Geranosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bordy2020a, p. 1: "A mainly Pliensbachian age is obtained for the Clarens Formation"; p. 13: the upper Stormberg records "a sedimentation record from the middle Norian to early Toarcian". The upper Clarens maximum age is 187.5 ± 1.6 Ma (p. 10, Table 1).
- sciscio2017a, accepted manuscript, p. 7: "the intercalation of the uppermost Clarens Formation with the lowermost lava flows", whose ages "fall into the range 183±1.0 Ma (Jourdan et al., 2007)".
- hesselbo2020a, p. 1001: the base of the Toarcian is 184.2 Ma on GTS 2020, against "182.7 Ma (revised as 183.7 Ma in GTS2016)" on GTS 2012, so lavas at 183 Ma are Toarcian.
- sereno2012a, p. 11: "*Heterodontosaurus* and the fragmentary *Geranosaurus* were collected in lower levels of the overlying … Clarens Formation"; the referred skull SAM-PK-K1332 is "in the Upper Elliot Formation" (p. 16).

**Ruled out.**
- *Rhaetian, Upper Keuper.* Haughton (1924) concluded "this would denote a Rhaetic age for the Cave Sandstone" (haughton1924a, p. 491), and Crompton and Charig (1962) "probably of Upper Keuper age" (crompton1962a, p. 1075); both predate the radiometric work.
- *81.6 Ma for the lavas.* Jourdan and colleagues print "81.6 ± 0.7 Ma" on one page (jourdan2008a, p. 16) and "181.6 ± 0.7 Ma" elsewhere in the same paper; the 81.6 is the paper's own slip.

**Open.** The *Heterodontosaurus* holotype's exact level: Sereno's sentence concerns the genus, and Crompton and Charig place the holotype "In the transitional beds of the Cave Sandstone, below the basalt" (p. 1075). Olsen and Galton's "Early Jurassic (Hettangian-Pliensbachian)" (olsen1984a, p. 87) covers the whole upper Stormberg assemblage. Jourdan and colleagues (2007), the source of the 183 Ma lava ages, have not been read.

# Clarens

## What is the age of the Clarens Formation?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Mainly Pliensbachian, reaching from the late Sinemurian to the early Toarcian, where its top interfingers with the Drakensberg lavas. Governs `stages: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on Clarens, and `stage: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian]` on lower Clarens taxa such as [*Heterodontosaurus tucki*](../../../genera/H/Heterodontosaurus.yml) and [*Geranosaurus atavus*](../../../genera/G/Geranosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bordy2020a, p. 1: "A mainly Pliensbachian age is obtained for the Clarens Formation"; p. 13: the upper Stormberg records "a sedimentation record from the middle Norian to early Toarcian". The upper Clarens maximum age is 187.5 ± 1.6 Ma (p. 10, Table 1).
- sciscio2017a, accepted manuscript, p. 7: "the intercalation of the uppermost Clarens Formation with the lowermost lava flows", whose ages "fall into the range 183±1.0 Ma (Jourdan et al., 2007)".
- hesselbo2020a, p. 1001: the base of the Toarcian is 184.2 Ma on GTS 2020, against "182.7 Ma (revised as 183.7 Ma in GTS2016)" on GTS 2012, so lavas at 183 Ma are Toarcian.
- sereno2012a, p. 11: "*Heterodontosaurus* and the fragmentary *Geranosaurus* were collected in lower levels of the overlying … Clarens Formation"; the referred skull SAM-PK-K1332 is "in the Upper Elliot Formation" (p. 16).

**Ruled out.**
- *Rhaetian, Upper Keuper.* Haughton (1924) concluded "this would denote a Rhaetic age for the Cave Sandstone" (haughton1924a, p. 491), and Crompton and Charig (1962) "probably of Upper Keuper age" (crompton1962a, p. 1075); both predate the radiometric work.
- *81.6 Ma for the lavas.* Jourdan and colleagues print "81.6 ± 0.7 Ma" on one page (jourdan2008a, p. 16) and "181.6 ± 0.7 Ma" elsewhere in the same paper; the 81.6 is the paper's own slip.

**Open.** The *Heterodontosaurus* holotype's exact level: Sereno's sentence concerns the genus, and Crompton and Charig place the holotype "In the transitional beds of the Cave Sandstone, below the basalt" (p. 1075). Olsen and Galton's "Early Jurassic (Hettangian-Pliensbachian)" (olsen1984a, p. 87) covers the whole upper Stormberg assemblage. Jourdan and colleagues (2007), the source of the 183 Ma lava ages, have not been read.

# Cañón del Colorado

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Cañón del Colorado?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Early Jurassic, Hettangian–Toarcian, from Martínez and Colombi (2011), who give the formation the biochron of *Massospondylus*, the sister taxon of the *Adeopapposaurus* from its middle member. The Late Triassic that Casamiquela and Miolano (1983) and Milana and colleagues (1995) proposed rests on the referral of the fossils to prosauropods and on regional geology, and Martínez and Colombi report it as the earlier reading they replace. Governs `stages: [Hettangian, Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` and `period: [Early Jurassic]` on [Cañón del Colorado](../../../stratigraphy/c/cañón-del-colorado.yml).

**Evidence.**
- martínez2011b, p. 96, Abstract: "using biostratigraphic correlation of the paleovertebrates located in middle member, a Lower Jurassic (Hettangian-Toarcian) age is suggested for the Cañón del Colorado Formation".
- martínez2011b, p. 106, Edad de la Formación Cañón del Colorado: the occurrence of *Massospondylus* "is associated with strata of age ranging between the Hettangian and the Toarcian (Lower Jurassic)"; "Therefore, this age is assigned to the Cañón del Colorado Formation, which bears paleovertebrates closely related to *Massospondylus*" (translated).
- martínez2011b, p. 106, Conclusiones: the formation "is assigned an Early Jurassic age (Hettangian-Toarcian) since this is the biochron of *Massospondylus*, the phylogenetically sister taxon of *Adeopapposaurus*" (translated).
- martínez2011b, p. 97: "Based on the discovery of vertebral remains assigned to a prosauropod dinosaur, Casamiquela and Miolano (1983) assigned it an Upper Triassic age"; "On the basis of the aforementioned and other new finds of prosauropod fossils and considering the numerous Triassic outcrops in the Cuyana region, Milana et al. (1995) assigned this formation to the Upper Triassic" (translated).
- martínez2011b, p. 97: Milana "recognized the low-angle unconformity that separates the Cañón del Colorado Formation from the Río Salado Formation, of Neogene age" (translated).
- milana1995a, p. 44A: a locality "in the Sierra de Mogna mountains 60 km North of the city of San Juan" has yielded two specimens "of a new prosauropod of the plateosaurid type" from the formation, "which has been assigned a Tertiary ages [sic] but lies above the Patquia Formation of Late Permian age".
- milana1995a, p. 45A: "Age determination is based on the known stratigraphic range of plateosaurids; however, the authors are inclined to a Late Triassic age assignment based on regional geologic considerations".

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Triassic, from Casamiquela and Miolano (1983) and Milana and colleagues (1995).* Both rest on referring the fossils to prosauropods, which Milana and colleagues compare by "the known stratigraphic range of plateosaurids", and the Late Triassic they settle on comes from "regional geologic considerations" rather than from the fauna. Martínez and Colombi (2011) report both as earlier assignments and date the formation from *Adeopapposaurus* and its sister taxon *Massospondylus* instead, so the Late Triassic is a superseded epoch and stays out of `period`.
- *Upper Triassic to Lower Jurassic, from the title of Milana and colleagues (1995).* A paper's title does not date a unit, and the abstract's own text leans to the Late Triassic.
- *Tertiary.* Milana and colleagues (1995) report it as an earlier assignment while noting that the formation lies above the Late Permian Patquia Formation, and Martínez and Colombi (2011) report the unconformity that separates it from the overlying Neogene Río Salado Formation.

**Open.** The Hettangian–Toarcian is a faunal correlation through the biochron of a sister taxon; none of the works cited gives a radiometric age for the formation. A numeric age from the formation would test it.

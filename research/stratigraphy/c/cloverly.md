# Cloverly

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Cloverly?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Valanginian to Cenomanian, from D'Emic and colleagues (2019). They give stage ages member by member: Valanginian–Hauterivian for the fluvial deposits at the base, Barremian–early Albian for the flora of the Little Sheep Mudstone Member, and mid–late Albian to early Cenomanian for the Himes Member and Greybull Sandstone. Their Cenomanian is stated in the abstract as part of the formation's span; the discussion adds only that the uppermost beds may reach the earliest Cenomanian. Darton (1906), who named the formation, found no fossils bearing on its age and placed it across the Lower–Upper Cretaceous transition by correlation with the Lakota sandstone, Fuson formation and Dakota sandstone of the Black Hills. That correlation names no stage and adds nothing to the dated span. Governs `stages` and `period` on [Cloverly](../../../stratigraphy/c/cloverly.yml).

**Evidence.**
- d'emic2019a, p. 3: deposition "spanned the Valanginian–Cenomanian stages (ca. 140 Ma–98 Ma), a longer time interval than the commonly cited Aptian–Albian depositional timeframe"; "In the mid–late Albian to early Cenomanian (ca. 109–98 Ma), sediment sourced from the east was deposited as the Himes Member and Greybull Sandstone".
- d'emic2019a, p. 30: "the shift from fluvially dominated deposition during the Valanginian–Hauterivian to lacustrine-dominated deposition in the Aptian".
- d'emic2019a, p. 32: "The flora of the Little Sheep Mudstone Member near Ten Sleep, Wyoming, dates to the Barremian–early Albian and lacks angiosperms".
- d'emic2019a, p. 29: the Greybull Sandstone, Sykes Mountain Formation and Thermopolis Shale were deposited between ca. 103.6 Ma and the early Cenomanian, which "opens up the possibility that the uppermost Cloverly Formation and Greybull Sandstone were deposited in the earliest Cenomanian".
- darton1906a, p. 50: exposures are most extensive "in the vicinity of Cloverly, the locality from which the formation was given its name".
- darton1906a, p. 53: the formation "is believed to represent the Lakota sandstone, Fuson formation, and Dakota sandstone of the Black Hills region"; "So far the formation has not yielded any fossils which throw light on its age, for only a few fragments of leaves and pine needles have been observed. On the basis of the above correlation it represents the later deposits of the lower Cretaceous and the earliest deposits of the upper Cretaceous."

**Ruled out.**
- *Darton's correlation as a dating.* Darton (1906) states that no fossil bearing on the age had been found, so the Lower to Upper Cretaceous placement rests on correlation with Black Hills units alone. It names no stage and cannot add or remove one.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does the Cloverly Formation begin in the Berriasian?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** No. D'Emic and colleagues name the Valanginian as the formation's lowest stage; their "ca. 140 Ma" is an approximate bound inferred from other work, not a dated sample, so the stage they name governs. Governs the Valanginian start of `stages` on [Cloverly](../../../stratigraphy/c/cloverly.yml).

**Evidence.**
- d'emic2019a, abstract: deposition "spanned the Valanginian–Cenomanian stages (ca. 140 Ma–98 Ma)"; the lowest dated sample, CL-7 at the base of the Little Sheep Mudstone Member about 2.5 m above the Pryor Conglomerate, gives 129.4 ± 3.4 Ma (Table 3).

**Ruled out.**
- *Berriasian from "ca. 140 Ma".* On the current chart 140 Ma is late Berriasian, but the figure rests on the absence of younger zircons in Pryor equivalents, a lull in arc magmatism and Berriasian–Hauterivian palynology from beds below the Pryor, not on a date from the Cloverly; CONTRIBUTING's rule on reading ages on the chart follows the stage a paper names when its number is not a measured date.

**Open.** Nothing.

## How should a Cloverly taxon be dated?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** By member, using D'Emic and colleagues' (2019) member ages, not by the formation-wide range. Governs the stages of Cloverly taxa, such as [*Zephyrosaurus schaffi*](../../../genera/Z/Zephyrosaurus.yml) (provisionally Aptian), and the [Himes](../../../stratigraphy/h/himes.yml) Member's [Albian, Cenomanian].

**Evidence.**
- d'emic2019a, p. 3: "the Pryor Conglomerate, was deposited ca. 140–130 Ma"; the "Little Sheep Mudstone Member was deposited ca. 124–109 Ma"; "(ca. 109–98 Ma), sediment sourced from the east was deposited as the Himes Member and Greybull Sandstone".
- d'emic2019a, pp. 7, 9: the Pryor is Ostrom's Unit IV, the Little Sheep Unit V and the Himes Units VI–VII; p. 32: "Units IV–VII of Ostrom (1970) represent facies and cannot be used as timelines".
- zanno2023a, Fig. 21: *Zephyrosaurus* "(middle of the Little Sheep Member, Cloverly Formation [122], mean age ~116.5 Myr [109])", citing Oreska and colleagues (2013) and D'Emic and colleagues (2019).

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian–Albian for the whole formation.* Farke and colleagues (2014) are not an all-Albian source: they give the *Aquilops* horizon as "?middle–late Albian, between 104 and 109 million years old" (farke2014a, p. 1) and the basal Pryor as "pre-Aptian" (p. 4).

**Open.** *Zephyrosaurus*'s member. Oreska and colleagues (2013) report "cf. ZEPHYROSAURUS sp." teeth from localities in Units V and VI (oreska2013a, pp. 266–267, 278) and never mention *Z. schaffi* or its holotype, so Zanno and colleagues' "middle of the Little Sheep" for the holotype rests on a citation that does not concern it. Sues (1980), the describing paper, would give the holotype's horizon.

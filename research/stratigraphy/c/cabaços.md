# Cabaços

## Are the Vale Verde and Valverde beds other names for the Cabaços?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** Yes; both are added to the variants. Azerêdo and colleagues (2002) say the formation "basically corresponds to the Cabaços Beds and the Vale Verde Beds of earlier workers", and Kullberg and colleagues (2013) give it as "= Valverde Member". The two forms differ by more than spacing, so each is a printed name. Governs `variants` on [Cabaços](../../../stratigraphy/c/cabaços.yml).

## Does any work place the *Alocodon* horizon on either side of the Pedrógão disconformity?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No; held for Barrón and Azerêdo (2003). Answers the 2026-11-25 entry's Open line in part. Thulborn (1973) dates the *Alocodon* marls Upper Callovian on ostracods and gives no formation or section. Azerêdo and colleagues (2002) put Late Callovian ammonite beds below the disconformity at Pedrógão and the Oxfordian Cabaços above it, and Kullberg and colleagues (2013) do the same; neither mentions the teeth in its text. Mateus and Milàn (2009) place the horizon in the "Cabaços Fm., Oxfordian" in a table whose age is credited to Azerêdo and colleagues. A Callovian age measured on the marls matches only the beds below the disconformity, but no work states where the teeth lie. Barrón and Azerêdo's (2003) palynology of the Callovian–Oxfordian succession at Pedrógão, which Kullberg and colleagues list among studies of the Cabaços there, is the work most likely to settle it. Governs `stages` and `period` on [Cabaços](../../../stratigraphy/c/cabaços.yml), unchanged for now.

**Evidence.**
- thulborn1973a, p. 92: "direct comparisons with ostracod faunas elsewhere in Europe indicate an Upper Callovian age for the Pedrógão horizon".
- azerêdo2002a, p. 1350: the surface "represents a major sedimentary break above the 'Brenha' facies yielding ammonites of Late (but not latest) Callovian age, and below the deposits attributed to the Oxfordian".
- azerêdo2002a, p. 1347: "the deposits immediately below this disconformity are lignitic marls and limestones containing marine and reworked non-marine fossils".
- mateus2009b, p. 254, Table 1: "MG (ex-IPFUB P X 1), Cabaços Fm., Oxfordian, Pedrógão", with "Age by Azerêdo et al. 2002".

**Ruled out.**
- *Placing the horizon from Azerêdo and colleagues' Fig. 10.* The column marks reptile teeth both below and above the disconformity, and the text does not connect them to *Alocodon*.

**Open.** Barrón and Azerêdo (2003) has not been read.

## Does the Cabaços reach the Callovian?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Not on the formation's own sources, but the Callovian stays until the *Alocodon* horizon is placed. Azerêdo and colleagues (2002) attribute the formation to the Middle Oxfordian and add only that its lowermost part "could also encompass the Lower Oxfordian or even the uppermost Callovian"; a possibility is not a reading. Kullberg and colleagues (2013) give the Middle Oxfordian Plicatilis Zone, and Schneider and colleagues (2009) start the formation in the "?latest Early/Middle Oxfordian". Removing the Callovian would leave [*Alocodon kuehnei*](../../../genera/A/Alocodon.yml), placed in this formation at Pedrógão, outside its unit: Thulborn (1973) dates the Pedrógão horizon Upper Callovian on ostracods, while Azerêdo and colleagues put Late Callovian ammonites at Pedrógão below the disconformity that the Cabaços overlies. Which unit the *Alocodon* horizon belongs to is filed as a separate finding. Governs `stages: [Callovian, Oxfordian, Kimmeridgian]` and `period` on [Cabaços](../../../stratigraphy/c/cabaços.yml), unchanged for now.

**Evidence.**
- azerêdo2002a, p. 1342: "this alga is virtually absent in the lowermost part of the formation, which therefore could also encompass the Lower Oxfordian or even the uppermost Callovian".
- kullberg2013a, p. 227: the Cabaços Formation, "[Middle Oxfordian, Plicatilis Zone]" (translated).
- schneider2009a, p. 1951: "the Late Jurassic sedimentation started in the ?latest Early/Middle Oxfordian all across the Lusitanian Basin".
- thulborn1973a, p. 94: "All specimens were collected from marls of Upper Callovian age near the village of Pedrógão".

**Ruled out.**
- *Callovian from Azerêdo and colleagues' possibility.* A possibility is not a reading; the stage stays only pending the *Alocodon* finding.

**Open.** Whether the *Alocodon* horizon at Pedrógão lies in the Cabaços or in the Callovian beds below the disconformity. If below, the Callovian and Middle Jurassic leave this formation.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Cabaços?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Middle Oxfordian at its core, reaching the Late Oxfordian and Early Kimmeridgian in its middle part at Cesareda, with a base that may reach the latest Callovian. Azerêdo and colleagues (2002) put the formation directly above the basinwide Middle–Upper Jurassic disconformity; it "lacks good biostratigraphical markers" and is "traditionally attributed to the Middle Oxfordian" on the dasycladacean Heteroporella lusitanica, which occurs below or beside levels with Middle Oxfordian ammonites. Because that alga is nearly absent from the lowest beds, they allow that the base "could also encompass the Lower Oxfordian or even the uppermost Callovian", which would carry the unit below the Middle–Late Jurassic boundary. Kullberg and colleagues (2013) narrow the formation to the Plicatilis Zone of the Middle Oxfordian, give it 80 to 200 m (about 390 m in the Campelos-1 borehole), place it at the base of Choffat's Lusitanian "stage", and list the earlier names it takes in: Choffat's Cabaço beds in part, the Cabaço Limestone of Atrops and Marques (1986, 1988), and the Cabaços Formation of Leinfelder and Wilson (1989) in part. Schneider and colleagues (2009) date two layers from the middle of the formation at the Cesareda–Serra d'El Rei plateau by strontium isotopes, with large errors, to the Late Oxfordian to Early Kimmeridgian, and follow Azerêdo and colleagues in placing the brackish onset below them in the Middle Oxfordian. Choffat (1901) puts the Cabaço beds directly on the Callovian with Cosmoceras Dunkani, at about 500 m, and correlates them with the zone of Ammonites transversarius; the zone of Peltoceras bimammatum he gives to the Montejunto beds above. That corrects three points in the reading of Choffat (1901) that this record's reference note carried until 2026-11-23: it tied the Cabaço beds to the bimammatus zone, quoted Choffat's sentence on the bimammatum zone as if it concerned them, and put a lacuna beneath them in his table, where the lacuna stands in the Cintra column. Choffat's reading still keeps the Callovian below the unit rather than within it. Governs `stages: [Callovian, Oxfordian, Kimmeridgian]` and `period: [Middle Jurassic, Late Jurassic]` on [Cabaços](../../../stratigraphy/c/cabaços.yml).

**Evidence.**
- azerêdo2002a, p. 1341: "The lowermost Upper Jurassic deposits above the discontinuity belong to the Cabaços formation", which "basically corresponds to the Cabaços Beds and the Vale Verde Beds of earlier workers"; its thickness "ranges from 40 m to 150 m (locally 200 m?)"; the hiatus "spans at least from the latest Callovian to the end of the Early Oxfordian", but "some uppermost Callovian–Lower Oxfordian may exist".
- azerêdo2002a, p. 1342: "It lacks good biostratigraphical markers, but is traditionally attributed to the Middle Oxfordian"; Heteroporella lusitanica "has a basinwide occurrence succeeded by, or laterally equivalent to, levels with Middle Oxfordian ammonites"; "this alga is virtually absent in the lowermost part of the formation, which therefore could also encompass the Lower Oxfordian or even the uppermost Callovian".
- kullberg2013a, p. 227: "Cabaços Formation [Middle Oxfordian, Plicatilis Zone] (highly variable, between 80 to 200 m; approximately 390 m in the Campelos-1 borehole)", equal to the Cabaço beds in part of Choffat (1893, 1901), the Cabaço Limestone of Atrops and Marques (1986, 1988) and the Cabaços Formation in part of Leinfelder and Wilson (1989), "unit at the base of the 'stage' Lusitanian, defined by Choffat (1885, 1893, 1893a)" (translated).
- kullberg2013a, p. 253: the D9 discontinuity, from the base of the Upper Callovian to the Middle Oxfordian, "corresponds to a stratigraphic gap that comprises the zones of Lamberti (top of the Callovian) and Mariae and Cordatum (Lower Oxfordian)" (translated).
- schneider2009a, p. 1960: from two layers in the middle part of the formation at Cesareda, "Unfortunately, the relative errors of the measurements from these two samples are comparably large. Nevertheless, a Late Oxfordian to Early Kimmeridgian age (155.3–154.4 Ma; bimammatum-platynota zones) may be derived"; the initial transgression "most likely occurred during the Middle Oxfordian".
- schneider2009a, p. 1951: "The Oxfordian-Kimmeridgian stage boundary is interpreted sensu gallico herein"; sedimentation "started in the ?latest Early/Middle Oxfordian all across the Lusitanian Basin".
- choffat1901a, p. 116, under "A. Lusitanian Stage": "The upper bed of the Callovian is formed by thin strata containing Cosmoceras Dunkani"; above it the Cabaço beds, 200 m and 300 m, of whose upper bed "this bed corresponds to the zone of Ammonites transversarius" (translated).
- choffat1901a, p. 117: of the Montejunto beds, "Considerations … lead me to regard this member as representing the zone of Peltoceras bimammatum" (translated).
- choffat1901a, correlation table ("Essai de parallélisme d'une partie du Malm portugais", between pp. 128 and 129): in the column south of Torres-Vedras, "Couches de Cabaço, à Aspidoceras faustum, etc. … 500m" rest on "Callovien à Cosmoceras Dunkani" and lie in the row correlated with the "Zone à Ammonites transversarius"; "Lacune" stands in the Cintra column.

**Ruled out.**
- *Middle Oxfordian only.* Kullberg and colleagues (2013) give the Plicatilis Zone, but Schneider and colleagues (2009) date the middle of the formation at Cesareda to the Late Oxfordian and Early Kimmeridgian, and the union of the readings keeps both.
- *A lacuna beneath the Cabaço beds in Choffat's table, and a bimammatus age for them.* A misreading of Choffat (1901): the lacuna is in the Cintra column, and the bimammatum sentence concerns the Montejunto beds. The Cabaço beds sit on the Callovian and correlate with the transversarius zone.

**Open.** The Callovian, and with it the Middle Jurassic in `period`, rests only on Azerêdo and colleagues' "could also encompass … or even the uppermost Callovian", a possibility from the near-absence of one alga. Choffat (1901) puts the unit above the Callovian, Schneider and colleagues (2009) start Late Jurassic sedimentation in the "?latest Early/Middle Oxfordian", and Kullberg and colleagues (2013) put the top Callovian Lamberti Zone inside the D9 gap. Whether a stated possibility licenses a stage is the question to settle; the methods entry on epochs in `period` treats a possibility as no reading.

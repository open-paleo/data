# Chinle

## Is Atchley and colleagues' 227.6 Ma a depositional age, and is it Carnian?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** Yes to both; the Carnian stays. Answers the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Atchley and colleagues (2013), now read, date sample SS-24, a tuffaceous sandstone 2 m above the base of the formation in the Mesa Redondo Member, to 227.604 ± 0.082/0.13/0.28 Ma by CA-TIMS U–Pb on single zircons. Their methods call such dates the "maximum depositional age of the sample", but their results argue that the dated tuffaceous beds, conforming to stratigraphic order, are "reliable indicators of depositional age"; an own argued depositional reading counts, as for the [Winton](../w/winton.md). At its widest uncertainty the date runs from 227.32 to 227.88 Ma, older than the 227.3 Ma base of the Norian on the ICS chart v2024/12. Their "most of the Chinle Formation is Norian" concerns the bulk of the formation and does not exclude the base. Governs the Carnian in `stages` and the note on atchley2013a on [Chinle](../../../stratigraphy/c/chinle.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- atchley2013a, p. 1153: "five form a coherent cluster with a weighted mean 206Pb/238U age of 227.604 ± 0.082/0.13/0.28 Ma (MSWD = 1.5, Fig. 4)".
- atchley2013a, p. 1151: the dates "are interpreted as the maximum depositional age of the sample"; "The closely spaced and dated tuffaceous beds that now encompass the entire succession have mutually resolvable ages that conform to the stratigraphic order, substantiating their interpretation as reliable indicators of depositional age."
- atchley2013a, p. 1153: "therefore, the age of sample SS-24 suggests that most of the Chinle Formation is Norian in age".

**Ruled out.**
- *Dropping the Carnian as a maximum age.* The authors argue the dated beds give depositional ages.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Chinle Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Carnian to Rhaetian, unchanged, with the Carnian confined to the very base. The Carnian rests on one number: a radioisotopic date of about 227.6 Ma from the lower Mesa Redondo Member that Martz and Parker (2017) relay, which is older than the 227.3 Ma base of the Norian given by Ogg and colleagues (2020) and so falls in the Carnian on the current chart. Martz and Parker themselves put the base of the formation roughly at the base of the Norian, above the Tr-3 unconformity, and their oldest faunachron in the earliest early Norian. The Rhaetian comes from their Apachean, "during the Rhaetian", which takes in the Owl Rock, Rock Point and Church Rock Members, and from Lucas and Hunt's (1992) "late Norian/Rhaetian" for the Rock Point; the Triassic–Jurassic boundary lies above, in the Moenave Formation. Governs `stages: [Carnian, Norian, Rhaetian]` on [Chinle](../../../stratigraphy/c/chinle.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- martz2017a, p. 72: the Tr-3 unconformity "at the base of the Chinle Formation is indeed a major erosional hiatus" that "may make the base of the Chinle Formation roughly equivalent to the base of the Norian stage".
- martz2017a, p. 105: "A radioisotopic date of about 227.6 Ma is available from the lower Mesa Redondo Formation"; "The Triassic-Jurassic boundary, which is well calibrated at 201.3 Ma", "may lie between the Dinosaur Canyon Member and the overlying Whitmore Point Member ... or even higher in the Whitmore Point Member".
- martz2017a, p. 112: "The Otischalkian estimated holochron extends from about 227 to 224 Ma ... during the earliest Lacian (earliest early Norian)"; "The Apachean estimated holochron extends from about 207 to 202 Ma ... during the Rhaetian", and its holochronozone takes in "most of the Owl Rock Member, Rock Point Member, and Church Rock Member" of the Chinle Formation.
- ogg2020a, p. 933: "the base-Norian, based on this working definition and Kent et al. (2017) chron ages, has a projected age of 227.3 Ma".
- hunt1998a, p. 513: "Chinle Group strata range from late Carnian (early Tuvalian) to Rhaetian in age", with four faunachrons, "the Otischalkian (late Carnian: early Tuvalian), Adamanian (late Carnian: late Tuvalian), Revueltian (early-late Norian: Lacian-Sevatian) and Apachean (Rhaetian)", both credited to earlier work. The paper works at group rank throughout; "Chinle Formation" appears in it only in the titles of works in its reference list.
- lucas1992a, p. 151: the Triassic strata of the area "are assigned to the Middle Triassic Moenkopi Formation and the Upper Triassic Chinle Group"; p. 158: the "siltstone member" is in places "removed by post-Triassic, pre-Entrada erosion", the Petrified Forest Formation being "disconformably overlain by either the Rock Point Formation or the Middle Jurassic Entrada"; p. 166: the Rock Point fauna includes a new phytosaur genus, and "A diverse body of evidence suggests that these strata are of late Norian/Rhaetian age".
- cather2013a, p. 110: in north-central New Mexico the Chinle's map units "do not correspond well to the various formations previously proposed by stratigraphers for the Chinle Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *A late Carnian (early Tuvalian) base, after Hunt and colleagues (1998).* The range is the Chinle Group's, about 25 formations across the western United States, not this formation's, and it rests on a calibration of the faunachrons that made the Otischalkian and Adamanian late Carnian. On Martz and Parker's (2017) recalibration the Otischalkian is earliest early Norian, so the Carnian of that scheme does not carry over to the formation.
- *Carnian through much of the lower formation.* The same older calibration; it does not survive the lengthened Norian, under which only a date older than 227.3 Ma places a horizon in the Carnian.

**Open.** Whether the 227.6 Ma date is a depositional age, and whether its uncertainty excludes the Norian. Martz and Parker relay it from Atchley and colleagues (2013) without an error or the kind of date, and 227.6 Ma lies only 0.3 Myr above the base of the Norian. If it is a maximum depositional age, it is a limit and gives no stage, and the formation's Carnian would have no support among the works cited. Atchley and colleagues (2013) would settle it.

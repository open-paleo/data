# Castrillo de la Reina

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Castrillo de la Reina?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Late Barremian to early Aptian. Torcida Fernández-Baldor and colleagues (2011) regard the upper section of the formation as Late Barremian to Early Aptian, citing Martín-Closas and Alonso Millán (1998). Dieudonné and colleagues (2026) give the same range for the formation's continental sediments, which lie mostly in the vicinity of Salas de los Infantes, and support it through the neighboring units: the formation is not strictly correlated with the surrounding deposits but is laterally contiguous with the Abejar and Leza formations; the Leza holds a Barremian–Albian dasyclad and a Barremian charophyte, and its marine-influenced top, following Suarez-Gonzalez (2015), may match the highest sea-level peaks of the period, "generally dated in the middle-upper portion of the lower Aptian". The 2026 statement is not wholly independent of the 2011 one: the two papers share an author, Torcida Fernández-Baldor, and both draw on Martín-Closas and Alonso Millán (1998); the Leza argument is the 2026 paper's own addition. Governs `stages: [Barremian, Aptian]` and `period: [Early Cretaceous]` on [Castrillo de la Reina](../../../stratigraphy/c/castrillo-de-la-reina.yml).

**Evidence.**
- torcidafernández-baldor2011a, p. 537: "Upper section of the Castrillo de la Reina Formation (Tenadas de los Vallejos II locality), regarded as Late Barremian to Early Aptian in age (Martín-Closas and Alonso Millán 1998)".
- torcidafernández-baldor2011a, p. 539, Table 1: "Castrillo de la Reina Formation (Late Barremian–Early Aptian), Burgos, Spain".
- dieudonné2026a, p. 1, Introduction: "The upper Barremian to lower Aptian continental sediments of the Castrillo de la Reina Formation mostly lie in the vicinity of the Salas de los Infantes municipality".
- dieudonné2026a, p. 7, Type locality & horizon: the formation "is laterally contiguous with the fluvial deposits of the Abejar Formation and the Leza Formation"; the top of the Leza "might correspond to 'the highest sea-level peaks ... generally dated in the middle-upper portion of the lower Aptian'", which "supports an upper Barremian to lower Aptian age for the Castrillo de la Reina Formation".
- suarez-gonzalez2015b, p. 2, Abstract: "the strongest marine influence at the top of the Leza Fm might correspond to the highest sea-level peak of this transgressive phase, which is generally dated in the middle-upper portion of the lower Aptian".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Martín-Closas and Alonso Millán (1998), the source both papers cite for the formation's age and for the Leza charophyte, has not been read. Suarez-Gonzalez (2015) is a doctoral thesis and dates the Leza, not the Castrillo de la Reina, so it adds support to the published datings and carries no value alone.

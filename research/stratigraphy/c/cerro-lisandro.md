# Cerro Lisandro

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Cerro Lisandro Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian and Turonian, from two original readings. Hugo and Leanza (2001) assign the formation to the late Cenomanian–early Turonian from their age analysis of the Neuquén Group, and Leanza and colleagues (2004) repeat that age with Hugo and Leanza as the source. Garrido (2010), finding neither absolute dates nor biostratigraphy for the unit, assigns it tentatively to the middle–late Turonian on its stratigraphic relations, and González Riga and Ortiz David (2013) give the same span after him. Garrido also relays Legarreta and Gulisano's (1989) 91 to 90 Ma, which he places in the middle Turonian and which lies inside the Turonian on the current chart. The union of the two readings is Cenomanian and Turonian. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian]` on [Cerro Lisandro](../../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-lisandro.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hugo2001a, p. 14, Edad y correlaciones: following the age analysis of the Neuquén Group, the formation "is assigned to the upper Cenomanian - lower Turonian" (translated).
- hugo2001a, p. 2, Abstract: the Neuquén Group's "age ranges from early Cenomanian (97 ± 3 Ma) to early Campanian (74 ± 3 Ma)"; it is subdivided into "the Cenomanian-lower Turonian Limay Subgroup comprising the Candeleros, Huincul and Cerro Lisandro Formations; the upper Turonian-Coniacian Río Neuquén Subgroup including the Portezuelo and Plottier Formations; and the Santonian-lower Campanian Río Colorado Subgroup comprising the Bajo de la Carpa and Anacleto Formations". The Resumen on p. 1 says the same in Spanish.
- leanza2004a, p. 69, Cerro Lisandro Formation: "The age of the Cerro Lisandro Formation can be regarded as Late Cenomanian–Early Turonian (Hugo and Leanza, 2001a)".
- garrido2010a, p. 140: "There are no absolute dates or biostratigraphic studies that allow a certain age to be established for the Cerro Lisandro Fm."; after Legarreta and Gulisano (1989), "these deposits would lie between 91 and 90 Ma, corresponding to the Middle Turonian (International Stratigraphic Chart, 2009)" (translated).
- garrido2010a, pp. 140–141: Hugo and Leanza (2001) and Leanza and colleagues (2004) "place this unit between the Late Cenomanian and the Early Turonian"; "On the basis of the available data and its stratigraphic relations, the Cerro Lisandro Fm. is tentatively assigned in this work to the Middle – Upper Turonian" (translated).
- gonzálezriga2013a, p. 6, Horizon and locality: "Uppermost levels of the Cerro Lisandro Formation (late Cenomanian-early Turonian after Hugo and Leanza [2001], middle Turonian after Legarreta and Gulisano [1989]; middle-late Turonian after Garrido [2010])".

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Cenomanian, from the group's 97 ± 3 Ma.* Hugo and Leanza give that figure for the base of the whole Neuquén Group, and their own date for this formation begins in the late Cenomanian. A group's range is not a dating of one of its formations.

**Open.** Legarreta and Gulisano (1989) has not been read; Garrido relays its 91 to 90 Ma, and whether the figure is a measured date or an estimate is unknown. It adds no stage either way.

## Is the formation Cerro Lisandro or Lisandro?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** Cerro Lisandro: the unit was first named "Estratos de Cerro Lisandro", later shortened to Lisandro, and the full original name was restored by Leanza and Hugo (1997). Governs `name` on [Cerro Lisandro](../../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-lisandro.yml) and `location.formation` on the records that name it.

**Evidence.**
- garrido2010a, p. 139: "Herrero Ducloux (en Fossa Mancini et al., 1938) definió a esta unidad bajo el nombre de "Estratos de Cerro Lisandro"" ("Herrero Ducloux defined this unit under the name 'Cerro Lisandro Strata'", translated) and "Finalmente, Leanza & Hugo (1997) reintroducen su denominación original completa, quedando reconocida de esta forma bajo el nombre de Fm. Cerro Lisandro" ("Finally, Leanza and Hugo (1997) reintroduce its full original name, so that it is now recognized as the Cerro Lisandro Formation", translated).
- garrido2010a, p. 126: the "Fm. Lisandro sensu Ramos, 1981" is the same unit.

**Ruled out.**
- *Lisandro.* A later shortening of the original name.

**Open.** Herrero Ducloux (1938) and Leanza and Hugo (1997) have not been read; the history is Garrido's.

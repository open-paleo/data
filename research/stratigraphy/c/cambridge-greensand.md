# Cambridge Greensand

## Does Kirkland and colleagues' (2013) "upper Albian" date the Cambridge Greensand?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** No. It is the age of *Anoplosaurus*, whose material is thought to be reworked from the Gault beneath, and the same paper places that taxon in the Gault. The member's depositional age is Cenomanian. Governs `stages: [Albian, Cenomanian]` on [Cambridge Greensand](../../../stratigraphy/c/cambridge-greensand.yml), to which this reading adds nothing.

**Evidence.**
- barrett2020a, p. 2, Geological background: "Although the depositional age of the Cambridge Greensand Member is thought to be Cenomanian on the basis of its foraminiferan fauna (Hart, 1973), many fossils within it … were reworked from the underlying Albian Gault Formation", "suggesting an upper Albian age for these specimens".
- kirkland2013a, p. 1, Introduction: Seeley "described the juvenile nodosaurid Anoplosaurus curtonotus … from the uppermost Lower Cretaceous (upper Albian) Cambridge Greensand"; p. 34: "The juvenile Anoplosaurus from the Albian Gault Clays of southern England".

**Ruled out.**
- *Albian from Kirkland and colleagues (2013).* The label attaches to a fossil, and on p. 34 the same paper gives that fossil to the Gault Clays. Which of the two units yielded the *Anoplosaurus* syntypes is itself unresolved (see [*Anoplosaurus*](../../genera/A/Anoplosaurus.md)).
- *Albian from Barrett and Bonsor (2020).* Their "upper Albian" is "for these specimens", the reworked fauna, set against a Cenomanian depositional age.
- *Early Cretaceous from White (1973).* A catalogue entry, which cannot carry a value alone ([methods](../../methods.md)).

**Open.** What supports the Albian on the entry. Neither cited reference gives it for the member. Pereda-Suberbiola and Barrett (1999) write that "recent work suggests a Late Albian or Early Cenomanian age for the deposits (Rawson et al. 1978; N.C. Fraser, pers. comm.)" (pereda-suberbiola1999a, p. 185), which is relayed and partly a personal communication; Barrett, a co-author, gives the depositional age as Cenomanian in 2020. Whether the 2020 statement supersedes the 1999 one decides whether the Albian stays.

# Cedar Mountain

## What do the cited works say about the age and extent of the Cedar Mountain?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Berriasian to Cenomanian, the union of the published readings. Joeckel and colleagues (2019) date the Yellow Cat Member, low in the formation, Berriasian to Hauterivian on a palynoflora, with detrital zircon maxima in the same span. Kirkland and Madsen (2007) give Barremian to Albian for most of the formation and an early Cenomanian ash in the Mussentuchit Member at its top, and Sprinkel and colleagues (2012) give Barremian(?) to earliest Cenomanian in central and southern Utah. Sprinkel and colleagues date the northeastern Utah beds early to late Albian; that is a regional age, since they hold those beds to be older than the Mussentuchit, and it does not narrow the whole unit. Its Albian rests on a maximum of 104.46 Ma, older than the base of the Cenomanian, so it stays Albian on the current chart. Mori's (2009) member-by-member zircon brackets, from a thesis, add no stage outside this span. Kirkland and colleagues (2016) date the strata mapped as Cedar Mountain in southwestern Utah latest Albian to earliest Cenomanian, which also adds no stage. Governs `stages` and `period` on [Cedar Mountain](../../../stratigraphy/c/cedar-mountain.yml).

**Evidence.**
- joeckel2019a, p. ?, Abstract: youngest single grain ages from the Yellow Cat Member "ranging from 142.5±2.7 Ma to 133.7 ± 2.7 Ma (Berriasian–late Valanginian)"; one palaeosol gives "a robust Concordia Age of 136.3±1.3 (Valanginian)"; the palynoflora "is assigned to the middle Berriasian to early Hauterivian stages".
- joeckel2019a, p. ?, introduction: "Together, these advances firmly assign Berriasian-Hauterivian ages to strata in the lower Cedar Mountain Formation".
- kirkland2007a, p. 23: "paleontological and geological evidence suggests an age range of Albian-Barremian is represented in the Cedar Mountain Formation".
- kirkland2007a, p. 17: an ash "within the Mussentuchit Member, indicates an early Cenomanian age" and "supports a correlation with the silicious marine Mowry Shale to the north", "near the base of the Upper Cretaceous".
- sprinkel2012a, p. 8: in central and southern Utah the formation "is considered Barremian(?) to earliest Cenomanian based on dinosaur biostratigraphy" and "radiometric data".
- sprinkel2012a, p. 1: "Its age in northeastern Utah is early to late Albian from a radiometric age (detrital U-Pb zircons) of 104.46 ± 0.95 Ma associated with a well-preserved sauropod skull, chemostratigraphic analysis, and palynology in the overlying Dakota Formation".
- sprinkel2012a, p. 12: the uppermost northeastern beds resemble the Mussentuchit Member, but "radiometric data, dinosaur biostratigraphy, and palynomorph assemblages from sections in central Utah indicate these beds are not time equivalent".
- mori2009a, p. 30: "the Yellow Cat/Poison Strip Members range from 125 to 121 Ma (Early Aptian)", "the Ruby Ranch Member ranges from 120 - 117 Ma to 111 Ma (Late Aptian to earliest Albian)", and "the Mussentuchit Member ranges from about 109 - 105 Ma to 99 to 96 Ma (mid-Albian to mid-Cenomanian) and perhaps as young as 91 Ma (Turonian)".
- mori2009a, p. 30: with young zircons in the overlying Dakota Sandstone, the Mussentuchit Member "may range to late Cenomanian or early Turonian. These anomalously young ages need to checked in a future study".
- mori2009a, p. 7: "based on plant biostratigraphy, the Cedar Mountain Formation was determined to be between Barremian and Cenomanian".
- kirkland2016a, p. 155: smectitic strata "mapped by UGS geologists as Cedar Mountain Formation in southwestern Utah" are "all of latest Albian to earliest Cenomanian age".
- kirkland2007a, p. 77: the basal Cretaceous strata of southern Utah "are not contiguous with the Cedar Mountain Formation in its type area in central Utah", and their conglomerates "are derived from different source areas".

**Ruled out.**
- *A Turonian top.* Mori (2009) lets the Mussentuchit Member reach the early Turonian on anomalously young zircons from the overlying Dakota Sandstone, and asks that they be checked. That is a possibility, not a reading, and it comes from a thesis, which cannot carry a stage alone.
- *Early Aptian for the Yellow Cat and Poison Strip members.* Mori's (2009) label for 125 to 121 Ma follows an older chart. On ICS chart v2024/12, with the base of the Aptian at 121.4 Ma, the span is almost entirely Barremian, a stage the formation already has.
- *Albian for the whole formation.* Sprinkel and colleagues (2012) date only the northeastern Utah beds early to late Albian, and hold them not time equivalent to the Mussentuchit Member, so the age applies to that region.

**Open.** Whether the smectitic strata of southwestern Utah belong to the formation. Kirkland and Madsen (2007) argue against the name there, because the strata are not contiguous with the type area and their conglomerates came from other source areas; Kirkland and colleagues (2016) correlate the strata mapped under the name with the Mussentuchit Member. The answer changes no stage.

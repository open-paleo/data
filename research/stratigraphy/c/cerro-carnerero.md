# Cerro Carnerero

## Does Pol and colleagues' Pliensbachian–Toarcian date the Cerro Carnerero?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No; the stages are dropped and the unit keeps its Early Jurassic `period`. Supersedes the 2026-11-25 entry on the stages. Pol and colleagues (2022) state that there are no radiometric dates for the unit and reach "Pliensbachian or the early Toarcian" only between Pliensbachian ammonites below, relayed from Riccardi (1992), and the overlying Lonco Trapial; a bracket between two limits gives no stage. Riccardi (1992) gives the unit no age of its own. Governs the absent `stages` and the note on pol2022a on [Cerro Carnerero](../../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-carnerero.yml).

**Evidence.**
- pol2022a, p. 135: "Although there are no radiometric dates for this unit, there are ammonites from underlying horizons biostratigraphically dated as Pliensbachian (Riccardi 1992)"; "It is therefore likely that the Cerro Carnerero Formation was deposited at some point during the Pliensbachian or the early Toarcian."

**Ruled out.**
- *Pliensbachian–Toarcian from Pol and colleagues (2022).* A bracket.

**Open.** A date or index fossil from the unit itself.

## What does Riccardi and colleagues' (1992) chapter say lies beneath the Cerro Carnerero?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Marine beds with late Pliensbachian to early Toarcian ammonites, on which the Carnerero "probably" lies unconformably. Answers the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. The chapter gives the Carnerero no age of its own, and it relays the ammonites from earlier work. The beds beneath reach the early Toarcian, so the member is at least partly Toarcian. Pol and colleagues' (2022) "Pliensbachian or the early Toarcian" still contributes both stages, since an either-or contributes every stage it allows. The chapter's "Toarcian to Middle Jurassic" for the Lonco Trapial Group comes from stratigraphic relationships that the U–Pb bracket in [Lonco Trapial](../l/lonco-trapial.md) supersedes. Governs `stages: [Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on [Cerro Carnerero](../../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-carnerero.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- riccardi1992a, p. 134: "The marine facies, called the Osta Arena or Lomas Chatas and the Mulanguiñeu Formations, yielded late Pliensbachian–early Toarcian ammonites (Musacchio and Riccardi 1971; Blasco et al. 1979a; Hillebrandt 1987)".
- riccardi1992a, p. 134: "The Osta Arena and Mulanguiñeu Formations probably are unconformably overlain by 350 m of red and white tuffs and reddish and greenish siltstones with intercalated conglomerates of the Carnerero or Cerro Ferrarotti Formation, which yielded the sauropod Amygdalodon patagonicus Cabrera".
- riccardi1992a, p. 134: "Stratigraphic relationships suggest a Toarcian to Middle Jurassic age for the Lonco Trapial Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *Toarcian alone, from the underlying beds.* The beds span the late Pliensbachian and early Toarcian, the contact is only "probably" an unconformity, and the chapter does not say the Carnerero lies on the Toarcian part everywhere; Pol and colleagues' either-or keeps the Pliensbachian.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Cerro Carnerero?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Pliensbachian–Toarcian, unchanged, on Pol and colleagues (2022), who place the unit between Pliensbachian ammonites below and the Lonco Trapial above and conclude it "was deposited at some point during the Pliensbachian or the early Toarcian". Rauhut's (2003) readings, Toarcian to Bajocian and, as most likely, late Toarcian to Aalenian, came before the U–Pb dating. Whether the unit lies beneath the volcanic rocks of the Lonco Trapial, as Rauhut, Pol and colleagues and Page and colleagues (1999) describe it, or is a member inside the formation, as Nullo and Proserpio (1975) make it, it is older than the 178.77 Ma that bounds the Lonco Trapial from above, which Cúneo and colleagues (2013) place in the Toarcian; neither the Aalenian nor the Bajocian survives. Adds to the 2026-10-01 entries in [Lonco Trapial](../l/lonco-trapial.md) and [Lonco Trapial: group or formation](../../topics/lonco-trapial-group-or-formation.md), and supersedes nothing in them. Governs `stages: [Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on [Cerro Carnerero](../../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-carnerero.yml).

**Evidence.**
- pol2022a, p. 131, Abstract: "The earliest sauropod from the Jurassic of South America is Amygdalodon patagonicus from the Cerro Carnerero Formation (Pliensbachian–early Toarcian)".
- pol2022a, p. 135, Locality and Age: Cañadón Puelman, "southwest of Cerro Carnerero", Chubut; the unit "has been regarded as Early to Middle Jurassic by different authors"; "there are ammonites from underlying horizons biostratigraphically dated as Pliensbachian"; the overlying Lonco Trapial "may not be synchronous in all areas in which it has been recognized. It is therefore likely that the Cerro Carnerero Formation was deposited at some point during the Pliensbachian or the early Toarcian".
- pol2022a, p. 157, Table 1: Amygdalodon patagonicus, Cerro Carnerero, "Pliensbachian–early Toarcian".
- rauhut2003c, p. 173, Abstract: "from the Cerro Carnerero Formation (Toarcian-Bajocian)".
- rauhut2003c, p. 174: the material's age, "Although usually cited as Bajocian", "has also been regarded as Early Jurassic by some authors"; the unit "unconformably overlies marine units of early Toarcian age" and "is overlain by the Lonco Trapial (= Cañadón Puelman) Formation"; "Page et al. (1999: 466) noted that the volcanism in this area started in the Aalenian, so a late Toarcian to Aalenian age seems to be most likely for the Cerro Carnerero Formation, but more data is needed to confirm this".
- rauhut2003c, p. 179, Type locality and horizon: "The section is now considered to belong to the Cerro Carnerero Formation, Toarcian to Bajocian".
- page1999a, p. 465: Cerro Carnerero is used for "the purely sedimentary sequences that underlie the volcanic rocks in the Sierra de Agnia and overlie the marine Liassic sequences" (translated).
- page1999a, p. 466: of the absolute ages reviewed for the Lonco Trapial, between 176 and 146 Ma, "The oldest values place the beginning of magmatism during the Aalenian" (translated).
- cúneo2013a, p. 1271 (in-press version, p. 5), Geological implications: "the Lonco Trapial Formation is now bracketed between 188.95 Ma and 178.77 Ma, entirely within the Early Jurassic (Pliensbachian–Toarcian)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Toarcian to Bajocian, after Rauhut (2003).* Rauhut relays it from earlier regional work, and the Bajocian age "usually cited" for Amygdalodon goes with it. Below or inside the Lonco Trapial, the unit is older than the 178.77 Ma that bounds that formation from above, which Cúneo and colleagues (2013) place in the Toarcian; the Aalenian and Bajocian are younger still.
- *Late Toarcian to Aalenian, Rauhut's (2003) preferred estimate.* It rests on the Aalenian onset of volcanism that Page and colleagues (1999) draw from absolute ages of 176 to 146 Ma, and Rauhut calls for more data. Those are the older dates that the U–Pb bracket of 188.95 to 178.77 Ma replaced (see [Lonco Trapial](../l/lonco-trapial.md)); on that bracket the volcanism lies wholly in the Pliensbachian–Toarcian, and the Cerro Carnerero is no younger than Toarcian.

**Open.** What lies beneath the unit. Pol and colleagues (2022) cite Pliensbachian ammonites from underlying horizons, while Rauhut (2003) has the unit unconformably overlying marine units of early Toarcian age, and Page and colleagues (1999) say only "Liassic". If the underlying marine beds are early Toarcian where the unit rests on them, the unit is Toarcian and its Pliensbachian has no support among the works cited. Both papers cite the same chapter for it, Riccardi and colleagues (1992), which would settle it.

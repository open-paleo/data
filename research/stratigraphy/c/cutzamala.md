# Cutzamala

## Does any work read date the Cutzamala Santonian, and do its hadrosaurs and pollen share a section?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** No to the first, yes to the second; the Santonian is dropped and the formation takes the Maastrichtian. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry on the stages and answers its Open line. The Santonian traces only to Ramírez-Velasco and colleagues (2012), who give the Barranca Los Bonetes strata "between 86.8 and 84 Ma" from a radiometric date and magnetostratigraphy they cite rather than report: Benammi and colleagues (2006), a conference abstract, and Mariscal-Ramos (2006), a master's thesis. A second-hand date adds no stage until its original is read, and a thesis never sole-sources a value. The 84 Ma lava that Martini and colleagues (2009) take from Mariscal-Ramos and colleagues (2005) is "a possible maximum age", a limit. Benammi and colleagues (2005) draw the pollen level and the three hadrosaur levels in one column at Barranca Los Bonetes, the pollen near its base, below all three. They read the pollen by first occurrences, which give "a maximum age of Maastrichtian", and make the deposits Late Cretaceous on the hadrosaurs; the Santonian appears in their paper only as the lower end of pollen ranges. Martini and colleagues give the Maastrichtian as "the youngest age for the folded part" of the formation. Governs `stages: [Maastrichtian]` and the notes on benammi2005a and ramírez-velasco2012a on [Cutzamala](../../../stratigraphy/c/cutzamala.yml), and `stage: [Maastrichtian]` on [*Huehuecanauhtlus tiquichensis*](../../../genera/H/Huehuecanauhtlus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ramírez-velasco2012a, p. 383: "According to radiometric data (Benammi et al. 2006) and magnetostratigraphic analysis (Mariscal-Ramos 2006), the fossiliferous strata outcropping in Barranca Los Bonetes are Santonian (Late Cretaceous) in age, specifically dating back between 86.8 and 84 Ma, and corresponding to the upper part of chron C34n."
- benammi2005a, p. 431, Fig. 2: one column at Barranca Los Bonetes marks "Po: nivel de polen" on a lacustrine limestone near its base and "F: nivel de restos de dinosaurios" at three levels, F1 to F3, above it.
- benammi2005a, p. 432: the age "was reconsidered based on the same sedimentary sequence where the hadrosaur remains were found" (translated).
- benammi2005a, p. 432: "the taxa found in this volcano-sedimentary basin have a stratigraphic range from the Santonian to the Paleocene" (translated).
- benammi2005a, p. 433: "the mere presence of Nudopollis indicates a maximum age of Maastrichtian" (translated).
- benammi2005a, p. 433: "The discovery of hadrosaur remains makes it possible to resolve this conflict about the age of the deposits, which henceforth can be considered as Late Cretaceous" (translated).
- martini2009a, p. 159: "Mariscal-Ramos et al. (2005) reported a radiometric age of 84 Ma for a lava flow interbedded within the basal beds near the dinosaur site, indicating a possible maximum age of late Santonian".
- martini2009a, p. 161: "considering that the youngest age for the folded part of the continental succession is Maastrichtian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Santonian from Ramírez-Velasco and colleagues (2012), and from Ramírez-Velasco (2022) after them.* A second-hand date whose originals are an abstract and a master's thesis.
- *Santonian from the pollen.* It is the base of the taxa's ranges, which Benammi and colleagues narrow by first occurrences to no older than the Maastrichtian.

**Open.** Benammi and colleagues (2006) and Mariscal-Ramos (2006) have not been read; they would show whether the magnetostratigraphy places the beds in chron C34n. Neither Benammi and colleagues (2005) nor Martini and colleagues (2009) call the hadrosaur beds Maastrichtian in so many words. The Paleocene or earliest Eocene of the upper part stays in the dispute.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Cutzamala Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The age is unsettled between the Late Cretaceous and the Paleogene, and the works cited give it as a dispute rather than a range. Martini and colleagues (2009) find the age "not well constrained": a lava flow in the basal beds sets a possible maximum of late Santonian, pollen and hadrosaur remains put the lower, folded part in the Late Cretaceous and Maastrichtian, and they suggest a Paleocene or earliest Eocene age for the upper, unfolded part, for which they have no data. Villanueva-Amadoz and colleagues (2014) set out the older disagreement at La Barranca de los Bonetes, where palynology read as younger than the late Paleocene and hadrosaur remains read as Late Cretaceous come from one section. The two readings there rest partly on the same pollen association: the younger reading is based on the last occurrence of its taxa, while Martini and colleagues read *Momipites* from the same area on its first occurrence, which is Maastrichtian. Governs `dispute` on [Cutzamala](../../../stratigraphy/c/cutzamala.yml).

**Evidence.**
- martini2009a, p. 159: the formation "was introduced by Campa-Uranga and Ramirez (1979) to describe a thick succession of continental red beds widely exposed along the Cutzamala River basin, east of the village of San Lucas"; a shortening episode during deposition "makes it possible to subdivide the Cutzamala Formation into a lower folded part and an upper unfolded part".
- martini2009a, p. 159: "The age of the Cutzamala Formation is not well constrained"; at Los Bonetes canyon hadrosaur bones give "a Late Cretaceous age to the lower part", pollen of the *Momipites* groups has a first occurrence that "is Maastrichtian in age", and "a radiometric age of 84 Ma for a lava flow interbedded within the basal beds near the dinosaur site" indicates "a possible maximum age of late Santonian".
- martini2009a, p. 161: "No data are available for the upper part of the Cutzamala Formation", and from the Maastrichtian youngest age of the folded part and the early Eocene intrusions that cut it, "we suggest a Paleocene and/or earliest Eocene age for the upper undeformed part".
- villanueva-amadoz2014a, p. 109: the 1979 Late Cretaceous assignment "provoked a debate among different authors who disagreed between Late Cretaceous or Paleogene" (translated).
- villanueva-amadoz2014a, p. 109: by the palynology of Martínez-Hernández and colleagues (2000) at La Barranca de los Bonetes, "the palynological association studied determines an age younger than the late Paleocene", a reading "based according to Benammi et al. (2005) on the last occurrence of these taxa" (translated).
- villanueva-amadoz2014a, p. 109: Benammi and colleagues (2005) found hadrosaur remains "in the same stratigraphic column (La Barranca de los Bonetes) where the aforementioned palynological samples were collected", which "attributed these deposits to the Late Cretaceous in a broad sense"; the association "was restudied in greater detail by Benammi et al. (2005)" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *A late Santonian age for the base, from the 84 Ma flow.* Martini and colleagues give the flow as "a possible maximum age", and a lava flow in the basal beds is a limit, not an age, under the rule in [methods](../../methods.md).

**Open.** Which stages the formation takes. Its Santonian rests on Ramírez-Velasco (2022), who gives the *Huehuecanauhtlus* horizon as "from the Late Santonian" after Ramírez-Velasco and colleagues (2012); that paper assigns the horizon to an unnamed formation and takes the Santonian from radiometric data and magnetostratigraphy it cites rather than reports. Neither the Maastrichtian of the lower part nor the Paleocene or earliest Eocene of the upper part that Martini and colleagues give is among the formation's stages. The primary report of the hadrosaur remains and the restudied pollen at La Barranca de los Bonetes, Benammi and colleagues (2005), bears on both questions.

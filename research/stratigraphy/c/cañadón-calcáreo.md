# Cañadón Calcáreo

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Cañadón Calcáreo?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Late Jurassic, Oxfordian to Tithonian. Cúneo and colleagues (2013) date tuffs at the top of the lower lacustrine section at Puesto el Quemado and Puesto Almada by U–Pb at 157.449 and 157.387 Ma, as (maximum) depositional ages, and put the start of deposition before about 158 Ma, probably in the Oxfordian. They place the upper fossil interval essentially in the Kimmeridgian with the early Tithonian not ruled out, and judge an extension beyond the Late Jurassic unlikely. Rauhut and Pol (2017) give "Late Jurassic (Oxfordian–Tithonian)" in the abstract and "Oxfordian–?Tithonian" in the text; Carballido and colleagues (2011) give "most probably Oxfordian-Tithonian". On the ICS chart v2024/12 the Kimmeridgian begins at 154.8 Ma, so the 157.4 Ma tuffs lie in the Oxfordian. Rauhut and Pol's "latest Oxfordian" for the date, and Reutter and colleagues' (2026) "Oxfordian-Kimmeridgian boundary", match the GTS 2012 scale that Cúneo and colleagues draw in their Fig. 3, on which the Kimmeridgian began at 157.3 ± 1.0 Ma. The rocks at Puesto Almada that other workers call the Puesto Almada Member of the Cañadón Asfalto Formation are Late Jurassic under either name (on the naming, see [*Pandoravenator*](../../genera/P/Pandoravenator.md) and the dispute on [Cañadón Asfalto](../../../stratigraphy/c/cañadón-asfalto.yml)). Governs `stages: [Oxfordian, Kimmeridgian, Tithonian]` and `period: [Late Jurassic]` on [Cañadón Calcáreo](../../../stratigraphy/c/cañadón-calcáreo.yml).

**Evidence.**
- rauhut2017a, p. 539, Abstract: "A fragmentary postcranial skeleton from the Late Jurassic (Oxfordian–Tithonian) Cañadón Calcáreo Formation".
- rauhut2017a, p. 540, Geological and Palaeontological Setting: "The Oxfordian–?Tithonian Cañadón Calcáreo Formation is a unit of terrestrial sediments that were deposited in the Cañadón Asfalto Basin"; "It represents the post-rift phase of the basin"; it "comprises a mainly lacustrine basal part, followed by fluvial and overbank deposits"; "A tuff at the top of the lacustrine section at Puesto Almada has recently been dated at 157 Ma (latest Oxfordian; Cúneo et al., 2013)"; the section "has been referred to as Puesto Almada Member of the Cañadón Asfalto Formation by other workers ..., but, despite the differences in nomenclature, there is agreement in the Late Jurassic age of these rocks".
- rauhut2017a, p. 541, Geological and Palaeontological Setting: the formation "has yielded an abundant fish fauna from the basal lacustrine part (the Almada fauna ...), including the coccolepid *Condorlepis groeberi* ... and the basal teleost *Luisiella feruglioi*".
- rauhut2017a, p. 562, Conclusions: the Cañadón Calcáreo Formation fauna is "a Late Jurassic assemblage previously represented by diplodocoid and macronarian neosauropods, basal crocodylomorphs, and coccolepid and basal teleosts fishes".
- cúneo2013a, p. 1267 (in-press version, p. 1), Abstract: "The diverse palynofloral assemblage of the Cañadón Calcáreo Formation that was once considered to be early Cretaceous in age, is now entirely constrained to the Late Jurassic".
- cúneo2013a, p. 1269 (in-press version, p. 3), 2. Regional geology and stratigraphy: "The sequence of fluvial to lacustrine sandstones, conglomerates, laminated shales and tuff that overlies the Cañadón Asfalto (and older) strata with an unconformity is known as the Cañadón Calcáreo Formation"; its age "has been estimated to be Late Jurassic on the basis of its vertebrate fossils ..., and Early Cretaceous on the basis of its palynofloral and invertebrate contents (Volkheimer et al., 2009; Gallego et al., 2011)".
- cúneo2013a, pp. 1270–1271 (in-press version, pp. 4–5), 3.1.3. Cañadón Calcáreo Formation: weighted mean dates of "157.449 ± 0.056/0.090/0.19 Ma" (Puesto el Quemado) and "157.387 ± 0.045/0.082/0.19 Ma" (Puesto Almada); "Both dates constrain the (maximum) depositional age of the Cañadón Calcáreo Formation at the top of its lower lacustrine section".
- cúneo2013a, p. 1271 (in-press version, p. 5), Fig. 3: "Jurassic timescale based on GTS 2012", with the Oxfordian–Kimmeridgian boundary at "157.3 ±1.0".
- cúneo2013a, p. 1272 (in-press version, p. 6), 4.1. Geological implications: deposition "started prior to ca. 158 Ma, probably in the Oxfordian, and continued during Late Jurassic time"; "its fossil content ... makes its extension beyond the Late Jurassic (i.e., Kimmeridgian or Tithonian) unlikely".
- cúneo2013a, p. 1273 (in-press version, p. 7), 4.2.5. Late Jurassic faunal assemblages: the upper fossil interval lies about 100 m above the 157.449 Ma tuff, which "constrains the age of the fossil interval essentially to the Kimmeridgian, although an early Tithonian age cannot be ruled out based on the available geochronologic data".
- cúneo2013a, p. 1274 (in-press version, p. 8), 4.2.6. Late Jurassic plant assemblages: "Although the assemblage was considered to be Early Cretaceous in age (Volkheimer et al., 2009), further analyses and new collections ... suggest an older, Late Jurassic (late Oxfordian to early Tithonian) age"; "The Late Jurassic palynofloral age is supported by the new U–Pb geochronologic results from the Cañadón Calcáreo Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Cretaceous.* Cúneo and colleagues (2013) attribute it to Volkheimer and colleagues (2009) and Gallego and colleagues (2011), on the palynoflora and invertebrates; the first is the paper that called the palynoflora Early Cretaceous. Cúneo and colleagues set the two readings out and reject the Cretaceous one: further palynological work gives late Oxfordian to early Tithonian, and the U–Pb dates of about 157.4 Ma at the top of the lower lacustrine section support it.

**Open.** No work cited dates the upper part of the formation directly (Cúneo and colleagues 2013, p. 1272: "no direct age constraints presently exists for the upper Cañadón Calcáreo Formation"), so the Tithonian rests on Carballido and colleagues' (2011) "most probably Oxfordian-Tithonian", Rauhut and Pol's (2017) abstract, and an early Tithonian that Cúneo and colleagues leave open. A date from the upper fluvial section would settle it.

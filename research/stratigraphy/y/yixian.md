# Yixian

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Yixian?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Barremian, as the 2026-10-01 entry concludes; this entry examines the papers that entry's Open line left aside and the earlier argon dates. Zhong and colleagues (2021) name no stage for their bracket. On the ICS chart v2024/12, whose Barremian begins at 125.77 Ma, the onset of 125.755 ± 0.061 Ma is Barremian at its central value but its uncertainty reaches 0.046 Myr into the latest Hauterivian; the rest of the bracket is Barremian. On GTS2020, whose Barremian begins at 126.5 Ma, the whole bracket is Barremian. The "Barremian–Aptian" of Pittman and colleagues (2020) and Tong and colleagues (2021) is a label carried with other works' records, not a dating, and the Hauterivian of Zhou and colleagues (2006) and Jiang and Sha (2006) rests on argon and other radiometric ages that the U-Pb bracket postdates. Governs `stages: [Barremian]` on [Yixian](../../../stratigraphy/y/yixian.yml).

**Evidence.**
- zhong2021a, p. 1: the tuffs were "collected from the top (CY18–20), middle (CY17–17) and bottom (CY17–18 and CY17–9) of the Yixian Formation in the Jin-Yang basin", and the ages bracket "its duration to 1.633 ± 0.078 Myr"; earlier ages "within individual units do not always show a consistency in stratigraphically upward deceasing trend".
- gale2020a, p. 1067: "The Barremian Stage lasted 5.1 Myr from 126.5 ± 0.7 to 121.4 ± 0.6 Ma".
- zhou2006b, p. 101: the type horizon is the "lowest part of the Yixian Formation (Hauterivian, Early Cretaceous)"; "A basalt sample from the lower part of the Jianshangou sequence at Sihetun has yielded a 40Ar/39Ar date of 128.4 ± 0.2 Ma".
- zhou2006b, p. 112: "Because the upper parts of the Lujiatun beds of the Yixian Formation in western Liaoning have been dated as 128.4 ± 0.2 Ma by 40Ar/39Ar radiometric techniques (Wang et al., 2001), the age of these beds appears to be Hauterivian (132.0–127.0 Ma)".
- jiang2006a, p. 215: published ages mostly "range from 119 to 133 Ma (latest Valanginian to early Aptian per the International Stratigraphic Chart (Gradstein et al., 2004)), with a concentration around 125 Ma (Barremian)"; "The age of the Yixian Formation is, therefore, late Hauterivian to early Barremian."
- jiang2006a, p. 210: "Abundant vertebrate fossils have been recovered from what are clearly correlatable beds of the Yixian Formation in the Dawangzhangzi, Daxinfangzi, Songzhangzi, Ershilipu, Fanzhangzi, and Shanzhui areas".
- pittman2020c, p. 49: "The Barremian-Aptian Yixian Formation and Aptian Jiufotang Formation of northeastern China, which yield part of the Jehol Biota"; p. 42: "the ~125 Ma Yixian Formation in Liaoning province".
- tong2021a, p. 10, Phylogenetic Assignment: "*Manchurochelys* Endo and Shikama, 1942 (Yixian Formation (Barremian–Aptian), Liaoning, China"; p. 12, Table 1: "Yixian Fm. Early Cretaceous (Barremian–Aptian)" for *O. liaoxiensis*, *M. manchoukuoensis* and *X. ningchengensis*, each row with a citation to the work that reported the turtle.
- sereno2010a, p. 28: the type horizon of *Psittacosaurus lujiatunensis* and of *P. major* is "Lujiatun Beds, lowermost Yixian Formation (late Barremian or earliest Albian)".
- sereno2010a, p. 22: "The youngest psittacosaurs in Liaoning (*P. mongoliensis, P. meileyingensis*) lie below the Aptian-Albian boundary (ca. 112 Ma)".
- swisher2002a, p. 136, Abstract: the Bed 6 Sihetun sanidine "gave a mean age of 125.0 ± 0.18 (1SD) ± 0.04 (SE) Ma"; the Hengdaozi tuff "125.0 ± 0.19 (1SD) ± 0.04 (SE) Ma, which is indistinguishable from the Sihetun tuff"; the Tuchengzi Formation tuff "139.4 ± 0.19 (1SD) ± 0.05 (SE) Ma"; dating of the Yixian tuffs points "to a middle Early Cretaceous age".

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian, from Pittman and colleagues (2020) and Tong and colleagues (2021).* Neither paper dates the formation. Pittman and colleagues' only figure for it in the chapter is "~125 Ma", which is Barremian on the current chart; Tong and colleagues' ages accompany turtle records they cite. The U-Pb bracket of Zhong and colleagues (2021) lies in the Barremian.
- *Albian, from Sereno's (2010) type-horizon lines.* "Late Barremian or earliest Albian" is a hedge between stages that are not adjacent and adds none. The same paper places the same beds "straddling the Barremian-Aptian boundary" (p. 22), so "earliest Albian" reads as a slip for earliest Aptian (an inference). Sereno's dates are themselves Barremian on the current chart, as the 2026-10-01 entry records.
- *Hauterivian.* Zhou and colleagues (2006) date the lowest beds Hauterivian on Wang and colleagues' (2001) 40Ar/39Ar date of 128.4 ± 0.2 Ma, which they give on p. 101 as from the lower Jianshangou sequence at Sihetun and on p. 112 as from the upper Lujiatun beds. Jiang and Sha (2006) read a late Hauterivian to early Barremian age from a compilation of Ar–Ar, K–Ar, U–Pb and Rb–Sr ages, placed on the chart of Gradstein and colleagues (2004). Both are superseded by the U-Pb bracket of Zhong and colleagues (2021), who find the earlier ages inconsistent within units. That bracket's onset reaches the Hauterivian only within its uncertainty, at 0.046 Myr, and its central value is Barremian.
- *A Valanginian floor from Swisher and colleagues (2002).* Their 139.4 Ma comes from the underlying Tuchengzi Formation; their Yixian tuffs date to 125.0 Ma, which Zhong and colleagues' U-Pb ages supersede.

**Open.** Nothing beyond the 2026-10-01 entry.

## Do *Graciliraptor* and *Jeholosaurus* have a source placing them in the Lujiatun beds?

*2026-10-10*

**Conclusion.** *Graciliraptor* does; *Jeholosaurus* does not. The Chinese text of the *Graciliraptor* paper places the holotype in the Lujiatun Bed by name, and its English horizon line calls the unit "the lowest member of the Yixian Formation". The *Jeholosaurus* paper gives Lujiatun only as the place, with the horizon as the first section of the lower Yixian. Later work naming *Jeholosaurus* among Lujiatun Beds taxa does so in a faunal list that also names *Dilong* and *Incisivosaurus*, which the entry below leaves without the member, so it does not count. With the member recorded, *Graciliraptor*'s `part: lower` would place it within the bed, which no source does. Answers the open question of the entry below. Governs `location.member: Lujiatun` and the absence of `location.part` on [*Graciliraptor lujiatunensis*](../../../genera/G/Graciliraptor.yml), and the absence of `location.member` on [*Jeholosaurus shangyuanensis*](../../../genera/J/Jeholosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- xu2004a, p. 111 (translated): "The holotype of *Graciliraptor lujiatunensis* comes from the Lujiatun Bed at the base of the Yixian Formation"; the abstract reports the specimen as "from this horizon", the Lujiatun Bed.
- xu2004a, p. 113, Locality and horizon: "Lujiatun, Beipiao City, western Liaoning, China; the lowest member of the Yixian Formation".
- xu2000b, p. 318 (translated): the fossil "comes from the grey-white tuffaceous sandstone of the first section of the lower Yixian Formation at Lujiatun, Shangyuan Town, Beipiao City"; Type locality and horizon: "Lujiatun, Shangyuan, Beipiao City, Liaoning Province, China; Early Cretaceous, Yixian Formation".
- yang2020a, p. 32: "Jeholosaurus shangyuanensis, also from the Lujiatun Beds of western Liaoning".

**Ruled out.**
- *`member: Lujiatun` on Jeholosaurus.* Its describing paper names Lujiatun only as the place. Yang and colleagues (2020) state the unit for the taxon, not for the holotype, in the same list of Lujiatun Beds taxa that includes *Dilong* and *Incisivosaurus*.
- *`part: lower` on Graciliraptor.* "Base" and "lowest" locate the bed within the formation; with the bed as the member, the position is already recorded.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which Lujiatun-locality species take `member: Lujiatun`?

*2026-10-10*

**Conclusion.** Only *Mei long*, whose describing paper places the specimen in the Lujiatun beds as a unit. The other six name Lujiatun only as a place, with the horizon given as the lowest, basal or lower part of the Yixian Formation or the formation alone, and under the rule applied for [*Dilong*](../../genera/D/Dilong.md) and [*Liaoceratops*](../../genera/L/Liaoceratops.md) a place name matching the unit name is not a member assignment. Governs `location.member` on [*Mei long*](../../../genera/M/Mei.yml) and its absence on [*Incisivosaurus gauthieri*](../../../genera/I/Incisivosaurus.yml), [*Sinovenator changii*](../../../genera/S/Sinovenator.yml), [*Sinusonasus magnodens*](../../../genera/S/Sinusonasus.yml), [*Liaoningvenator curriei*](../../../genera/L/Liaoningvenator.yml), [*Daliansaurus liaoningensis*](../../../genera/D/Daliansaurus.yml) and [*Dilong paradoxus*](../../../genera/D/Dilong.yml).

**Evidence.**
- xu2004c, p. 838: "Lujiatun, Shangyuan, Beipiao City, western Liaoning, China; lowest more fluvial, volcaniclastic beds of Yixian Formation"; p. 840: "Exceptionally well-preserved vertebrate specimens have been collected from the Lujiatun beds over the last few years".
- xu2002d, p. 291: "Lujiatun, Shangyuan, Beipiao City, Liaoning, China; lowest part of Yixian Formation".
- xu2002a, p. 780: "Lujiatun and Yanzigou, Shanyuan, western Liaoning, China; lowest part of Yixian Formation".
- shen2017a, p. 360: "Lujiatun, Shangyuan, Beipiao City, Liaoning Province, China; fluvial and volcaniclastic beds of the basal Yixian Formation".
- xu2004d, p. 680: "Lujiatun, Beipiao, western Liaoning; … fine sand beds of the lower part of the Yixian Formation".
- xu2004b, p. 22: "Lujiatun locality, Beipiao, Liaoning Province, China; Yixian Formation"; shen2017b: "Lujiatun, Beipiao City, Liaoning Province; Yixian Formation, Lower Cretaceous".

**Ruled out.**
- *`member: Lujiatun` on all seven.* For six of them the only link to the unit is the place name.

**Open.** Whether *Graciliraptor* and *Jeholosaurus*, which already carry the member, have a source placing them in the Lujiatun beds.

## Are the Yixian's subdivisions members or beds?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** Members, following Wang and colleagues (2016), who formally raise the Lujiatun, Jianshangou and other beds to member rank. No later source argues against the rank: Chang and colleagues (2017) dispute Wang and colleagues' column, not the rank, and most vertebrate papers simply keep the older word "Bed". Dakangpu and Dawangzhangzi are one horizon (see [Dawangzhangzi](../d/dawangzhangzi.md)). Governs `rank: member` on [Lujiatun](../../../stratigraphy/l/lujiatun.yml), [Jianshangou](../../../stratigraphy/j/jianshangou.yml) and [Dawangzhangzi](../../../stratigraphy/d/dawangzhangzi.yml), and `location.member` on Yixian species records.

**Evidence.**
- wang2016a, p. 113: they "raise the rank of several units traditionally called "beds" to formal member rank"; p. 114: "raise bed names with priority to member rank where they correspond to defensible lithologic units"; p. 115: "We propose to raise the rank of the Lujiatun bed to member"; p. 117, for the Jianshangou: "formally designate it a member", noting that their use "closely corresponds to the use of that name in Wang et al. (2004)".
- yang2020a, p. 1: "reported from the Lujiatun Beds (Yixian Fm, Lower Cretaceous)"; bell2022a, p. 13: "most likely from the Jianshangou Bed, Yixian Formation"; qiu2025a, p. 2: "The Dawangzhangzi bed of Yixian Formation". These keep the older rank word without discussing it.

**Ruled out.**
- *Chang and colleagues (2017) as contesting the rank.* Their objection is to the order of the units: "we disagreed with the new stratigraphic column proposed by Wang et al. (2016), which renamed the Lower Lava Unit as the Xiatulaigou Member and still kept the Lujiatun as the lowest part of the Yixian Formation. The new age data clearly indicate that the Jiangshangou and Lujiatun units were deposited at the same time" (chang2017a, accepted manuscript, p. 12). They write "Unit" throughout without arguing for it.

**Open.** Whether the Lujiatun lies below the Jianshangou or beside it, which Chang and colleagues' dates bear on; that affects the members' order, not their rank.

## Is the Yixian Formation Barremian, or does it reach the Aptian?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Barremian, on U-Pb dates from the Jin-Yang basin; the Aptian readings are older-chart labels for Barremian-age dates, and the Valanginian–Hauterivian readings rest on earlier Ar/Ar ages. Governs `stages: [Barremian]` on Yixian, its members (for example [Dawangzhangzi](../../../stratigraphy/d/dawangzhangzi.yml) and [Lujiatun](../../../stratigraphy/l/lujiatun.yml)) and its species, such as [*Jeholornis curvipes*](../../../genera/J/Jeholornis.yml) and [*Zhongjianosaurus yangi*](../../../genera/Z/Zhongjianosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- zhong2021a, p. 1: the "onset at 125.755 ± 0.061 Ma (CY17–9) and termination at 124.122 ± 0.048 Ma (CY18–20) of the Yixian Formation", from samples "in the Jin-Yang basin". The paper names no stage; Barremian is our placement of these dates on the current chart.

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian.* Lefèvre and colleagues give *J. curvipes* as "lower Aptian" on a date "of 124.6 ± 0.3 Mya" (lefèvre2014a, p. 791, relaying Yang and colleagues 2007); Xu and Qin give *Zhongjianosaurus* "Possibly Yixian Formation, Aptian, Cretaceous (Swisher et al., 2001)" (xu2017a, p. 130); Sereno (2010) places the beds "no older than 123–125 Ma, straddling the Barremian-Aptian boundary (Gradstein et al. 2004)" (sereno2010a, p. 22). These dates fall in the Barremian on the current chart; that the other papers used the older Aptian base is an inference, since only Sereno names his timescale.
- *Valanginian–Hauterivian.* Jin and colleagues give "older than 128 million years and younger than 139 million years (Lower Valanginian–Lower Barremian; Swisher et al., 2002)" (jin2012a), from earlier Ar/Ar work; Zhong and colleagues note that earlier ages are inconsistent "between different dating methods (i.e. 40Ar/39Ar and U-Pb …)".
- *A radiometric "Zhou et al. 2007".* Sereno cites "(He at al. 2006; Zhou et al. 2007)" (p. 22), but his only Zhou 2007 is a psittacosaur CT paper; his reference list has an uncited Zhu and colleagues (2007) Ar/Ar dating paper, probably the one meant (our inference).

**Open.** The papers that still give "Barremian–Aptian" (for example Pittman and colleagues 2020) are not separately examined here. *Yutyrannus*'s formation rests on a dealer's account given only in its supplement: "acquired … from a fossil dealer", "This strongly suggests that the specimens were collected from the Yixian Formation" (xu2012a, supplementary information, p. 2, §1).

## Does the Yongkang keep the Turonian?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No; the Turonian is dropped. Supersedes the 2026-09-30 entry on the stages and answers the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Luo and Yu's (2004) 113–90 Ma, "Aptian 期到 Turonian 期", takes its younger end from relayed ages of the Xiaoxiong Formation, which they place above the Chaochuan and leave outside the group they define as the Guantou, Chaochuan and Fangyan. Li and colleagues' (2014) younger end of 91 Ma rests on Tiantai Basin tuffs dated by He and colleagues (2012), in a sequence they call "equivalent to the upper Yongkang Group"; their own youngest date for the group is 104 ± 2 Ma. Both are second-hand, and the second is the age of a correlative. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian, Cenomanian]`, the notes on luo2004a and li2014c and the pointer to he2012a on [Yongkang](../../../stratigraphy/y/yongkang.yml); its genera, from the Chaochuan, and the Chaochuan itself are unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- luo2004a, p. 396: "朝川组之上小雄组粗面岩、石英粗面岩和碱长流纹岩,同位素年龄为 90~91.4 Ma。永康群同位素年龄在 113~90 Ma 间。即相当于 Aptian 期到 Turonian 期。" ["the trachyte, quartz trachyte and alkali-feldspar rhyolite of the Xiaoxiong Formation above the Chaochuan Formation give isotopic ages of 90–91.4 Ma. The isotopic ages of the Yongkang Group thus lie between 113 and 90 Ma, corresponding to the Aptian through the Turonian", translated].
- li2014c, p. 1612: "six younger interbedded tuff layers yield zircon U-Pb ages of 99–91 Ma (Figure 3C, He et al. 2013)".
- li2014c, p. 1618: the central and southern Tiantai Basin sequence of "coarse sandstones, 104–91 Ma ignimbrites, and Fangyan conglomerates", after Cui and colleagues (2010) and He and colleagues (2012), is "equivalent to the upper Yongkang Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *Turonian from Luo and Yu (2004).* The Xiaoxiong lies above the group they define, and its ages are relayed.
- *Turonian from Li and colleagues (2014).* A relayed date from a unit they correlate with the group, in another basin.

**Open.** A date from the Fangyan Formation, the group's top.

## How do Luo and Yu (2004) trace the Yongkang Group's name and date its formations?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Luo and Yu (2004) trace the name to the Yongkang Formation that the Zhejiang Petroleum Geological Team established in the Yongkang Basin in 1959. The Zhejiang Regional Geological Survey Brigade renamed its Guantou and Chaochuan members as formations in 1962 and re-established its Fangyan Conglomerate as the Fangyan Formation; Ju Tianyin, Chen Qishi and others then grouped the three as the Yongkang Group, and the brigade later added the Keshan Formation of the Xinchang Basin and the Xiaopingtian Formation of the Xianju Basin. Luo and Yu use the group for the Guantou, Chaochuan and Fangyan only. They give volcanic rocks of the Guantou ages of 113 to 103 Ma and those of the Chaochuan 101 to 96 Ma, and take the group's 90 Ma end from the Xiaoxiong Formation, which overlies the Chaochuan in coastal basins; the group-wide 113–90 Ma bracket and its Aptian–Turonian reading are in the 2026-09-30 entry. Governs `stages` on [Yongkang](../../../stratigraphy/y/yongkang.yml) and the group's constituent formations, among them the `parent` of [Chaochuan](../../../stratigraphy/c/chaochuan.yml).

**Evidence.**
- luo2004a, p. 395: the group's name "derives from the Yongkang Formation established in the Yongkang Basin by the Zhejiang Petroleum Geological Team (1959)"; "The Zhejiang Regional Geological Survey Brigade (1962) renamed the Guantou and Chaochuan members the Guantou Formation and Chaochuan Formation, and reestablished the Fangyan Conglomerate as the Fangyan Formation"; "Ju Tianyin, Chen Qishi and others then grouped these three formations together as the Yongkang Group" (translated).
- luo2004a, p. 395: the brigade "also assigned the Keshan Formation, established in the Xinchang Basin, and the Xiaopingtian Formation, established in the Xianju Basin, to the Yongkang Group"; "the Yongkang Group as used in this paper includes the Guantou, Chaochuan and Fangyan formations" (translated).
- luo2004a, p. 396: "Nine samples from the Guantou Formation give ages between 113 and 103 Ma"; "The volcanic rocks of the Chaochuan Formation give ages of 101–96 Ma"; the volcanic rocks of the Xiaoxiong Formation "above the Chaochuan Formation give isotopic ages of 90–91.4 Ma" (translated).

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Luo and Yu (2004) do not say whether the Xiaoxiong Formation, which supplies the 90 Ma end of their bracket, belongs to the Yongkang Group, which they restrict to the Guantou, Chaochuan and Fangyan. A dating of the Fangyan, or a statement placing the Xiaoxiong in or out of the group, would settle whether the group's own rocks reach the Turonian.

## Where does the Yongkang Group's Aptian come from?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** From group-level isotopic ages, not from a child formation. Governs `stages` (Aptian–Turonian) on Yongkang, and keeps the [Chaochuan](../../../stratigraphy/c/chaochuan.yml)'s range separate.

**Evidence.**
- luo2004a, p. 396: "永康群同位素年龄在 113~90 Ma 间。即相当于 Aptian 期到 Turonian 期" ("the isotopic ages of the Yongkang Group lie between 113 and 90 Ma, corresponding to the Aptian through the Turonian", translated); the Guantou Formation is "110 Ma, 正好属于 Aptian 期" ("110 Ma, exactly in the Aptian", translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *The Chaochuan Formation as the source.* Pond and colleagues write "both from the Albian–Aptian Chaochuan Formation" (pond2023a, p. 38), giving the range without evidence for either stage.

**Open.** The Chaochuan's own age, which the registry records as disputed.

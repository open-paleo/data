# Yan'an

## What age do the cited works give the Yan'an Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Late Toarcian to Bajocian. Xu and colleagues (2018) note that the formation itself has no radiometric date, and bracket it by its biota, by dated volcanics beneath correlated units in Beijing and Liaoning, and by its place between the Fuxian Formation below and the Zhiluo and Anding formations above. The published stage estimates they relay run from late Toarcian (narrowest at the base) to Aalenian–Bajocian, and the narrower dates of the other works cited, Aalenian–Bajocian in Dai and colleagues (2022) and Aalenian in Mannion and colleagues (2025), fall inside the range. Xu and colleagues also record that the formation has been divided into four or five members. Governs `stages: [Toarcian, Aalenian, Bajocian]` and `period: [Early Jurassic, Middle Jurassic]` on [Yan'an](../../../stratigraphy/y/yan-an.yml).

**Evidence.**
- xu2018b, Supplementary Note 2, p. 4: "The formation has been divided into four or five members".
- xu2018b, Supplementary Note 2, p. 4: "no radiometric constraints have been obtained for the Yanan Formation itself", and its age "has been estimated on the basis of biostratigraphy and its relationships to under- and overlying units".
- xu2018b, Supplementary Note 2, p. 4: volcanics beneath the correlated Yaopo and Beipiao formations, dated at 191–180 Ma, are "consistent with the late Toarcian age for the Yanan Formation" and suggest "it is no older than this", but do "not rule out an Aalenian or younger age".
- xu2018b, Supplementary Note 2, p. 4: the underlying Fuxian Formation has "a Pliensbachian–early Toarcian age" in more recent studies.
- xu2018b, Supplementary Note 2, p. 5: "it would be unlikely for the Yanan Formation to be much older than late Toarcian given the radiometric constraints and the age of the Fuxian Formation, and equally improbable for it to be younger than Bajocian given the requirement to fit the overlying Zhiluo, and potentially also Anding, formations into the late Middle Jurassic".
- xu2018b, Supplementary Note 2, p. 5: published estimates include "Toarcian–Aalenian", "narrowed to late Toarcian", and "Aalenian–Bajocian based on conchostracan biostratigraphy"; tectonic studies also support "an Aalenian–Bajocian age".

**Ruled out.**
- *Older than late Toarcian.* Xu and colleagues (2018) rule it out from the 191–180 Ma volcanics beneath correlated units and the Pliensbachian–early Toarcian age of the underlying Fuxian Formation.
- *Bathonian or younger.* Xu and colleagues (2018) rule it out because the overlying Zhiluo, and possibly the Anding, must fit into the late Middle Jurassic above it.

**Open.** No radiometric date on the formation itself; one would narrow the range. The stage estimates Xu and colleagues relay come from earlier Chinese studies that none of the works cited here quotes; those studies would show what evidence each estimate rests on.

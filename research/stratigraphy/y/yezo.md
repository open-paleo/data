# Yezo

## Does the Yezo Group take in the Hakobuchi beds, and what do Matsumoto and Obata's correlation units add to its age?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The group takes in the Hakobuchi beds under Takashima and colleagues' (2004) scheme. Their Hakobuchi Formation, erected in that paper as the top of the group, "corresponds to the Hakobuchi Group" of Matsumoto (1942), which earlier schemes kept as a separate group above the Yezo. Matsumoto and Obata (1979) give the group's age in Japanese correlation units, "K3–K5, part of K6a", and their text ties stages to K6a1 alone, in the appendix on the *Nipponosaurus* horizon. Their Fig. 1 sets the K1a–K6b columns over a row of stages, cell for cell from K1a over the Berriasian to K6b over the Maastrichtian; read that way, K3–K5 is Aptian to Santonian, inside the stages the group already carries, so the paper adds no stage. Governs `parent: Yezo` on [Hakobuchi](../../../stratigraphy/h/hakobuchi.yml), and the absence of any stage from Matsumoto and Obata on [Yezo](../../../stratigraphy/y/yezo.yml).

**Evidence.**
- takashima2004a, p. 383, 3.9. Hakobuchi Formation (new): "This formation is defined by the predominance of HCS and TCS sandstones and conglomerates, and corresponds to the Hakobuchi Group of Matsumoto (1942)."
- takashima2004a, p. 368, 3. Stratigraphy of the Yezo Group: "this group basically consists of a monotonous sequence of sandstone and mudstone and their alternating beds".
- hayakawa2005a, p. 240: the Cretaceous strata "include those of the Yezu Group (Upper Albian-Santonian) and the Hakobuchi Group (Campanian-Paleocene)".
- matsumoto1979a, p. 47: from "the marine Yezo Group (K3–K5, part of K6a), the Izumi Group (K6), the Choshi Cretaceous (K2, K3a), and the Miyako Group (K3a to part of K3b)", plant fossils, pollen and other non-marine fossils are "sometimes admixed" (translated). This is the only passage that names the group.
- matsumoto1979a, p. 46, Fig. 1: the head of the chart runs "K1a" to "K6b" across the Early and Late Cretaceous, and its foot runs "Ber." to "Maas.", with K3a and K3b over "Apt." and "Alb." and K5a and K5b over "Con." and "San.".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Hakobuchi as a separate group above the Yezo.* The scheme Hayakawa and colleagues (2005) still give, with the Yezo ending in the Santonian. Takashima and colleagues (2004) make the Hakobuchi a formation of the Yezo Group.

**Open.** Whether Matsumoto and Obata intend Fig. 1 as an equivalence of the K units with stages, or only as a schematic time axis. On either reading the paper adds no stage to the group.

## Does the Yezo Group take the Danian?

*2026-10-03*

Supersedes the 2026-09-30 entry's closing clause: the stage list does include the Danian, so its absence here rests on the evidence below, not on the list.

**Conclusion.** No. The group's top formation reaches the Paleocene, but no source places the Cretaceous–Paleogene boundary within it, so the period includes the Paleocene and the stages stop at the Maastrichtian. Governs the absence of `Danian` from the stages of Yezo and of the [Hakobuchi Formation](../../../stratigraphy/h/hakobuchi.yml).

**Evidence.**
- takashima2004a, p. 384: "The Cretaceous/Palaeogene boundary sequence has not been detected in the Hakobuchi Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Danian from a stated span.* A span such as "Lower Campanian–Paleocene" (takashima2004a, p. 365) gives the period, not a stage; a unit takes the Danian only when a source places the boundary within it.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does the Yezo Group reach the Paleocene?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Yes, under Takashima and colleagues' (2004) scheme, which runs from the Lower Aptian Soashibetsugawa Formation to the Lower Campanian–Paleocene [Hakobuchi Formation](../../../stratigraphy/h/hakobuchi.yml). Governs `period: [Early Cretaceous, Late Cretaceous, Paleocene]` and `stages` (Aptian–Maastrichtian) on Yezo; the Paleocene part has no stage in the registry's stage list.

**Evidence.**
- takashima2004a, p. 365, abstract: "the Hakobuchi Formation (Lower Campanian–Paleocene shallow-marine sandstone-conglomerate unit)" at the top and "the Soashibetsugawa Formation (Lower Aptian mudstone unit)" at the base.

**Ruled out.**
- *An upper Albian base.* Earlier schemes started the group in the upper Albian (as compared in hayakawa2005a); Takashima and colleagues' base is Lower Aptian.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Escucha

## What do the primary biostratigraphic papers give for the age of the Escucha?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Late Aptian to early Albian. De Gea and colleagues (2008) date the basal Escucha at Alcaine to the upper part of the late Aptian from nannofossils and planktonic foraminifera. Peyrot and colleagues (2007a) give the miospores of their Oliete Sub-basin sections, up to a sample at the top of the formation, an upper Aptian age, and the dinocysts of their lowest level an Upper Aptian–Lower Albian age; Peyrot and colleagues (2007b) relay the same range. The late Aptian that Kirkland and colleagues (2013) relay now has primary sources; the Albian rests on the dinocyst range and on the older Lower–Middle Albian readings Peyrot and colleagues report from earlier palynology. Both Peyrot papers' age charts (Fig. 1c) draw the formation into the middle Albian, higher than their text dates it; the text, which states the evidence, decides. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on [Escucha](../../../stratigraphy/e/escucha.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- degea2008a, p. 116: "the basal part of the Escucha Fm. in the Alcaine sector corresponds to the upper part of the late Aptian" (translated).
- peyrot2007a, p. 135: "suggests an Upper Aptian age for the studied material"; pp. 135–136: "indicate an Upper Aptian–Lower Albian age for the oldest level studied".
- peyrot2007a, p. 140: "sample BC was obtained from unit E3 (Fig. 2) at the top of the Escucha Formation".
- peyrot2007b, p. 473: "new palynological data from this unit in the Oliete Sub-basin permits to consider an Upper Aptian–Lower Albian age for these deposits".

**Ruled out.**
- *Middle Albian, from the Fig. 1c charts.* The charts are drawn without stated evidence and outrun the ages the same papers derive in their text.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do Kirkland and colleagues (2013) say about the age of the Escucha?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Kirkland and colleagues (2013) report the earlier reading of the formation as early to middle Albian, between Aptian strata below and the upper Albian Utrillas Formation above, and set against it a late Aptian for the lower Escucha from calcareous plankton and palynomorphs and an Albian for the upper part northeast of Teruel from marine ostracods. Each is relayed from the works it cites, not determined in the paper, and counts once under its original. They follow a division of the formation in two by a regional unconformity, in place of the three members it was first divided into. For the *Europelta* site at Ariño, in the lower Escucha, they report a palynomorph, ostracod and charophyte analysis that puts it "completely" in the early Albian. Governs `Aptian` and `Albian` in `stages` on [Escucha](../../../stratigraphy/e/escucha.yml), and `stage: [Albian]` on [*Europelta carbonensis*](../../../genera/E/Europelta.yml).

**Evidence.**
- kirkland2013a, p. 1, Abstract: "the lower Albian carbonaceous member of the Escucha Formation near Ariño in northeastern Teruel, Spain".
- kirkland2013a, p. 2, Geological Setting: "The Formación Lignitos de Escucha and overlying Formación Arenas de Utrillas were initially described in 1971".
- kirkland2013a, p. 2, Geological Setting: "Initially, the Escucha Formation was divided into three members"; "the upper ''fluvial'' member has been reinterpreted as an eolian depositional sequence separated from the underlying portions of the Escucha Formation by a regional unconformity. We recognize this bipartite division".
- kirkland2013a, p. 2, Geological Setting: "The geologic age of the Escucha Formation has been considered to be early to middle Albian. It overlies Aptian strata in central basinal settings and is, in turn, overlain by the upper Albian Utrillas Formation".
- kirkland2013a, p. 2, Geological Setting: "both calcareous plankton (foraminifera and nanoplankton) and palynomorphs indicate that the lower Escucha Formation is late Aptian in age".
- kirkland2013a, p. 2, Geological Setting: "Marine ostracods have been reported from the upper Escucha Formation northeast of Teruel that confirm an Albian age for the upper portion of these strata in this area".
- kirkland2013a, p. 8, Locality and Horizon: "a plant debris bed in the lower Escucha Formation"; "Elsewhere, the Escucha Formation has been interpreted as late Aptian to early Albian in age"; "An analysis of the palynomorphs, ostracods, and charophytes from AR-1 indicates that the site is completely of early Albian age".
- mcdonald2012a, p. 61: "lower Albian beds in the upper Aptian–lower Albian Escucha Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *A late Aptian age for the* Europelta *site.* The bonebed lies in the lower Escucha, which the plankton and palynomorphs date late Aptian elsewhere, but the analysis of the site itself places it in the early Albian. A statement about the horizon decides over one about the formation.
- *An Albian-only formation.* The early to middle Albian is the reading the paper reports as the earlier view and then qualifies with the late Aptian of the lower Escucha, and McDonald and colleagues (2012) give the formation as "upper Aptian–lower Albian".

**Open.** Nothing.

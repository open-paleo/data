# Enciso

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Enciso Group?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Late Barremian to early Aptian, possibly reaching the earliest late Aptian at the top. Suarez-Gonzalez and colleagues (2013) date the group directly: they found marine microfossils in two levels of the Enciso facies near Soto en Cameros, at levels that correlate with the uppermost Leza Formation, and place the Leza, the Jubera and the Enciso in one depositional sequence of late Barremian to early Aptian age. The lower bound comes from the dasycladale Salpingoporella urladanasi (Barremian–Albian) and the upper from the foraminifer cf. Istriloculina (Berriasian–Aptian, mostly Barremian–Aptian); the early Aptian is refined by correlating the transgressive upper Leza with the early Aptian sea-level peak. Suarez-Gonzalez and colleagues (2015) date only the Leza Formation, "upper Barremian–lower Aptian"; they state the lateral passage between the Leza and the Enciso in both directions, so their age reaches the group only through that equivalence and constrains only its Leza-equivalent part, leaving the group free to reach higher than the Leza does. One of the paper's four uses of "Enciso" is the town, in a figure-legend key, not the unit. Isasmendi and colleagues (2024), after the two Suarez-Gonzalez papers and Hernán's (2018) thesis, give the group as latest Barremian to early Aptian, mostly early Aptian, with "likely lowermost upper Aptian deposits" at the top; the upper Aptian is still Aptian, so it adds no stage. Governs `stages: [Barremian, Aptian]` on [Enciso](../../../stratigraphy/e/enciso.yml).

**Evidence.**
- suarez-gonzalez2013a, p. 297: "we have also re-examined the microfossil content of the Enciso Gr facies in the area surrounding Soto en Cameros"; "we have found previously unrecorded marine microfossils (dasycladales and foraminifers) in two levels of the Enciso Gr"; they lie "in levels stratigraphically correlatable with the uppermost part of the Leza River section of the Leza Fm".
- suarez-gonzalez2013a, p. 300: the data "support the assignment of an upper Barremian-lower Aptian age for the Enciso Gr", since the dasycladales "have a Barremian-Albian distribution" and the foraminifers (cf. Istriloculina) "have a Berriasian-Aptian distribution, but are most commonly cited in Barremian-Aptian deposits".
- suarez-gonzalez2013a, p. 306: "the Leza Fm, the Jubera Fm and the Enciso Gr belong to the same depositional sequence, upper Barremian - lower Aptian in age"; the upper Leza "can be correlated with the highest sea-level peak of the late Barremian-early Aptian period, which is widely observed in the middle-upper part of the early Aptian".
- suarez-gonzalez2013a, p. 288: earlier workers "assigned a Berriasian age to the Enciso Gr", "dated the Enciso Gr as Hauterivian-Barremian", "redefined the age of the Enciso Gr as early Valanginian", and "further refined the age of the Enciso Gr, using ostracods and charophytes, as Barremian".
- suarez-gonzalez2013a, p. 289: "the Leza Fm is part of the Enciso Gr, which is upper Barremian-lower Aptian in age"; Schudack and Schudack (2009) changed the ostracod biostratigraphy, "considering the Enciso Gr as upper Valanginian-Barremian in age".
- suarez-gonzalez2013a, p. 291, Fig. 5: "Comparative chronostratigraphic chart summarising the different ages and lithostratigraphic frameworks that have been proposed".
- schudack2009a, p. 151: "the middle and upper parts of the Enciso group are Hauterivian-Barremian in age, most probably Barremian in its upper part, but not Aptian"; "we prefer to propose a Valanginian age for the lower part of the Enciso group".
- suarez-gonzalez2015a, p. 97: "The Leza Formation is a mixed carbonate–clastic unit, upper Barremian–lower Aptian in age"; "the Leza Fm changes laterally, towards the south, to the overlying Enciso Group" (Fig. 1); "Towards the NE margin of the basin, the Enciso Group changes laterally to the Leza Fm".
- suarez-gonzalez2015a, p. 114, figure legend: "E, Enciso", among the "initials of main towns of the area".
- isasmendi2024a, p. 3, Geographical and geological setting: "recent studies date the Enciso Group from the latest Barremian to the early Aptian, with most of the unit being early Aptian in age, with likely lowermost upper Aptian deposits in the uppermost part of the group".
- isasmendi2024a, p. 5, Systematic palaeontology: "the type locality is most likely to be lower Aptian within the Enciso Group"; p. 2, Introduction: "the Early Cretaceous Enciso Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *Berriasian, early Valanginian, Hauterivian to Barremian, or Barremian alone.* Readings from 1966 to 1987, on ostracods, mapping and charophytes, that Suarez-Gonzalez and colleagues (2013) tabulate and replace with the late Barremian to early Aptian on their microfossils and sequence framework.
- *Valanginian to Barremian, not Aptian.* Schudack and Schudack's (2009) revised ostracod biostratigraphy, the one dissent after 2000 among the works cited. Suarez-Gonzalez and colleagues (2013) cite it and place the whole group in the late Barremian to early Aptian sequence, and Isasmendi and colleagues (2024) and Rauhut and colleagues (2025) follow the Barremian–Aptian.

**Open.** The marine microfossils lie at levels correlated with the uppermost Leza, in the upper part of the group, and their ranges allow the Barremian; they do not test Schudack and Schudack's Valanginian for the lower part of the group. That exclusion rests on the depositional-sequence framework that puts the whole group in one late Barremian to early Aptian sequence. A dating of the lower Enciso would settle it.

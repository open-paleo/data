# Ejinhoro

## Is the *Sinornithoides* site at Huamuxiao in the Ejinhoro Formation?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** Yes. Russell and Dong (1993) report the exposures west of the village "locally referred to the Ejinhoroqi Formation". Russell and Zhao (1996), who also make Huamuxiao the type locality of *Psittacosaurus ordosensis*, put the red sandstones and greyish mudstones near the village in the Ejinhoro, while noting that the locality is mapped in the Zhidan Group; they find its sequence closer to the map's description of the Ejinhoro. It is the Yangpo case of the 2026-11-23 entry, with the same answer. Governs `location.formation: Ejinhoro` on [*Sinornithoides youngi*](../../../genera/S/Sinornithoides.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- russell1993a, p. 2170: "The exposures are locally referred to the Ejinhoroqi Formation."
- russell1996a, p. 645: "The locality is mapped as occurring within the Zhidan Group, although the sequence appears to conform better to 'brick red with greyish green clastics' referred to the Ejinhoro Formation".

**Ruled out.** - *The Zhidan Group, from the regional map.* Russell and Zhao record the mapping and still assign the beds to the Ejinhoro, and no work cited moves them.

**Open.** How the Ejinhoro and the Zhidan Group are drawn in the Ordos Basin, as in the 2026-11-23 entry.

## Is the Yangpo locality of *Psittacosaurus neimongoliensis* in the Ejinhoro Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Russell and Zhao (1996) assign the locality near Yangpo village to the Ejinhoro Formation, after Dong's (1993) description of *Wuerhosaurus ordosensis*, while noting that its greenish-yellow siltstones and reddish sandstones do not match the regional map's characterization of the unit as "brick red with greyish green clastics". They find that facies instead at localities the map places in the Zhidan Group. They keep the Ejinhoro assignment, and none of the works cited for the unit moves the locality to another unit. They give the formation no age. Governs `location.formation` of *Psittacosaurus neimongoliensis* on [*Psittacosaurus*](../../../genera/P/Psittacosaurus.yml); it governs no stage on [Ejinhoro](../../../stratigraphy/e/ejinhoro.yml).

**Evidence.**
- russell1996a, p. 638: "Ejinhoro Formation (Dong 1993, p. 2175), on east–west-trending ridge of greenish-yellow siltstones intercalated with reddish, locally chaotically bedded, ledge-forming sandstones; 1 km southeast of Yangpo village".
- russell1996a, p. 638: "Strata do not appear to conform to characterization of Ejinhoro Formation as \"brick red with greyish green clastics\"".
- russell1996a, p. 638: "a partial skeleton of a stegosaur (Wuerhosaurus ordosensis Dong, 1993), sauropod teeth (cf. Chiayusaurus sp.), and pterosaur bones".
- russell1996a, p. 645: "The locality is mapped as occurring within the Zhidan Group, although the sequence appears to conform better to \"brick red with greyish green clastics\" referred to the Ejinhoro Formation" (Huamuxiao, the *P. ordosensis* type locality).
- russell1996a, p. 645: "The locality is mapped within the Zhidan Group, although lithologically it more closely resembles \"brick red with greyish green clastics\"" (Ahlingbola).
- russell1996a, p. 646: the Luohandong Formation sandstones "resemble the \"greyish green clastic\" facies of the Ejinhoro Formation more closely than the \"rhythmic clastics with marl\" of the Zhidan Group".
- russell1996a, p. 646: "The age of most of these new psittacosaur occurrences is uncertain."
- dong1993b, p. 2175: "All specimens were collected in the Lower Cretaceous Ejinhoro Formation (Dong 1992) of the Ordos Basin, Inner Mongolia".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Yangpo beds belong to another unit.* The mismatch with the map's description could suggest it, but Russell and Zhao (1996) record the mismatch and still assign the beds to the Ejinhoro, and their own survey finds the map's Ejinhoro facies at three localities mapped in the Zhidan Group, so the map's lithological description does not sort the Ordos beds between the two units. None of the works cited for the unit assigns the locality elsewhere.
- *A Barremian–Albian age for the formation from Russell and Zhao (1996).* Their statement that "Records of Psittacosaurus in China, Mongolia and Siberia appear to be Barremian to Albian in age" (p. 646) is the range of the genus across Asia, not a dating of the Ejinhoro; of their own Ordos occurrences they say only that the age of most is uncertain.

**Open.** How the Ejinhoro and the Zhidan Group are drawn in the Ordos Basin. The regional geology of Nei Mongol (Bureau of Geology and Mineral Resources of Nei Mongol Autonomous Region, 1991), whose map Russell and Zhao quote, would settle it; it has not been read.

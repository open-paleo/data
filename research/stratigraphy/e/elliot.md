# Elliot

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Elliot?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Norian to Pliensbachian, as the 2026-09-30 entry concludes; this entry records the readings behind it. The lower Elliot's Carnian rests on two arguments: Olsen and Galton (1984) cite a traversodont cynodont, a rauisuchid, and the unit's lateral passage into gray Molteno beds whose lower part yielded Carnian plants. Harris and colleagues (2002) show that the traversodont argument rests on a mistaken assumption, the Norian readings of Lucas and Hancox (2001) and Harris and colleagues (2002) follow, and the detrital-zircon ages of Bordy and colleagues (2020) put the lower Elliot in the middle Norian to Rhaetian. Galton and Upchurch (2004) list the Carnian readings beside the Norian ones without choosing between them. The upper Elliot's Hettangian–Sinemurian is the magnetostratigraphic correlation of Sciscio and colleagues (2017), which Bordy and colleagues (2020) adopt without being able to test it; Olsen and Galton (1984) gave the upper Elliot and Clarens together Hettangian–Pliensbachian. Bordy and colleagues give the whole formation about 30 million years, from about 220 to about 190 Ma. Governs `period: [Late Triassic, Early Jurassic]` and `stages: [Norian, Rhaetian, Hettangian, Sinemurian, Pliensbachian]` on [Elliot](../../../stratigraphy/e/elliot.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton2004a, p. 232: "The earliest prosauropod fauna is from the Lower Elliot Formation (age as Carnian, Shubin and Sues 1991; Carnian and/or Norian, Olsen and Galton 1984; Norian, Lucas and Hancox 2001; early to mid-Norian, Harris et al. 2002)"; p. 253: "*Euskelosaurus browni*, from the late Carnian or early Norian of southern Africa".
- olsen1984a, p. 90: "Traversodont cynodonts are unknown above beds confidently dated as Carnian"; "There is also a rauisuchid from the lower Elliot"; "the lower Elliot appears to be in part a red, lateral equivalent of the upper gray beds of the Molteno Formation, the lower part of which has produced plant assemblages of Carnian age"; "We conclude that the skeletal remains support a Late Triassic (Carnian and/or Norian) age for this deposit."
- olsen1984a, p. 87, Abstract: "Comparison of the faunal assemblages with those of the European type section strongly suggests that the lower Stormberg assemblage is Late Triassic (Carnian-Norian) in age while the upper Stormberg assemblage is Early Jurassic (Hettangian-Pliensbachian) in age", the upper Stormberg being the upper Elliot and Clarens.
- harris2002a, p. 251: "*Euskelosaurus browni* Huxley, 1866, *Blikanasaurus cromptoni* Galton and Heerden, 1985, and *Melanorosaurus readi* Haughton, 1924, all from the lower Elliot Formation", are "of probable Revueltian (early-mid Norian) age"; "traversodontids are known from strata as young as Rhaetian".
- lucas2001a, p. 5: "Tetrapod fossils suggest the Molteno Formation in southern Africa is no older than late Carnian, whereas the overlying lower Elliot Formation is most likely Norian in age. However, these are not strong correlations, and additional data are needed to confirm correlation of the southern African Upper Triassic strata."
- sciscio2017a, accepted manuscript, p. 1, Highlights: "Global magnetostratigraphic correlations and palaeopole calculations support a Late Triassic-Early Jurassic age for the Elliot Formation."
- sciscio2017a, accepted manuscript, p. 35: "This correlation places the UEF EF6.2r – EF9n as Hettangian–Sinemurian in age", by tying the Elliot magnetozones to the Hartford Basin and to the St. Audrie's Bay/East Quantoxhead composite.
- sciscio2017a, accepted manuscript, p. 8: "Norman et al. (2011) speculated that the age of the UEF may be younger and either spans the Pliensbachian-Toarcian or the upper Sinemurian based on comparative work by Yates et al. (2004) and personal observations presented by Irmis (2010)."
- bordy2020a, p. 1, Abstract: "the first geochronological assessment of the unit across the basin, using U-Pb dates derived from detrital zircons"; "A mainly Pliensbachian age is obtained for the Clarens Formation."
- bordy2020a, p. 4: "The age of the upper boundary of the Stormberg Group succession is provided by the conformably overlying, Toarcian-aged continental flood basalts".
- bordy2020a, p. 13: "the TJB-bearing Elliot Formation represents a ~30-million-year-long depositional episode (from ~220 to ~190 Ma ago)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Carnian, and Carnian or Norian.* Olsen and Galton's (1984) traversodont argument is the one Harris and colleagues (2002) refute for the earlier Carnian placements, and the Molteno plants date the lower Molteno, not the Elliot. Galton and Upchurch's (2004) "late Carnian or early Norian" for *Euskelosaurus* and their list of ages are a compilation, not a dating. Bordy and colleagues' (2020) zircon ages begin the lower Elliot in the middle Norian.
- *Toarcian, for the upper Elliot.* Norman and colleagues (2011) give "Pliensbachian-Toarcian" only as one of two possibilities, after Yates and colleagues (2004), whose own Pliensbachian or Toarcian concerns the *Massospondylus* Range Zone of the upper Elliot and Clarens together. Bordy and colleagues (2020) date the overlying Clarens as mainly Pliensbachian, beneath conformable Toarcian basalts. That the upper Elliot therefore stops short of the Toarcian is an inference from superposition, and it answers the Open question of the 2026-09-30 entry.
- *Upper Sinemurian alone, for the upper Elliot.* Norman and colleagues' preferred reading rests on a personal communication from R. Irmis, which is not a published determination.

**Open.** Whether the Elliot takes a group as its parent. Lucas and Hancox (2001) call it "the now defunct 'Stormberg Group'" (lucas2001a, p. 5) yet write "The lowest formation of the Stormberg Group is the Molteno Formation" (p. 5) and caption their Figure 1 "Stratigraphic distribution of tetrapod taxa in the Stormberg Group of southern Africa" (p. 6); Bordy and colleagues (2020) frame their work as "A chronostratigraphic framework for the upper Stormberg Group". A source on the group's current standing would settle it.

## Is sample Q6's maximum age 191.9 or 191.1 Ma?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** 191.9 ± 1.5 Ma. The 191.1 in Table 1's preferred-MDA column is a slip: it matches none of the table's metrics, and the paper's stated preferred metric, YC2σ(2+), gives 191.9, the figure its text uses. Either value is Pliensbachian on the current chart. Governs the Q6 figure quoted on [Elliot](../../../stratigraphy/e/elliot.yml), [Clarens](../../../stratigraphy/c/clarens.yml) and [*Ngwevu intloko*](../../../genera/N/Ngwevu.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bordy2020a, p. 10: "Our preferred method for estimating the MDA (Table 1) is the weighted mean of the youngest cluster comprised of two or more grains overlapping with the youngest one at 2σ internal error (YC2σ[2+])"; Table 1, Q6: preferred MDA 191.1 ± 1.5, YSG 190 ± 2.4, YC1σ(2+) 193.4 ± 1.2, YC2σ(2+) 191.9 ± 1.5, YC2σ(3+) 467.4 ± 2.5, YPP 193.
- bordy2020a, p. 17: "Q6: <191.9 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *191.1 Ma.* In every other row the preferred value equals one of the metric columns, usually YC2σ(2+); Q6's 191.1 equals none, and reads as 191.9 with two digits transposed.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What stages does the Elliot Formation span?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Norian–Pliensbachian on the current timescale: the lower Elliot is middle Norian–Rhaetian and the upper Elliot Hettangian–Sinemurian in Bordy and colleagues' (2020) labels, but its uppermost maximum age falls in the Pliensbachian on GTS 2020. Governs `stages` (Norian–Pliensbachian) on Elliot, and the upper Elliot stages of [*Eocursor parvus*](../../../genera/E/Eocursor.yml), [*Antetonitrus ingenipes*](../../../genera/A/Antetonitrus.yml) and [*Ngwevu intloko*](../../../genera/N/Ngwevu.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bordy2020a, p. 1: "a mid-Norian–Rhaetian age for the lower Elliot Formation and a Hettangian–Sinemurian age for the upper Elliot Formation"; p. 4, Fig. 2 caption: the stages follow the "International Chronostratigraphic Chart (v2018/08; ICS, 2018)".
- bordy2020a, p. 17: the uppermost upper Elliot sample is "Q6: <191.9 Ma" (Table 1, p. 10, gives a preferred maximum of 191.1 ± 1.5 Ma); "The new maximum depositional ages in this study do not assist in validating this correlation" with the magnetostratigraphy.
- hesselbo2020a, p. 1001: "The Sinemurian/Pliensbachian boundary is assigned an age of 192.9 Myr". Either Q6 value is Pliensbachian on GTS 2020; that Bordy and colleagues' labels used an older boundary is our inference.

**Ruled out.**
- *Carnian.* Earlier Carnian ages "were based on the mistaken assumption that traversodontid cynodonts … are unknown from strata younger than Carnian" (harris2002a, p. 251).
- *Lower Elliot for Eocursor and Antetonitrus.* They were described from the lower Elliot ("the Late Triassic (?Norian) Lower Elliot Formation", butler2007a, p. 2041; yates2003a, pp. 1753–1754). *Eocursor* "has recently been reinterpreted as coming from the uEF (Olsen et al., 2010; McPhee et al., 2017)" (bordy2020a, p. 8), and *Antetonitrus* is "another large-bodied upper Elliot Formation taxon" (mcphee2018a, in-press version, p. 3).

**Open.** Whether the Toarcian reaches the upper Elliot. Yates and colleagues consider "a Pliensbachian or Toarcian age would seem reasonable" (yates2004b, p. 504) for the whole *Massospondylus* Range Zone, upper Elliot and Clarens together; the overlying Clarens makes a Toarcian upper Elliot unlikely (our inference).

# Elliot

## What stages does the Elliot Formation span?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Norian–Pliensbachian on the current timescale: the lower Elliot is middle Norian–Rhaetian and the upper Elliot Hettangian–Sinemurian in Bordy and colleagues' (2020) labels, but its uppermost maximum age falls in the Pliensbachian on GTS 2020. Governs `stages` (Norian–Pliensbachian) on Elliot, and the upper Elliot stages of [*Eocursor parvus*](../../../genera/E/Eocursor.yml), [*Antetonitrus ingenipes*](../../../genera/A/Antetonitrus.yml) and [*Ngwevu intloko*](../../../genera/N/Ngwevu.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bordy2020a, p. 1: "a mid-Norian–Rhaetian age for the lower Elliot Formation and a Hettangian–Sinemurian age for the upper Elliot Formation"; p. 4, Fig. 2 caption: the stages follow the "International Chronostratigraphic Chart (v2018/08; ICS, 2018)".
- bordy2020a, p. 17: the uppermost upper Elliot sample is "Q6: <191.9 Ma" (Table 1, p. 10, gives a preferred maximum of 191.1 ± 1.5 Ma); "The new maximum depositional ages in this study do not assist in validating this correlation" with the magnetostratigraphy.
- hesselbo2020a, p. 1001: "The Sinemurian/Pliensbachian boundary is assigned an age of 192.9 Myr". Either Q6 value is Pliensbachian on GTS 2020; that Bordy and colleagues' labels used an older boundary is our inference.

**Ruled out.**
- *Carnian.* Earlier Carnian ages "were based on the mistaken assumption that traversodontid cynodonts … are unknown from strata younger than Carnian" (harris2002a, p. 251).
- *Lower Elliot for Eocursor and Antetonitrus.* They were described from the lower Elliot ("the Late Triassic (?Norian) Lower Elliot Formation", butler2007a, p. 2041; yates2003a, pp. 1753–1754). *Eocursor* "has recently been reinterpreted as coming from the uEF (Olsen et al., 2010; McPhee et al., 2017)" (bordy2020a, p. 8), and *Antetonitrus* is "another large-bodied upper Elliot Formation taxon" (mcphee2018a, in-press version, p. 3).

**Open.** Whether the Toarcian reaches the upper Elliot. Yates and colleagues consider "a Pliensbachian or Toarcian age would seem reasonable" (yates2004b, p. 504) for the whole *Massospondylus* Range Zone, upper Elliot and Clarens together; the overlying Clarens makes a Toarcian upper Elliot unlikely (our inference).

# Lonco Trapial

## What readings did the U–Pb dates replace for the Lonco Trapial, and what else do the dating papers say?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Adds to the 2026-10-01 entry on the formation's age and supersedes nothing in it. Before Cúneo and colleagues (2013), the formation was dated Middle to Late Jurassic, 176 to 146 Ma, on K–Ar ages, and Nullo and Proserpio (1975) placed it in the Bajocian–Bathonian on its lithology, flora and radiometric dates. Pol and colleagues (2011) still used a Middle Jurassic Lonco Trapial as the upper age limit on the Las Leoneras Formation, while noting that the flora at its base had been re-dated to the Early Jurassic. Cúneo and colleagues (2013) bracket the formation with U–Pb dates of 188.946 ± 0.096 Ma below and 178.766 ± 0.092 Ma above, and restrict the basal flora at Cañadón del Zaino to the Pliensbachian; Pol and colleagues (2020) date its peak volcanic activity directly and tie it to the Karoo–Ferrar province and the early Toarcian anoxic event. Governs `period: [Early Jurassic]` and `stages: [Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on [Lonco Trapial](../../../stratigraphy/l/lonco-trapial.yml).

**Evidence.**
- nullo1975a, p. 133, Resumen: on the basis of the formations' "lithologic characteristics, floristic content and radiometric datings", the Chon Aike and Lonco Trapial are correlated, "placing them in the Bajocian-Bathonian" (translated).
- cúneo2013a, p. 1269 (in-press version, p. 3), Regional geology and stratigraphy: the formation's "conglomeratic facies and its volcanic-dominated facies have been give alternative names (e.g., Taquetrén Formation, Cerro Carnerero Formation and Cañadón Puelman beds)"; "its age is thought to be Middle to Late Jurassic (176–146 Ma) based on K–Ar geochronology".
- cúneo2013a, p. 1269 (in-press version, p. 3), Las Leoneras Formation and Cañadón Asfalto Formation: a tuff "about 40 m below the basal agglomerates of the overlying Lonco Trapial Formation" gives "a weighted mean 206Pb/238U date of 188.946± 0.096/0.13/0.24 Ma", which "places a maximum limit on the age of the Lonco Trapial Fm."; a tuff in the lower Cañadón Asfalto gives "178.766±0.092/0.13/0.23 Ma", which "also places a minimum limit on the age of the Lonco Trapial Formation".
- cúneo2013a, p. 1271 (in-press version, p. 5), Geological implications: the bracket "is significantly older than the Middle to Late Jurassic ages previously assigned to this unit".
- cúneo2013a, pp. 1272–1273 (in-press version, pp. 6–7), Early Jurassic plant assemblages: for the floras "from the basal strata of the Lonco Trapial Formation at the Cañadón del Zaino locality, Taquetrén Hill", the new dates confirm the Early Jurassic estimate "and, more specifically, restricts them to the Pliensbachian".
- pol2011b, p. 4, Geological Setting: the Las Leoneras Formation "is unconformably covered by andesite and volcaniclastic deposits of the Lonco Trapial Formation (Middle Jurassic; [28–35])".
- pol2011b, p. 5, Systematic Paleontology: "The age of the Las Leoneras Formation is certainly constrained by the Middle Jurassic dating of the volcanic facies of the overlying Lonco Trapial Formation"; "the base of the Lonco Trapial Formation in this region contains sedimentary facies with a well preserved taphoflora that was originally regarded as Middle Jurassic in age [29–30], although new evidence suggests this taphoflora is Early Jurassic in age".
- pol2020a, p. 3, Radioisotopic geochronology: the formation has "a previously determined age bracket of 188.9– 178.8 Ma (Pliensbachian–Toarcian [16,17]) based on tuff ages from the stratigraphically adjacent formations"; the new date "is congruent with previous geochronology [16] and provides a direct age constraint on its peak volcanic activity", indicating "an overall age overlap with the Karoo–Ferrar large igneous province of South Africa–Antarctica", proposed as the trigger of changes "recorded at this time together with marine mass extinctions associated with the early Toarcian oceanic anoxic event (T-OAE)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Middle to Late Jurassic, 176 to 146 Ma, from K–Ar dating.* Cúneo and colleagues (2013) report it as the age the formation was thought to have, and their U–Pb bracket of 188.95 to 178.77 Ma is "significantly older". The K–Ar ages are not U–Pb results and do not survive them.
- *Middle Jurassic, as Pol and colleagues (2011) use it.* The paper takes the Middle Jurassic from the earlier dating literature it cites and uses it only as a ceiling on the Las Leoneras; it already reports that the taphoflora at the base of the formation is Early Jurassic. The U–Pb bracket of Cúneo and colleagues (2013), two years later, places the whole formation in the Early Jurassic.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What is the age of the Lonco Trapial Formation?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Pliensbachian–Toarcian, from U-Pb dates bracketing the formation and one direct date within it. Governs `stages: [Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on Lonco Trapial and on its [Cerro Carnerero Member](../../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-carnerero.yml), and the stages of [*Amygdalodon patagonicus*](../../../genera/A/Amygdalodon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cúneo2013a, p. 1271 (in-press version, p. 5), Geological implications: "the Lonco Trapial Formation is now bracketed between 188.95 Ma and 178.77 Ma, entirely within the Early Jurassic (Pliensbachian–Toarcian)". The two ends are tuffs in the Las Leoneras Formation below and the lower Cañadón Asfalto Formation above (p. 1269).
- pol2020a, p. 3, Radioisotopic geochronology: "Our new U–Pb zircon date of 180.330 ± 0.068 Ma from the massive pyroclastic interval of the Lonco Trapial Formation".
- zaffarana2020a, p. 8, Geochronology: "The U–Pb zircon age of the rhyolite dike of Lonco Trapial Formation (sample G4-231) is 177.26 ± 0.96 Ma". The paper's other dates for the formation are K–Ar (191.6 ± 5.2 Ma) and Ar–Ar (~185 Ma, ~178 Ma); none falls outside the Early Jurassic.

**Ruled out.**
- *Bajocian–Bathonian, or Middle Jurassic generally.* This is Nullo and Proserpio's (1975) age from K–Ar dating, and the paper is not consistent about it: the abstracts give "Bayociano-Bathoniano" (nullo1975a, p. 133) and "Bathonian-Bayocian" (p. 134), while the conclusions give "Bayociano-Caloviano" (p. 148). Along the way the basalt discussion reaches "Aaleniano-eoBayociano" (p. 146), an intermediate step, then rejects a 173 Ma date and arrives at Callovian (p. 147). The U-Pb dates above supersede all of these.
- *"Probably Bathonian."* Rauhut (2003) reports this from earlier regional work, "probably Bathonian in the area concerned" (rauhut2003c, p. 174), and on the same page favors "a late Toarcian to Aalenian age" for the Cerro Carnerero. Neither is a U-Pb result.
- *185 Ma from Pol and colleagues (2020).* Pol and colleagues (2022) give the formation as "radiometrically dated at 185 Ma; Pol et al. 2020" (pol2022a, p. 135). The only Lonco Trapial date in that paper is 180.330 ± 0.068 Ma (pol2020a, p. 3); 185 Ma does not appear in it. The stages are Pliensbachian–Toarcian either way.

**Open.** Nothing.

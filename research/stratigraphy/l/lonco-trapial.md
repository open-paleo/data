# Lonco Trapial

## What is the age of the Lonco Trapial Formation?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Pliensbachian–Toarcian, from U-Pb dates bracketing the formation and one direct date within it. Governs `stages: [Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on Lonco Trapial and on its [Cerro Carnerero Member](../../../stratigraphy/c/cerro-carnerero.yml), and the stages of [*Amygdalodon patagonicus*](../../../genera/A/Amygdalodon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cúneo2013a, p. 1271 (in-press version, p. 5), Geological implications: "the Lonco Trapial Formation is now bracketed between 188.95 Ma and 178.77 Ma, entirely within the Early Jurassic (Pliensbachian–Toarcian)". The two ends are tuffs in the Las Leoneras Formation below and the lower Cañadón Asfalto Formation above (p. 1269).
- pol2020a, p. 3, Radioisotopic geochronology: "Our new U–Pb zircon date of 180.330 ± 0.068 Ma from the massive pyroclastic interval of the Lonco Trapial Formation".
- zaffarana2020a, p. 8, Geochronology: "The U–Pb zircon age of the rhyolite dike of Lonco Trapial Formation (sample G4-231) is 177.26 ± 0.96 Ma". The paper's other dates for the formation are K–Ar (191.6 ± 5.2 Ma) and Ar–Ar (~185 Ma, ~178 Ma); none falls outside the Early Jurassic.

**Ruled out.**
- *Bajocian–Bathonian, or Middle Jurassic generally.* This is Nullo and Proserpio's (1975) age from K–Ar dating, and the paper is not consistent about it: the abstracts give "Bayociano-Bathoniano" (nullo1975a, p. 133) and "Bathonian-Bayocian" (p. 134), while the conclusions give "Bayociano-Caloviano" (p. 148). Along the way the basalt discussion reaches "Aaleniano-eoBayociano" (p. 146), an intermediate step, then rejects a 173 Ma date and arrives at Callovian (p. 147). The U-Pb dates above supersede all of these.
- *"Probably Bathonian."* Rauhut (2003) reports this from earlier regional work, "probably Bathonian in the area concerned" (rauhut2003c, p. 174), and on the same page favors "a late Toarcian to Aalenian age" for the Cerro Carnerero. Neither is a U-Pb result.
- *185 Ma from Pol and colleagues (2020).* Pol and colleagues (2022) give the formation as "radiometrically dated at 185 Ma; Pol et al. 2020" (pol2022a, p. 135). The only Lonco Trapial date in that paper is 180.330 ± 0.068 Ma (pol2020a, p. 3); 185 Ma does not appear in it. The stages are Pliensbachian–Toarcian either way.

**Open.** Nothing.

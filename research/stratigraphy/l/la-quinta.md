# La Quinta

## Does Langer and colleagues' Late Triassic start add the Late Triassic?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No; it stays out of `period`. Confirms the 2026-11-23 entry. Langer and colleagues (2014) write that "the radioisotopic dates and scant fossil evidence suggest" deposition began in the Late Triassic, in their own voice, but the dates and fossils are ones they relay from earlier work, among them a U–Pb date of 229 ± 15 Ma from the basal tuff in the Mérida Andes; a relayed date adds nothing until its original is read. Their own date, 200.72 Ma from the fossil bed, is a maximum depositional age. Governs `period` on [La Quinta](../../../stratigraphy/l/la-quinta.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- langer2014a, p. 3: "Together, the radioisotopic dates and scant fossil evidence suggest that the deposition of the La Quinta Formation started in the Late Triassic."

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Triassic.* Relayed dates and fossils.

**Open.** The original of the 229 ± 15 Ma date has not been read.

## Do the earlier readings that Langer and colleagues (2014) review widen the age of the La Quinta?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** No. Langer and colleagues (2014) review readings from the type area in the Mérida Andes and from the Perijá Andes that run from the Late Triassic to the Early Cretaceous: conchostracans from the Late Triassic or Early Jurassic low in the unit to the Late Jurassic or Early Cretaceous high in it, volcanic dates from the Middle to Late Jurassic, and a bulk zircon date from the basal tuff that, with the fossils, they take to suggest deposition began in the Late Triassic. Each is relayed from earlier work that has not been read, and all come before the zircon dating tied to fossil horizons at both ends of the unit: an earliest Jurassic maximum for the bonebeds near La Grita in Venezuela (Langer and colleagues 2014, Barrett and colleagues 2014; see the 2026-08-05 entry on [*Tachiraptor*](../../genera/T/Tachiraptor.md)) and about 175 Ma beneath the *Perijasaurus* horizons near Manaure in Colombia (Rincón and colleagues 2022). The formation is given on both sides of the border: in the Venezuelan Andes by Langer and colleagues and Barrett and colleagues, and in the Serranía del Perijá, "shared by Colombia and Venezuela (like the La Quinta Formation)", by Rincón and colleagues. Governs `period` and `stages` on [La Quinta](../../../stratigraphy/l/la-quinta.yml).

**Evidence.**
- langer2014a, p. 1, title: "the earliest Jurassic of the La Quinta formation, Venezuelan Andes".
- langer2014a, p. 3, Introduction: in the Perijá Andes "abundant estherid conchostracans range in age from Late Triassic/Early Jurassic, in the lower part of the unit, to Late Jurassic/Early Cretaceous, in its upper part".
- langer2014a, p. 3, Introduction: "Past geochronological studies of the La Quinta Formation have produced equivocal results. A U–Pb zircon date (bulk multi-grain zircon analyses) of 229 ± 15 Ma from the La Grita dacitic tuff at the base of the La Quinta Formation in the Mérida Andes"; K–Ar dates from the same tuff "much younger at 149 ± 10 Ma and 122.5 ± 7.7 Ma".
- langer2014a, p. 3, Introduction: "Reported U–Pb, Rb–Sr and K–Ar dates from volcanic rocks of the La Quinta Formation in the Perijá Andes [21] range from Middle to Late Jurassic (approx. 170–140 Myr ago). Together, the radioisotopic dates and scant fossil evidence suggest that the deposition of the La Quinta Formation started in the Late Triassic."
- barrett2014a, p. 2, Locality and horizon: earlier estimates for the unit "were poorly constrained and ranged from Late Permian–Lower Cretaceous"; the bonebed is placed "in the earliest Jurassic (Hettangian)".
- rincón2022a, p. 6, Type Locality and Horizon: "Magmatic zircons from two subvolcanic samples underlying the inferred fossiliferous horizons yielded U/Pb ages of 174.8 ± 1.2 Ma and 175.5 ± 1.4 Ma"; "latest Toarcian or earliest Aalenian in age".
- rincón2022a, p. 6, Etymology: the Serranía del Perijá "is a part of the cordillera shared by Colombia and Venezuela (like the La Quinta Formation)".

**Ruled out.**
- *A Late Triassic start.* The suggestion rests on a bulk multi-grain date of 229 ± 15 Ma that the paper places among "equivocal results", and on fossils whose ranges are broad; it is relayed, and the paper's own dating gives only an earliest Jurassic maximum for one bed. No work cited dates any part of the unit to the Triassic by a method tied to a horizon.
- *A Late Jurassic or Early Cretaceous top.* The conchostracan range and the Middle to Late Jurassic volcanic dates from the Perijá Andes are relayed from earlier work, with no horizon or sample given in the paper; the K–Ar dates from the basal tuff in the type area the paper attributes to "thermal perturbation of the K–Ar system at a younger date".

**Open.** The age of the top and base of the unit outside the dated fossil horizons. The Perijá study that Langer and colleagues cite for the conchostracans and volcanic dates would show whether any of its dates is tied to a horizon in the unit; it has not been read.

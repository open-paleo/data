## Where does Molnar's late Campanian ceiling come from?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** From a potassium-argon date in the overlying El Gallo, not from Kilmer; confirms the corrected reading of the 2026-11-23 entry. Molnar (1974) presumes the formation "late Campanian or earlier" from a 73 ± 2 Ma date in the middle third of the El Gallo, given as a personal communication and read as late Campanian on the time scale of Kulp (1961); a date from an overlying unit is a limit, and a personal communication is not a published determination. Kilmer (1963) is credited only with the unit names and the description of the rocks. Governs the notes on molnar1974a and kilmer1963a on [La Bocana Roja](../../../stratigraphy/l/la-bocana-roja.yml); `stages` and `dispute` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- molnar1974a, pp. 1009–1010: "a potassium-argon date of 73 ± 2 million years has been obtained from the middle third of the … "El Gallo" (Morris, pers. comm., 1972)".
- molnar1974a, p. 1010: "The "La Bocana Roja" may thus be presumed late Campanian or earlier in age."

**Ruled out.**
- *A ceiling from Kilmer's correlation.* Molnar credits Kilmer with no age.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works rest their ages for the La Bocana Roja Formation on?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Adds to the 2026-09-30 entry without changing it. Molnar (1974), after Kilmer (1963), describes the formation as red mudstones and tuffs unconformably below the El Gallo Formation. He takes a late Campanian ceiling from a potassium-argon date of 73 ± 2 million years in the middle third of the El Gallo, and a Cenomanian floor from its hadrosaur fossils, a group that first appears in the Cenomanian. Kane and colleagues (2022) describe about 675 m of fluvial sandstones and mudstones whose base on the Alisitos Formation is not exposed, set aside Kilmer's suggestion that the formation might equal the lowermost El Gallo, and date its upper part to a maximum of 93.6 ± 1.1 Ma, noting that the earlier Campanian age rested "largely" on *Labocania*. Rivera-Sylva and Longrich (2024) treat that date as a maximum, take a minimum of 75.84 Ma from tuffs in the El Gallo, and argue for the mid- or late Campanian from *Labocania*'s teratophoneine affinities and from a lambeosaur ischium that "may be referable" to *Magnapaulia*. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian, Coniacian, Santonian, Campanian]` and the dispute on [La Bocana Roja](../../../stratigraphy/l/la-bocana-roja.yml).

**Evidence.**
- molnar1974a, p. 1009: Kilmer "described this as red mudstones and tuffs unconformably underlying his 'El Gallo Formation'"; "a potassium-argon date of 73 ± 2 million years has been obtained from the middle third of the 'El Gallo'".
- molnar1974a, p. 1010: the date "would be late Campanian"; "The 'La Bocana Roja' may thus be presumed late Campanian or earlier in age. It is probably not older than Cenomanian, since it does contain hadrosaurian fossils which first appear in the record in the Cenomanian".
- kane2022a, p. 2, La Bocana Roja Formation: "a c. 675 m thick succession of fluvial sandstones and mudstones"; "The basal contact with the underlying Alisitos Formation is not exposed and initially Kilmer (1963) had suggested that the Bocana Roja might be a stratigraphic equivalent of the lowermost El Gallo Formation; subsequent dating work and the results presented below suggest that the latter is significantly older."
- kane2022a, p. 7, Depositional Ages: "The new data yield an MDA for the La Bocana Roja Fm. of 93.6±1.1 Ma (Turonian). Previous work suggested a Campanian age, based largely on the presence of a theropod dinosaur fossil, Labocania (Molnar, 1974)."
- rivera-sylva2024a, p. 263: "Labocania is referable to the Teratophoneini, a clade known only from the Campanian"; "A lambeosaur ischium collected from the same beds resembles and may be referable to the Late Campanian genus Magnapaulia, again implying a Campanian age."

**Ruled out.**
- *Molnar's late Campanian ceiling as resting on Kilmer's correlation with the lowermost El Gallo.* Molnar (1974, p. 1009) relays Kilmer's formation as unconformably below the El Gallo and dates the ceiling from the middle third of the El Gallo; he does not mention the correlation that Kane and colleagues (2022) set aside, so their rejection of it leaves his ceiling in place.
- *The dinosaur fauna as an independent Campanian dating.* Of Rivera-Sylva and Longrich's two fossils, *Labocania* is the one whose horizon is in question (see [*Labocania*](../../genera/L/Labocania.md)), and the ischium is referred to *Magnapaulia* only tentatively.

**Open.** Nothing beyond the 2026-09-30 entry.

## Is the La Bocana Roja Formation Turonian or Campanian?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Disputed. A detrital-zircon maximum age makes the upper part no older than Cenomanian–Turonian; the dinosaur fauna argues for the middle or late Campanian, and the overlying El Gallo Formation sets a minimum of about 75.8 Ma. Governs `stages` (Cenomanian–Campanian) and the dispute on La Bocana Roja, and bears on `stage` for [*Labocania anomala*](../../../genera/L/Labocania.yml).

**Evidence.**
- kane2022a, p. 9: "date the upper part of the fluvial bedrock (La Bocana Roja Fm.) at 93.6±1.1 Ma (Cenomanian-Turonian)"; p. 8: "we revise the age considerably from Campanian to Turonian (93.6±1.1 Ma)". Its p. 7 gives the stage as "(Turonian)".
- rivera-sylva2024a, p. 263: "because detrital zircons are reworked from older sediments, this is only a maximum age"; "The dinosaur fauna therefore strongly argues for a mid- or late Campanian age"; "suggest a minimum age of 75.84 ± 0.05 Ma for the La Bocana Roja Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Turonian as a settled age.* The zircon date is a maximum, as Rivera-Sylva and colleagues note.
- *Bivalves and ammonites as independent support for the Campanian.* Rivera-Sylva and colleagues' "late middle Campanian" from bivalves and ammonites (p. 263) concerns the overlying Punta Baja Formation and is relayed from Kane and colleagues, who discount the bivalve age (kane2022a, p. 7).

**Open.** The age within that bracket. The 75.84 Ma minimum is, in Rivera-Sylva and colleagues' account, the base of the El Disecado Member of the El Gallo; Kane and colleagues give an El Gallo maximum age of 86.8 ± 1.8 Ma "Immediately above the contact" (p. 7), so 75.84 Ma may not date the very base of the El Gallo. The *Labocania* site LACM 7257 has absorbed LACM 6755, which is listed as "El Gallo Fm. (El Diseado [sic] member)" (rivera-sylva2024a, p. 263); if that listing applies, the horizon would be El Gallo, not La Bocana Roja.

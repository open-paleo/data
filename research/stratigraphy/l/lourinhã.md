# Lourinhã Formation

## Does the registry hold the Santa Cruz package twice?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No. Under Mateus and colleagues' (2017) scheme, which the registry follows, the coastal package is the [Praia Azul](../../../stratigraphy/p/praia-azul.yml) Member of the Lourinhã, and the [Sobral](../../../stratigraphy/s/sobral.yml) entry is limited to the Farta Pão member inland (see [Sobral](../s/sobral.md)). Answers the Open line of the 2026-11-23 entry. Governs the members of [Lourinhã](../../../stratigraphy/l/lourinhã.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.** None.

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Where does the Lourinhã begin, and which beds belong to it?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The formation begins in the Kimmeridgian. Schneider and colleagues (2009) have its fluvial sandstones laid down in the northernmost part of the basin while the Alcobaça and other formations accumulated elsewhere, then spread southward to the Torres Vedras area, so that it spans the Kimmeridgian and Tithonian; their strontium-isotope ages on oysters contradict the model of Pena dos Reis and colleagues (1996), in which the formation includes the Arranhó strata and starts only in the late Early Tithonian. Mateus and Milàn (2009) describe some 400 m of alluvial clay and sandstone deposited in the Kimmeridgian and Tithonian, after Hill (1989), and Mateus and colleagues (2017), a field guide devoted to the formation that gives it formation rank and, in its title, an Upper Jurassic to lowermost Cretaceous span, date its lower coastal members upper Kimmeridgian. Which beds belong to the formation has shifted with each author: Hill's (1989) Praia Azul member and the Sobral were moved into the Farta Pão Formation, where Schneider and colleagues keep the Sobral as a member, while Pena dos Reis and colleagues treated the Sobral in part as the formation's basal member; the Sobral's parent is settled in [Sobral](../s/sobral.md). The Berriasian at the top is the subject of the 2026-10-14 entry. Governs `stages` (the Kimmeridgian) on [Lourinhã](../../../stratigraphy/l/lourinhã.yml).

**Evidence.**
- schneider2009a, p. 1952: "Contemporaneous with the aforementioned formations fluvial sandstones of the Lourinhã formation were deposited in the northernmost sector of the basin", and the formation "gradually expanded southward to the Torres Vedras area and covered the entire NLB and large parts of the CLB. Stratigraphically, the unit spans the entire Kimmeridgian and Tithonian".
- schneider2009a, p. 1952: "the Praia Azul member of the coastal area south of Santa Cruz, which was assigned to the Lourinhã formation by Hill (1989) and the Sobral formation were also included in the Farta Pao formation"; "the Sobral formation was partially also considered a basal member of the Lourinhã formation"; "Herein, we propose four members for the Farta Pao formation", the first being the Sobral member.
- schneider2009a, p. 1966: Arranhó II ages "range from the latest Kimmeridgian (151.4 Ma = beckeri zone) to the late Early Tithonian (147.6 Ma = admirandum/biruncinatum zone)"; "The isotope results strongly contradict the model of Pena dos Reis et al. (1996), in which the Arranhó is included in the Lourinhã formation, the latter assumed to have started in the late Early Tithonian".
- schneider2009a, p. 1967: the Lourinhã siliciclastics "extended southward. By the Early Kimmeridgian, they had replaced the Cabo Mondego formation in the NLB".
- penadosreis1996a, p. ?: the highstand "corresponds to the overlying prograding siliciclastics of the upper part of the unit including the Lourinhã formation base (particularly the Sobral member p.p.)".
- mateus2009b, p. 245: "The 400-m-thick Lourinhã Formation" was deposited "during the early rifting of the Atlantic Ocean in the Kimmeridgian and Tithonian"; the succession "shows several shifts between flood-plain mud and fluvial sands that favour preservation and fossilization of tracks"; deposition "in latest Jurassic, Tithonian–Kimmeridgian times (Hill 1989)"; "thick beds of red and green clay, interbedded with massive fluvial sandstone bodies".
- mateus2009b, p. 255: "The Lourinhã Formation (Kimmeridgian ⁄ Tithonian) contains a rich and diverse dinosaur ichno- and somatofossil fauna".
- mateus2017a, p. 75: the title, "The Lourinhã Formation: the Upper Jurassic to lowermost Cretaceous of the Lusitanian Basin, Portugal"; "the Praia da Amoreira and Porto Novo members (…; upper Kimmeridgian)", "the Praia Azul Member (…; uppermost Kimmeridgian to lowermost Tithonian)"; "The last fieldtrip stop at Porto da Calada examines the top of the Assenta Member of the Lourinhã Formation (…; upper Tithonian to lowermost Berriasian) and the Porto da Calada Formation (…; Berriasian) and thus includes the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary".
- mateus2017a, p. 79: "the thickness of Lourinhã deposits can reach almost 400 m".

**Ruled out.**
- *A start in the late Early Tithonian.* The model of Pena dos Reis and colleagues (1996), as Schneider and colleagues (2009) describe it, puts the Arranhó strata in the formation and starts the formation in the late Early Tithonian. Schneider and colleagues' strontium-isotope ages put the Arranhó II between the latest Kimmeridgian and the late Early Tithonian, in part coeval with the Sobral and Arranhó I, and they write that the results "strongly contradict" the model; they also have the formation in the northern basin by the Early Kimmeridgian. The Berriasian that Pena dos Reis and colleagues give for the formation's top is a separate question, kept in the 2026-10-14 entry.

**Open.** Hill's (1989) Praia Azul member. Mateus and colleagues (2017) keep a Praia Azul Member in the Lourinhã, while Schneider and colleagues place its strata at Santa Cruz in the Sobral, Arranhó I and Arranhó II members of the Farta Pão. Which formation holds those beds is not settled by the works cited here.

## Does the Lourinhã reach the Berriasian?

*2026-10-14*

**Conclusion.** Yes, at its top: Kimmeridgian to Berriasian, with the disagreement kept in the dispute. The Berriasian does not rest on the Assenta Member alone: Pena dos Reis and colleagues (1996) give it for the formation, and Salminen and colleagues' (2014) own magnetostratigraphy places the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary 8 m below the formation's top at Porto da Calada, which agrees with Mateus and colleagues' (2017) earliest Berriasian for the last metres of the Assenta. That the 8 m lies within the Lourinhã is our reading of their log: the boundary at "around 52 m" and the base of the overlying Porto da Calada Formation "at our 60 m". Governs `stages: [Kimmeridgian, Tithonian, Berriasian]` and the dispute on [Lourinhã](../../../stratigraphy/l/lourinhã.yml).

**Evidence.**
- penadosreis1996a: "The Tithonian and Berriasian are represented in the center and North by the Lourinhã formation".
- salminen2014a: the boundary at "around 52 m of the log", an improvement on Rey (1993), "which tentatively located the J–K boundary at the base of the Porto da Calada Formation (at our 60 m)"; the same paper's background calls the Assenta "Tithonian in age".
- mateus2017a: "the last few meters of the Assenta Mb. can be considered as earliest Berriasian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Salminen and colleagues (2014) as a Tithonian-only reading.* Their background says so; their result does not.

**Open.** Where the boundary falls elsewhere in the formation; the magnetostratigraphy is tentative and from one section.

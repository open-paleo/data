# Lujiatun

## Does the Lujiatun cite Zhou and colleagues (2006) for its age?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** It did not, and now does. Zhou and colleagues (2006) give "the upper parts of the Lujiatun beds" a 40Ar/39Ar date of 128.4 ± 0.2 Ma after Wang and colleagues (2001), the date the same paper gives on p. 101 for a basalt in the lower Jianshangou sequence at Sihetun, and read the beds as Hauterivian from it. The Hauterivian stays ruled out, as the 2026-11-23 entry records for the Wang and colleagues date. Governs the pointers to zhou2006b and wang2001c on [Lujiatun](../../../stratigraphy/l/lujiatun.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- zhou2006b, p. 112: "Because the upper parts of the Lujiatun beds of the Yixian Formation in western Liaoning have been dated as 128.4 ± 0.2 Ma by 40Ar/39Ar radiometric techniques (Wang et al., 2001), the age of these beds appears to be Hauterivian (132.0–127.0 Ma)".
- zhou2006b, p. 101: "A basalt sample from the lower part of the Jianshangou sequence at Sihetun has yielded a 40Ar/39Ar date of 128.4 ± 0.2 Ma".

**Ruled out.** Nothing new.

**Open.** Nothing.

## How do the argon dates on and above the Lujiatun bear on its age?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** They leave the Barremian of the 2026-10-01 entry standing. Three argon results bear on the unit: He and colleagues' (2006) 123.2 ± 1.0 Ma on K-feldspar from the fossil-bearing tuff, which Chang and colleagues (2017) recalculate to 124.4 ± 1.0 Ma; Chang and colleagues' 125.92 ± 0.62 Ma on basalts directly above the unit, a minimum age for it; and Wang and colleagues' (2001) basalt at Huangbanjigou, which put the unit before 128 Ma. Zhong and colleagues (2021) date the unit's own tuffs by U-Pb on single zircons (CA-ID-IRMS) to an onset of 125.755 ± 0.061 Ma, find that the earlier Lujiatun ages are younger than the lava above them, and resolve the Jianshangou as younger than the Lujiatun. Their dates take precedence over the argon results, and Chang and colleagues' minimum age agrees with them within its uncertainty. Governs `stages: [Barremian]` on [Lujiatun](../../../stratigraphy/l/lujiatun.yml).

**Evidence.**
- he2006a, p. 1, Abstract: "40Ar/39Ar step heating analyses on bulk K-feldspars from the fossil bearing tuff gave a weighted mean age of 123.2 ± 1.0 Ma (2σ)".
- he2006a, p. 1, Sampling: "Samples L3003 and L3004 were collected from the middle and upper part of the tuffs".
- he2006a, p. 1, Introduction: "The age of the Lujiatun bed was tentatively proposed to be older than 128Ma (Hauterivian) [Wang et al., 2001] based on a 40Ar/39Ar date of the basalt presumably capping the Lujiatun bed at the Huangbanjigou section, which is about 6 km from the Lujiatun locality"; "the relationship between the Huanbanjigou basalt and the fossil-bearing tuffs of the Lujiatun bed is ambiguous".
- he2006a, p. 3, Results: "123.2 ± 1.0 Ma(2σ) reflect the time since eruption of fossil-bearing tuff in the Lujiatun bed".
- he2006a, p. 3, Discussion and Conclusions: "Our result (123.2 ± 1.0 Ma, Aptian) does not support previous assumptions that the bed is much older than the Jianshagou bed"; "the Lujiatun bed was most likely deposited at the same time as the Jianshangou bed of the lower Yixian Formation".
- chang2017a, p. 403: "126.0 ± 0.8 Ma for a basaltic sample at Lujiatun village and 125.8 ± 1.0 Ma for a basaltic sample at Daziying village", combined "in a weighted mean age of 125.92 ± 0.62 Ma"; He and colleagues' date recalculated "is 124.4 ± 1.0 Ma".
- chang2017a, p. 403: the mean "marginally overlaps with the recalculated age of 124.4 ± 1.0 Ma from (He et al., 2006), but sanidine age from the stratigraphically younger Jianshangou formation constraint the age of the Lujiatun formation to a minimum age of 125.0 Ma"; the Huangbanjigou basalt, recalculated to "129.4 ± 0.8 Ma", is "about 6 km away from Lujiatun village".
- zhong2021a, p. 1: "the existing ages of the Lujiatun Unit (LJT Unit) are younger than those of the immediate overlying Lower Lava Unit"; "It therefore casts serious doubt on the robustness of these dating results"; "The new CA-ID-IRMS ages show significant improvement in analytical precision compared with literature data"; "The ages of the LJT and JSG Units are resolvable given the extraordinary precision achieved in this study, with the JSG Unit being sequentially deposited later than the LJT Unit".

**Ruled out.**
- *Older than 128 Ma (Hauterivian), from the basalt at Huangbanjigou.* Wang and colleagues (2001) date a basalt presumed to cap the unit about 6 km from Lujiatun village; He and colleagues (2006) call its relation to the fossil-bearing tuffs ambiguous, and the U-Pb onset in the unit itself is 125.755 Ma.
- *Latest Hauterivian, from Chang and colleagues' (2017) Lower Lava mean.* On the ICS chart v2024/12 the central value of 125.92 Ma falls in the latest Hauterivian. It is an argon age on the lava above the unit, its ± 0.62 Myr spans the U-Pb onset, and the U-Pb dates of Zhong and colleagues (2021) are measured on the unit's own tuffs at far higher precision, so they decide the stage.
- *The Lujiatun as contemporaneous with the Jianshangou.* He and colleagues (2006) and Chang and colleagues (2017) read their argon dates that way; Zhong and colleagues (2021) resolve the Jianshangou as deposited after the Lujiatun. This settles the order left open in the 2026-10-04 entry of [Yixian](../y/yixian.md) as far as these dates go; it does not change the stage.

**Open.** Nothing beyond the 2026-10-01 entry.

## What stage are the Lujiatun Beds?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Barremian, by a narrow margin at the base: 125.755 Ma is 0.015 Myr above the base of the Barremian on the ICS chart (125.77 Ma) and 0.75 Myr above it in GTS 2020 (126.5 ± 0.7 Ma). It is clear of the Aptian by more than 4 Myr. Governs `stages: [Barremian]` on Lujiatun and the Barremian on the Lujiatun-horizon species, such as [*Psittacosaurus lujiatunensis*](../../../genera/P/Psittacosaurus.yml) and [*Jeholosaurus shangyuanensis*](../../../genera/J/Jeholosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- zhong2021a, p. 1: the Lujiatun Unit's "onset at 125.755 ± 0.061 Ma (CY17–9)" by CA-ID-TIMS U-Pb on zircon (±0.27 Ma with systematic uncertainty; sample CY17-18 gives 125.684 Ma), an "extraordinarily short duration of the LJT Unit (<71 ± 86 Kyr)", and the Yixian's "termination at 124.122 ± 0.048 Ma".
- gale2020a, p. 1067: "The Barremian Stage lasted 5.1 Myr from 126.5 ± 0.7 to 121.4 ± 0.6 Ma". GTS 2020 gives no numerical age for the early/late Barremian boundary in its text (p. 1031); its Fig. 27.9 (p. 1047) plots 125.755 Ma in the lower Barremian.
- chang2017a, p. 403: "weighted mean age of 125.92 ± 0.62 Ma" on basalts "from the lowest part of the Lower Lava Unit, immediately above its contact with the Lujiatun Unit" (p. 401), a minimum age for the beds.

**Ruled out.**
- *Barremian–Aptian boundary, or earliest Aptian.* He and colleagues (2006) give "123.2 ± 1.0 Ma, Aptian"; Chang and colleagues recalculate it to "124.4 ± 1.0 Ma" (chang2017a, p. 403), and Zhong and colleagues note that "the existing ages of the Lujiatun Unit … are younger than those of the immediate overlying Lower Lava Unit" (zhong2021a, p. 1). The Aptian readings combine that date with the older Aptian bases of GTS 2004 (125.0 Ma) or GTS 2012 (126.3 Ma; gale2020a, p. 1066, Table 27.4).
- *Late Barremian.* Nothing in GTS 2020 supports it for 125.755 Ma, and its Fig. 27.9 places that age in the lower Barremian.
- *"Late Barremian or earliest Albian."* Sereno (2010) prints this in two type-horizon blocks (sereno2010a, p. 28), while the text puts the beds "no older than 123–125 Ma, straddling the Barremian-Aptian boundary" (p. 22). The Albian appears to be a slip (our reading).
- *Hauterivian.* Sereno and colleagues (2009) give "Lujiatun Beds [Hauterivian-Barremian stages, ~130 million years ago (Ma)]" (sereno2009a, p. 419) and "(Barremian-Aptian, ~125 Ma)" on p. 422; Cuenca-Bescós and colleagues (2003) plot "Lujiatum Beds, Yixian Formation (Hauterivian …)" on a map (cuenca-bescós2003a, p. 580, Fig. 6). Neither has a date that supports it.

**Open.** Whether the Barremian holds on a future chart. The margin against the ICS base is smaller than the date's systematic uncertainty, and the Lower Lava minimum (125.92 ± 0.62 Ma) would fall in the latest Hauterivian on that chart.

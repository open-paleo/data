# La Amarga

## What are the original sources of the La Amarga's Barremian–early Aptian?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** Leanza (2003) and Leanza and colleagues (2004), now cited. Leanza ascribes the formation to the late Barremian–early Aptian by correlating it with the Huitrín Formation through the unconformities at its base and top; Leanza and colleagues give "Barremian–Early Aptian" after Leanza and Hugo (1995, 1997). Governs the pointers to leanza2003a and leanza2004a on [La Amarga](../../../stratigraphy/l/la-amarga.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- leanza2003a, p. 1: "La Formación La Amarga puede correlacionarse con la Formación Huitrín y se atribuye al Barremiano superior – Aptiano inferior" ["The La Amarga Formation can be correlated with the Huitrín Formation and is assigned to the Late Barremian – Early Aptian", translated].
- leanza2004a, p. 65: "Based on basin analysis, stratigraphical relationships and tectonosedimentary aspects, this unit may be regarded as Barremian–Early Aptian in age (Leanza and Hugo, 1995, 1997)."

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the La Amarga?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Hauterivian to Aptian, the union of the formation's two published datings. Salgado and colleagues (2006) group under the formation a thick continental package of Early Cretaceous age, place the *Zapalasaurus* type in its Piedra Parada Member, "whose age is regarded as Barremian-lower Aptian", and review earlier datings: a Barremian age for the basal section from its microfossils, a Barremian–Aptian assignment, a Hauterivian–Barremian one from correlation with the upper part of the Agrio Formation, and Leanza and Hugo's (1995, 1997) late Hauterivian to Barremian for the three members, the formation being "finally" assigned to the Barremian–early Aptian by Leanza (2003) and Leanza and colleagues (2004). Leanza and Hugo's own wording, and what the 2004 range rests on, are set out in the [Puesto Antigual](../p/puesto-antigual.md) note. Governs `stages: [Hauterivian, Barremian, Aptian]` and `period: [Early Cretaceous]` on [La Amarga](../../../stratigraphy/l/la-amarga.yml).

**Evidence.**
- salgado2006b, p. 695, abstract: the Piedra Parada Member of the La Amarga Formation, "whose age is regarded as Barremian-lower Aptian".
- salgado2006b, p. 696 (translated): the levels "are assigned to the La Amarga Formation, under which designation is grouped a thick package of continental sediments of lower Cretaceous age"; later works assign the unit "to the Barremian–Aptian …, and, by its correlation with the upper part of the Agrio Formation, to the Hauterivian–Barremian"; Leanza and Hugo "assigned to these units an age ranging from the late Hauterivian to the Barremian", "finally being assigned to the Barremian–early Aptian interval".
- leanza1995a, p. 30: the formation "is usually assigned to the Late Hauterivian / Barremian" (translated).
- leanza2004a, p. 65: "this unit may be regarded as Barremian–Early Aptian in age (Leanza and Hugo, 1995, 1997)".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Whether the late Hauterivian stands. Leanza (2003), an author of the 1995 and 1997 dating, gives the unconformity at the base of the formation "an intra-Barremian value, it being probable that the late upper Hauterivian and the early Barremian had no sedimentary records in this region" (leanza2003a, p. 20, translated). Read as the author narrowing his own earlier dating, that would move the Hauterivian out of the stages under the rule on counting each original statement once in [methods](../../methods.md). The other source of a Hauterivian, the correlation with the upper Agrio Formation that Salgado and colleagues report, has not been read.

# Los Blanquitos

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Los Blanquitos?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian–Maastrichtian on Powell (1979), with the Santonian from Carrano and colleagues (2012). Powell places the Arroyo Morterito dinosaurs in the upper section of the formation, dated "Upper Senonian", and argues for a "Senonian (Campanian-Maestrichtian)" age from the titanosaurid sauropods, which Huene (1929) had assigned to the Senonian. The overlying Yacoraite Formation, with its carnosaurs, is limited to the highest part of the same interval, the Maastrichtian. Radiometric measurements from other formations of the Pirgua Subgroup (La Yesera and Las Curtiembres) lead Powell to infer the same Campanian–Maastrichtian age, which Powell calls consistent with the fossils. Carrano and colleagues (2012) give the formation "late Santonian–?Campanian". Governs `stages: [Santonian, Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Los Blanquitos](../../../stratigraphy/l/los-blanquitos.yml).

**Evidence.**
- powell1979a, p. 193: "The fossiliferous bed corresponds to the upper section of the Los Blanquitos Formation (Pirgua Subgroup, Salta Group) of the Upper Cretaceous (Upper Senonian)" (translated).
- powell1979a, p. 203: "Huene (1929) assigned the titanosaurids of Patagonia to the Senonian" (translated).
- powell1979a, p. 204: "the Senonian (Campanian-Maestrichtian) age of the Los Blanquitos Formation can be reaffirmed"; the Yacoraite Formation, "which lies on the Los Blanquitos Formation corresponding to the upper Senonian", is limited "to the higher part of the same interval (Maestrichtian)"; radiometric measurements "from the Pirgua Subgroup (La Yesera and Las Curtiembres Formations) permit inferring a Campanian-Maestrichtian age for the Los Blanquitos Formation" (translated).
- carrano2012a, p. 256: "from the Los Blanquitos Formation (late Santonian–?Campanian) of Arroyo El Morterito (Campanian)".

**Ruled out.** Nothing was weighed and rejected.

**Open.** The Santonian rests on Carrano and colleagues (2012) alone, who give no basis for it, and the same sentence dates the Arroyo El Morterito locality Campanian (carrano2012a, p. 256). Powell's (1979) radiometric support comes from other formations of the subgroup, not from the Los Blanquitos itself. A dated study of the formation, or the source of Carrano and colleagues' late Santonian, would settle whether the Santonian stays.

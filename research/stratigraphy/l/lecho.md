# Lecho

## Does the Campanian stand on the Lecho?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Yes. Answers the first point of the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Powell (2003) restricts the formation to "the Early Maastrichtian or, with less probability, the Late Campanian"; a paper's own undecided dating contributes every stage it allows, however it ranks them. Marquillas and colleagues (2005) and Montano and colleagues (2022) date nothing inside the formation: the first place it at the base of a Maastrichtian to Early Paleocene subgroup, above volcanics dated 70 ± 5 Ma, and the second model the base of the overlying Yacoraite at about 68.1 Ma by extrapolation. A 70 ± 5 Ma floor reaches into the Campanian and does not exclude it. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Lecho](../../../stratigraphy/l/lecho.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- powell2003a, p. 7: the "Lecho Formation must be restricted to the Early Maastrichtian or, with less probability, the Late Campanian".
- marquillas2005a, p. 100: the Palmar Largo volcanic rocks "lie between the top of Los Blanquitos Formation and the base of the Balbuena Subgroup in the Lomas de Olmedo subbasin. They are dated as having 70±5 Ma in age (K/Ar)".
- marquillas2005a, p. 101: "The Balbuena Subgroup (Fig. 5) was accumulated during the Maastrichtian to Early Paleocene".
- montano2022a, p. 2521: "the sedimentation onset (SB1) and end (SB5) were estimated at 68.1 ± 0.9 Ma and 62.3 ± 0.5 Ma, respectively".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian only, as later work treats it.* Powell's either-or is a published reading, and the bounds read here allow it.

**Open.** The second point of the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line, the road to El Brete, stands.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Lecho Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian to Maastrichtian. Powell (2003) infers a Campanian–Maastrichtian age from the close similarity of *Saltasaurus loricatus* to *Neuquensaurus australis* of the Río Colorado (Campanian) and Allen (Early Maastrichtian) formations, and narrows it to the Early Maastrichtian or, less probably, the Late Campanian, given the partly Maastrichtian age of the Yacoraite Formation's Bolivian equivalent and the radiometric Campanian age of the Las Curtiembres Formation. Hendrickx and colleagues (2024) call the formation Maastrichtian and date the *Noasaurus* occurrence to the early or mid Maastrichtian, beneath the Maastrichtian–Danian Yacoraite; they report that the formation is "typically regarded" as either (?Upper) Campanian–Maastrichtian or strictly Maastrichtian, and that the latest literature on it considers it Maastrichtian. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Lecho](../../../stratigraphy/l/lecho.yml).

**Evidence.**
- powell2003a, p. 6: "Lecho Formation, Balbuena Subgroup, Salta Group. The fossiliferous level lies 22 m above the base of this unit."
- powell2003a, p. 6: "There is a close similarity between Saltasaurus loricatus and Neuquensaurus australis, from the Rio Colorado Formation (Campanian) and the Allen Formation (Early Maastrichtian) in the Neuquina Basin. This fact, and the absence of closely related titanosaurids in the earlier formation, suggests a Campanian-Maastrichtian age for the Lecho Formation."
- powell2003a, p. 7: the "Lecho Formation must be restricted to the Early Maastrichtian or, with less probability, the Late Campanian".
- hendrickx2024a, p. 1, Abstract: "the Maastrichtian Lecho Formation of Salta, Argentina".
- hendrickx2024a, p. 6, Fig. 2 caption: "the stratigraphic occurrence of *Noasaurus* and other plant, invertebrate and dinosaur fossils within the Maastrichtian Lecho Formation and Maastrichtian–Danian Yacoraite Formation of the Balbuena Subgroup, Salta Group".
- hendrickx2024a, p. 7: "The Lecho Formation is typically regarded as belonging to the latest stages of the Cretaceous, either as (?Upper) Campanian–Maastrichtian ... or strictly Maastrichtian"; "The latest literature on the Lecho Formation ... however, considers it to be Maastrichtian in age."
- hendrickx2024a, p. 12: "Lecho Formation, Balbuena Subgroup, Salta Group; early or mid Maastrichtian, Upper Cretaceous".

**Ruled out.** Nothing among the works cited here.

**Open.** Two points. Whether the Campanian should stand: it rests on Powell's faunal correlation, which he himself ranks below an Early Maastrichtian age, and Hendrickx and colleagues report later work as Maastrichtian only. And the road to El Brete: Powell (2003) places the estancia "11 km west of El Tala, upon National Route 9" (powell2003a, p. 6), while Hendrickx and colleagues place the fossil site "2.48 km north of the National Route 6" (hendrickx2024a, p. 6). The two may describe different roads, one passing the estancia and one nearest the site; a map of the area would settle it.

# Lias

## What lithology and boundaries does Cox and colleagues' (1999) definition give the Lias Group?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Cox and colleagues (1999) define the group as predominantly gray, well-bedded marine calcareous mudstone and silty mudstone, with argillaceous limestone mainly in the lower part, siltstone and sandstone mainly in the upper part, and ironstone mainly in the middle. In most areas it rests non-sequentially on the Penarth Group or older strata, and its top is commonly an eroded surface below the Inferior Oolite, or in the Cleveland Basin below the Ravenscar Group or Dogger Formation. Its twelve formations include the Blue Lias, Scunthorpe Mudstone and Charmouth Mudstone. Its thickness, age and lower-boundary formations are in the 2026-10-14 entry of [Lias Group](../../topics/lias-group.md). Governs `lithology` on [Lias](../../../stratigraphy/l/lias.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cox1999a, p. 6, §5.1 Lias Group, Lithology: "Predominantly grey, well bedded, marine calcareous mudstone and silty mudstone; thin tabular or nodular beds of argillaceous limestone ('cementstone'), particularly in the lower part; thicker units of siltstone and sandstone, particularly in the upper part, and ironstone, particularly in the middle part."
- cox1999a, p. 6, §5.1 Lias Group, Lower boundary: "In most areas, markedly non-sequential on Penarth Group or older strata".
- cox1999a, p. 6, §5.1 Lias Group, Upper boundary: "Commonly an eroded surface at the base of the lowest limestone or sandstone of the Inferior Oolite, or (in the Cleveland Basin) of the Ravenscar Group or Dogger Formation."
- cox1999a, p. 6, §5.1 Lias Group, Subdivisions: twelve formations, among them the "Blue Lias Formation", "Charmouth Mudstone Formation" and "Scunthorpe Mudstone Formation".

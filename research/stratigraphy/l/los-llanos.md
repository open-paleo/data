# Los Llanos

## How firmly do Jiménez Velandia and colleagues (2025) date the Los Llanos Formation to the Campanian?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian. Jiménez Velandia and colleagues (2025) call the formation Campanian without qualification in the abstract, the geological setting and the discussion, on charophytes and freshwater ostracods from palustrine muddy facies of the Tama pedotype. Their systematic section hedges the same age ("possibly Campanian") on the same microfossils, together with vertebrates; it restates the one reading with less confidence and adds no other stage. The paper also describes one of two abelisaurid specimens earlier reported only in preliminary form from the formation. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` on [Los Llanos](../../../stratigraphy/l/los-llanos.yml).

**Evidence.**
- jiménezvelandia2025a, p. 505, Abstract: "It was found at the Tama locality, in outcrops of the Campanian Los Llanos Formation".
- jiménezvelandia2025a, p. 506: the formation "yields a vertebrate faunal assemblage that includes two preliminarily reported abelisaurid specimens"; "Here, we describe one of these abelisaurid specimens in detail"; the remains lie "28 meters above the contact with the underlying Permian Patquía Formation".
- jiménezvelandia2025a, p. 509: "an association of microfossils in palustrine muddy facies of the Tama pedotype, including charophytes and freshwater ostracodes …, indicates a Campanian age for the Llanos Formation".
- jiménezvelandia2025a, p. 510: "The holotype was found in a paleosol level of the Tama pedotype of the Los Llanos Formation, approximately 28 meters above the contact with the underlying late Paleozoic Patquía Formation"; "A Late Cretaceous age (possibly Campanian) has been suggested for the Los Llanos Formation based on an association of microfossils, represented by charophytes, freshwater ostracods … and vertebrates".
- jiménezvelandia2025a, p. 524: "the Campanian beds of the Los Llanos Formation in La Rioja Province".

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Cretaceous with no stage, on the strength of the systematic section's hedge.* The hedge rests on the same microfossils that the paper elsewhere says "indicates a Campanian age", and the abstract and discussion state Campanian outright. It qualifies the paper's one reading rather than offering another.

**Open.** The microfossil studies the paper relays for the age have not been read.

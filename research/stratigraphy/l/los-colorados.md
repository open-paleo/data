# Los Colorados

## On what ground does the Los Colorados's Rhaetian stay out?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** On the rules, not on the magnetostratigraphy; the Norian stands. Restates the ground of the 2026-11-23 entry, which ruled the Rhaetian out because the magnetostratigraphy contradicts it, a ground the union rule does not give. Heckert and Lucas (2000) list the formation's theropods among "Rhaetian and probable Rhaetian" ones with no basis given for the formation's age, a passing label in a compilation. Olsen and colleagues (2010) give the formation as "likely at least in part Rhaetian" by correlation through the Fleming Fjord Formation, the age of correlative units, and say that "these correlations require more rigorous independent confirmation"; the magnetostratigraphy of the formation itself, reported by Langer and colleagues (2018), puts its upper strata at about 213 to 216 Ma, Norian. Governs the notes on heckert2000a and olsen2010a on [Los Colorados](../../../stratigraphy/l/los-colorados.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- heckert2000a, p. 70: "Rhaetian and probable Rhaetian theropods include … poorly known theropods from the Los Colorados Formation in Argentina."
- olsen2010a, p. 222: "The Los Colorados Formation is likely at least in part Rhaetian, as is the upper Petrified Forest and Owl Rock members, although again these correlations require more rigorous independent confirmation."

**Ruled out.**
- *Rhaetian.* A compilation label, and the age of correlative units.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Do the Rhaetian of Heckert and Lucas (2000) or the Early Jurassic affinities found by Shubin and Sues (1991) extend the Los Colorados beyond the Norian?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** No. The stage stays Norian, as the 2026-10-01 entry concludes. Heckert and Lucas (2000) count the Los Colorados theropods among the "Rhaetian and probable Rhaetian" ones on a correlation through other tetrapods, chiefly aetosaurs, and Shubin and Sues (1991) find the formation's assemblage clustering weakly with Early Jurassic ones. Both are faunal readings of the fauna that the La Esquina Member yields, and the magnetostratigraphic correlation reported by Langer and colleagues (2018) puts the strata of that fauna at about 213 to 216 Ma, in the Norian. Governs `stages: [Norian]` and `period: [Late Triassic]` on [Los Colorados](../../../stratigraphy/l/los-colorados.yml).

**Evidence.**
- heckert2000a, p. 63, Abstract: the Rhaetian theropod record includes "a poorly known theropod fauna from the Los Colorados Formation of Argentina".
- heckert2000a, p. 70: "Rhaetian and probable Rhaetian theropods include" … "poorly known theropods from the Los Colorados Formation in Argentina".
- arcucci2003a, p. 227: "The age of the Los Colorados Formation has been widely considered as Norian, according to both biostratigraphic and chronostratigraphic analysis. Recently Heckert and Lucas (2000) have referred the Los Colorados Formation to the Rhaethian, basing this statement upon correlations of other tetrapod genera, mainly aetosaurs."
- arcucci2003a, p. 227: "This kind of chronostratigraphical correlation based on co-generic comparisons needs great understanding of the phylogenetic relationships, which is not available at present. Moreover, recognition of the existence of the Rhaethian, a European stage typical of marine facies, is difficult in this predominantly terrestrial South American Triassic basin"; "we consider the age of the Los Colorados Formation as the latest part of the Late Triassic".
- shubin1991a, p. 220: the assemblage, "generally considered to be Late Triassic in age, weakly clustered with those from the Early Jurassic, emphasizing its peculiar, seemingly intermediate faunal composition".
- shubin1991a, p. 222: "The cluster analysis supports the impression that this assemblage may either represent an intermediate fauna or that sampling of this formation may have encompassed both Late Triassic and Early Jurassic strata. It is impossible to ascertain from the published information" … "whether, in fact, all these tetrapod taxa were recovered from the same stratigraphic level."
- langer2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 8: "magnetostratigraphic correlation of upper Los Colorados Formation strata, coeval with those yielding the La Esquina fauna" … "places them between ca. 213 and 216 Ma".
- mcphee2017b, p. 457: "The Los Colorados fauna is restricted to the La Esquina Member (upper third of the formation), with an upper age bound of ~213 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *Rhaetian, from Heckert and Lucas (2000).* The age is a correlation through aetosaurs and other tetrapod genera, not a dating of the rock. Arcucci and Coria (2003) doubt both the method, which they say needs phylogenetic relationships not then available, and the use of a European marine stage in a terrestrial basin. The theropods it concerns come from the fauna whose strata the magnetostratigraphy places at about 213 to 216 Ma, which is Norian on either GTS2020 base of the Rhaetian.
- *Early Jurassic, from the cluster analysis of Shubin and Sues (1991).* Shubin and Sues leave it open, as either an intermediate fauna or a sample mixing Late Triassic and Early Jurassic strata, and give no age. The fauna is now known to come from the La Esquina Member in the upper third of the formation, and the same magnetostratigraphy places those strata in the Norian, superseding the Early Jurassic suggestion.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What stages does the Los Colorados span?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Norian. The papers that place the fauna give the late Norian, and the magnetostratigraphic ages in the record fall in the Norian on either GTS2020 base of the Rhaetian. Governs `stages: [Norian]` and `period: [Late Triassic]` on [Los Colorados](../../../stratigraphy/l/los-colorados.yml).

**Evidence.**
- martínez2011a, p. 209: "the overlying Los Colorados Formation near the close of the Late Triassic (late Norian)".
- mcphee2017b, p. 457: "The Los Colorados fauna is restricted to the La Esquina Member (upper third of the formation), with an upper age bound of ~213 Ma".
- weishampel2004b, p. 528, under 63.1, Provincia de La Rioja: "3. Los Colorados Formation … Age: Norian (Bonaparte 1973; Stipanicic 1983)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Cretaceous.* Reig (1963) reports, in his review of earlier datings, that Bodenbender (1911) assigned "those we now distinguish as Los Colorados Beds" to the "Andean Upper Cretaceous" (reig1963a, p. 17, translated). Reig gives it as history, not as his own reading, and he goes on to the Triassic schemes that replaced it.
- *Rhaetian, from the same review.* Reig also reports that Frenguelli (1948) restricted the beds "to the Rhaetian" and that Groeber and Stipanicic (1952) kept "the Los Colorados in the Rhaetian with a certain hesitation" (reig1963a, p. 17, translated). These are the older faunal correlations that the record's notes already treat as superseded by the magnetostratigraphic ages.
- *Middle Triassic.* Reig gives the *Herrerasaurus* material, which includes a femur found "en los Estratos de Los Colorados", the provenance "'Estratos de Ischigualasto' y 'Estratos de Los Colorados' … Mesotriásico superior" ("Upper Middle Triassic", reig1963a, p. 9). The age is his dating of the Ischigualasto fauna, which he concludes "represents the highest Middle Triassic" (Conclusions, translated), carried onto a provenance line that names both units. It does not date the Los Colorados.
- *Carnian.* The distribution chapter of *The Dinosauria* gives the La Rioja Los Colorados entry as Norian. The "Age: Carnian (Bonaparte 1973; Stipanicic 1983)" printed beside it, in the next column, belongs to the San Juan entry for the Ischigualasto Formation (weishampel2004b, p. 528).

**Open.** Nothing.

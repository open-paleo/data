# Lytle

## What rank and age do the cited works give the Lytle?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** A member of the Purgatoire Formation, of Aptian–Albian age. Finlay (1916) divided the Purgatoire into a lower Lytle Sandstone Member and an upper Glencairn Shale Member, as Mateer (1987) reports, and Brill and Carpenter (2007) and McDonald and colleagues (2010) use the Lytle Member of the Purgatoire. Mateer instead follows Gustason and Kauffman (1985) in making the Lytle the basal formation of a fourfold Dakota Group, and rejects the Purgatoire-Dakota division. The age has one original statement: Mateer's rare ostracods and bivalves from the Lytle, which "indicate an Aptian-Albian age", with homotaxial units to the north and west supporting "Aptian-middle Albian". Brill and Carpenter cite Mateer and Tschudy and colleagues (1984) for their Aptian–Albian, and McDonald and colleagues cite Brill and Carpenter and the same palynological paper, so neither adds a separate dating. Governs `rank: member`, `parent: Purgatoire` and `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on [Lytle](../../../stratigraphy/l/lytle.yml).

**Evidence.**
- mateer1987a, p. 223, Abstract: "The twofold division of the Dakota Group (Purgatoire and Dakota formations) is rejected in favor of a fourfold division comprising the Lytle, Plainview …, Glencairn … and Muddy formations".
- mateer1987a, p. 224: "Finlay divided the Purgatoire Formation into the lower Lytle Sandstone Member and the overlying Glencairn Shale Member"; "The terminology used in this paper largely follows that of Gustason and Kauffman (1985), recognizing the Lytle, Plainview, Glencairn and Muddy formations within the Dakota Group in Colorado".
- mateer1987a, p. 224, Lytle Formation: "The Lytle Formation is the basal formation of the Dakota Group throughout eastern Colorado"; it "unconformably overlies the Jurassic Morrison Formation" and varies in thickness "from zero to several tens of m (36 m at the type section just south of Colorado Springs"; it "reflects a fluvial system that, during the ?Aptian-early Albian, infilled" the relief cut into the Morrison.
- mateer1987a, p. 226: "The upper boundary with the Plainview Formation is sharp and disconformable".
- mateer1987a, p. 226: "Body fossils are extremely scarce in the Lytle, but the rare ostracods and bivalves do indicate an Aptian-Albian age"; fossils from homotaxial units "support an Aptian-middle Albian age for the Lytle"; "Clearly, more fossil data from the Lytle itself are required to firmly establish its age."
- brill2007a, p. 56: "Based on ostracods, bivalves, and palynomorphs, the Lytle Member is Aptian-Albian (Mateer 1987; Tschudy et al. 1984)".
- mcdonald2010b, p. 32, Discussion: "the Aptian–Albian Lytle Member of the Purgatoire Formation of Colorado [11,42]", citing Brill and Carpenter (2007) and Tschudy and colleagues (1984).

**Ruled out.**
- *Lytle Formation of the Dakota Group.* Mateer (1987), after Gustason and Kauffman (1985), ranks the Lytle a formation and drops the Purgatoire. The registry keeps the Purgatoire as a formation with no Dakota Group parent (see [Purgatoire](../p/purgatoire.md), 2026-10-14), so the Lytle stays its member, the rank the *Theiophytalia* works use.

**Open.** The age rests on scarce fossils from the Lytle itself, as Mateer (1987, p. 226) says; more fossil data or a radiometric date from the unit would narrow it.

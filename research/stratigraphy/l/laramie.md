# Laramie

## What do Carpenter and Young (2002) say about the age of the Laramie?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Maastrichtian, by its dinosaur fauna. Carpenter and Young (2002) report dinosaurs from the Upper Cretaceous strata of the Denver Basin at sites in the Laramie, Arapahoe and Denver Formations. They read the numerical dominance of ceratopsians as showing that ceratopsians dominated the basin's Maastrichtian dinosaur fauna and, as Lehman (1987, 2001) had noted, place the basin's dinosaurs in the southernmost part of the *Triceratops*-dominated Maastrichtian or Lancian fauna. The Maastrichtian is a faunal correlation of the basin as a whole, relayed from Lehman, not a dating of the Laramie's own beds. The same discussion finds semiarticulated specimens only in the delta plain environment of the Laramie at Locality 9 and in the more distal part of the basin at Locality 4. Governs `stages: [Maastrichtian]` on [Laramie](../../../stratigraphy/l/laramie.yml).

**Evidence.**
- carpenter2002a, p. 237: "Dinosaurs were first reported from Upper Cretaceous strata of the Denver Basin, Colorado, in 1867"; "dinosaurian fossils have been found at a variety of sites in the Laramie, Arapahoe, and Denver Formations".
- carpenter2002a, p. 250: "Semiarticulated specimens have been found only in the delta plain environment of the Laramie Formation at Locality 9 and in the more distal part of the basin at Locality 4"; ceratopsians "dominated the Maastrichtian dinosaur fauna of the Denver Basin"; "As Lehman (1987, 2001) has noted, the dinosaurs of the Denver Basin are the southernmost part of the *Triceratops*-dominated Maastrichtian or Lancian fauna".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

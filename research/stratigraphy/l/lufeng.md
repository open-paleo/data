# Lufeng

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Lufeng Formation?

*2026-11-22*

**Conclusion.** Of the works on the unit whose age statements were read for this entry, all place the Lufeng Formation in the Early Jurassic except Bien (1940), whose Upper Triassic for the Lower Lufeng Series is a Keuper correlation made before the Yubacun Formation was separated beneath it. The base is Hettangian on the dated core of Chen and colleagues (2026), which also outweighs the Sinemurian floor that Pang and colleagues' (2002) Yubacun correlation implies; the top is Huang and colleagues' (2005) upper Toarcian. Olsen and Galton's (1984) Early Jurassic relays Sigogneau-Russell and Sun (1981) and Cui (1976), and You and colleagues (2014) relay Luo and Wu (1995) and Huang and colleagues, so each counts with its source once. Governs `period: [Early Jurassic]` and `stages: [Hettangian, Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on [Lufeng](../../../stratigraphy/l/lufeng.yml), unchanged. It adds to the 2026-10-01 entry and supersedes none.

**Evidence.**
- bien1940a, p. 229: through these fossils "the lower part of the so-called 'Younger Red Bed Formation' of Lufeng has been accurately dated as of Upper Triassic in age"; p. 232, in the argument for the name "Lufeng Series", not the summary (p. 233): the Lower Lufeng Series "has been reliably dated as of Upper Triassic in age (uppermost middle Keuper), while the *Hybodus*- and Unionid-bearing Upper Lufeng Series might still be of Upper Triassic (upper Keuper), though the possibilities of its being post-Trias, or Lias are not completely ruled out". Bien's own dating is in Keuper and Lias; the only stage names in the paper are in an earlier French reading it quotes (pp. 230–231).
- bien1940a, p. 231: the series is divided "at the level of the insignificant red sandstone", and the dark red and dull purplish beds "should be regarded as one single stratigraphic horizon"; the Upper Lufeng Series begins above the dark red beds.
- you2014b, p. 234, Type locality and horizon: the Shawan Member "is equivalent to the Dull Purplish Beds of the Lower Lufeng Formation (Series) as defined by Bien (1940)"; the formation "overlies the Lower Jurassic Yubacun Formation and is disconformably overlain by the Middle Jurassic Chuanjie Formation"; its age "is probably early - middle Early Jurassic (Hettangian - Pliensbachian), based on biostratigraphic correlations (Luo & Wu 1995)", and "Magnetostratigraphic analysis has indicated late Sinemurian – ?Toarcian for the age of the Lufeng Formation (Huang et al. 2005)".
- olsen1984a, p. 93: the Lower Lufeng Series shares "morganucodontid mammals, tritylodontids, plateosaurid prosauropod dinosaurs, fabrosaurid and heterodontosaurid ornithischian dinosaurs" and "protosuchian crocodiles" with the upper Stormberg, and "Floral evidence strongly suggests that the lower Lufeng, too, is Early Jurassic in age", citing Cui (1976) and Sigogneau-Russell and Sun (1981); p. 87: the upper Stormberg assemblage "is Early Jurassic (Hettangian-Pliensbachian) in age".
- sigogneau-russell1981a, p. 277, under "V - Early Jurassic": the purple beds are "differently allocated according to the various authors either to the Rhaetic or to the earliest Jurassic", while "The upper level at least, composed of dark red beds (mudstones and sandstones), is now admitted, both by geologists and paleontologists, to be Liassic", because "the same coal level underlying the lower Lufeng in Yunnan has also been found in Szechuan where it is dated as late Triassic on evidence from fossil plants"; pp. 277–278: "further study is necessary before its age can be definitively set"; p. 278: from the base of the dark red beds come "the famous Chinese 'Triassic' mammalian specimens", the quotation marks the paper's own.
- huang2005a, p. 324, Table 1: the Fengjiahe Formation (J₁f) and the Lufeng Formation (J₁l), with its Zhangjiaao and Shawan members, under the Lower Jurassic, and the Shezi Formation (T₃sh) beneath; p. 325: "The upper part of the Lufeng Formation, above site 103, can roughly be correlated with the alternating-polarity zone of the upper Toarcian", while the sites "from the base of the Zhangjiaao Member through the Shawan Member, have unstable rock magnetism and disordered directions" (translated); p. 326: the Yubacun Formation, "the variegated transitional beds between the Upper Triassic Shezi Formation and the Lower Jurassic Lufeng Formation, should be assigned to the Jurassic" (translated). Its one Rhaetian is a polarity zone in the Yubacun Formation at the Yangjiazhuang section, sites 734–711, "spanning the Early Jurassic Hettangian to the Late Triassic Rhaetian" (p. 326, translated), below this unit; the paper names no Norian.
- chen2026a, pp. 2–3, Results: "In drill core WK23-1, the Triassic–Jurassic transitional Yubacun Formation is stratigraphically bounded by the overlying Lufeng Formation (Lower Jurassic) at 15.23 m depth and the underlying Shezi Formation (Upper Triassic)"; p. 3, Results: the main carbon-isotope excursion "remains low through the upper Yubacun Formation and into the basal Lufeng Formation"; p. 4, Carbon–mercury dynamics: the timescale is anchored "to the established high-precision U-Pb age for the onset of the I-CIE (and thus the ETME) at 201.51 ± 0.15 Ma". The core is "in the Wande section of Kunming Basin" (p. 2).
- pang2002a, p. 6: "冯家河组(原下禄丰组)" ("the Fengjiahe Formation (former Lower Lufeng Formation)", translated); the Yubacun section "似应属于三叠系—侏罗系混合极性(TJ-M)超时" ("seems to belong to the Triassic–Jurassic mixed-polarity (TJ-M) superchron", translated), set against a 1995 polarity compilation for the Rhaetian–Sinemurian; p. 7: "舍资组划归诺利阶—瑞替阶" ("the Shezi Formation [is assigned] to the Norian–Rhaetian", translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Triassic, after Bien (1940).* Bien dates the Lower Lufeng Series, which is this unit, to the "uppermost middle Keuper" on its vertebrates. That is a correlation to the German Keuper, not to a stage, made before the Yubacun Formation was established between the Shezi and the Lufeng (Pang and colleagues 2002); the Triassic–Jurassic boundary now lies inside the Yubacun (see the 2026-10-01 entry). Bien's hedge toward the Lias concerns the Upper Lufeng Series, which lies above the dark red beds, not this unit (our reading of p. 231).
- *Middle to Late Triassic, up to the Carnian.* An earlier French reading Bien quotes for the "couches versicolores", which take in all the beds below the wine-red beds, as "Middle and Upper Triassic, or only Upper ... reaching up into the Carnian" (bien1940a, p. 231, translated), made "on stratigraphic and tectonic grounds" without fossils. Bien's vertebrates already moved the lower beds up to the Keuper.
- *Rhaetian for the purple beds.* Sigogneau-Russell and Sun (1981) report that "various authors" put the lower level in the Rhaetic or the earliest Jurassic, and name none of them in the passage. A reported spread adds no stage until each reading is traced to its source, and the Triassic–Jurassic boundary lies below the Lufeng.
- *A Sinemurian base, after Pang and colleagues (2002).* Their "Hettangian–lower Sinemurian" for the Yubacun would start this unit no earlier than the Sinemurian. Their equivalence correlates a mixed-polarity section against a 1995 polarity compilation with no numerical anchor, while the age model of Chen and colleagues (2026) is anchored to a U-Pb date and puts the topmost Yubacun at about 200.17 Ma, in the Hettangian. The two are different sections: the Yubacun type section in Lufeng County and the Wande core in the Kunming Basin.

**Open.** Whether the base of the Lufeng has the same age in Lufeng County as in the Kunming Basin core; the base may be diachronous between the two areas, and a dated horizon at the base of the formation in Lufeng County would settle it. The top rests on Huang and colleagues' (2005) polarity correlation, which they could not carry through the lower members.

## Does Wang and colleagues' "Sinemurian to Pliensbachian" put the Zhangjiawa Member in the Pliensbachian?

*2026-10-23*

**Conclusion.** No. Wang and colleagues (2025) reach the upper unit of the Lufeng Formation without naming the member, and their range is an inference from below: the underlying Yubacun Formation is Hettangian to early Sinemurian, so the upper Lufeng is "probably constrained" to younger ages. That sets a floor, not a top, and the paper gives no evidence for the Pliensbachian end. Governs `stages: [Hettangian, Sinemurian]` on [Zhangjiawa](../../../stratigraphy/z/zhangjiawa.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- wang2025a, p. 7: "The upper unit of the Lufeng Formation, which has also produced a large number of non-sauropodan sauropodomorphs, is then probably constrained from Sinemurian to Pliensbachian"; p. 2: the Yubacun Formation is "Hettangian to early Sinemurian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Pliensbachian for the Zhangjiawa.* The upper bound has no stated basis, and a younger age inferred from an older unit beneath is a limit, not a dating of the member.

**Open.** A date from within the upper Lufeng would settle its top.

## Does the upper member keep the headword Zhangjiawa?

*2026-10-06*

**Conclusion.** Yes. "Zhangjiawa" is the name the English-language vertebrate literature prints for the member, and the registry follows English usage. Zhangjia'ao, the romanization of 张家坳, and Zhangjiaao stay as variants. Governs the headword and `variants` of [Zhangjiawa](../../../stratigraphy/z/zhangjiawa.yml). Settles the **Open** line of the entry on the member's name, whose "appears once" covered only the sources read for that note.

**Evidence.**
- irmis2004a, p. 11: "Zhangjiawa Member"; norman2007a, p. 865: "Zhangjiawa Member (Fang et al., 2000)". The name also appears in luo1994a, lucas2001b, irmis2008a, barrett2005b, zhang2009b and zhang2026b, and once in the translation of fang2000a.
- zhang2018a, p. 1: "the Zhangjiaao Member (upper Dark Red Beds)"; hu2026a, p. 3: "Zhangjiaao Member".

**Ruled out.**
- *Renaming to Zhangjia'ao.* It matches the characters, but fewer English papers use it than "Zhangjiawa", and the records' unit names follow what English-language papers print.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the Lufeng Formation Hettangian–Toarcian, with no Triassic?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Yes. The Triassic–Jurassic boundary lies inside the underlying [Yubacun Formation](../../../stratigraphy/y/yubacun.yml), so the base of the Lufeng is Hettangian; its top is Toarcian. Governs `stages: [Hettangian, Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on Lufeng and the Hettangian on its [Shawan Member](../../../stratigraphy/s/shawan.yml).

**Evidence.**
- pang2002a, pp. 1–2: the type-section sequence "from top to bottom is the Fengjiahe Formation, the Yubacun Formation, and the Shezi Formation" (translated); p. 7: the Yubacun "大体相当海相的赫唐阶—下辛涅缪尔阶" ("roughly equivalent to the marine Hettangian–lower Sinemurian", translated), from floras, bivalves, ostracods and magnetostratigraphy.
- chen2026a, pp. 2, 5: drill core WK23-1, Wande section, Kunming Basin; the Triassic–Jurassic boundary "placed at 111.29 m, 201.4 Ma based on tuning relative to I-CIE onset at 201.51 Ma" within the Yubacun, and the *Wudingloong* horizon "in the topmost Yubacun Formation at approximately ~200.17 Ma".
- hesselbo2020a, p. 1004, Table 26.4: Hettangian 201.4–199.5 Ma. Chen and colleagues give no stage for the Lufeng; reading its base as Hettangian follows from a Yubacun top younger than ~200.17 Ma and older than 199.5 Ma (our inference).
- wang2025a, p. 2: "Lower Jurassic Yubacun Formation (Hettangian to early Sinemurian, probably 200.17 Ma)", citing Chen and colleagues' preprint.

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Triassic.* The *Stratigraphic Lexicon of China* gives the Lufeng Group "Late Triassic" (zhang2009b, p. 1480), the older usage. The boundary now lies below the Lufeng.
- *A Sinemurian base.* Huang and colleagues (2005) bracket the Lufeng at "大致属于上西涅缪尔阶至图阿尔阶" ("roughly upper Sinemurian to Toarcian", translated; huang2005a, p. 328) from a magnetic polarity correlation without a numerical anchor; the body text reads middle Sinemurian for one site range (p. 325). Chen and colleagues' astrochronology supersedes the base.
- *The Shawan Member's Hettangian from crest biomechanics.* Liang and colleagues relay "the Shawan member of the lower Lufeng Formation (Hettangian) (Xing et al. 2015)" (liang2024a, author's manuscript, p. 4). The dating basis is Chen and colleagues (2026).

**Open.** The age of the Lufeng's upper part, and whether the Pliensbachian assigned to some Fengjiahe taxa has a source; it was not traced for this note.

## How is the upper Lufeng member spelled?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** The Chinese is 张家坳, romanized Zhangjia'ao; "Zhangjiawa" appears once, in a translation. Governs the headword and variants of [Zhangjiawa](../../../stratigraphy/z/zhangjiawa.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huang2005a, pp. 323–325: "张家坳段", printed the same way in all four places (checked against the page images).
- fang2000a, English translation, p. 1: "the Lufeng Fm. (including the Shawan and Zhangjia'ao members)"; the translation writes Zhangjia'ao five times and "Zhangjiawa Member (J1lfzh)" once (p. 5).

**Ruled out.**
- *Zhangjiaba, Zhangjiawu.* Readings of 坳 as 坝 or 坞 are errors in a machine transcription of Huang and colleagues (2005), not variants in print.
- *"Dull Purplish Beds" and "Dark Red Beds" as Fang's terms.* Fang's translation uses "dark purple beds" and "deep red beds" (pp. 5–6); "Dull Purplish" and "Dark Red Beds" are Bien's (1940) terms, used by Young and by Barrett and colleagues.

**Open.** Whether to change the headword to Zhangjia'ao. "Zhangjiawa" would correspond to 张家洼, which no source read for this note prints.

# Lufeng

## Is the Lufeng Formation Hettangian–Toarcian, with no Triassic?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Yes. The Triassic–Jurassic boundary lies inside the underlying [Yubacun Formation](../../../stratigraphy/y/yubacun.yml), so the base of the Lufeng is Hettangian; its top is Toarcian. Governs `stages: [Hettangian, Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian]` on Lufeng and the Hettangian on its [Shawan Member](../../../stratigraphy/s/shawan.yml).

**Evidence.**
- pang2002a, pp. 1–2: the type-section sequence "from top to bottom is the Fengjiahe Formation, the Yubacun Formation, and the Shezi Formation" (translated); p. 7: the Yubacun "大体相当海相的赫唐阶—下辛涅缪尔阶" ("roughly equivalent to the marine Hettangian–lower Sinemurian", translated), from floras, bivalves, ostracods and magnetostratigraphy.
- chen2026a, pp. 2, 5: drill core WK23-1, Wande section, Kunming Basin; the Triassic–Jurassic boundary "placed at 111.29 m, 201.4 Ma based on tuning relative to I-CIE onset at 201.51 Ma" within the Yubacun, and the *Wudingloong* horizon "in the topmost Yubacun Formation at approximately ~200.17 Ma".
- hesselbo2020a, p. 1004, Table 26.4: Hettangian 201.4–199.5 Ma. Chen and colleagues give no stage for the Lufeng; reading its base as Hettangian follows from a Yubacun top younger than ~200.17 Ma and older than 199.5 Ma (our inference).
- wang2025a, p. 2: "Lower Jurassic Yubacun Formation (Hettangian to early Sinemurian, probably 200.17 Ma)", citing Chen and colleagues' preprint.

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Triassic.* The *Stratigraphic Lexicon of China* gives the Lufeng Group "Late Triassic" (zhang2009b, p. 1480), the older usage. The boundary now lies below the Lufeng.
- *A Sinemurian base.* Huang and colleagues (2005) bracket the Lufeng at "大致属于上西涅缪尔阶至图阿尔阶" ("roughly upper Sinemurian to Toarcian", translated; huang2005a, p. 328) from a magnetic polarity correlation without a numerical anchor; the body text reads middle Sinemurian for one site range (p. 325). Chen and colleagues' astrochronology supersedes the base.
- *The Shawan Member's Hettangian from crest biomechanics.* Liang and colleagues relay "the Shawan member of the lower Lufeng Formation (Hettangian) (Xing et al. 2015)" (liang2024a, author's manuscript, p. 4). The dating basis is Chen and colleagues (2026).

**Open.** The age of the Lufeng's upper part, and whether the Pliensbachian assigned to some Fengjiahe taxa has a source; it was not traced for this note.

## How is the upper Lufeng member spelled?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** The Chinese is 张家坳, romanized Zhangjia'ao; "Zhangjiawa" appears once, in a translation. Governs the headword and variants of [Zhangjiawa](../../../stratigraphy/z/zhangjiawa.yml).

**Evidence.**
- huang2005a, pp. 323–325: "张家坳段", printed the same way in all four places (checked against the page images).
- fang2000a, English translation, p. 1: "the Lufeng Fm. (including the Shawan and Zhangjia'ao members)"; the translation writes Zhangjia'ao five times and "Zhangjiawa Member (J1lfzh)" once (p. 5).

**Ruled out.**
- *Zhangjiaba, Zhangjiawu.* Readings of 坳 as 坝 or 坞 are errors in a machine transcription of Huang and colleagues (2005), not variants in print.
- *"Dull Purplish Beds" and "Dark Red Beds" as Fang's terms.* Fang's translation uses "dark purple beds" and "deep red beds" (pp. 5–6); "Dull Purplish" and "Dark Red Beds" are Bien's (1940) terms, used by Young and by Barrett and colleagues.

**Open.** Whether to change the headword to Zhangjia'ao. "Zhangjiawa" would correspond to 张家洼, which no source read for this note prints.

# Liangtoutang

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Liangtoutang, and what did the name Laijia cover?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Albian and Cenomanian. Zheng and colleagues (2012) report earlier radiometric dates of 105.9 to 103.2 Ma, which are Albian, and correlation pointing to the early Late Cretaceous, and place the formation in the "Albian–Cenomanian"; He and colleagues (2012) date three tuffs in it at about 96 to 99 Ma, which is Cenomanian. Yu and colleagues (2010) relay zircon ages from a regional survey that bracket the formation's lower part: 108 Ma from the underlying Tangshang Formation, 101 Ma from a volcanic layer less than 10 m below the beds with honeycomb eggs and dinosaurs, and 94 Ma near the top of the overlying Chichengshan. They put the honeycomb eggs in the lower Liangtoutang, so the 101 Ma layer probably lies at or just below the formation's base; that placement is inferred, as the paper names no formation for the layer. Governs `stages: [Albian, Cenomanian]` on [Liangtoutang](../../../stratigraphy/l/liangtoutang.yml).

The older name Laijia covered more than this formation. Yu and colleagues (2010) report that the 1982 provincial scheme called a unit above the Tangshang the Laijia Formation, in two members, and that the two were renamed the Liangtoutang and Chichengshan Formations in 1996; the passage concerns the Lishui Basin, where the scheme was applied, and their Table 1 shows the same two-member Laijia above the Tangshang in the 1989 provincial geology. He and colleagues (2012) use Laijia for the unit between the Tangshang and Chichengshan alone.

**Evidence.**
- zheng2012a, p. 209: "The Liangtoutang Formation consists predominantly of red sandstones, representing a shallow lacustrine environment. Fossil eggs and dinosaur bones are abundant in the formation".
- zheng2012a, p. 209: "The formation has been assigned previously to late Early Cretaceous, and radiometrically dated at 105.9–103.2 Ma".
- zheng2012a, p. 210: "Fossil comparison and stratigraphic correlation also indicate that the Liangtoutang Formation was deposited during the early Late Cretaceous"; "Taking into account the radiometric dates and stratigraphic correlation, we place it in the mid-Cretaceous, Albian–Cenomanian."
- zheng2012a, p. 210, Systematic palaeontology: "Stratigraphic horizon. Albian–Cenomanian, Liangtoutang Formation."; p. 208, Keywords: "Albian–Cenomanian".
- zheng2012a, p. 208, Abstract: "the Liangtoutang Formation (Aptian–Cenomanian)".
- he2012a, p. 659: "Our dating results suggest that the ages of the Laijia and Chichengshan formations are about 96–99 Ma (Cenomanian) and 91–94 Ma (Turonian), respectively".
- yu2010a, p. 96: the upper member of the Chaochuan Formation was called "the Laijia Formation, divided into two lithologic members (in 1996 the two members of the Laijia Formation were renamed the Liangtoutang and Chichengshan formations)" (translated).
- yu2010a, p. 97: honeycomb eggs occur "the lower parts of the Liangtoutang, Jinhua and Chaochuan formations, that is, the lowest horizon of the egg-bearing rocks" (translated).
- yu2010a, pp. 98–99: the lower Tangshang volcanics have "108 Ma (zircon U-Pb age)"; "a volcanic rock layer less than 10 m below the beds yielding honeycomb eggs and dinosaurs ... has a zircon U-Pb age of 101 Ma, and the volcanic rocks near the top of the Chichengshan Formation have a zircon U-Pb age of 94 Ma" (translated), each footnoted to the 2004 Shengxian sheet survey report.

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian to Cenomanian.* Only the abstract of Zheng and colleagues (2012) prints it. The keywords, the stratigraphic horizon and the age discussion of the same paper give Albian–Cenomanian, and the dates it cites are Albian, so the Aptian is a slip that the paper itself contradicts.
- *Late Early Cretaceous alone.* Zheng and colleagues (2012) report it as the earlier assignment and replace it with Albian–Cenomanian; the tuff ages of He and colleagues (2012) are Cenomanian.

**Open.** Whether the variant Laijia should carry a note that its earlier sense included the Chichengshan.

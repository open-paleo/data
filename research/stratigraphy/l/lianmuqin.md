# Lianmuqin

## Where does the Lianmuqin's Valanginian to Albian range come from in Zheng and colleagues (2023)?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Corrects "ostracods and pollen" in the 2026-09-30 entry's Evidence: the fossil Zheng and colleagues cite is the clam shrimp *Cratostracus*, not an ostracod, and that entry's conclusion is unchanged. The range comes from the paper's own age model, which joins a dated tuff at the formation's base to fossils relayed from earlier works. The tuff is the marker "that separates the Shengjinkou and Lianmuqin Formations" near Urho, so its 135.2 ± 0.9 Ma date limits the age of the formation's base, which "could have been deposited as early as the Valanginian". The clam shrimp *Cratostracus*, which also occurs in the Aptian Guantou Formation, and a *Cicatricosisporites-Interulobites-Classopollis-Tricolpites* pollen assemblage with angiosperm pollen give "an Aptian–Albian age for the Lianmuqin Formation of the southern Junggar Basin". The model puts "the Lianmuqin Formation as Valanginian to Albian", the top formation of the Tugulu Group. Governs `stages: [Valanginian, Hauterivian, Barremian, Aptian, Albian]` and `parent: Tugulu` on [Lianmuqin](../../../stratigraphy/l/lianmuqin.yml).

**Evidence.**
- zheng2023a, p. 2: "the Tugulu Group consists (in ascending order) of the Qingshuihe, Hutubihe, Shengjinkou, and Lianmuqin Formations"; "A distinct, white tuffaceous siltstone ..., lying ∼30 m below the vertebrate-bearing layers, serves as a marker horizon that separates the Shengjinkou and Lianmuqin Formations in the NW Junggar Basin".
- zheng2023a, p. 5: "The Lianmuqin Formation contains the clam shrimp fossil *Cratostracus* sp., which also occurs in the Guantou Formation (Aptian)"; the pollen "includes the *Cicatricosisporites-Interulobites-Classopollis-Tricolpites* assemblage along with occasional angiosperm pollens"; "our isotopic age from the top of the Shengjinkou Formation indicates that the Lianmuqin Formation could have been deposited as early as the Valanginian"; the age model gives "the Lianmuqin Formation as Valanginian to Albian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian–Albian only, on the fossils.* The clam shrimp and pollen are the paper's only evidence that dates the formation positively, but Zheng and colleagues do not confine it to the Aptian and Albian: their tuff below the formation lets it begin in the Valanginian, and their age model runs from Valanginian to Albian.

**Open.** Nothing beyond the 2026-09-30 entry's Open line.

## What stages do the Wuerho Lianmuqin taxa take?

*2026-10-10*

**Conclusion.** Valanginian to Albian, the only range stated for their horizon and, as the 2026-09-30 entry has it, the formation's own range. The Barremian–Aptian these records carried came from a reading of Xu and colleagues (2018) that the paper does not support: its "(Barremian-Aptian?)" is the *Xiyunykus* horizon at Wucaiwan. Governs `stage` on [*Xinjiangovenator parvus*](../../../genera/X/Xinjiangovenator.yml), [*Tugulusaurus faciles*](../../../genera/T/Tugulusaurus.yml), [*Phaedrolosaurus ilikensis*](../../../genera/P/Phaedrolosaurus.yml) and [*Kelmayisaurus petrolicus*](../../../genera/K/Kelmayisaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- rauhut2005c, for *Tugulusaurus*, *Phaedrolosaurus* and *Xinjiangovenator*: "Locality and Horizon-Wuerho, Lianmugin Formation, Xinjiang, China. Age-?Valanginian-Albian (Shen and Mateer, 1992)".
- brusatte2012c: "Lianmugin Formation, Tugulu Group (Early Cretaceous: ?Valanginian-Albian)"; the age "is not known with certainty, but falls somewhere within the Valanginian to the Albian".
- xu2018c, p. 2853, for *Xiyunykus*: "Wucaiwan area, Junggar Basin, Xinjiang, China; Upper Cretaceous (Barremian-Aptian?) upper part of the Tugulu Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *Barremian–Aptian for the Wuerho taxa.* Xu and colleagues give it only for the Wucaiwan horizon; Fowler and Hall (2020) relay it for *Tugulusaurus* from the same paper.

**Open.** Shen and Mateer (1992), the source Rauhut and Xu and the Dinosauria tables give for "?Valanginian-Albian", has not been read; nor has Zhao (1980), which Zheng and colleagues use for the position of the vertebrate layer in the lower Lianmuqin.

## Is the Lianmuqin Formation Valanginian?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** No. The Valanginian date is a maximum age from a tuff below the formation; the Lianmuqin itself is Valanginian at the oldest and may be as young as Albian. Governs `stages` on Lianmuqin (Valanginian–Albian), and keeps species from the lower Lianmuqin, such as [*Wuerhosaurus homheni*](../../../genera/W/Wuerhosaurus.yml) and [*Tugulusaurus faciles*](../../../genera/T/Tugulusaurus.yml), from being narrowed to the Valanginian.

**Evidence.**
- zheng2023a, p. 1 (online-first version): a CA-ID-TIMS "U-Pb age of 135.2 ± 0.9 Ma" gives "a Valanginian maximum age for the Urho fauna".
- zheng2023a, pp. 2, 5: the tuff is "from the top of the Shengjinkou Formation", "lying ∼30 m below the vertebrate-bearing layers"; the date "does not preclude a post-Valanginian depositional age".
- zheng2023a, p. 5: ostracods and pollen give "an Aptian–Albian age for the Lianmuqin Formation of the southern Junggar Basin", relayed from earlier work.

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Valanginian.* Paul (2024) gives "AGE Early Cretaceous, late Valanginian" with "lower Lianmuqin" for *Psittacosaurus? xinjiangensis* (paul2024a, p. 188) and for *Tugulusaurus* (paul2024b, p. 228), citing no source. Jia and colleagues (2024) give the Lianmuqin as "in the late Valanginian age (Zheng et al. 2023)" (jia2024a, p. 8), reading Zheng and colleagues' maximum as an age.
- *Upper Cretaceous.* Xu and colleagues (2018) write "Upper Cretaceous (Barremian-Aptian?) upper part of the Tugulu Group" (xu2018c, p. 2853); the stages they give are Early Cretaceous, so "Upper" appears to be a slip (an inference).

**Open.** How young the lower Lianmuqin at Urho is. Zheng and colleagues (2023, p. 5) call the southern-Junggar Aptian–Albian "a maximum depositional age" for it, which, read literally, conflicts with treating the formation as bounded only from below. Tucker and colleagues (2022) note "it is unclear which of its formations dates to this age" (tucker2022a, p. 17). Barremian–Aptian assignments for the Tugulu Group (fowler2020b, accepted manuscript, p. 4; meso2024a, p. 142) are relays of Xu and colleagues (2018) and Eberth and colleagues (2001).

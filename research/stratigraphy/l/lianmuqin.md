# Lianmuqin

## Is the Lianmuqin Formation Valanginian?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** No. The Valanginian date is a maximum age from a tuff below the formation; the Lianmuqin itself is Valanginian at the oldest and may be as young as Albian. Governs `stages` on Lianmuqin (Valanginian–Albian), and keeps species from the lower Lianmuqin, such as [*Wuerhosaurus homheni*](../../../genera/W/Wuerhosaurus.yml) and [*Tugulusaurus faciles*](../../../genera/T/Tugulusaurus.yml), from being narrowed to the Valanginian.

**Evidence.**
- zheng2023a, p. 1 (online-first version): a CA-ID-TIMS "U-Pb age of 135.2 ± 0.9 Ma" gives "a Valanginian maximum age for the Urho fauna".
- zheng2023a, pp. 2, 5: the tuff is "from the top of the Shengjinkou Formation", "lying ∼30 m below the vertebrate-bearing layers"; the date "does not preclude a post-Valanginian depositional age".
- zheng2023a, p. 5: ostracods and pollen give "an Aptian–Albian age for the Lianmuqin Formation of the southern Junggar Basin", relayed from earlier work.

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Valanginian.* Paul (2024) gives "AGE Early Cretaceous, late Valanginian" with "lower Lianmuqin" for *Psittacosaurus? xinjiangensis* (paul2024a, p. 188) and for *Tugulusaurus* (paul2024b, p. 228), citing no source. Jia and colleagues (2024) give the Lianmuqin as "in the late Valanginian age (Zheng et al. 2023)" (jia2024a, p. 8), reading Zheng and colleagues' maximum as an age.
- *Upper Cretaceous.* Xu and colleagues (2018) write "Upper Cretaceous (Barremian-Aptian?) upper part of the Tugulu Group" (xu2018c, p. 2853); the stages they give are Early Cretaceous, so "Upper" appears to be a slip (an inference).

**Open.** How young the lower Lianmuqin at Urho is. Zheng and colleagues (2023, p. 5) call the southern-Junggar Aptian–Albian "a maximum depositional age" for it, which, read literally, conflicts with treating the formation as bounded only from below. Tucker and colleagues (2022) note "it is unclear which of its formations dates to this age" (tucker2022a, p. 17). Barremian–Aptian assignments for the Tugulu Group (fowler2020b, accepted manuscript, p. 4; meso2024a, p. 142) are relays of Xu and colleagues (2018) and Eberth and colleagues (2001).

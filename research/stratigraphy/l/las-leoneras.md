# Las Leoneras

## Does the Las Leoneras keep the Toarcian?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No; the Toarcian is dropped. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry on the stages and answers its Open line. Pol and colleagues (2011) relay "Pliensbachian–Toarcian" from Figari and Courtade (1993) and "Upper Sinemurian–Toarcian" from Page and colleagues (1999), and add that the beds have no direct date. Page and colleagues give the formation only as Liassic "con reservas", from its stratigraphic position; the chart in their paper that draws it through the Toarcian is Figari and Courtade's. Both readings therefore trace to Figari and Courtade (1993), which has not been read, and a reported reading adds a stage only once its original is read. Cúneo and colleagues (2013) date a tuff in the upper formation, about 40 m below the Lonco Trapial, at 188.946 ± 0.096 Ma, early Pliensbachian, and give the formation "a Sinemurian to Pliensbachian age". Governs `stages: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian]` and the notes on pol2011b, cúneo2013a and page1999a on [Las Leoneras](../../../stratigraphy/l/las-leoneras.yml); [*Leonerasaurus taquetrensis*](../../../genera/L/Leonerasaurus.yml), Pliensbachian, is unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- pol2011b, p. 5: the formation is "more specifically referred to the Pliensbachian–Toarcian [32] or Upper Sinemurian–Toarcian [33], although no direct datings of these sediments are available".
- page1999a, p. 462: "La edad de esta unidad, liásica con reservas, surge de su posición estratigráfica" ["The age of this unit, Liassic with reservations, stems from its stratigraphic position", translated].
- page1999a, p. 468, Cuadro 2, captioned "(FIGARI Y COURTADE 1993)": "F. LAS LEONERAS" drawn from the upper Sinemurian to the top of the Toarcian.
- cúneo2013a, p. 3: tuff sample LL041012-2 was "collected from the Las Leoneras Formation in its type locality (Cañadón Las Leoneras, Fig. 1), about 40 m below the basal agglomerates of the overlying Lonco Trapial Formation"; p. 5, Table 1: "188.946 | 0.096".
- cúneo2013a, p. 8: "The results indicate a Sinemurian to Pliensbachian age for the Las Leoneras Formation and its equivalents".

**Ruled out.**
- *Toarcian from Pol and colleagues (2011) and Page and colleagues (1999).* Both relay Figari and Courtade (1993), unread.

**Open.** Figari and Courtade (1993) has not been read; it would show what their Toarcian rests on.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Las Leoneras?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Early Jurassic. Pol and colleagues (2011) report that the formation had been referred to the Pliensbachian–Toarcian or to the late Sinemurian–Toarcian, though no direct date for it existed, and judge its rift setting and its position below the Lonco Trapial Formation "consistent with a Lower Jurassic age". Cúneo and colleagues (2013) supply a direct date: a tuff about 40 m below the base of the Lonco Trapial gives 188.946 ± 0.096 Ma, and they place the start of deposition "most probably in the latest Sinemurian" and the formation as a whole in the Sinemurian to Pliensbachian. The Middle Jurassic upper limit that Pol and colleagues (2011) take from the Lonco Trapial is treated in [Lonco Trapial](lonco-trapial.md). Governs `period: [Early Jurassic]` on [Las Leoneras](../../../stratigraphy/l/las-leoneras.yml).

**Evidence.**
- pol2011b, p. 5, Systematic Paleontology: the formation was "more specifically referred to the Pliensbachian–Toarcian [32] or Upper Sinemurian–Toarcian [33], although no direct datings of these sediments are available".
- pol2011b, p. 5, Systematic Paleontology: "the geological context of the area and the stratigraphic relationship with the Lonco Trapial Formation are consistent with a Lower Jurassic age for the Las Leoneras Formation. However, an Upper Triassic age cannot be completely ruled out at the moment, as there is not a well-defined lower constraint for the age of this unit."
- cúneo2013a, p. 1269 (in-press version, p. 3), Las Leoneras Formation: a tuff "about 40 m below the basal agglomerates of the overlying Lonco Trapial Formation" gives "a weighted mean 206Pb/238U date of 188.946± 0.096/0.13/0.24 Ma".
- cúneo2013a, p. 1271 (in-press version, p. 5), Geological implications: deposition of the formation "over the basement igneous and metamorphic rocks (Mamil Choike Formation) started in Early Jurassic, most probably in the latest Sinemurian".
- cúneo2013a, p. 1274 (in-press version, p. 8), Concluding remarks: "The results indicate a Sinemurian to Pliensbachian age for the Las Leoneras Formation and its equivalents".

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Triassic.* Pol and colleagues (2011) say only that it "cannot be completely ruled out at the moment", because the formation has no well-defined lower limit. That is a possibility, not a reading, and it adds no epoch under the rule on epochs in `period` in [methods](../../methods.md). Cúneo and colleagues (2013) later date a tuff within the formation to the Pliensbachian and place its base in the latest Sinemurian.

**Open.** The Toarcian in `stages` rests on the "Pliensbachian–Toarcian" and "Upper Sinemurian–Toarcian" readings that Pol and colleagues (2011) relay from Figari and Courtade (1993) and Page and colleagues (1999). Cúneo and colleagues (2013) date the formation Sinemurian to Pliensbachian and the overlying Lonco Trapial Pliensbachian–Toarcian. Reading the two relayed works would show whether either is an original dating that carries the Toarcian.

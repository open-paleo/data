# Lameta

## On what ground do Chatterjee's Santonian and Huene and Matley's stages stay out?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** On the rules; the Maastrichtian stands. Confirms the 2026-11-23 entry's rulings and restates one ground. Chatterjee's (1978) "The lower limit may extend down to Santonian" names a stage only as a possible extension of his bound, which [methods](../../methods.md) now excludes for stages as for epochs. Huene and Matley (1933) date Pisdura by correlation with the Madagascan beds that Depéret and Lemoine dated, the age of a correlative unit, and take Bara Simla to be "a little older", supported by an "age not high in the Upper Cretaceous" from its fauna's grade, a bound; their Turonian and lowest Senonian rest on these, not on the later work that the 2026-11-23 entry cites against them. Governs `stages` on [Lameta](../../../stratigraphy/l/lameta.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- chatterjee1978a, p. 571: "The lower limit may extend down to Santonian as the megalosaur is highly advanced and the tyrannosaur is highly primitive."
- huene1933a, p. 71: "From the presence of the last-mentioned species and genus, the fauna of Pisdura is inferred to be contemporaneous with the fauna described from Madagascar by Depéret."
- huene1933a, p. 71: "For the present, therefore, we may take the fossiliferous beds at Jubbulpore to be Turonian and the Pisdura beds to be lowest Senonian."

**Ruled out.**
- *Santonian from Chatterjee.* A possibility beyond his bound.
- *Turonian and lowest Senonian from Huene and Matley.* The age of a correlative unit, and a bound.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Lameta?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Maastrichtian. Jain and Bandyopadhyay (1997) date the Titanosaurus colberti horizon at Dongargaon Maastrichtian, following the review of the Dongargaon biota by Mohabey and colleagues (1993) and the advanced grade of the new titanosaur, and name four works that give the formation in general the same age. Wilson and colleagues (2003) place the Rajasaurus skeleton at Rahioli in Maastrichtian beds, below an egg-bearing limestone equivalent to the Main Lameta limestone of Bara Simla, whose Maastrichtian they relay from its microfossils and its position beneath the Deccan Trap basalts. Chatterjee (1978) reads the whole unit as Late Cretaceous, puts its upper limit in the Maastrichtian by correlation with the Ariyalur Stage, and puts the lower third above the Turonian that Huene and Matley (1933) had given it. Governs `stages: [Maastrichtian]` on [Lameta](../../../stratigraphy/l/lameta.yml).

**Evidence.**
- jain1997a, p. 117: "Age—Late Cretaceous (Maastrichtian)", for the Titanosaurus colberti horizon in the Lameta Formation at Dongargaon.
- jain1997a, p. 116: the Dongargaon biota suggested "a terminal Cretaceous (=Maastrichtian) age"; the new titanosaurid "strongly points to a Maastrichtian age"; "A Maastrichtian age for the Lameta Formation, in general, is also suggested by Buffetaut (1987), Dogra et al. (1988), Udhoji and Mohabey (1991), and Chatterjee (1992)".
- wilson2003a, p. 2: Fig. 1 maps "principal Late Cretaceous (Maastrichtian) dinosaur localities in the Lameta Formation"; the skeleton came from "latest Cretaceous (Maastrichtian) beds near Rahioli".
- wilson2003a, p. 7: the bones lie in "a conglomeratic layer" beneath an egg-bearing limestone "equivalent to the 'Main Lameta limestone' at Bara Simla", which "has been regarded as latest Cretaceous (Maastrichtian) in age on the basis of microvertebrates and associated microfossils" and "its stratigraphic position below the Deccan Trap flood basalts".
- chatterjee1978a, p. 574: the Indosuchus horizon is "Lameta Group in the Narmada Valley, India, late Cretaceous".
- chatterjee1978a, p. 571: "Huene and Matley (1933) considered the age of the Lameta Group to be Turonian"; "the correlation of the Lameta with Ariyalur Stage adds strength to the Maestrichtian assignment of the upper limit of the group".
- chatterjee1978a, p. 571: the lower third is "at least younger than the Turonian as tyrannosaurs are known to occur in the highest Cretaceous"; "The lower limit may extend down to Santonian as the megalosaur is highly advanced and the tyrannosaur is highly primitive".
- huene1933a, p. 71: "we may take the fossiliferous beds at Jubbulpore to be Turonian and the Pisdura beds to be lowest Senonian"; allosaurids "occur plentifully in the Carnosaur bed at Bara Simla, for such assemblages are nowhere known in the uppermost part of the Cretaceous system"; the coelurids, compsognathids and "Lametasaurus, a not very highly specialised Nodosaurian, also testify to an age not high in the Upper Cretaceous".

**Ruled out.**
- *Turonian at Jubbulpore and lowest Senonian at Pisdura.* Huene and Matley (1933) correlate the Pisdura fauna with the Madagascan beds that Depéret (1896) dated Turonian, and argue from the plentiful allosaurids, the coelurids and compsognathids, and the little-specialized nodosaur Lametasaurus that the beds lie low in the Upper Cretaceous. Chatterjee (1978) counters with the tyrannosaur Indosuchus, which puts the lower third above the Turonian, and the later works cited date the formation Maastrichtian.
- *Santonian or Campanian for the lower third.* Chatterjee (1978) says only that the lower limit "may extend down to Santonian", from the advanced megalosaur and the primitive tyrannosaur. It is a possibility drawn from faunal grade, not a determination, and adds no stage.
- *Paleocene to Eocene, post-Cretaceous, or Santonian to Maastrichtian at Dongargaon.* Earlier readings of the Dongargaon exposure that Jain and Bandyopadhyay (1997) list before concluding on a Maastrichtian age. The Paleocene–Eocene dates the fish fauna, whose stratigraphic level they call "not very clear" (jain1997a, p. 114).

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does the Carnosaur Bed have a stage of its own?

*2026-10-06*

Supersedes the Maastrichtian the Carnosaur Bed carried until 2026-10-06, and settles the question the Sauropod Bed entry below left open.

**Conclusion.** No. Carrano and Sampson's (2008) Maastrichtian follows "Lameta Formation" in their occurrence line, and the Matley (1921) they credit gives no Maastrichtian: he dates only the Lameta as a whole, as Albian or Cenomanian. So the bed takes the Lameta's epoch and no stage (see [methods](../../methods.md#a-parent-units-range-repeated-for-a-child-unit-is-the-parents-statement)). The eight taxa from the bed keep the Maastrichtian, the Lameta's whole range. The name is Huene and Matley's (1933), not Matley's (1921). Governs `period: [Late Cretaceous]` and the absence of `stages` on the Carnosaur Bed of [Lameta](../../../stratigraphy/l/lameta.yml).

**Evidence.**
- carrano2008a, p. 195, Occurrence: "Bara Simla Hill, Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh, India; 'Carnosaur Bed', 'infratrappean beds' of the Lameta Formation; Maastrichtian, Late Cretaceous (Matley 1921)"; and the specimens "derived from a single layer in the formation, termed the 'Carnosaur Bed' (Matley 1921)".
- matley1921a, p. 154: at "the top of the greensand zone", "a large mass of bones was found immediately below the Main Limestone"; p. 155: the "Megalosaurian bone-mass".
- matley1921a, p. 164, Summary: "the Lameta Group Albian or Cenomanian or both".
- huene1933a, p. 3: the fossiliferous layers "will be referred to, in ascending order, as the Carnosaur bed, the Ossiferous Conglomerate, and the Sauropod bed"; the Carnosaur bed lies "At the top of the greensand zone, immediately under the Main Limestone".

**Ruled out.**
- *Carnosaur Bed, Maastrichtian.* The stage follows the formation's name, and the source credited for the occurrence dates the formation differently.
- *Matley (1921) as the source of the name.* The paper describes the horizon as a "Megalosaurian bone-mass" and never uses "Carnosaur"; Huene and Matley name the bed.

**Open.** Nothing for the bed. Matley's own Albian–Cenomanian and Huene and Matley's Turonian are readings of the formation that later work replaces with the Maastrichtian.

## Does the Sauropod Bed have a stage of its own?

*2026-10-06*

Supersedes the Maastrichtian the Sauropod Bed carried until 2026-10-06.

**Conclusion.** No. Its only source for a stage, Chure's (2000) compiled entry for *Orthogoniosaurus matleyi*, gives "AGE: Maastrichtian" beside "HORIZON: Lameta Formation", an age the compilation takes from Weishampel (1990) unless it says otherwise. That dates the formation, and a compiled entry cannot carry a value alone (see [methods](../../methods.md#a-reference-work-corroborates-but-almost-never-carries-a-value-alone)), so the bed takes the Lameta's epoch and no stage. The three taxa from the bed keep the Maastrichtian, which is the Lameta's whole range. Governs `period: [Late Cretaceous]` and the absence of `stages` on the Sauropod Bed of [Lameta](../../../stratigraphy/l/lameta.yml).

**Evidence.**
- chure2000a, p. 428, *Orthogoniosaurus matleyi*: "LOCALITY: Sauropod Bed, 50 yards northeast of and slightly stratigraphically above the camosaur bed"; "HORIZON: Lameta Formation"; "AGE: Maastrichtian".
- chure2000a, pp. 160, 243: "AGE: Unless stated otherwise, the age is that listed in Weishampel (1990)."

**Ruled out.**
- *Sauropod Bed, Maastrichtian.* The age is the formation's, compiled from a reference work, and no primary source read dates the bed.

**Open.** The Carnosaur Bed's Maastrichtian rests on Carrano and colleagues' (2008) "'Carnosaur Bed', 'infratrappean beds' of the Lameta Formation; Maastrichtian, Late Cretaceous", the same construction, and has not been re-examined.

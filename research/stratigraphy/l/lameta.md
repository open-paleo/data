# Lameta

## Does the Carnosaur Bed have a stage of its own?

*2026-10-06*

Supersedes the Maastrichtian the Carnosaur Bed carried until 2026-10-06, and settles the question the Sauropod Bed entry below left open.

**Conclusion.** No. Carrano and Sampson's (2008) Maastrichtian follows "Lameta Formation" in their occurrence line, and the Matley (1921) they credit gives no Maastrichtian: he dates only the Lameta as a whole, as Albian or Cenomanian. So the bed takes the Lameta's epoch and no stage (see [methods](../../methods.md#a-parent-units-range-repeated-for-a-child-unit-is-the-parents-statement)). The eight taxa from the bed keep the Maastrichtian, the Lameta's whole range. The name is Huene and Matley's (1933), not Matley's (1921). Governs `period: [Late Cretaceous]` and the absence of `stages` on the Carnosaur Bed of [Lameta](../../../stratigraphy/l/lameta.yml).

**Evidence.**
- carrano2008a, p. 195, Occurrence: "Bara Simla Hill, Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh, India; 'Carnosaur Bed', 'infratrappean beds' of the Lameta Formation; Maastrichtian, Late Cretaceous (Matley 1921)"; and the specimens "derived from a single layer in the formation, termed the 'Carnosaur Bed' (Matley 1921)".
- matley1921a, p. 154: at "the top of the greensand zone", "a large mass of bones was found immediately below the Main Limestone"; p. 155: the "Megalosaurian bone-mass".
- matley1921a, p. 164, Summary: "the Lameta Group Albian or Cenomanian or both".
- huene1933a, p. 3: the fossiliferous layers "will be referred to, in ascending order, as the Carnosaur bed, the Ossiferous Conglomerate, and the Sauropod bed"; the Carnosaur bed lies "At the top of the greensand zone, immediately under the Main Limestone".

**Ruled out.**
- *Carnosaur Bed, Maastrichtian.* The stage follows the formation's name, and the source credited for the occurrence dates the formation differently.
- *Matley (1921) as the source of the name.* The paper describes the horizon as a "Megalosaurian bone-mass" and never uses "Carnosaur"; Huene and Matley name the bed.

**Open.** Nothing for the bed. Matley's own Albian–Cenomanian and Huene and Matley's Turonian are readings of the formation that later work replaces with the Maastrichtian.

## Does the Sauropod Bed have a stage of its own?

*2026-10-06*

Supersedes the Maastrichtian the Sauropod Bed carried until 2026-10-06.

**Conclusion.** No. Its only source for a stage, Chure's (2000) compiled entry for *Orthogoniosaurus matleyi*, gives "AGE: Maastrichtian" beside "HORIZON: Lameta Formation", an age the compilation takes from Weishampel (1990) unless it says otherwise. That dates the formation, and a compiled entry cannot carry a value alone (see [methods](../../methods.md#a-reference-work-corroborates-but-almost-never-carries-a-value-alone)), so the bed takes the Lameta's epoch and no stage. The three taxa from the bed keep the Maastrichtian, which is the Lameta's whole range. Governs `period: [Late Cretaceous]` and the absence of `stages` on the Sauropod Bed of [Lameta](../../../stratigraphy/l/lameta.yml).

**Evidence.**
- chure2000a, p. 428, *Orthogoniosaurus matleyi*: "LOCALITY: Sauropod Bed, 50 yards northeast of and slightly stratigraphically above the camosaur bed"; "HORIZON: Lameta Formation"; "AGE: Maastrichtian".
- chure2000a, pp. 160, 243: "AGE: Unless stated otherwise, the age is that listed in Weishampel (1990)."

**Ruled out.**
- *Sauropod Bed, Maastrichtian.* The age is the formation's, compiled from a reference work, and no primary source read dates the bed.

**Open.** The Carnosaur Bed's Maastrichtian rests on Carrano and colleagues' (2008) "'Carnosaur Bed', 'infratrappean beds' of the Lameta Formation; Maastrichtian, Late Cretaceous", the same construction, and has not been re-examined.

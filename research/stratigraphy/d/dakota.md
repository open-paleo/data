# Dakota

## Is the Naturita another name for the Dakota?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** Yes; it is added to the variants. Kirkland and colleagues (2016) refer the Upper Cretaceous strata above the Cedar Mountain on the Colorado Plateau to the Naturita Formation, after Young (1960, 1965) and later workers, and write "Naturita Formation (= Dakota Formation)" throughout; their Fig. 6 caption gives "Dakota Formation = Naturita Formation.". It is the same rock under another name, not a separate unit. Governs `variants` on [Dakota](../../../stratigraphy/d/dakota.yml).

**Evidence.**
- kirkland2016a, p. 105: "Young (1960, 1965) proposed to reassign these overlying strata to the Naturita Formation".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Dakota?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Albian to Cenomanian. Sprinkel and colleagues (2012) date the formation in northeastern Utah middle to late Albian from palynomorphs, among them marine dinoflagellate cysts in its basal mudstone and shale unit, and a bentonite in its middle carbonaceous interval gives 101.4 ± 0.4 Ma, older than the 100.5 Ma base of the Cenomanian, so the Albian stands on the current chart (see [methods](../../methods.md)). The same paper relays a Cenomanian Dakota to the south, on mollusks, radiometric ages and palynology, and Kirkland and colleagues (2016) give the formation's microvertebrate faunas elsewhere in Utah a "middle to late Cenomanian" age in their own voice. Eaton (1960) places the Kansas Dakota in the uppermost Lower Cretaceous. Governs `stages` and `period` on [Dakota](../../../stratigraphy/d/dakota.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sprinkel2012a, p. 10: "The Dakota Formation yields middle to late Albian palynoflora in northeastern Utah", from samples in "the basal mudstone and shale unit" and "the carbonaceous shale unit".
- sprinkel2012a, p. 10: the basal mudstone and shale unit "contains an assemblage of dinoflagellate cysts that indicate a marine to marginal marine environment of middle to late Albian age".
- sprinkel2012a, p. 12: a bentonite in the middle carbonaceous interval gives "a final age of 101.4 ± 0.4 Ma", which "is compatible with the late Albian age for this interval indicated by palynomorphs".
- sprinkel2012a, p. 12: "To the south, the Dakota Formation is Cenomanian based on the contained molluscan assemblage ... radiometric ages ... and palynology".
- kirkland2016a, p. 169: earlier work bracketed the Cedar Mountain by dating the "Dakota Formations (Albian-Cenomanian) (Sprinkel and others, 2012)"; palynomorphs and microfaunas support "an Albian age for the top of the Cedar Mountain Formation and at least the lower part of the Dakota Formation".
- kirkland2016a, p. 171: "middle to late Albian palynomorphs obtained from the base of the overlying basal Naturita Formation (= Dakota Formation)".
- kirkland2016a, p. 177: "extensive microvertebrate faunas in the middle to late Cenomanian (95 to 93 Ma) Naturita Formation (= Dakota Formation of others".
- kirkland2016a, p. 105: "As these strata are Upper Cretaceous and not Lower Cretaceous", the beds above the Cedar Mountain on the Colorado Plateau "are now being more properly referred to the Naturita Formation".
- eaton1960a, p. 5: the *Silvisaurus* site lies "about one third of the way up in the Dakota (now classified as uppermost Lower Cretaceous in the Kansas region)".

**Ruled out.**
- *A Turonian top.* Kirkland and colleagues (2016) give the Utah microvertebrate faunas "(95 to 93 Ma)", and on ICS chart v2024/12 the 93 Ma end falls just inside the Turonian, which begins at 93.9 Ma. The paper states the stage, "middle to late Cenomanian", and a stated stage is carried over rather than re-placed from its number (see [methods](../../methods.md)).
- *A wholly Late Cretaceous formation.* Kirkland and colleagues (2016) call the Colorado Plateau strata above the Cedar Mountain "Upper Cretaceous and not Lower Cretaceous", but the same paper relays middle to late Albian palynomorphs from the base of the formation in northeastern Utah, which Sprinkel and colleagues (2012) report from their own samples with a 101.4 Ma bentonite above them.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Dockum

## Does Martz and Parker's Apachean add the Rhaetian to the Dockum?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No; the group stays Carnian–Norian. Martz and Parker (2017) calibrate the Apachean outside the Dockum, and correlate the Dockum of northeastern New Mexico, which holds the Redonda Formation, to it "using tentative lithostratigraphic correlations", the biostratigraphic data being "mostly unavailable"; that is the age of a correlative unit. Lehman and Chatterjee's (2005) Rhaetian is a possibility. The Chinle's Rhaetian rests on its own record and is not affected. Governs `stages` on [Dockum](../../../stratigraphy/d/dockum.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- martz2017a, p. 93: the Dockum Group of northeastern New Mexico is "correlated to the estimated biochronozones using tentative lithostratigraphic correlations (Fig. 14), even though explicit biostratigraphic data is mostly unavailable for these local study areas".
- lehman2005a, p. 347: "Hence, it is possible that strata correlative with the Rhaetian Stage are indeed present in the Dockum Group."

**Ruled out.**
- *Rhaetian.* The age of a correlative unit, and a possibility.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does the 210 Ma date from the Cooper Canyon put the Dockum Group in the Rhaetian?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** No. Lehman and Chatterjee (2005) find that the group's tetrapods support "only" the Carnian and Norian, with the stage boundary near the Trujillo–Cooper Canyon contact. Their Rhaetian is a possibility raised by an Rb/Sr age of about 210 Ma on detrital biotite that they place in the upper Cooper Canyon, read against a Rhaetian of 215–210 Ma; they add that no direct biostratigraphic correlation with the Rhaetian can be made. Long and Lehman (1994), the source of the date, give it as model ages of two biotite samples and call the group "Carnian to Norian". Governs the absence of the Rhaetian from `stages: [Carnian, Norian]` on [Dockum](../../../stratigraphy/d/dockum.yml), subject to the Open line.

**Evidence.**
- lehman2005a, p. 326: "In eastern New Mexico, a fifth unit, the Redonda Formation, is included as the uppermost formation in the Dockum Group."
- lehman2005a, p. 346: the Keuper has been correlated with "Carnian (231–223 Ma), Norian (223–215 Ma) and Rhaetian (215–210 Ma; Aigner and Bachmann 1992)".
- lehman2005a, p. 347: "Long and Lehman (1994) dated detrital biotite samples (likely derived through reworking of ash) from the upper part of the Cooper Canyon Formation that yielded a Rb/Sr isotopic age of approximately 210 Ma. Hence, it is possible that strata correlative with the Rhaetian Stage are indeed present in the Dockum Group"; no typical Rhaetic fauna has been found in the Redonda or Cooper Canyon, "indicating that direct biostratigraphic correlation between these strata and the Rhaetian Stage cannot be made"; and "the 'Apachean' cannot be supported".
- lehman2005a, p. 348: "the stratigraphic distribution of Dockum tetrapods supports only correlation with strata equivalent to the Carnian and Norian Stages of Germany. The boundary between the Trujillo and the Cooper Canyon Formations approximates the stage boundary."
- long1994a, p. 197: "Redbeds of the Upper Triassic (Carnian to Norian) Dockum Group", with the Trujillo Sandstone and Cooper Canyon Formation "in the upper Dockum"; "Model ages of two rather unradiogenic biotite samples are" about 210 Ma, "possibly registering Triassic volcanism". The abstract does not say where in the section the biotites were collected.
- ogg2020a, p. 933: the two candidate bases of the Rhaetian have implied ages of 209.5 and 205.7 Ma, and "the Mi. posthernsteini s.str. (205.7 Ma) is used as the base-Rhaetian for the summary scales in this GTS2020".
- martz2017a, p. 112: "The Apachean estimated holochron extends from about 207 to 202 Ma ... during the Rhaetian", and its holochronozone "encompasses most of the Redonda Formation of the Dockum Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *Rhaetian from the 210 Ma Rb/Sr age, after Lehman and Chatterjee (2005).* The paper offers it as a possibility, not a dating, and in the same paragraph says no biostratigraphic correlation with the Rhaetian can be made. The Rhaetian it was read against began at 215 Ma; on the current chart 210 Ma is older than either candidate base of the Rhaetian, 209.5 or 205.7 Ma, and so falls in the Norian (our arithmetic). The number is also a model age on detrital biotite, which at most limits how old the beds can be.

**Open.** Whether Martz and Parker's (2017) Apachean adds the Rhaetian. They place most of the Redonda Formation, the uppermost formation of the group in eastern New Mexico, in an Apachean of about 207 to 202 Ma "during the Rhaetian", the statement the Chinle's Rhaetian rests on (see [Chinle](../c/chinle.md)); Lehman and Chatterjee (2005) reject the Apachean as a biochron. Martz and Parker (2017) are not among the works cited for the Dockum, and 207 Ma lies below the base of the Rhaetian used in the current chart.

## Does the Dockum Group include Middle Triassic beds?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** No. The Dockum is Late Triassic (Carnian–Norian); the Middle Triassic beds once included are now the Anton Chico Formation beneath it. Governs `period: [Late Triassic]` and `stages: [Carnian, Norian]` on Dockum.

**Evidence.**
- lehman2005a, p. 326: "the Anton Chico Formation, underlies the Dockum Group in eastern New Mexico".
- lucas1994a, p. 105: "the lowermost member of the Santa Rosa Formation, as then identified, is of Middle Triassic (early Anisian) age (Lucas and Morales, 1985)" and "Lucas and Hunt (1987) renamed the lowermost member of the Santa Rosa Formation the Anton Chico Formation".
- lucas1995a, p. 5: "the Camp Springs Member is of Late Triassic age and disconformably overlies Upper Permian strata".
- martz2013a, p. 339 (abstract): the *Postosuchus* Post Quarry is "possibly late Lacian or earliest Alaunian (late early Norian or earliest middle Norian), between 220 and 215 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *Middle Triassic from Galton (1985).* Galton's captions place *Postosuchus* in the "Dockum Formation (Middle Triassic) of Texas, U.S.A., after CHATTERJEE (1985)" (galton1985c, p. 6, Fig. 3B) and repeat the age for a poposaurid (p. 8, Fig. 4E). The Post Quarry is Norian.
- *Lucas and Morales's "Middle Triassic amphibian" as Dockum evidence.* The beds were the lowermost Santa Rosa, later split off as the Anton Chico (lucas1994a, p. 105).

**Open.** Nothing.

# Dalangshan

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Dalangshan?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Maastrichtian. Xing and colleagues (2024) report tyrannosauroid teeth from the formation's second member in the southeastern Sanshui Basin and date that member Maastrichtian after Zhang (1984). Zhang correlates Member 1 with the Zhenshui Member of the Nanxiong Formation and Member 2 with the Pingling of the Nanxiong Basin, on ostracod and charophyte assemblages that are largely shared. He moves the top of the Cretaceous in the Nanxiong Basin up to the top of the Pingling, where dinosaurs, *Cypridea* and *Latochara* disappear, and so places Member 2 in "the very latest Late Cretaceous". His correlation table sets Member 1 against the "Maestichtian" and Member 2 against a "Danian" row that it draws inside the Upper Cretaceous, so the label stands for the uppermost Cretaceous of his scheme, not the Paleocene stage. The whole formation is therefore Maastrichtian, as Xing and colleagues give it. Governs `stages: [Maastrichtian]` and `period: [Late Cretaceous]` on [Dalangshan](../../../stratigraphy/d/dalangshan.yml).

**Evidence.**
- xing2024b, p. 1, Introduction: both sites "belong to the Dalangshan Formation, which is Maastrichtian and lays at the terminal boundary of the Cretaceous (Zhang, 1984)".
- xing2024b, p. 2, Geological setting: "The exposed Upper Cretaceous Dalangshan Formation in the Sanshui Basin (Bureau of Geology and Mineral Resources of Guangdong Province, 1988)"; each tooth "was collected from the second member of the Dalangshan Formation, in the southeastern district"; Zhang (1984) concluded "that the second member of the Dalangshan Formation is comparable to the Pingling Member of the Shanghu Formation of the Nanxiong Group, with both belonging to the Maastrichtian stage and having nearly identical ostracod faunas".
- zhang1984a, p. 38 (translated): "Member 1 of the Dalangshan Formation is equivalent to the Zhenshui Member of the Nanxiong Formation, and Member 2 is correlated with the Pingling Member of the Shanghu Formation"; "The top of the Cretaceous in the Nanxiong Basin should therefore be moved up to the boundary between the Pingling Member and the Xiahui Member of the Shanghu Formation"; Member 2's "age should be the very latest Late Cretaceous".
- zhang1984a, p. 39, correlation table: Dalangshan Member 2 and the Pingling Member in the "Danian" row, Member 1 and the Zhenshui Member in the "Maestichtian" row, both rows under "Upper Cretaceous" (translated).
- zhang1984a, p. 40, English abstract: "the Dalangshan formasion is comparable with the upper part of the Nanxiong formation and the lower part of the Shanghu formation, their age being considered to be the late stage of Late Cretaceous".

**Ruled out.**
- *Danian for Member 2, from Zhang's table.* The table's "Danian" row lies inside its Upper Cretaceous, and Zhang's text puts the Cretaceous top at the top of Member 2's correlative (zhang1984a, pp. 38–39). No work cited places the Cretaceous–Paleogene boundary inside the formation. Xing and colleagues' "both belonging to the Maastrichtian stage" is their rendering of Zhang, whose table does not use that label for Member 2; the age is the same.
- *Paleocene, by correlation with the Shanghu Formation.* Zhang reports that some workers put the Dalangshan in the Paleocene because its microfossils match the Pingling and "the Shanghu Formation is generally accepted as Paleocene". He rejects it: dinosaur eggs found in the Pingling in 1982 show that it "is still within the range zone of the dinosaur fauna" (zhang1984a, p. 38, translated).

**Open.** Nothing.

# Darbazin

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Darbazin Formation and the level of its base?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The Stratigraphic Dictionary places the formation in the Senonian, "the upper subformation possibly to the Campanian as well", and relays Muzafarova's Coniacian–Santonian and Minakova's Cenomanian–Turonian for the lower subformation. Nessov (1997) places the unit mainly in the Campanian and is explicit that its lowest beds are hard to separate from the underlying Syuksyuk: the same interval is "lower Campanian?–middle Campanian" at the "Gray Mesa" exposure of Kyrkkuduk II and "Santonian or Santonian-Lower Campanian" at Darbaza, so the base sits in the Santonian in one part of the Pritashkent Chuly and in the lower Campanian in another. Bone beds near the contact go to one unit or the other: Nessov (1997) puts the Kazgurt bed in the upper Syuksyuk after Suslov (1987), and Nessov (1995) moves the Syuk-Syuk wells bed, which Rozhdestvensky (1964) had put in the lower Darbazin, to the Syuk-Syuk Formation and dates it Santonian, rejecting the two oldest readings of it by name in the same sentence. Among the works cited, the Coniacian comes only from Muzafarova's range as the dictionary relays it and from the dictionary's Senonian. The formation is named after Darbaza Station, which is why its Russian adjectival form is Darbazinskaya. Governs `stages: [Coniacian, Santonian, Campanian]` on [Darbazin](../../../stratigraphy/d/darbazin.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- vereshchagin1979a, p. 117: the entry is headed "Upper Cretaceous" and reads "Named after Darbaza Station, Pritashkent district, Central Asia. N. E. Minakova, 1941"; p. 118: "Upper Darbazin subformation—see Temirchin Formation", and the formation "Belongs to the Senonian, the upper subformation possibly to the Campanian as well. The unit's author referred the lower subformation to the Cenomanian—Turonian; R. F. Muzafarova ... to the Coniacian—Santonian" (translated).
- nessov1997a, p. ?, Chapter 1, on the Campanian complexes of southern Kazakhstan: at Kyrkkuduk II, "Vertebrate remains come from gray sandstones of the lower part of the Darbaza Formation (Early?–Middle Campanian)" (translated).
- nessov1997a, p. ?, catalog of localities, Kazakhstan, locality 55: Kyrkkuduk II, "'Gray Mesa' exposure. Gray sandstones of the lower Darbazinsky Subsuite, lower Campanian?–middle Campanian" (translated).
- nessov1997a, p. ?, catalog of localities, Kazakhstan, locality 28: Darbaza, "Syuksyukian Formation or, more likely, lower part of the Darbazinian Formation, Santonian or Santonian-Lower Campanian" (translated).
- nessov1997a, p. ?, catalog of localities, Kazakhstan, locality 31: Kazgurt, "Upper part of the Syuksyukian Formation (Suslov, 1987), Santonian (apparently erroneously, the bone-bearing level is sometimes noted in the lower part of the Darbazinian Formation)" (translated).
- nessov1995a, p. 101, locality 99: Syuk-Syuk wells, "Lower part of the Darbazin Formation (Rozhdestvensky, 1964a). Syuk-Syuk Formation (Stratigraphic Dictionary, 1979), rather than the Beleutin Formation ... Upper Cretaceous, Santonian. The age was earlier regarded as younger than that for *Jaxartosaurus* at Alymtau ... and was dated as early Santonian ..., but it is not late Turonian (Belenky and Mirkamalova, 1965) nor Cenomanian - Turonian (Rozhdestvensky, 1964b; Rozhdestvensky and Khozatsky, 1965)" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *A late Turonian or Cenomanian–Turonian age for the Syuk-Syuk wells bed.* Belenky and Mirkamalova (1965) give the late Turonian, and Rozhdestvensky (1964b) and Rozhdestvensky and Khozatsky (1965) the Cenomanian–Turonian. Nessov (1995) rejects both by name and dates the bed Santonian, in the same sentence that assigns it to the Syuk-Syuk Formation rather than the lower Darbazin.
- *The Kazgurt bone bed in the lower Darbazin.* Nessov (1997) calls this attribution "apparently erroneous" and puts the bed in the upper Syuksyuk, after Suslov (1987).

**Open.** The stages leave out Minakova's Cenomanian–Turonian for the lower subformation, which the dictionary relays and the dispute records as contested, and Rozhdestvensky's (1964) "no later than the Upper Turonian" for the dinosaur band in the lower Darbazin. Of the works cited, only Nessov (1995) argues against the Turonian readings, and only for the Syuk-Syuk wells bed. Minakova (1941), Muzafarova (1963) and Belenky (1961, 1966) have not been read; they would show what each early reading rests on.

# De-na-zin

## Does Newman's early Maastrichtian stand for the De-na-zin?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** Yes; the member is Campanian to Maastrichtian. Supersedes the 2026-11-25 entry of the [Lower Kirtland](../../topics/lower-kirtland-maastrichtian.md) topic on the De-na-zin. Newman (1987) gives the upper shale member, between the Farmington and the Naashoibito, as "most likely early Maastrichtian in age, based on stratigraphic position alone", a stage he names from position, not a number he measured; a stated stage is carried over, even where the author's boundary definition differs from the current chart. Sullivan (1999) gives the member late Campanian. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` and the note on newman1987a on [De-na-zin](../../../stratigraphy/d/de-na-zin.yml); its genera, all Campanian, are unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- newman1987a, p. 159: "the upper shale member of the Kirtland is most likely early Maastrichtian in age, based on stratigraphic position alone".
- sullivan1999a, p. 126: the De-na-zin Member is "now considered to be late Campanian in age".

**Ruled out.**
- *Re-placing Newman's stage by his zonal definition.* A stage the author names is carried over.

**Open.** A date from the upper part of the member.

## What strata did Hunt and Lucas (1992) name the De-na-zin Member?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The lower part of Reeside's (1924) "upper shale member". Reeside's unit ran from the Farmington Member to the Ojo Alamo Sandstone and took in what is now the Naashoibito Member; Bauer (1916) had called the same strata the upper shale. Hunt and Lucas (1992) name "the upper shale member of Reeside (1924)" the De-na-zin, but bound it above at the lower conglomerate of the Naashoibito, so the member is Reeside's unit without the Naashoibito. That reading of their bounds is an inference from their text: they do not say in so many words that the name excludes part of Reeside's unit. The type area is in the NW¼ of sec. 19, T24N, R11W, where the member is about 30 m thick. Governs the name and `lithology` on [De-na-zin](../../../stratigraphy/d/de-na-zin.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hunt1992a, p. 220, De-na-zin Member: Reeside's informal "upper shale member", between the Farmington Member and the Ojo Alamo Sandstone, "included what is now termed the Naashoibito Member of the Kirtland Formation"; "Bauer (1916) had referred to this unit as the upper shale".
- hunt1992a, p. 220, De-na-zin Member: "We here name the upper shale member of Reeside (1924) the De-na-zin Member for outcrops in NW¼ sec. 19, T24N, R11W"; it "is about 30 m thick in its type area and consists of green mudstone, siltstone and minor sandstone", and "forms a slope break between the Farmington Member and the lower conglomerate of the Naashoibito Member".

**Ruled out.**
- *The whole of Reeside's "upper shale member", Naashoibito included, as the De-na-zin.* The naming sentence alone could be read this way, but Hunt and Lucas place the member's top at the lower conglomerate of the Naashoibito, and they note that Reeside's unit included the Naashoibito.

**Open.** Reeside (1924) and Bauer (1916) are known here only through Hunt and Lucas's account; reading them would show where each put the top of the upper shale.

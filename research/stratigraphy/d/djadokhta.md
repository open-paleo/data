# Djadokhta

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Djadokhta?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian, as the 2026-09-30 entry concludes; this entry sets out the readings behind each cited work. The biochronological datings run from Coniacian–Santonian (Barsbold 1983, from a mammal correlation with North America; the tables of Khand and colleagues 2000) through ?upper Santonian and/or ?lower Campanian (Gradziński and colleagues 1977) and early Campanian (Averianov 1997) to late Campanian or early Maastrichtian (Lillegraven and McKenna 1986). Dashzeveg and colleagues (2005) note that these estimates had "little consensus" and no evidence "independent of faunal comparisons", and their magnetostratigraphy places the Flaming Cliffs section in the late Campanian. Khand and colleagues' text and Dingus and colleagues (2008) both give the formation, or its fauna at Ukhaa Tolgod, as Campanian. Tucker and colleagues' (2025) eggshell date bears on the Djadokhta only through the correlation of the dated beds with the Baruungoyot, and none of the works cited gives a radiometric date from the Djadokhta itself. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` on [Djadokhta](../../../stratigraphy/d/djadokhta.yml).

**Evidence.**
- khand2000a, p. 61: "Fossil criteria indicate that the formation is Campanian in age"; "Loope et al. [15] followed by Lillegraven and McKenna [16] assigned the age of this formation to late Campanian-early Maastrichtian"; the Djadokhta is the "Bayan Dzak Formation by Barsbold [12]".
- khand2000a, Table 1 and Table 2: the Djadokhta stands against the Coniacian–Santonian, with the Baruungoyot in the Campanian.
- khand2000a, p. 61: "No physical contact between the Djadokhta and Baruungoyot Formations has yet been discovered"; Dashzeveg and colleagues (1995) found that sampling at Ukhaa Tolgod "blurs the distinction between the Djadokhta assemblages and the allegedly slightly younger Baruungoyot assemblages"; "we continue to recognize both the Djadokhta and Baruungoyot Formations with assignment pending further publications".
- barsbold1983a (translated), p. 84: the mammal fauna "from the lower part of the horizon contained in the Djadokhtaskaya Svita deposits fills the blank between the Albian forms from the Trinity Sandstone of Texas and the Campanian from the Judith River deposits of Montana", so that "the age of the Djadokhtaskaya horizon, i.e. actually the Djadokhtaskaya Svita, is defined as within the Coniacian-Santonian interval" (translated); the upper part of the horizon, "represented by the Barungoyotskaya Svita", is Campanian, and "the lower and upper age limits of the Djadokhtaskaya horizon are considered to correspond to the Lower Senonian and Campanian" (translated).
- gradziński1977a, p. 281, Abstract: "As "best guesses" the estimated stages are: Djadokhta Formation — ?upper Santonian and/or ?lower Campanian".
- dashzeveg2005a, p. 2: Gradziński and colleagues (1977) "estimated the age of the Djadokhta Formation as ?upper Santonian and/or ?lower Campanian".
- lillegraven1986a, p. 58: "the Djadokhta Formation is therefore approximately late Campanian or even early Maastrichtian in age".
- averianov1997a, p. 254: the evidence "supports in part the conclusion of Gradziński et al. (1977) in suggesting a somewhat earlier, perhaps early Campanian age for the Djadokhta Formation".
- dingus2008a, p. 30: "Faunas from the Djadokhta Formation have been assigned a Campanian–Early Maastrichtian age by Lillegraven and McKenna (1986) and Averianov (1997), based primarily on biochronologic interpretations".
- dingus2008a, p. 31: "the fauna from Tugrugyin Shireh must be somewhat younger than that from Bayn Dzak, although the extent of this age difference is presently uncertain"; "pending further evidence and analysis, the vertebrate fauna at Ukhaa Tolgod (table 1) is thought here to reflect a Campanian age".
- dashzeveg2005a, p. 17: "There are no radioisotopic dates from beds in the sequence that produce the Bayn Dzak fauna"; "there has historically been little consensus among biochronologic age estimates and no evidence independent of faunal comparisons".
- dashzeveg2005a, p. 18: deposition "during the rapid sequence of polarity changes in the late part of the Campanian", a correlation that "cannot yet be certainly established".
- tucker2025a, p. 5, Fig. 4 caption: "a near syndepositional age of 75.35 Ma and lithostratigraphically coeval with the Baruungoyot Formation".
- tucker2025a, p. 6: "Other than the Bayanshiree Formation (lower post-rift), this is the first time the uppermost post-rift sequences in the Eastern Gobi Basin have been radiometrically calibrated".

**Ruled out.**
- *Coniacian–Santonian.* Barsbold (1983) relays it from the mammal correlation with North America, and Khand and colleagues' Tables 1 and 2 carry it, while their own text gives the Campanian. It is a biochronological dating made before the magnetostratigraphy, which places the unit in the late Campanian; Gradziński and colleagues (1977), with Kielan-Jaworowska among the authors, had already moved the estimate up to ?upper Santonian and/or ?lower Campanian.
- *?Upper Santonian.* Gradziński and colleagues' (1977) "best guesses", questioned in their own wording, also predate the magnetostratigraphy. Averianov (1997) agrees only "in part", and only as far as an early Campanian age.
- *Early Maastrichtian.* Lillegraven and McKenna (1986) give "late Campanian or even early Maastrichtian", and Khand and colleagues relay it. It is a biochronological estimate that predates the magnetostratigraphy. Recalibrating Dashzeveg and colleagues' 75 to 71 Ma window on the current chart (base of the Maastrichtian at 72.2 Ma, gale2020a, p. 1041) lets it reach the earliest Maastrichtian, but none of the works cited dates the unit there.
- *Averianov (1997) as a source for a Campanian to early Maastrichtian age.* Dingus and colleagues (2008) cite Averianov for it; Averianov suggests "perhaps early Campanian".
- *Loope and colleagues (1998) as a source for late Campanian to early Maastrichtian.* Khand and colleagues cite them for it; their paper calls the beds Late Cretaceous and names no stage.
- *Turonian.* None of the works cited places the unit in the Turonian; the Cenomanian to early Maastrichtian span that Dashzeveg and colleagues report (p. 17) is a list of earlier readings, not a range anyone assigned.

**Open.** Whether the stage union admits the ?upper Santonian of Gradziński and colleagues (1977) and the early Maastrichtian of Lillegraven and McKenna (1986): no later work cited states that the magnetostratigraphy supersedes them.

## Is Bayanzag another name for the Djadokhta?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** Yes. Shuvalov lists the Bayanzag Svita of Khand (1974) with the Djadokhta Formation and Svita as names for the same red beds. Governs the `Bayanzag` variant on [Djadokhta](../../../stratigraphy/d/djadokhta.yml).

**Evidence.**
- shuvalov2000a: the Baruungoyot Svita "unites the red sediments of the Djadokhta Formation (Berkey and Morris, 1927; …), the Djadokhta Svita (Barsbold, 1972, 1983), or the Bayanzag Svita (Khand, 1974)".
- khand2000a: the "Bayanzag Formation" of the East Gobi ostracod scheme lies above the Bayanshiree, and basalt overlying its red beds gives K-Ar ages of 71.6 ± 1.6 to 73.5 ± 1.6 Ma.

**Ruled out.**
- *A separate Bayanzag unit that the variant would collide with.* Benton (2000) lists "Bayanzag Svita" as a gazetteer entry without equating it to anything (benton2000c), and no record names a Bayanzag unit.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Why is the formation spelled Djadokhta rather than Djadochta?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** Djadokhta is the spelling Berkey and Morris adopted in 1927, kept when the formation was formally redefined in 1977 and kept unchanged in Benton's (2000) system for Mongolian names. Djadochta is closer to the name as first printed in 1923, "Dja-doch-ta", so neither form is invented; the record follows the emended name in use since the formal redefinition. Governs `name` and `variants` on [Djadokhta](../../../stratigraphy/d/djadokhta.yml), and `location.formation` on the records that name it.

**Evidence.**
- granger1923a, p. 8, in Berkey's appendix: "Dja-doch-ta Formation (Protoceratops Beds)".
- berkey1927a, p. 356: "The Djadokhta formation".
- gradziński1977a, p. 284: "Introduced as „Dja-doch-ta" by Berkey (in Granger & Gregory 1923, p. 8), then modified to „Djadokhta" (Berkey & Morris 1927). According to Dr. R. Barsbold (see Lefeld 1971, p. 101), the term „Djadokhta" is a result of the incorrect spelling and transliteration of a local Mongolian geographic name"; and "The original formational names are maintained in the diagnoses".
- benton2000c, p. xxii: "Some, fortunately, do not change: Choibalsan, Djadokhta, Gobi, Nemegt"; p. xxiv: "Djadokhta Formation/ Svita". Benton does not print "Djadochta".

**Ruled out.**
- *Djadohta as Berkey and Morris's spelling.* Khand and colleagues write "Djadohta Formation by Berky and Morris" (khand2000a, p. 61); Berkey and Morris print "Djadokhta" (p. 356).

**Open.** Nothing. Epithets coined from the 1923 form, such as *djadochtaensis*, keep their spelling under the Code.

## What stage are the Djadokhta Formation and the Bayn Dzak Member?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Campanian. The Cenomanian–Maastrichtian span in older literature is a list of other authors' views. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` on Djadokhta and on its [Bayn Dzak Member](../../../stratigraphy/b/bayn-dzak.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dashzeveg2005a, p. 18: "late part of the Campanian incorporating the end of Chron 33 and Chron 32 between about 75 and 71 Ma (Gradstein et al., 1995). However, this tentative correlation … cannot yet be certainly established".
- gale2020a, p. 1041: the base of the Maastrichtian is at "Chron C32n.2n.88" and "approximately 72.2 Ma". Reading Dashzeveg and colleagues' 75–71 Ma window against this, it is almost entirely Campanian (our inference).
- tucker2025a, p. 6: "the combined age of 75.35 ± 0.74/ 1.5 Ma", which "should be treated as a maximum depositional age".

**Ruled out.**
- *Cenomanian to early Maastrichtian.* Dashzeveg and colleagues report that "According to different authors, its age has been reported to range from Cenomanian to Early Maastrichtian" (p. 17) before giving their own magnetostratigraphy.
- *Djadokhta older than, and separated from, the Baruungoyot.* No contact between the two has been found, and "they could be partly coeval" (marjanović2021a, p. 13, relaying Dingus and colleagues 2008 and others); Tucker and colleagues' dated beds are "lithostratigraphically coeval with the Baruungoyot Formation" (tucker2025a, p. 5, Fig. 2 caption).

**Open.** The magnetostratigraphy of Hicks and colleagues (2001), reported as "C32 to C31" for the Djadokhta, appeared only as a conference abstract with no follow-up paper (marjanović2021a, p. 13). Tanabe and colleagues (2023) have not been read.

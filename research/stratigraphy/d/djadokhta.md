# Djadokhta

## What stage are the Djadokhta Formation and the Bayn Dzak Member?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Campanian. The Cenomanian–Maastrichtian span in older literature is a list of other authors' views. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` on Djadokhta and on its [Bayn Dzak Member](../../../stratigraphy/b/bayn-dzak.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dashzeveg2005a, p. 18: "late part of the Campanian incorporating the end of Chron 33 and Chron 32 between about 75 and 71 Ma (Gradstein et al., 1995). However, this tentative correlation … cannot yet be certainly established".
- gale2020a, p. 1041: the base of the Maastrichtian is at "Chron C32n.2n.88" and "approximately 72.2 Ma". Reading Dashzeveg and colleagues' 75–71 Ma window against this, it is almost entirely Campanian (our inference).
- tucker2025a, p. 6: "the combined age of 75.35 ± 0.74/ 1.5 Ma", which "should be treated as a maximum depositional age".

**Ruled out.**
- *Cenomanian to early Maastrichtian.* Dashzeveg and colleagues report that "According to different authors, its age has been reported to range from Cenomanian to Early Maastrichtian" (p. 17) before giving their own magnetostratigraphy.
- *Djadokhta older than, and separated from, the Baruungoyot.* No contact between the two has been found, and "they could be partly coeval" (marjanović2021a, p. 13, relaying Dingus and colleagues 2008 and others); Tucker and colleagues' dated beds are "lithostratigraphically coeval with the Baruungoyot Formation" (tucker2025a, p. 5, Fig. 2 caption).

**Open.** The magnetostratigraphy of Hicks and colleagues (2001), reported as "C32 to C31" for the Djadokhta, appeared only as a conference abstract with no follow-up paper (marjanović2021a, p. 13). Tanabe and colleagues (2023) have not been read.

# Dinosaur Beds

## Does the Dinosaur Beds' Barremian or Aptian rest on a reading?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** The Aptian does, the Barremian does not; the unit becomes Aptian. Supersedes the 2026-11-25 entry. No work read gives the beds a Barremian: Widlansky and colleagues (2018) credit their Barremian–Aptian to Colin and Jacobs (1990), whose ostracods give "an Aptian minimum age" and only allow an older age ("cannot be excluded"), and whose Barremian is the age of other West African beds. Gorscak and colleagues (2014) give "the Aptian Dinosaur Beds" in their own voice, with no basis, and that reading carries the Aptian. Governs `stages: [Aptian]` and the notes on widlansky2018a and gorscak2014a on [Dinosaur Beds](../../../stratigraphy/d/dinosaur-beds.yml); [*Malawisaurus*](../../../genera/M/Malawisaurus.yml) and [*Karongasaurus*](../../../genera/K/Karongasaurus.yml) are unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- colin1990a, p. 1025: the ostracods "allow confirmation of an Aptian minimum age for these beds".
- colin1990a, p. 1026: "However, an older (Lower Cretaceous, pre-Aptian) age cannot be excluded."
- gorscak2014a, p. 1133: "the Aptian Dinosaur Beds (DB) of Malawi".

**Ruled out.**
- *Barremian.* A misrelay of Colin and Jacobs (1990).

**Open.** Unchanged: no radiometric or magnetostratigraphic date bears on the beds.

## What do Colin and Jacobs (1990) say about the age of the Dinosaur Beds?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Aptian or older within the Early Cretaceous. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry where it says the Barremian rests on Colin and Jacobs (1990): their ostracods from the Upper Member give "an Aptian minimum age", an age "no younger than Aptian", and they state that a pre-Aptian Early Cretaceous age "cannot be excluded"; they name no Barremian age for the beds. The Barremian is Widlansky and colleagues' (2018) reading of that evidence, and it fits the paper, which reports the ostracod genus from West African beds "presumably of Barremian age". Their vertebrate correlation is likewise relayed: "an Early Cretaceous age, perhaps as young as Aptian". Governs `stages: [Barremian, Aptian]` on [Dinosaur Beds](../../../stratigraphy/d/dinosaur-beds.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- colin1990a, p. 1025: "allow confirmation of an Aptian minimum age for these beds. This is based on the presence of the genus Hourcqia".
- colin1990a, p. 1026: "indicate an age no younger than Aptian … However, an older (Lower Cretaceous, pre-Aptian) age cannot be excluded."
- colin1990a, p. 1026: "this genus is also known from older sediments, presumably of Barremian age".
- colin1990a, p. 1025: "an Early Cretaceous age, perhaps as young as Aptian, is proposed for the Dinosaur Beds".

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian alone.* Colin and Jacobs give the Aptian as the youngest age the ostracods allow, not as the only one.
- *Barremian on Colin and Jacobs's own authority.* They leave the pre-Aptian open without naming a stage.

**Open.** No radiometric or magnetostratigraphic date bears on the beds.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Dinosaur Beds?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Barremian and Aptian. The Aptian is the age traditionally given from the vertebrate fauna and ostracods, and Gomani (2005) and Gorscak and colleagues (2019) both write it with a query. The Barremian rests on Colin and Jacobs (1990), relayed by Widlansky and colleagues (2018), whose ostracods give "strongest support" to a Barremian–Aptian age. No radiometric or magnetostratigraphic date bears on the beds. Governs `period: [Early Cretaceous]` and `stages: [Barremian, Aptian]` on [Dinosaur Beds](../../../stratigraphy/d/dinosaur-beds.yml).

**Evidence.**
- widlansky2018a, p. ? (accepted manuscript, p. 33, 5.2 Correlation to the Malawi Dinosaur Beds): "The Malawi Dinosaur Beds have traditionally been assigned an Aptian age, based on the vertebrate fauna as well as a lacustrine ostracod assemblage"; the ostracods are assigned to the Cyprididae "and tentatively to the genus Horcquia (Colin and Jacobs, 1990)".
- widlansky2018a, p. ? (accepted manuscript, pp. 33–34, same section): the genus "is known from primarily Aptian deposits in western Africa and South America, and constrain the age of the Dinosaur Beds to Late Jurassic – Aptian, with strongest support for a Barremanian [sic] – Aptian age (Colin and Jacobs, 1990)".
- widlansky2018a, p. ? (accepted manuscript, p. 34, same section): the beds "lack rigorous age constraint from radiometric dating", and "no magnetostratigraphic study of the Dinosaur Beds has been conducted to support the Aptian age assignment"; faunal comparisons "suggest the Malawi Dinosaur Beds are younger than the Jurassic Tendaguru Beds of Tanzania".
- dixey1928a, p. 56: "it remains in this paper to offer an account of the Dinosaur Beds themselves"; the beds "lie within the Nyasan Rift Valley at an average distance of about 8 miles from the north-western shores of Lake Nyasa" and "extend from Florence Bay northwestwards to the Songwe River"; "Throughout the greater part of their extent the Dinosaur Beds rest upon the crystalline rocks", and in some areas on "the non-resistant mudstones that form the local upper Karroo".
- dixey1928a, p. 64: "The Dinosaur Beds of Tanganyika Territory are included within the Tendaguru Series, which constitute the transition beds between the Upper Jurassic and the Lower Cretaceous".
- dixey1928a, p. 66: the rivers that laid down the Nyasaland beds probably drained "by way of the Ruhuhu Valley, and thence eastwards to the site of the deposition of the Tendaguru Series", given "the probable former extension of the early Cretaceous sediments down the Ruhuhu Valley".

**Ruled out.**
- *A Late Jurassic part of the range.* The ostracod genus constrains the beds "to Late Jurassic – Aptian", which is an outer bound set by the genus's known range, not a reading of the beds' age; the same sentence gives the strongest support to Barremian–Aptian. It adds no stage and no epoch.
- *A stage from Dixey (1928).* The describing account names no international stage. Its age statements are comparisons: with the Tendaguru Series, which it places at the transition from the Upper Jurassic to the Lower Cretaceous, and with the Cretaceous of the Ruhuhu Valley, toward which it has the beds' rivers drain. It assigns the Nyasaland beds no age of its own, and Widlansky and colleagues (2018) report faunal comparisons that make them younger than the Tendaguru Beds.

**Open.** The beds have no radiometric or magnetostratigraphic date. Colin and Jacobs (1990), the source of the Barremian, has not been read; Widlansky and colleagues (2018) is read here only in its accepted-manuscript form.

## Is the Malawi "Dinosaur Beds" a formation?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** No formal rank. Dixey (1928) introduced the unit as "a new formation" in the generic sense of the time, and every later paper read for this note uses "Dinosaur Beds" without a rank, with lower and upper parts. Governs the empty `rank` on Dinosaur Beds and `location.part: upper` on [*Malawisaurus dixeyi*](../../../genera/M/Malawisaurus.yml); bears on [*Karongasaurus gittelmani*](../../../genera/K/Karongasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dixey1928a, p. 55: "a new formation existed there that had not previously been recognised"; p. 61: "The lower Dinosaur Beds can generally be distinguished in the field"; "The upper Dinosaur Beds are characterised by". Dixey is the sole author; Haughton "communicated" the paper.
- jacobs1993a, p. 525: "Upper member of the Early Cretaceous Dinosaur Beds (Lupata Group); Mwakasyunguti area".
- gomani2005a, p. 2: "the same area of the Dinosaur Beds (Dixey 1928; Jacobs et al. 1990, 1992)", "of Early Cretaceous (?Aptian) age"; gorscak2014a, p. 1133: "the Aptian Dinosaur Beds (DB) of Malawi"; gorscak2017a, p. 2 and gorscak2019a, p. 1 likewise.

**Ruled out.**
- *Dinosaur Beds Formation.* Mannion and colleagues (2026) write "the Aptian Dinosaur Beds Formation of Malawi (Jacobs et al. 1993, Gomani 2005)" (mannion2026a, p. 2); neither source they cite uses the rank.
- *Karoo-aged beds, or a label no one erected.* Dixey (1928, p. 55) erected the unit, and the beds are Cretaceous.

**Open.** The rank of Jacobs and colleagues' "Upper member" and their "Lupata Group" assignment, which the registry does not record.

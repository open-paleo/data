# Densuș-Ciula

## How do the cited works date the members of the Densuș-Ciula Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian and Maastrichtian, unchanged, with the top of the formation in the late Maastrichtian. Csiki-Sava and colleagues (2016) date the lower member from a basal tuff near Răchitova, a K-Ar age of 69.8 ± 1.3 Ma that they place in the early Maastrichtian, above upper Campanian marine beds. They take the upper part of the middle member, near Tuștea, to the early late Maastrichtian from assumed sedimentation rates, and the upper member, from its position above the middle, to the later late Maastrichtian; localities high in the succession, at General Berthelot and Crăguiș, are "probable late Maastrichtian". Albert and colleagues (2025), combining mapping with zircon U-Pb dating, sedimentology and micropaleontology, move the base of the lower member into the middle of the upper Campanian, and name Csiki-Sava and colleagues (2016) among the works that had started the formation in the early Maastrichtian. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` and `period: [Late Cretaceous]` on [Densuș-Ciula](../../../stratigraphy/d/densuș-ciula.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- csiki-sava2016a, p. 666: "A basal tuff layer sampled from this subunit near Răchitova yielded a K–Ar radiometric age of 69.8 ± 1.3 Ma, placing it within the early Maastrichtian", the beds "lying unconformably (an angular unconformity) on top of upper Campanian marine deposits".
- csiki-sava2016a, p. 668: "the age of the upper part of this subunit, near Tuștea, can be constrained to the early part of the late Maastrichtian"; "this assumption is yet to be tested in detail".
- csiki-sava2016a, p. 670: the upper member's deposits "were long considered to be devoid of both a volcanoclastic component and of vertebrate fossils, and were thus referred to the lower Paleogene"; "these beds are now also considered to be Maastrichtian"; "due to their stratigraphic position overlying the deposits of the 'middle' member, the age of these beds can be constrained to the later part of the late Maastrichtian".
- csiki-sava2016a, p. 679: "Because the deposits from Densuș lie unconformably above upper Campanian flysch beds", and correlative deposits at Răchitova "have been radiometrically dated to the early Maastrichtian", the Densuș beds "can be constrained with some confidence to the early Maastrichtian".
- csiki-sava2016a, p. 690: "probable late Maastrichtian localities are also located towards the upper part of the Densuș-Ciula Formation succession, at General Berthelot ... and Crăguiș", "although more precise age constraints are still needed".
- albert2025a, p. 1, title: "Evidence of their late Campanian-early Maastrichtian syntectonic deposition"; abstract: "detailed geological mapping combined with results of zircon U–Pb geochronology, sedimentology, vertebrate palaeontology and micropalaeontology", showing that the shift from marine to continental deposition "occurred significantly earlier (by middle late Campanian) than hitherto considered", the lower formation being "previously thought to be restricted to the Maastrichtian".
- albert2025a, p. 27, 5.1: "it was assumed that deposition of the continental beds (grouped in the Densuș-Ciula Formation) started sometime during the early Maastrichtian (Grigorescu, 1992; Csiki-Sava et al., 2016)"; "we propose that the base of the continental Densuș-Ciula Formation should be placed in the middle part of the upper Campanian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Paleogene for the upper member.* Earlier works referred it to the lower Paleogene because no volcaniclastic material or vertebrates had been found in it; Csiki-Sava and colleagues (2016) report both and place the member in the late Maastrichtian.
- *A base in the early Maastrichtian, from the 69.8 Ma tuff.* Adds a source to the 2026-09-30 entry's ruling against a Maastrichtian-only formation: Albert and colleagues (2025) name Csiki-Sava and colleagues (2016) among the works whose early Maastrichtian start they replace.

**Open.** The late Maastrichtian of the middle and upper members rests on assumed sedimentation rates and stratigraphic position, which Csiki-Sava and colleagues say is "yet to be tested in detail". A date from the upper member would fix the top of the formation.

## Does the Densuș-Ciula Formation reach into the Campanian?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Yes: it covers much of the upper Campanian as well as the Maastrichtian. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on Densuș-Ciula.

**Evidence.**
- albert2025a, p. 1: the formation "covers a good part of the upper Campanian"; the syntectonic reading applies to the "earliest dinosaur-bearing strata".
- magyar2026a, p. 2, relays it.

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian only.* Earlier compilations give the formation as Maastrichtian in tables (norman2004a, Table 19.1; ősi2012a, Table 2; augustin2022a, p. 2). Albert and colleagues (2025) supersede them.

**Open.** Nothing.

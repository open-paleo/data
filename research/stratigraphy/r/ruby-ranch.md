# Ruby Ranch

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Ruby Ranch Member?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Aptian and Albian, unchanged, on dinosaur biostratigraphy. Sprinkel and colleagues (2012) give "Aptian to middle Albian" as one of the tentative ages first assigned to the lower Cedar Mountain members on their dinosaurs. Kirkland and Madsen (2007), after Kirkland, Lucas and Estep (1998), date the member's faunas to 115–105 Ma, a span that lies within the Aptian and Albian on the ICS chart v2024/12 (our placement), and describe isotope work on its carbonate nodules as a basis for correlation within the Aptian–Albian. Detrital zircons give only limits. Kirkland and Madsen report ages of 109 to 116 Ma near the Price River II Quarry from Burton and colleagues (2006), then explain them as reworked grains, because the samples lie above a calcrete wrongly taken as the member's base. Mori's (2009) master's thesis brackets the member between about 115 and 111 Ma, widened in its summary to "120 - 117 Ma to 111 Ma (Late Aptian to earliest Albian)". The bracket rests on maximum depositional ages, 117.2 ± 3.7 Ma just above the base at the Bodily nodosaur site and 115.0 Ma for the lower Cedar Mountain at Long Walk quarry, from samples up to about 35 m above the Morrison contact, and its top is inferred from 112–111 Ma grains reworked into the Mussentuchit Member. Sprinkel and colleagues (2012) find that the bulk of the formation around Dinosaur National Monument best fits the Ruby Ranch, but hold it unwarranted to use the central Utah member names there. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on [Ruby Ranch](../../../stratigraphy/r/ruby-ranch.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- sprinkel2012a, p. 2: "The lower three were initially assigned tentative ages based on dinosaur biostratigraphy: Barremian for the Yellow Cat Member in the northern Paradox Basin, Aptian for the Poison Strip Member, and Aptian to middle Albian for the Ruby Ranch Member".
- sprinkel2012a, p. 12: "The bulk of strata exhibited in our sections of the Cedar Mountain Formation best fit the Ruby Ranch Member"; p. 13: "it is unwarranted to use the member names of the Cedar Mountain Formation defined by Kirkland and others (1997) and Kirkland and others (1999) in central Utah for the Cedar Mountain Formation in northeastern Utah because not all members may be represented".
- kirkland2007a, p. 15: "Similar dinosaur faunas are known across much of North America, suggesting these rocks formed 115-105 million years ago"; detrital zircon ages "ranging from 109 to 116 Ma" near the Price River II Quarry "are all from the uppermost portion of the Ruby Ranch and Mussentuchit Members, above a massive calcrete (figure 14), which was mistakenly interpreted as the base of Ruby Ranch Member and therefore the ages are thought to indicate the presence of zircons reworked from lower in the section (Ludvigson, 2006, personal commun.)".
- kirkland2007a, p. 16: carbonate nodule isotope studies are "forming a basis for chronostratigraphic correlations within the Aptian-Albian time interval".
- mori2009a, Abstract: "Detrital zircons coarsely bracket the temporal span of the Ruby Ranch Member between about 115 Ma to 111 Ma".
- mori2009a, pp. 27–28: at the Bodily nodosaur site "the fluvial sandstone above the quarry taken as the base of the Ruby Ranch Member", and "the maximal depositional age calculated by Age Pick from the youngest 5 grains is 117.2 ± 3.7 Ma"; p. 28: at Long Walk quarry "79 dated grains, produces a maximum depositional age of 115.0 Ma for the bracketed section"; p. 21: lwq-DSS "was from and exhumed river channel about 35 meters above the Morrison-Cedar Mountain contact".
- mori2009a, p. 29: "the 112 to 111 Ma peaks of bns-5 and bns-9 (Fig. 5) represent crystals reworked from the sediment in the upper Ruby Ranch Member"; p. 30: "the Ruby Ranch Member ranges from 120 - 117 Ma to 111 Ma (Late Aptian to earliest Albian)".

**Ruled out.**
- *The 109–116 Ma detrital zircons as the member's age near Price River.* Kirkland and Madsen (2007) set them aside as reworked, on a personal communication, and Mori (2009, p. 29) places the Price River II Quarry near the base of the Mussentuchit. A detrital age is a maximum in any case and adds no stage.
- *Late Aptian to earliest Albian as the member's full span, after Mori (2009).* A thesis carries no value alone, and its bracket is built from maximum depositional ages and reworked grains; both its readings fall inside the Aptian–Albian the published works give.

**Open.** No published radiometric date from the member is among the works cited. Burton and colleagues (2006), an abstract, has not been read.

# Rock Point

## Do the Chama Basin beds date the Rock Point?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No; the record covers the true Rock Point and becomes Rhaetian, and the dispute is removed. Every Norian reading in the dispute concerns the Chinle at Ghost Ranch in the Chama Basin: Long and Murry's (1995) "?Early Norian" for beds in Rio Arriba County, Litwin and colleagues' (1991) palynology of the siltstone member, older than the latest Norian, and Rinehart and colleagues' (2009) late Norian for the Whitaker quarry. Whether those beds are Rock Point is unresolved: Lucas and Hunt and Rinehart and colleagues call them so, Zeigler and Geissman (2009) find them "not time equivalent to true Rock Point strata in Utah and Arizona", Cather and colleagues (2013) favor the siltstone member, and the [*Coelophysis*](../../../genera/C/Coelophysis.yml) and [*Daemonosaurus*](../../../genera/D/Daemonosaurus.yml) records name no member for that reason. A reading of beds the records keep out of the unit does not date it, and Novas and colleagues' (2021) "Rhaetian Rock Point Member" at the Coelophysis Quarry falls under the same rule. For the Rock Point of the Four Corners area, Martz and Parker (2017) place the member in an Apachean holochronozone "during the Rhaetian", and Lucas and Hunt (1993) give the Apachean, which they find in the Rock Point of Utah and Colorado, a Rhaetian age, adding that it "might be of late Norian age", which allows the Norian without adopting it ([methods](../../methods.md)). No reading of the true Rock Point gives the Norian. Supersedes the 2026-09-30 conclusion that the Rhaetian is not established. Governs `stages: [Rhaetian]` and the absent `dispute` on [Rock Point](../../../stratigraphy/r/rock-point.yml); no taxon record names the unit.

**Evidence.**
- long1995a, p. 186: "?Early Norian; upper Petrified Forest or Siltstone or Rock Point Member, Chinle Formation, Rio Arriba County, New Mexico".
- litwin1991a, p. 280: "these samples are younger than late Carnian, but older than the latest Norian".
- rinehart2009a, p. 24: "We favor the late Norian age for the Whitaker quarry".
- zeigler2009a, p. 28: "uppermost strata in northern New Mexico are not time equivalent to true Rock Point strata in Utah and Arizona nor to the Redonda Formation in eastern New Mexico".
- martz2017a, p. 112: "The Apachean estimated holochron extends from about 207 to 202 Ma (an interval of about 5 million years) during the Rhaetian"; the holochronozone takes in "most of the Owl Rock Member, Rock Point Member, and Church Rock Member".
- lucas1993a, p. 328: "Apachean footprints are also known from the Rock Point Formation of Utah and Colorado"; p. 329: "We consider Apachean to be of Rhaetian age".

**Ruled out.** - *Norian, from the Chama Basin readings.* They date beds the records do not place in the Rock Point.
- *Norian, from Lucas and Hunt's hedge.* A possibility they allow, not a reading.

**Open.** Whether the Chama Basin beds at Ghost Ranch are Rock Point. If a later work settles that they are, their Norian readings count and the dispute returns.

## Who equated the Apachean with the Rhaetian, and on what?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** Lucas and Hunt (1993), on the reasoning of Hunt (1991), and with a hedge. They take the Apachean to be Rhaetian because its tetrapods are more advanced than those of the late Norian Knollenmergel, and add that the assignment "is not certain, and it might be of late Norian age". Hunt and colleagues (1998) give "Apachean (Rhaetian)" after Lucas and Hunt alone, and cite Lucas (1993) only, with Lucas and Hunt, for the Chinle Group's overall late Carnian to Rhaetian range. The dispute now names Lucas and Hunt and their hedge, and the note on Lucas (1993) is narrowed. Governs the `dispute` summary and the notes on lucas1993b and the pointer to hunt1991b on [Rock Point](../../../stratigraphy/r/rock-point.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lucas1993a, p. 329: "We consider Apachean to be of Rhaetian age because its tetrapods are more evolutionarily advanced than those of the late Norian German Knollenmergel (Hunt, 1991a)"; "assignment of a Rhaetian age to the Apachean is not certain, and it might be of late Norian age".
- hunt1998a, p. 513: "Chinle Group strata range from late Carnian (early Tuvalian) to Rhaetian in age (Lucas 1993; Lucas & Hunt 1993b)"; "Apachean (Rhaetian) (Lucas & Hunt 1993b)".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Hunt (1991), an abstract, has not been read.

## Is the Rock Point ever Hettangian, and where does the Apachean's Rhaetian come from?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Adds to the 2026-09-30 entry and supersedes nothing in it. The Hettangian is ruled out. It comes from Zeigler and Geissman (2009), whose "Rhaetian to possibly earliest Hettangian" dates the uppermost Chinle strata of the Chama Basin, beds they say are not time-equivalent to the true Rock Point; Rinehart and colleagues (2009) relay it as a date for the Rock Point in the Chama Basin, only to reject it. The Rhaetian that Hunt and colleagues (1998) give through the Apachean originates in Lucas and Hunt (1993), who name the Apachean, find it in the Rock Point Formation, and consider it Rhaetian while allowing that it may be late Norian. Hunt and colleagues assign the Rock Point by name only to the Apachean, but they call the fauna of the Ghost Ranch quarry, which they place in the Rock Point, Rhaetian outright; that rests on the same equivalence and on the Ghost Ranch correlation that the 2026-09-30 entry leaves unassigned. Cather and colleagues (2013) keep the name "siltstone member" for the north-central New Mexico beds until that correlation is settled. Governs `stages: [Norian, Rhaetian]` and `dispute` on [Rock Point](../../../stratigraphy/r/rock-point.yml).

**Evidence.**
- rinehart2009a, p. 24, Stratigraphy and Age: "Zeigler and Geissman (2009) recently assigned a late Rhaetian-Hettangian age to the Rock Point Formation in the Chama basin based on the similarity of its paleopoles to those of the Moenave-Wingate interval to the west. Clearly, the Whitaker quarry is Late Triassic in age (not Hettangian), and either late Norian or Rhaetian."
- rinehart2009a, p. 1, Abstract: "The Whitaker quarry lies near the center of the Upper Triassic Rock Point Formation of the Chinle Group in the Chama Basin of northern New Mexico".
- zeigler2009a, p. ?, abstract: "uppermost strata in northern New Mexico are not time equivalent to true Rock Point strata in Utah and Arizona nor to the Redonda Formation in eastern New Mexico. Uppermost Chinle Group strata in the Chama Basin are Rhaetian to possibly earliest Hettangian in age."
- lucas1993a, p. 327, Abstract: "Apachean is of Rhaetian age"; p. 328, Apachean: "Apachean tetrapods and/or footprints … are found … in the Rock Point Formation at Ghost Ranch in north-central New Mexico", and "Apachean footprints are also known from the Rock Point Formation of Utah and Colorado".
- lucas1993a, p. 329: "assignment of a Rhaetian age to the Apachean is not certain, and it might be of late Norian age".
- hunt1998a, p. 513, Biochronology: "Chinle Group strata range from late Carnian (early Tuvalian) to Rhaetian in age (Lucas 1993; Lucas & Hunt 1993b). Vertebrate fossil assemblages represent four land-vertebrate faunachrons: the Otischalkian (late Carnian: early Tuvalian), Adamanian (late Carnian: late Tuvalian), Revueltian (early-late Norian: Lacian-Sevatian) and Apachean (Rhaetian) (Lucas & Hunt 1993b)." The paper's Lucas & Hunt 1993b is lucas1993a.
- hunt1998a, p. 512, Fig. 1 caption; p. 521; p. 522, Apachean dinosaurs: the Rock Point is dated five times, each as "Apachean", among them "the Ghost Ranch quarry (Rock Point Formation)" (p. 522).
- hunt1998a, p. 519, Table 1: "Apachean (Rhaetian)" heads the row for "*Coelophysis bauri* - lectotype and numerous other skeletons (NM)"; p. 524, Discussion: "Rhaetian tetrapod faunas are depauperate in dinosaurs in east-central and northeastern New Mexico, but at the Ghost Ranch quarry over 95% of the specimens pertain to theropods".
- cather2013a, p. 110: after Lucas and Hunt's rename and the magnetostratigraphy that "called into question this correlation", "Until this controversy is resolved, we favor a return to the use of the term siltstone member for these beds."

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Rhaetian to Hettangian.* Zeigler and Geissman (2009) date Chama Basin beds that they themselves separate from the true Rock Point, and hedge the Hettangian as "possibly earliest". Rinehart and colleagues (2009), who report it as a date for the Rock Point, reject it for the Whitaker quarry ("not Hettangian").
- *That Hunt and colleagues (1998) never set the Rock Point beside a stage.* They call the Ghost Ranch quarry's fauna Rhaetian (p. 524), though only through the Apachean equivalence of Lucas and Hunt (1993) and a correlation that is itself contested.

**Open.** Whether the Chama Basin beds at Ghost Ranch are Rock Point at all; the 2026-09-30 entry and Cather and colleagues (2013) leave it to the magnetostratigraphic work Cather and colleagues cite, which has not been read. Zeigler and Geissman (2009) give that work's conclusion only in an abstract.

## Is the Rock Point Rhaetian?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Not established. Hunt (1998) calls it Apachean and equates the Apachean with the Rhaetian after Lucas and Hunt (1993); others read the Apachean as late Norian–Rhaetian or late Norian. Governs `stages: [Norian, Rhaetian]` on Rock Point, and the empty `location.member` on [*Coelophysis bauri*](../../../genera/C/Coelophysis.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hunt1998a, p. 512, Fig. 1 caption and pp. 521–522: "the younger (Apachean) Rock Point Formation"; every dated mention of the Rock Point in the paper is "Apachean", none a stage. p. 513: "Apachean (Rhaetian) (Lucas & Hunt 1993b)".
- rinehart2009a, p. 1: "Apachean (late Norian-Rhaetian)"; p. 24: "We favor the late Norian age for the Whitaker quarry".

**Ruled out.**
- *Rhaetian as an independent vote.* Hunt's "Apachean (Rhaetian)" is a relay of Lucas and Hunt (1993b), so a Rhaetian reached through it counts once. Novas and colleagues (2021) give "the Rhaetian Rock Point Member" for *Daemonosaurus* (novas2021a, pre-proof, p. 47) and "Rhaetian Owl Rock Member" on p. 22, contradicting themselves.
- *Coelophysis neotype in the Rock Point.* Hunt (1998, p. 521) puts "the neotype of *Coelophysis* (AMNH 7224) from the Ghost Ranch quarry that is the younger (Apachean) Rock Point Formation". The Ghost Ranch correlation with the Rock Point was "called into question … (Zeigler et al., 2008)" by magnetostratigraphy (cather2013a, p. 110), and Nesbitt and colleagues give the same beds in their own nomenclature (nesbitt2020a, p. 152), so no member is assigned.

**Open.** The name's scope. Lucas and Hunt (1992) "renamed these beds the Rock Point Formation for a unit at the top of the Chinle in the Four Corners area" (cather2013a, p. 110), applying an existing name (the type Rock Point is Harshbarger and colleagues 1957, per rinehart2009a, p. 21). The *C. bauri* syntypes are Revueltian, "early-middle Norian" (hunt1998a, p. 511).

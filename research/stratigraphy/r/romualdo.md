# Romualdo

## Does the Romualdo keep the Albian?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No; the formation is Aptian. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry on the stages. Arai and Assine (2020) date the formation upper Aptian from their own palynology, and report that "indisputably Albian palynomorphs" have not been published from it. No work read gives the Albian a reading: Naish and colleagues (2004) relay Pons and colleagues (1990), not read; Sues and colleagues' (2002) "probably younger than early Albian" is a bound inferred from Pons and colleagues' age for the Crato Formation below, and their "(Albian; Maisey, 2000)" credits a work their reference list does not contain; and Sales and Schultz (2017) give "Albian" in occurrence lines. [*Irritator challengeri*](../../../genera/I/Irritator.yml) and [*Aratasaurus museunacionali*](../../../genera/A/Aratasaurus.yml) take the formation's Aptian. Governs `stages: [Aptian]` and the notes on sues2002a and arai2020a on [Romualdo](../../../stratigraphy/r/romualdo.yml).

**Evidence.**
- arai2020a, p. 7: "although commonly referred to as Albian in the literature (Fig. 3), indisputably Albian palynomorphs … have not been reported thus far in any published work from the Romualdo Formation".
- sues2002a, p. 535: "Thus the concretion-bearing horizon of the Romualdo Member is probably younger than early Albian."

**Ruled out.**
- *Albian.* Relays, a bound and occurrence labels.

**Open.** Pons and colleagues (1990) have not been read.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Romualdo, and of which beds?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Aptian and Albian, the union of two readings that the [Santana Group](../s/santana.md) note leaves open. The Albian is given for the concretion level: Naish and colleagues (2004) relay a ?late Albian age from the palynology of Pons and colleagues (1990), Sues and colleagues (2002) put the concretion-bearing horizon "probably younger than early Albian", and Sales and Schultz (2017) date the *Irritator* and *Angaturama* holotypes Albian. The Aptian comes from the palynology of Arai and Assine (2020), who find the formation "almost entirely Aptian", and from Sayão and colleagues (2020), who place *Aratasaurus* in the lower strata, about 30 m below the concretion level, as Aptian while their title calls the formation Albian. Delcourt and colleagues (2025) follow Arai and Assine and call the upper concretion levels Aptian as well. Naish and colleagues treat the unit as the concretion-bearing member of the Santana Formation, deposited under brackish or quasi-marine conditions with an allochthonous dinosaur fauna; Sales and Schultz adopt formation rank after Valença and colleagues (2003) and report marine strata with echinoids and mollusks above the concretion beds in the west. Rank and parent are settled in [Rank that moves with the parent](../../topics/rank-that-moves-with-the-parent.md) and the Santana note. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on [Romualdo](../../../stratigraphy/r/romualdo.yml).

**Evidence.**
- naish2004a, title: "Santana Formation (?Albian, Early Cretaceous)".
- naish2004a, p. 57: "The calcareous nodule bearing Romualdo Member of the Santana Formation".
- naish2004a, p. 59: "The Romualdo Member was deposited under brackish or quasi-marine conditions"; "The dinosaur fauna is allochthonous and may have been derived from nearby shoreline environments".
- naish2004a, p. 60: "Romualdo Member of the Santana Formation, Araripe Group. Lower Cretaceous (Martill and Wilby, 1993). A ?late Albian age is inferred by Pons et al. (1990)".
- sues2002a, p. 535: Pons and colleagues suggested "a mid- to late Albian age for bituminous shales with fossils of the aspidorhynchid fish *Vinctifer* from the Romualdo Member".
- sues2002a, p. 536: "the concretion-bearing horizon of the Romualdo Member is probably younger than early Albian"; "On balance, the age of the Romualdo Member is probably late Early Cretaceous (Albian; …), but it could be as young as Cenomanian".
- sales2017a, p. 2, Geological settings: "For nomenclatural purposes, we will follow here Valença et al. [44], referring to the lithostratigraphic unit of the concretion-bearing beds as the Romualdo Formation"; "geological data indicate a transitional depositional setting for the majority of the stratigraphic sequence of the Romualdo Formation and a late Early Cretaceous (Albian) age"; "in the western portion of the formation and above the concretion-bearing beds, there are strata containing echinoids and mollusks".
- sales2017a, pp. 5 and 10, Systematic paleontology: for *Irritator* and for *Angaturama*, "Romualdo Formation; Albian, Lower Cretaceous".
- sayão2020a, title: "from the base of the Romualdo Formation (Albian)".
- sayão2020a, p. 2, Geological setting: the fossil horizon lies "Some 2.5 m above the conglomerate, at the base of the Romualdo Formation" and "about 30 m below the extremely fossiliferous horizon with calcareous concretions".
- sayão2020a, pp. 4–5, Horizon and locality: the specimen "was recovered from the lower strata of the Romualdo Formation (Aptian)", "in a dark shale located about 2.5 m above the contact with Ipubi Formation", citing Pons and colleagues (1990) among two sources for the age.
- arai2020a, p. 1, Abstract: "the age of the uppermost Romualdo Formation has been extended into the Albian"; "the Romualdo Formation is almost entirely Aptian in age".
- delcourt2025a, p. 1, Abstract: "The upper carbonate concretion levels of the Romualdo Formation (Aptian, Brazil)".
- delcourt2025a, p. 2, Introduction: "a world-famous Early Cretaceous (Aptian) fossil Konservat Lagerstätte that crops out along the margins of the Araripe Plateau", citing Arai and Assine (2020) among others; *Aratasaurus* "was recovered from the lower levels of that unit".

**Ruled out.**
- *Cenomanian.* Sues and colleagues (2002) say only that the member "could be as young as Cenomanian", after giving the Albian as probable. It is a possibility, not a reading, and adds no stage (see [methods](../../methods.md)).

**Open.** Whether the Albian stands against Arai and Assine's palynology, the question the Santana note's Open line leaves. Pons and colleagues (1990) are cited for the Albian of the concretion level by Naish and colleagues and by Sues and colleagues, and for the Aptian of the base by Sayão and colleagues; it has not been read here, and reading it would show whether it dates the base and the concretion level differently.

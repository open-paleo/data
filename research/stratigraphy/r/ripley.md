# Ripley

## What does Sohl (1960) say about the limits and age of the Ripley Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Sohl (1960) traces the name to Conrad (1858), who informally proposed a Ripley group for the strata on Owl Creek near Ripley, Tippah County, Mississippi; Hilgard (1860) first designated it formally and set its limits, and Stephenson and Monroe (1937) split the Owl Creek Formation off its top. In northern Mississippi Sohl divides the formation into five units, and its base grades down into the Demopolis Chalk. He places the Campanian–Maastrichtian boundary, tentatively, close to the base of the formation, so his reading puts nearly all of it in the Maastrichtian. Governs the Maastrichtian in `stages` on [Ripley](../../../stratigraphy/r/ripley.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sohl1960a, p. 9: "Conrad (1858, p. 324) informally proposed that the strata cropping out on Owl Creek in the vicinity of Ripley, Tippah County, Miss., might well be called the Ripley group"; "It remained for Hilgard (1860, p. 83) to more formally designate and ascribe limits to the Ripley sequence".
- sohl1960a, p. 10: "Stephenson and Monroe (1937, p. 808) divided the Ripley of Hilgard into two formations, the Ripley and the Owl Creek".
- sohl1960a, p. 1: "In northern Mississippi the formation can be divided into five units, which are, from oldest to youngest, transitional clay, Coon Creek tongue, McNairy sand member, sand of the upper part of the Ripley, and the Keownville limestone member"; "The base of the Ripley is gradational downward to the Demopolis chalk".
- sohl1960a, p. 8: "this places the boundary close to the base of the Ripley".
- sohl1960a, p. 11: "At present the best that can be done is to place the boundary on the basis of existing knowledge and accept it as tentative."

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

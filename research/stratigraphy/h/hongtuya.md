# Hongtuya

## Does any work give the Hongtuya the Maastrichtian?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No; the Maastrichtian is dropped, and the formation is Campanian. Answers the 2026-11-25 entry's Open line. Xing and colleagues (2014) give the formation's members "middle Campanian" and "late Campanian" and draw the whole formation below the Campanian–Maastrichtian line; An and colleagues (2015) conclude that it "was deposited during the Campanian"; the "upper Campanian–lower Maastrichtian" of Borinder and colleagues (2021) is for the Jiangjunding Formation above. [*Tanius sinensis*](../../../genera/T/Tanius.yml), whose horizon three works place in the Jiangjunding, keeps its Campanian–Maastrichtian with `resolution: horizon`. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` and the note on borinder2021a on [Hongtuya](../../../stratigraphy/h/hongtuya.yml).

**Evidence.**
- xing2014b, p. 1625, Table 1: the lower member of the Hongtuya Formation "middle Campanian", the middle member "late Campanian".
- an2015a, p. 85: "the Hongtuya Formation was deposited during the Campanian (from 83.6 ± 0.2 Ma to 72.1 ± 0.2 Ma)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian from Borinder and colleagues (2021).* Their age is the Jiangjunding's.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do Yan and Chen (2005) date, and in which unit?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Neither of their dates belongs to the Hongtuya as their citers report it. Answers the Yan and Chen part of the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Yan and Chen (2005) put the Daxizhuang basalt, whose Ar–Ar age of 73 Ma they take from an earlier paper by the first author, only in the Wangshi Group, never in the Hongtuya; Zhang and colleagues' (2019) report that Yan and Chen "obtained an isotope age of 73 Ma for the Jingangkou Formation (which they called the Hongtuya Formation)" is wrong on both counts. For the Hongtuya, Yan and Chen relay a K–Ar age of 70 Ma from the provincial lithostratigraphy of Zhang and Liu (1996); the Hongtuya section of that volume, in the chapter read, gives instead 85.52 Ma for a subvolcanic basalt southwest of Jiayue. Neither number adds a stage: the 70 Ma is second-hand and not found where it is credited, and a subvolcanic intrusion bounds its host rather than dating it. Governs `stages` on [Hongtuya](../../../stratigraphy/h/hongtuya.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- yan2005a, p. 11: "The host rock is an alkali basalt belonging to the Wangshi Group, with an Ar–Ar age of 73 Ma" (translated).
- yan2005a, p. 11: "The lower part of the Wangshi Group is dominated by basalt and belongs to the Hongtuya Formation, with a K–Ar age of 70 Ma" (translated).
- zhang1996a, p. 239: "Locally southwest of Jiayue, subvolcanic basalt is seen, with a K-Ar isotopic age of 85.52 Ma" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *73 Ma as a Hongtuya date.* Yan and Chen assign the Daxizhuang basalt only to the Wangshi Group.
- *70 Ma, Maastrichtian, for the Hongtuya.* It is credited to a volume whose Hongtuya account, in the copy read, prints 85.52 Ma instead.

**Open.** The Maastrichtian, which still rests on Borinder and colleagues (2021) citing Xing and colleagues (2014); Xing and colleagues' figures would settle it. Yan and colleagues (2005) has not been read.

## How does the provincial lithostratigraphy define the Hongtuya, and what dates do the cited works relay for it?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The Hongtuya is the formation that Liu Mingwei and colleagues named in 1994 and the provincial lithostratigraphy of Zhang and Liu (1996) adopts: brick-red and purplish-red fine sandstone and siltstone with sandy conglomerate, conformable between the Xingezhuang and Jingangkou formations, with a holostratotype of 448.56 m in the Wujiazhuang–Hongtuya section at Guliu, Laiyang, and a basalt member in the Zhucheng–Jiaozhou area. The volume treats the 1962 Yangjialing Conglomerate Formation and the 1992 Taocun Formation as synonyms of it, and lists the Laiyang hadrosaurs from Tianqiaotun and *Shantungosaurus* from Longgujian, Zhucheng, in its fauna. (The scheme itself, and the place of the Jiangjunding beds in it, are settled in the 2026-10-23 entry on [Wangshi](../w/wangshi.md).) The two radiometric figures relayed for the formation, 73 Ma from Yan and Chen (2005) through Zhang and colleagues (2019) and 72.9–73.5 Ma from Yan and colleagues (2005) through Borinder and colleagues (2021), are both Campanian on the ICS chart v2024/12, whose Maastrichtian begins at 72.1 Ma. The 73 Ma figure comes from a basalt at Daxizhuang in Jiaozhou; that it lies in the provincial basalt member, which the volume places in the upper part of the formation north of Jiaozhou City, is an inference from the two descriptions, not a statement in either. Governs `parent: Wangshi` and `lithology` on [Hongtuya](../../../stratigraphy/h/hongtuya.yml), and is consistent with its `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]`.

**Evidence.**
- zhang1996a, p. 238: "Named in 1994 by Liu Mingwei et al., with the locality at Hongtuya, Zhaowangzhuang Township, Laiyang City"; present definition: "A sequence of brick-red and purplish-red fine sandstone and siltstone interbedded with sandy conglomerate, lying conformably above the Xingezhuang Formation and below the Jingangkou Formation ... In the Zhucheng and Jiaozhou area it contains a basalt member"; "The holostratotype is the Wujiazhuang–Hongtuya section, Guliu Town, Laiyang City"; "Hongtuya Formation Total thickness 448.56 m" (translated).
- zhang1996a, p. 238, History: "In 1962 the First Petroleum Exploration Brigade of the Ministry of Geology called the equivalent strata in the Zhucheng area the Yangjialing Conglomerate Formation and the Fengtailing Sandstone Formation" (translated).
- zhang1996a, p. 239: in the Zhucheng–Jiaozhou subdivision, "In the area north of Jiaozhou City and at Zhangying, the upper part of this formation contains a basalt member" (translated).
- zhang1996a, p. 247: "Longgujian at Lübiao, Zhucheng, yields *Shantungosaurus giganteus* and *Tyrannosaurus* cf. *res*" (translated), in the hadrosaur fauna "found in the Hongtuya Formation".
- zhang1996a, p. 327, Appendix III, row 132: "Taocun Fm ... Synonym of Hongtuya Formation"; p. 328, row 166: "Yangjialing conglomerate Fm. ... Synonym of Hongtuya Formation" (translated).
- zhang2019a, p. 2, Introduction: "Yan & Chen (2005) obtained an isotope age of 73 Ma for the Jingangkou Formation (which they called the Hongtuya Formation) based on a basalt sample from the town of Daxizhuang in Jiaozhou City".
- borinder2021a, p. e1914642-3, Locality and Horizon: the Jiangjunding Formation outcrops near the *Tanius sinensis* site "stratigraphically range from the upper Campanian to lower Maastrichtian (Xing et al., 2014b). A radiometric date of 72.9–73.5 Ma was obtained from the uppermost section of the underlying Hongtuya Formation (Yan et al., 2005), providing a maximum lower age limit for the succession".

**Ruled out.**
- *72.9–73.5 Ma as a floor for the Jiangjunding dinosaur beds.* Borinder and colleagues (2021) read it so because, in the scheme of Hu and colleagues (2001), the Hongtuya lies below their Jiangjunding Formation. In the provincial scheme the Jiangjunding beds are part of the Hongtuya, and the dated basalt is from the Jiaozhou area, so no work cited fixes where the dated level lies relative to the Laiyang beds. An and colleagues (2015) instead treat a 73.5 Ma figure as a ceiling for the lower Hongtuya bone beds at Zhucheng (see the 2026-08-03 entry).
- *The Yangjialing Conglomerate and Taocun formations as units separate from the Hongtuya.* The provincial volume lists both as synonyms of it.

**Open.** Neither Yan and Chen (2005) nor Yan and colleagues (2005) has been read, so the method, the dated level, and whether the two relays describe one date are known only through Zhang and colleagues (2019) and Borinder and colleagues (2021). Reading them would show whether the basalt lies in the provincial basalt member.

## Are the Zhucheng bone beds in the Hongtuya or the Xingezhuang Formation?

*2026-08-03*

**Conclusion.** The Hongtuya, low in the formation. An and colleagues (2015), whose subject is the dinosaur bone beds of Zhucheng, place them in the Hongtuya and name *Zhuchengtyrannus* among its taxa. Governs `location.formation: Hongtuya` on [*Zhuchengtyrannus magnus*](../../../genera/Z/Zhuchengtyrannus.yml) and [*Zhuchengceratops inexpectus*](../../../genera/Z/Zhuchengceratops.yml), and `location.part: lower` on the latter.

**Evidence.**
- an2015a, p. 81: "Many dinosaur fossils, only found in the uppermost Upper Cretaceous strata, such as Sinoceratops zhuchengensis, Zhuchengtyrannus magnus, Tsintaosaurus spinorhinus, Tanius chinkankouenssi, and Tanius laiyangensis, have been reported in recent years from the Hongtuya Formation".
- an2015a, p. 81: "Dinosaur bones are mostly buried in the lower Hongtuya Formation, so the depositional age of the dinosaur bone beds must be >73.5 Ma".
- an2015a, p. 82: zircons from "conglomerates collected from the dinosaur bone beds of the Hongtuya Formation in Kugou".
- xu2010c, p. 2, Type locality and horizon: "Kugou, Zhucheng County, Shandong Province, China. Upper Cretaceous Wangshi Group".

**Ruled out.**
- *Xingezhuang Formation.* Jones (2026) prints "Xingezhuang Fm., China" for both species (jones2026a, Euceratopsia table; jones2026c, Tyrannosauridae table). The likely origin is Hone and colleagues (2011), who say the Zhucheng material "lies near the transition between the Xingezhuang Formation and the overlying Hongtuya Formation" (hone2011a, p. 496); the Xingezhuang is the formation below the Hongtuya in the same group. An and colleagues, who studied the bone beds directly, assign them to the Hongtuya.

**Open.** *Zhuchengceratops* is not in An and colleagues' list of taxa. Its record follows from Xu and colleagues' Kugou type locality and An and colleagues' statement about the Kugou bone beds; that the holotype lies in those beds is an inference, since neither paper places this specimen in the section.

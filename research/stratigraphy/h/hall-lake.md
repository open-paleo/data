# Hall Lake

## What do Lucas and colleagues (2019) give for the age of the Hall Lake?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** A late Maastrichtian lower part from dinosaurs, against Amato and colleagues' (2017) late Campanian tuff date for the same interval; the disagreement is live and is recorded as a dispute. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry where it says the dinosaurs' level in the unit is uncertain: Lucas and colleagues (2019), who raise the unit to a formation, place the bulk of the vertebrate assemblage 24–141 m above the base, including the ceratopsian skeletons "originally assigned to Torosaurus", and question the 73.2 Ma date because late Maastrichtian dinosaurs lie a short distance above the tuff. Their "may be Paleocene" for the upper part is a possibility they leave open while treating the formation as Cretaceous, and adds no epoch. Governs the `dispute` on [Hall Lake](../../../stratigraphy/h/hall-lake.yml); `stages` unchanged. [*Sierraceratops turneri*](../../../genera/S/Sierraceratops.yml) keeps Campanian–Maastrichtian.

**Evidence.**
- lucas2019a, p. 3: "Vertebrate biostratigraphy indicates that the lower part of the Hall Lake Formation is Lancian (late Maastrichtian) in age."
- lucas2019a, p. 28: "the bulk of the assemblage (including the most identifiable dinosaur fossils) comes from low in the formation, 24–141 m above its base"; "partial skeletons of a new ceratopsian genus originally assigned to Torosaurus (24–43 m above the base)".
- lucas2019a, p. 28: "this Campanian age is from a tuff stratigraphically just a short distance below late Maastrichtian dinosaur fossils, so we question its reliability".
- lucas2019a, p. 35: "Although treated here as units of Cretaceous age, the upper Hall Lake Formation and the Double Canyon Formation may be of Paleocene age."

**Ruled out.**
- *Paleocene for the upper part.* Lucas and colleagues raise it as a possibility only.
- *Dropping the Campanian.* The tuff date is published, and questioning it does not remove it under the union rule.

**Open.** Whether the 73.2 Ma tuff date stands. A second date from the lower Hall Lake would settle it.

## What does the U–Pb date of Amato and colleagues (2017) establish about the age of the Hall Lake?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The lower part of the unit is late Campanian, from a U–Pb zircon date, and the late Maastrichtian rests on dinosaur fossils whose level in the unit is uncertain. Amato and colleagues (2017) treat the unit as the Hall Lake Member of the McRae Formation; Dalman and colleagues (2021) report that it was raised to a formation, with the McRae raised to a group, so the two papers name the same rocks. The "pink tuff", 10 to 15 m above the base in the Cutter Sag, gives 73.2 ± 0.7 Ma, and with three tuffs in the underlying Jose Creek Member it places "at least the lower 15 m" of the unit at 75–73 Ma, in the late Campanian. The paper names the two earlier dinosaur finds, a *Tyrannosaurus rex* jaw fragment and *Torosaurus* horn and frill fragments, which "indicate a late Maastrichtian age", but their levels in the member are uncertain because of faulting and Quaternary cover, and whether the unit reaches the Paleogene "is not known". Governs the Campanian in `stages` on [Hall Lake](../../../stratigraphy/h/hall-lake.yml); the Maastrichtian is unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- amato2017a, p. 1213, McRae Formation: "Ten to fifteen meters above the base of the Hall Lake Member in the Cutter Sag, there is an ash-fall tuff referred to as the 'pink tuff.'"
- amato2017a, p. 1213, McRae Formation: the most age-diagnostic fossils "were a jaw fragment of *Tyrannosaurus rex* and horn and frill fragments of *Torosaurus* from separate sites in the Hall Lake Member"; "Although both fossils indicate a late Maastrichtian age, the stratigraphic positions of the fossils within the Hall Lake Member are uncertain, because of faulting in the case of the *T. rex* fossil and isolation by Quaternary sediment for the *Torosaurus* fossils"; "Whether or not the Hall Lake Member crosses the Cretaceous-Paleogene boundary is not known."
- amato2017a, p. 1218, Volcanic Rocks from McRae Formation: of the pink tuff's zircons, "the other 22 were Campanian with a weighted mean 206Pb/238U age of 73.2 ± 0.7 Ma".
- amato2017a, p. 1220, Comparison of the Age of Onset of Deposition in the Love Ranch Basin with Other Laramide Basins in New Mexico: "it was assumed that sedimentation in the Love Ranch Basin began in Maastrichtian time, based on the presence of Upper Maastrichtian (ca. 68–66 Ma) dinosaur fossils in the upper member (Hall Lake)"; the tuffs "indicate that the middle and upper parts of the Jose Creek Member and at least the lower 15 m of the overlying Hall Lake Member are 75–73 Ma in age (late Campanian".
- dalman2021b, p. 2, Geologic setting: "The Hall Lake Formation was previously considered a member of the McRae Formation"; "it was raised to formational status by Lucas et al. (2019), who also raised the McRae to group level".

**Ruled out.**
- *Deposition beginning in the Maastrichtian.* The earlier assumption, which Amato and colleagues (2017) report, rested on the late Maastrichtian dinosaurs; they set it aside because the fossils' levels are uncertain and the tuffs date the lower part to the late Campanian.
- *An age reaching the Paleocene.* Amato and colleagues (2017) say only that whether the unit crosses the Cretaceous–Paleogene boundary "is not known". That is an open question, not a reading, and adds no epoch.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Hanson

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Hanson?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Early Jurassic, with the vertebrate-bearing lower part probably in the middle of the series. Smith and Pol (2007) bracket the formation rather than date it. Below, *Dicroidium odontopteroides* in the Falla Formation, 300 meters under the vertebrate-bearing layers, and pollen and spore assemblages from the middle Falla give a Carnian–Norian (Late Triassic) bound for the base. Above, a radiometric date of 177±2 Ma on the overlying Prebble Formation and Kirkpatrick Basalt gives an Aalenian (earliest Middle Jurassic) bound for the top. Citing further radiometric dates from the top of the formation, they relay the reading that its lower part, which includes the vertebrate-bearing horizons, is "probably middle Early Jurassic". That is consistent with the Sinemurian–Pliensbachian that Benson (2010) gives for *Cryolophosaurus ellioti*. Governs `stages: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian]` and `period: [Early Jurassic]` on [Hanson](../../../stratigraphy/h/hanson.yml).

**Evidence.**
- smith2007a, p. 659: "*Dicroidium odontopteroides* in the Falla Formation 300 meters below the vertebrate bearing layers of the Hanson Formation"; pollen and spore assemblages "provide an upper bound of Carnian–Norian (Late Triassic) for the base of the Hanson Formation"; "A radiometric date of 177±2 Ma of the overlying Prebble Formation and Kirkpatrick Basalt" "gives a lower bound of Aalenian (earliest Middle Jurassic) for the top of the Hanson Formation"; "the lower part of the Hanson Formation, which includes the vertebrate-bearing horizons, is probably middle Early Jurassic".

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Triassic or Middle Jurassic stages from the bracket.* The Carnian–Norian and the Aalenian are bounds set by units below and above the formation, not ages of the formation itself, and a bound licenses no stage (see [methods](../../methods.md), "A maximum depositional age, or a lava flow above or below, is a limit, not an age").

**Open.** Nothing.

# Hasandong

## Does the U–Pb age of a dinosaur tooth date the Hasandong?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** No. Sano and colleagues (2006) dated a dinosaur tooth from the formation at 115 ± 10 Ma, and Lee and colleagues (2010), who relay it, read that figure as the age of diagenesis, which follows burial: it is a minimum for deposition, not a depositional age. Lee and colleagues find it "slightly older" than their own late Aptian to late Albian dating but within it. Governs nothing beyond `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on [Hasandong](../../../stratigraphy/h/hasandong.yml), which it leaves unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- lee2010b, p. 650: "Sano et al. (2006) determined a U–Pb age of 115 ± 10 Ma (early Aptian) of a dinosaur tooth from the Hasandong Formation. Considering that this radiometric age of the dinosaur tooth represents the diagenetic age, the depositional age of the host Hasandong Formation would be slightly older than the early Aptian".
- lee2010b, p. 651: "The youngest Cretaceous zircon age is 118.0 ± 3.0 Ma for the Nakdong Formation, 109.0 ± 3.4 Ma for the Hasandong Formation, and 106.0 ± 1.9 Ma for the Jinju Formation"; "the youngest age becomes progressively younger up-sequence"; "The diagenetic age of dinosaur tooth from the Hasandong Formation (Sano et al. 2006) is slightly older than the proposed age of this study, but generally fits within the new chronological frame".

**Ruled out.**
- *An early Aptian or older Hasandong, from the tooth.* The "early Aptian" label is Lee and colleagues' for the central value; with its ± 10 Ma the figure spans 125 to 105 Ma, Barremian to Albian on the ICS chart v2024/12 (our arithmetic), and as a diagenetic age it gives only a minimum age for deposition. Lee and colleagues set it inside their younger frame rather than against it.

**Open.** Sano and colleagues (2006) have not been read; they would show whether the tooth's age was measured as diagenetic or is only read so by Lee and colleagues.

## Who reads the Hasandong as Aptian–Albian in yun2020a?

*2026-10-08*

**Conclusion.** Yun alone; the paper has a single author. Corrects "Yun and colleagues" in the 2026-09-30 entry's **Open** line; the reading and its weight are unchanged. Governs nothing on [Hasandong](../../../stratigraphy/h/hasandong.yml) beyond the yun2020a note.

**Evidence.**
- yun2020a: authored by Yun, giving "Aptian-Albian" (p. 2), on a relayed "118.0 ± 2.6 Ma (Upper Aptian)" U-Pb age and molluscan faunas (p. 4).

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the Hasandong Formation Hauterivian–Barremian or Aptian–Albian?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** Aptian–Albian, with a dispute: a detrital-zircon maximum age makes it no older than about 112 Ma (Albian), while relayed sources keep the Aptian. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` and the dispute on Hasandong, and the stages of [*Pukyongosaurus millenniumi*](../../../genera/P/Pukyongosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lee2010b, p. 651: youngest detrital zircon "109.0 ± 3.4 Ma for the Hasandong Formation"; p. 656: these are "each representing the maximum depositional age", and "the Sindong Group was deposited during the late Aptian to late Albian, which is younger than previously thought". The 109.0 Ma value is a single grain (Table 1, p. 652; the next grains are 111.4 and 112.2 Ma); even at its older limit (112.4 Ma) it falls in the Albian (our arithmetic).

**Ruled out.**
- *Hauterivian–Barremian.* Dong and colleagues report that the formation "generally been assigned to the Hauterivian on the basis of charophyte, spore and pollen study, although an early Aptian age was suggested from paleomagnetism" (dong2001a, p. 42); Barrett and colleagues give "Hasandong Formation (Hauterivian)" (barrett2002a, p. 1207), and Wilson and Upchurch (2009, wilson2009a, p. 234, Table 7) and Carrano and colleagues (2012, online Appendix 3) give Barremian or Hauterivian–Barremian, all relaying Lee and colleagues (1997, 2001). The zircon maximum is younger.

**Open.** The Aptian. A maximum depositional age does not set stages on its own, so the Aptian stands as the other side of the dispute: Sha (2007) correlates the Hasandong with the "mainly Aptian" Guilin Formation through a "Barremian-Albian (mainly Aptian) trigonioidid assemblage" (sha2007a, p. 80), and Yun and colleagues give "Aptian-Albian" (yun2020a, p. 2), relaying papers not read here (one of which may itself be a maximum age).

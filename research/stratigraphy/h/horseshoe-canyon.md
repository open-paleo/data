# Horseshoe Canyon

## What do the cited works say about the age and limits of the Horseshoe Canyon?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Late Campanian to Maastrichtian, with the stage boundary inside the formation. Lerbekmo and Braman (2002) assign the interval from the middle of the basal sandstone up to the Drumheller Marine Tongue to magnetozone 32n and place the Campanian–Maastrichtian boundary at the top of 32n.1n; Larson and colleagues (2010) put the boundary at about the base of the Drumheller Marine Tongue. Eberth and Braman (2012), the stratigraphic revision that divides the formation into seven members, give it a span of about 74.0 to 67 Ma, late Campanian to late Maastrichtian, where it is thickest and most complete, after Lerbekmo and Braman; the formation's base becomes older westward, which is how a base in 33n there agrees with Lerbekmo and Braman's 32n for the base at Drumheller (our inference). The two papers draw the top differently: Lerbekmo and Braman count the Whitemud and Battle as formations of the Edmonton Group, and Eberth and Braman make the Whitemud the formation's topmost member. They describe the same rock with the upper boundary in different places and do not disagree about its age. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Horseshoe Canyon](../../../stratigraphy/h/horseshoe-canyon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lerbekmo2002a, p. 539: the Red Deer Valley exposes "the marine Bearpaw Formation and the overlying continental Edmonton Group (Horseshoe Canyon, Whitemud, Battle, and Scollard formations)".
- lerbekmo2002a, p. 546: "The mixed polarity interval that extends downward from the Drumheller Marine Tongue (just below coal seam number 10) to the middle of the basal Horseshoe Canyon sandstone all belongs to magnetozone 32n"; the earlier reading of "the thick normal interval in the Horseshoe Canyon Formation below coal seam number 8 as being magnetozone 33n" rested on an assumed rate of sedimentation.
- lerbekmo2002a, p. 555: "The Campanian–Maastrichtian stage boundary, if placed at the base of the *H. constrictus* Zone, occurs at the top of 32n.1n"; the base of 33n is dated "at 79.34 Ma" in Wyoming; "The Dorothy Bentonite in the Red Deer Valley section, 20 m below the top of 33n, has yielded a Rb/Sr date on biotite of 73.2 Ma (H. Baadsgaard and J.F. Lerbekmo, unpublished data)".
- larson2010a, p. 1160: the formation "was deposited during late Campanian and early Maastrichtian time, with the boundary between these two stages approximately equivalent to the boundary between a lower coal- and mudstone-dominated succession and an overlying unit generally referred to as the Drumheller Marine Tongue".
- eberth2012a, p. 1053: "The Upper Cretaceous paralic to nonmarine Horseshoe Canyon Formation (HCFm) of southern Alberta is divided into seven mappable members: Strathmore, Drumheller, Horsethief, Morrin, Tolman, Carbon, and Whitemud (bottom to top)".
- eberth2012a, p. 1054: "To the west, the formation thickens and its base becomes progressively older"; "In its thickest and temporally most-complete expressions, the formation spans approximately 7 million years, ranging from late Campanian (∼74.0 Ma; magnetochron 33n) to late Maastrichtian (∼67 Ma; magnetochron 30n; Lerbekmo and Braman 2002)"; the Dorothy bentonite lies "in the upper part of the Bearpaw Formation at Dorothy".

**Ruled out.**
- *A disagreement about the formation's top.* Lerbekmo and Braman's Whitemud Formation and Eberth and Braman's Whitemud Member are the same beds placed outside or inside the formation.
- *33n for the thick normal interval low in the formation at Drumheller.* The reading of Lerbekmo and Coulter (1985), which Lerbekmo and Braman (2002) replace with 32n on the correlation of the Dorothy section with the Cypress Hills.
- *The 73.2 Ma Rb/Sr date as an age of the formation.* It comes from the Dorothy Bentonite in the upper Bearpaw Formation, below the Horseshoe Canyon, so it limits the formation from below at most. It is unpublished data given without an uncertainty, which by analogy with the rule on personal communications carries no reading of its own (see [methods](../../methods.md)). On the ICS chart v2024/12, whose Maastrichtian begins at 72.2 Ma, the figure is Campanian, 1.0 Myr below the base of the Maastrichtian. The 79.34 Ma is a date from Wyoming for the base of chron 33n, used to scale the chron, not a date of this formation.

**Open.** Nothing.

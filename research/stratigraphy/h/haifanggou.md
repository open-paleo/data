# Haifanggou

## Does the Haifanggou Formation reach the Oxfordian, and is the Jiulongshan the same unit?

*2026-10-06*

**Conclusion.** No, and yes. The formation is Bathonian–Callovian; its only Oxfordian is an older-chart label for a Callovian number. The Jiulongshan Formation is the same unit under its Hebei name. Governs `stages: [Bathonian, Callovian]`, `period: [Middle Jurassic]` and the `Jiulongshan` variant on [Haifanggou](../../../stratigraphy/h/haifanggou.yml). Consistent with the ranges the [Daohugou and Linglongta phases](../../topics/daohugou-and-linglongta-phases.md) note sets.

**Evidence.**
- xu2016b, p. 2231: the formation is "partially Bathonian but mainly Callovian", and "The Lanqi/Tiaojishan Formation is probably Oxfordian".
- chang2009b, abstract: "the whole Lanqi Formation was deposited in the Late Jurassic; consequently, the underlying Haifanggou Formation … [is] at least Middle Jurassic in age", from 40Ar/39Ar ages of about 160.7 Ma at the base of the Lanqi; introduction: "the Haifanggou Formation in Liaoning (also called the Jiulongshan Formation in Hebei Province)".
- sullivan2014a, p. 246: "the Jiulongshan (Haifanggou) Formation"; p. 248: the lower boundary age of the Tiaojishan "should also represent the upper boundary age of the Jiulongshan Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Oxfordian.* Wang and colleagues (2019) write that the formation "preserves the early phase of this biota (early Oxfordian)" and give "Oxfordian stage, 163 million years ago" for the *Ambopteryx* horizon (wang2019a, p. 256). On the current chart, whose Oxfordian begins at 161.5 Ma, 163 Ma is Callovian; the date is a personal communication; and Xu and colleagues (2016), one of the two works cited for the early Oxfordian, give Bathonian–Callovian.
- *"Middle and Late Jurassic Haifanggou Formation".* Pittman and colleagues (2020, pittman2020a, p. 44) in a compilation; the same volume places the unit in "the Middle Jurassic Bathonian" (p. 64).

**Open.** Whether the Daohugou strata belong to this unit or straddle its contact with the Tiaojishan, which Sullivan and colleagues (2014, p. 248) leave open.

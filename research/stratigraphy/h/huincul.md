# Huincul

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Huincul?

*2026-11-22*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian and Turonian: two readings are current, and they disagree over the top of the formation. Hugo and Leanza (2001) assign it to the upper Cenomanian on the age analysis of the Neuquén Group, giving the Turonian part of the Río Limay Subgroup's span to the Cerro Lisandro Formation above it, and Leanza and colleagues (2004) and Bellardini and colleagues (2023) keep it in the upper Cenomanian. Garrido (2010) weighs that reading against Legarreta and Gulisano's (1989) 93 to 91 Ma and Corbella and colleagues' (2004) fission-track date, and makes a tentative assignment to the upper Cenomanian and the Turonian, which Nogueira and colleagues (2024) and Simón and Salgado (2025) use; Apesteguía and colleagues (2016) give late Cenomanian to early Turonian. Simón and Salgado set the two readings side by side. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian]` and `dispute` on [Huincul](../../../stratigraphy/h/huincul.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hugo2001a, p. 13: "the Huincul Formation is assigned to the upper Cenomanian" (translated), "according to the general analysis carried out on the age of the Neuquén Group" (translated).
- hugo2001a, p. 10: the units of the Río Limay Subgroup "were deposited during the Cenomanian and lower Turonian" (translated); the Neuquén Group runs from "the lower Cenomanian (97 ± 3 Ma)" to "the lower Campanian (74 ± 3 Ma)" (translated).
- hugo2001a, p. 14: the Cerro Lisandro Formation "is assigned to the upper Cenomanian - lower Turonian" (translated).
- leanza2004a, p. 68: "The age of the Huincul Formation is probably Late Cenomanian", citing Hugo and Leanza (2001); the formation "conformably overlies the Candeleros Formation" and "is overlain conformably by the red claystones and siltstones of the Cerro Lisandro Formation"; "In the Cerro Policía region, a level of white tuff up to 2.5-m thick is present close to its base"; the Río Limay Subgroup's "age can be regarded as Cenomanian to Early Turonian".
- leanza2004a, p. 69: "The age of the Cerro Lisandro Formation can be regarded as Late Cenomanian–Early Turonian".
- garrido2010a, p. 138: "Although this unit has some microfloristic studies and radiometric data, the age of its deposits is not yet well established" (translated); Legarreta and Gulisano (1989) "place the Huincul Fm. between 93 and 91 Ma, situating it between the upper Cenomanian and the lower Turonian (International Stratigraphic Chart, 2009)" (translated); Hugo and Leanza (2001) and Leanza and colleagues (2004), "based on stratigraphic relations", "place it exclusively within the upper Cenomanian" (translated).
- garrido2010a, pp. 138–139: Corbella and colleagues' (2004) tuff "from the base of this unit in the Cerro Policía region" gave "88 ± 3 Ma", but "the degree of alteration presented by the analyzed tuff makes it necessary to have more data to corroborate the age put forward" (translated).
- garrido2010a, p. 139: "on the basis of the available data and its stratigraphic relations, the Huincul Fm. is tentatively assigned in this work an age between the Upper Cenomanian and the Turonian" (translated).
- corbella2004a, p. 227, Abstract: "the Huincul Formation was deposited between an age range from 84.1 to 91.9 My, that is from the base of the Turonian to the top of the Santonian".
- corbella2004a, p. 229: the tuff "is composed by reworked materials, including abundant glass shards strongly altered in zeolites"; the dated zircons come from "the white tuff level, outcropping at Agua de Las Vacas site", where the "88.0 ± 3.9 My population dominates the sample (fourteen out of twenty grains)".
- corbella2004a, p. 231: "the Huincul and Cerro Lisandro Formations would be younger than previously thought", deposition having "presumably started around the Early Turonian", though "it might have persisted up to the Late Santonian if we consider the 3.9 My error of the radiometric method".
- bellardini2023a, p. 2587, Abstract: "the lower section of Huincul Formation (upper Cenomanian)"; p. 2588: "the Upper Cretaceous outcrops of the Huincul Formation (upper Cenomanian)".
- bellardini2023a, p. 2589: at El Orejano "only the basal layers of the Huincul Formation are exposed", among outcrops of "the upper section of the Bajada del Agrio Group (Rayoso Formation, Aptian – Albian in age), and the lower section of the Neuquén Group (Candeleros, Huincul and Cerro Lisandro formations, Cenomanian – Turonian in age)".
- apesteguía2016a, p. 2, Introduction: "The fossiliferous Huincul Formation (Late Cenomanian-Early Turonian) is widely exposed in Neuquén and Río Negro provinces of northern Patagonia, Argentina"; p. 5, Systematic paleontology: "the Cenomanian to Turonian aged Huincul Formation".
- apesteguía2016a, p. 35, Discussion: the dinosaurs of the Huincul "are closely related to species found in mid-Cretaceous strata across North Africa", in the Kem Kem, Bahariya and Echkar, and its gigantic titanosaurs "are also known from the roughly coeval Bayo Overo Member of the Cerro Barcino Formation, central Patagonia, and from the Bajo Barreal Formation". The comparison rests on the formation's age.
- nogueira2024a, p. 1, Abstract: "the Huincul Formation (Cenomanian-Turonian, Upper Cretaceous) at the Pueblo Blanco Natural Reserve, Río Negro province, Argentina"; p. 1, Introduction: "The Huincul Formation (Neuquen Group; Cenomanian- Turonian; Garrido, 2010)"; p. 2, Locality and horizon: "yellowish and greenish fine- to medium-grained sandstones that can sometimes be tuffaceous", citing Leanza and colleagues (2004).
- simón2025a, p. 2, Systematic Paleontology: "Lower levels of the Huincul Formation, upper Cenomanian (Legarreta and Gulisano, 1989; Leanza, 1999), or upper Cenomanian-Turonian (Garrido, 2010)"; at Villa El Chocón the formation "overlies the Candeleros Formation, and is overlain by the pelitic levels of the Cerro Lisandro Formation", and its age is, "according to Legarreta and Gulisano (1989) and Leanza (1999), late Cenomanian (between 93 and 91 Ma)".
- simón2025a, p. 14, Discussion and conclusions: "the upper levels of the Huincul Formation, those that presumably correspond to the Turonian, no longer possess rebbachisaurids".

**Ruled out.**
- *Turonian to Santonian, from Corbella and colleagues' (2004) fission-track date.* The depositional range of 84.1 to 91.9 Ma runs from the Turonian to the Santonian on the ICS chart v2024/12, and the authors read it as making the formation younger than previously thought. The date comes from one zircon population in a reworked tuff whose glass is strongly altered; Garrido (2010) declines it on that alteration until more data corroborate it, and none of the later works quoted above follows it.
- *The Río Limay Subgroup's Cenomanian to early Turonian as the Huincul's age.* Hugo and Leanza (2001) and Leanza and colleagues (2004) give that span to the subgroup, give its Turonian part to the Cerro Lisandro Formation, and date the Huincul itself upper Cenomanian. Bellardini and colleagues' (2023) "Cenomanian – Turonian" is likewise one range for the Candeleros, Huincul and Cerro Lisandro together, in a paper that dates the Huincul upper Cenomanian. These spans are the parent's or the group's statements, not datings of the formation.

**Open.** Whether the formation reaches the Turonian rests on Garrido's tentative assignment and on Corbella and colleagues' rejected date; none of the works cited dates the upper part of the formation directly, and such a date would settle it. Legarreta and Gulisano (1989), the source of the 93 to 91 Ma, has not been read: Garrido (2010) gives that figure as upper Cenomanian to lower Turonian on the 2009 chart and Simón and Salgado (2025) as late Cenomanian. Whether it is a measured age or a stage given a number decides whether it is re-placed on the current chart, on which the whole interval falls in the Turonian.

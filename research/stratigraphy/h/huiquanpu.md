# Huiquanpu

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Huiquanpu?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Late Cretaceous, Cenomanian to Campanian. Pang and colleagues (2024) give the widest stated range from the charophytes, which "show a transitional character between the Early Cretaceous and the Late Cretaceous" and are placed in the Cenomanian–Campanian; the ostracods and the spore and pollen assemblage they date only to the middle to late Late Cretaceous, and paleomagnetic correlation with chrons 32, 32r, 33 and 33r places the upper dinosaur-bearing interval in the Campanian, at about 83–72 Ma. Pang and Cheng (2000) date the *Huabeisaurus*-bearing beds "Late Cretaceous probably Campanian" from the associated ankylosaurid *Tianzhenosaurus*, hadrosaurids, ostracods and charophytes. Every reading falls within the charophyte range. Pang and colleagues (2024) divide the formation by lithology into two members, numbered and not named, and place *Tianzhenosaurus chengi* in "the second member", the upper of the two, which is a position in the formation rather than a named member (see [methods](../../methods.md#a-member-takes-a-formal-name-a-position-goes-in-part)). Governs `period`, `stages` and `lithology` on the [Huiquanpu Formation](../../../stratigraphy/h/huiquanpu.yml).

**Evidence.**
- pang2024a, p. 41, Abstract: the new specimen was found "in the second member of the Upper Cretaceous Huiquanpu Formation" (translated).
- pang2024a, p. 52: the formation is "dominated by coarse clastic conglomerates and purple-red mudstones" (translated).
- pang2024a, p. 54, Fig. 8: "Upper Cretaceous Huiquanpu Formation, Member 2" above "Member 1" (translated).
- pang2024a, p. 55: the charophytes "show a transitional character between the Early Cretaceous and the Late Cretaceous, but the Late Cretaceous aspect is already quite clear, so assignment to the Late Cretaceous, Cenomanian–Campanian, is most suitable" (translated); the ostracod assemblage is "roughly the middle to late part" of the Late Cretaceous, and for the spores and pollen "the middle to late part being most suitable" (translated).
- pang2024a, p. 55: "the upper dinosaur-bearing interval is likewise middle to late Late Cretaceous, equivalent to the Campanian", correlated with chrons 32, 32r, 33 and 33r, "with an age of about 83–72 Ma" (translated).
- pang2000a, p. 124: the associated fauna and microfossils "support the view that the *Huabeisaurus*-bearing beds are Late Cretaceous probably Campanian in age".

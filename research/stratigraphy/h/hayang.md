# Hayang

## Does the Hayang keep the Albian?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** Held until Choi (1985) is read. The Albian rests on two kinds of evidence, neither yet a reading. Lee and colleagues (2010) make the group "equal to or younger than late Albian" from the maximum depositional age of the Sindong Group below, a limit; and the Aptian–Albian that they and Choi and Kwon (2019) relay comes from the palynology of Choi (1985), with Seo (1985), a thesis, and charophyte work. Lee and colleagues set that palynological age aside for "Cenomanian to early Campanian", and Jwa and colleagues (2009) date the Gusandong Tuff in the upper group to the middle Cenomanian from its magmatic zircons. The Albian of the [Hupyeongdong](../h/hupyeongdong.md) and its [Gugyedong](../g/gugyedong.md) Member rests on their position below the Sagok, whose 100–97 Ma is a bracket between a maximum depositional age and the tuff above. Dropping the Albian now would take it from all three units and from [*Ultrasaurus tabriensis*](../../../genera/U/Ultrasaurus.yml), which a reading of Choi (1985) could restore. Governs nothing yet; `stages` on [Hayang](../../../stratigraphy/h/hayang.yml) unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- lee2010b, p. 653: "Based on palynological data (Choi 1985; Seo 1985; Choi 1987, 1989; Choi & Park 1987), the Hayang Group was interpreted to have been deposited during the Aptian and Albian."
- jwa2009a, p. 265: "The zircon U−Pb ages of the Kusandong Tuff are 97.1 ± 2.0 Ma and 97.3 ± 1.8 Ma, indicating that the tuffaceous bed formed during the middle Cenomanian."

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Choi (1985) has not been read.

## What do Lee and colleagues (2010) and Choi and Kwon (2019) say about the age of the Hayang Group?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Albian to Campanian. Lee and colleagues (2010) place the group conformably on the Sindong Group, whose youngest detrital zircons put its top in the late Albian, so that the Hayang "should be equal to or younger than late Albian"; with the Gusandong Tuff in its upper part dated 97–96 Ma and the boundary with the Yucheon Group at 81–80 Ma, they interpret it as "deposited during the Cenomanian to early Campanian". The 81–80 Ma top is early Campanian on GTS2020 too, whose base of the Campanian is 83.7 Ma (Gale and colleagues, 2020). The Albian rests on the lower group: Choi and Kwon (2019) find their maximum depositional ages from the Euiseong subbasin, 104.9 Ma for the Jeomgok among them, in agreement with the Aptian–Albian of earlier palynological work, and Lee and Park (2020) relay maximum depositional ages of 109 to 105 Ma from the lower formations of the Milyang subbasin. Every zircon maximum depositional age from the group is younger than the base of the Albian at 113.2 Ma, which keeps the Aptian out. Governs `stages: [Albian, Cenomanian, Turonian, Coniacian, Santonian, Campanian]` and `period: [Early Cretaceous, Late Cretaceous]` on [Hayang](../../../stratigraphy/h/hayang.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lee2010b, p. 649: the Cretaceous groups of the Gyeongsang Basin are, "from oldest to youngest, the Sindong, Hayang, Yucheon, and the Bulkuksa intrusive Groups", and the Sindong Group "is overlain conformably by the Hayang Group on the eastern side".
- lee2010b, p. 653: "Based on palynological data" … "the Hayang Group was interpreted to have been deposited during the Aptian and Albian. However, the depositional age of the Hayang Group should be equal to or younger than late Albian. In the Hayang Group, the Gusandong Tuff, which occurs in the upper part of it, has been dated to be 97–96 Ma".
- lee2010b, p. 654: the boundary with the overlying Yucheon Group "has been suggested to be 81–80 Ma"; "Considering these time frames, the Hayang Group is interpreted to have been deposited during the Cenomanian to early Campanian."
- lee2010b, p. 656: the Sindong Group "was deposited during the late Aptian to late Albian", its youngest detrital zircon "106 Ma for the Jinju Formation".
- choi2019a, p. 574: given "the maximum depositional age of the Jinju Formation of the Sindong Group, the underlying unit of the Hayang Group, is about 106 Ma (Lee et al., 2010)", the meaningful maximum depositional ages are "the Jeomgok Formation (180829-2; 104.9 Ma), the Sagok Formation (180828-6; 99.8 Ma), and the Banyawol Formation (180828-2; 96 Ma)", which "agree well with the Aptian–Albian depositional age of the Hayang Group indicated by previous studies" (translated).
- choi2019a, p. 574: if the Gusandong Tuff erupted at 97 Ma, "the depositional age of the Sagok Formation can be constrained to about 100–97 Ma" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian, from palynology.* Lee and colleagues (2010) report the palynological Aptian–Albian and set it aside for a floor in the late Albian. Choi and Kwon (2019) accept the earlier range, but none of their ages, nor those Lee and Park (2020) relay, is older than the base of the Albian. The [Gugyedong](../g/gugyedong.md) entry of 2026-09-30 and the [Hupyeongdong](hupyeongdong.md) entry of 2026-10-03 reach the same result for those units.
- *A Cenomanian start, from Lee and colleagues (2010).* Their own floor is the late Albian, and the Cenomanian start is their interpretation above it. The later zircon ages from the lower group, in Choi and Kwon (2019) and Lee and Park (2020), are Albian numbers, and Choi and Kwon find them consistent with an Albian age. They are maximum depositional ages, limits rather than ages: they allow deposition in the Albian without proving it (see Open).

**Open.** No direct date from the lower Hayang Group appears in the works cited; the Albian rests on maximum depositional ages and on the earlier palynological range that Choi and Kwon accept. An eruption age from a tuff in the lower formations would show whether deposition began in the Albian.

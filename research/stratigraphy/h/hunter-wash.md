# Hunter Wash

## Who erected the Hunter Wash Member, and how is it defined?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Hunt and Lucas (1992) coin the name for the fine-grained strata between the Fruitland Formation and the Farmington Sandstone Member that Reeside (1924) called the "lower shale member" of the Kirtland. The type section is along Hunter Wash, in townships T24N, R12W and R13W, where the member is about 375 m thick. In their scheme it is the second of the Kirtland's five members, above the Bisti and below the Farmington. Governs `rank: member`, `parent: Kirtland` and `lithology` on [Hunter Wash](../../../stratigraphy/h/hunter-wash.yml). The member's age is treated in [Maastrichtian in the lower Kirtland members](../../topics/lower-kirtland-maastrichtian.md).

**Evidence.**
- hunt1992a, p. 217, Abstract: "The Kirtland consists, in ascending order, of the Bisti, Hunter Wash, Farmington, De-na-zin and Naashoibito Members."
- hunt1992a, p. 218, Hunter Wash Member: "Reeside (1924) included all the fine-grained strata between the Fruitland Formation and the Farmington Sandstone Member of the Kirtland Formation in a unit informally termed the lower shale. He referred to this unit as the 'lower shale member' of the Kirtland"; it "consists dominantly of green siltstone with minor white, lenticular sandstone, thin coal and rare volcanic ash beds"; "We coin the name Hunter Wash Member for this stratigraphic unit. The type section is along Hunter Wash (Fig. 5) in secs. 23–26, 27–28, 33–34, T24N, R13W, and secs. 8–9, 16–20, T24N, R12W".
- hunt1992a, p. 219: "The Hunter Wash Member is about 375 m thick in its type area"; Fig. 2 sets the member above the Bisti Member and below the Farmington Member, against Reeside's (1924) "lower shale member".

**Ruled out.**
- *The "Hunter Wash local fauna" as a name for the member's fauna.* Hunt and Lucas (1992) abandon that paleontological term, which earlier authors used for the vertebrate assemblages of the Fruitland and Kirtland below the Naashoibito Member, and say it "should not be confused with the rock-stratigraphic term Hunter Wash Member" (hunt1992a, p. 219).

**Open.** Nothing.

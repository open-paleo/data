# Hikagenosawa

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Hikagenosawa?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Late Albian to Cenomanian. In their revised scheme for the Yezo Group, Takashima and colleagues (2004) give the formation as the upper Albian to middle Cenomanian mudstone unit, and place the mid-Cenomanian event at its top. The ammonoids *Mortoniceras rostratum*, *Mariella bergeri*, *Mantelliceras saxbii* and *Cunningtoniceras cunningtoni*, of late Albian to middle Cenomanian age, occur in it, and its planktonic foraminifers, of the *Biticinella breggiensis* to *Rotalipora cushmani* zones, indicate a late Albian to late Cenomanian age. Their key-bed table dates the Kanajiri Sandstone Member, in the lower middle part of the formation, as latest Albian. The foraminiferal range reaches the late Cenomanian, beyond the middle Cenomanian the same paper gives for the formation's top; the difference lies within the Cenomanian. Governs `stages: [Albian, Cenomanian]` and `period: [Early Cretaceous, Late Cretaceous]` on [Hikagenosawa](../../../stratigraphy/h/hikagenosawa.yml).

**Evidence.**
- takashima2004a, p. 365, Abstract: "the Hikagenosawa Formation (Upper Albian–Middle Cenomanian mudstone-dominant unit)"; "The mid-Cenomanian event (MCE) horizon is identified at the top of the Hikagenosawa Formation".
- takashima2004a, p. 369, Table 1: KY-3, "Lower middle part of the Hikagenosawa Formation (Kanajiri Sst Mbr)", "Latest Albian".
- takashima2004a, p. 375: "The late Albian–middle Cenomanian ammonoids *Mortoniceras rostratum* (Sowerby), *Mariella bergeri* (Brongniart), *Mantelliceras saxbii* (Sharpe) and *Cunningtoniceras cunningtoni* (Sharpe) occur in the formation"; planktonic foraminifers "indicating a Late Albian–Late Cenomanian age".

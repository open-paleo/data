# Horsethief

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Horsethief Member?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian to Maastrichtian. Eberth and Kamo (2020) interpolate the member's base, the Drumheller–Horsethief boundary, at 72.2 ± 0.1 Ma and its top, the Horsethief–Morrin boundary, at 71.5 ± 0.1 Ma, from U-Pb ages on bentonites. From the magnetostratigraphy they place the Campanian–Maastrichtian boundary in the upper half of the member and give it an interpolated range of 71.8–71.4 Ma, so the lower member is Campanian and its upper part reaches the Maastrichtian. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Horsethief](../../../stratigraphy/h/horsethief.yml).

**Evidence.**
- eberth2020a, accepted manuscript, p. 15, Drumheller Member and the Drumheller bentonite zone: "We interpolate an age of 72.2 ± 0.1 Ma for the Drumheller-Horsethief boundary".
- eberth2020a, accepted manuscript, p. 16, Horsethief and Morrin members: "Our interpolated age for the overlying Horsethief-Morrin member boundary … is 71.5 ± 0.1 Ma".
- eberth2020a, accepted manuscript, p. 18, 31r-31n magnetochron boundary and the Campanian-Maastrichtian boundary: "we propose that the C-M boundary occurs somewhere in the upper one-half of the Horsethief Member".
- eberth2020a, accepted manuscript, p. 29, Conclusions: "we propose that the C-M boundary lies in the upper Horsethief Member, and conservatively assign it an interpolated age range of 71.8–71.4 Ma".
- eberth2020a, accepted manuscript, p. 46, Table 3: the TRB, in the member, is the "lowest position for base of Maastrichtian Stage", and the base of the Morrin Member the "highest position".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian throughout, from the numbers.* On the ICS chart v2024/12 the numeric base of the Maastrichtian is 72.2 Ma, the member's interpolated base, so placing the member's ages on the chart would make it wholly Maastrichtian. Eberth and Kamo (2020) place the stage boundary inside the member, in its upper part, and a stated stage placement is carried over rather than re-derived from the numbers ([methods](../../methods.md)).

**Open.** Where in the upper half the boundary lies. Eberth and Kamo (2020) cannot tell which of three normal subchrons in the section matches the one at the boundary elsewhere, and the section has no marine biostratigraphic marker to decide it. A marine biostratigraphic tie in the section, such as the ammonoid *B. baculus* they name, would settle it.

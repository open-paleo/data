# Belle Fourche

## Does the Belle Fourche Member, or the Frontier Formation, reach the Albian?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** No. The Albian–Cenomanian boundary lies below the Mowry Shale, which underlies the Frontier; the old "latest Albian" for the base of the Belle Fourche reflects a boundary once placed at the top of the Mowry. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian]` on Belle Fourche, the Frontier's Cenomanian base, and `stage: [Cenomanian]` on [*Stegopelta landerensis*](../../../genera/S/Stegopelta.yml) and [*Nodosaurus textilis*](../../../genera/N/Nodosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- singer2020a, p. 1665: "Topmost Bentonite, Mowry Shale, Kaycee, Wyoming, USA, 97.52 ± 0.09 Ma • Clay Spur Bentonite, Mowry Shale, Casper, Wyoming, 98.17 ± 0.11 Ma"; p. 1670: "historic usage of the bentonite atop the Mowry Shale … as demarcating the Albian–Cenomanian boundary", and "Only recently (Scott et al., 2018; Plint et. al., 2018) has the placement of the Albian–Cenomanian boundary below the Mowry Shale/Fish Scales Formation been widely accepted"; p. 1676: the Shell Creek Shale (100–99.3 Ma) and its equivalents "likely record the Albian–Cenomanian boundary".
- cobban1990a, p. B1: "the Belle Fourche Member of early to late Cenomanian age"; "Seven of the twelve established ammonite zones of middle and late Cenomanian age … can be recognized in the Belle Fourche Member".

**Ruled out.**
- *Latest Albian for the Stegopelta horizon.* Carpenter and Kirkland (1998) give "Base of Belle Fourche Member (latest Albian or earliest Cenomanian: Merewether, 1983), Frontier" (carpenter1998c, p. 263), on the old boundary.
- *An Early Cretaceous Frontier base in Utah.* Merewether and colleagues report that "the basal part of the Chalk Creek in some areas could be Early Cretaceous, as proposed by Ryer (1977b)", above "the Lower Cretaceous Aspen Shale" (merewether1984a, p. 5); a long-ranging ammonite "Metengonoceras sp. (Albian and early Cenomanian)" (merewether2010a, p. 14) does not fix the Albian. That the Aspen is the Mowry's equivalent is our inference.

**Open.** The Belle Fourche's top varies "from early late Cenomanian … to early Turonian … and the top of the member probably reflects differential erosion" (merewether1996a, p. T28).

# Belle Fourche

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Belle Fourche Member, and which units share its name?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian, with a diachronous top that reaches the early Turonian in one area on one zonation. Merewether and colleagues (1979), the specialist treatment of the parent formation, give the member a Cenomanian age; a search of their text finds no Albian. Merewether (1996) dates its middle and upper parts by ammonites and bivalves as middle Cenomanian to early Turonian, leaves a lower part early or middle Cenomanian, and puts the top in the early late Cenomanian in east-central Natrona County and in the early Turonian in southern and central Johnson County. In Natrona and Converse counties the late Cenomanian top is overlain disconformably by middle Turonian beds. Carpenter and Kirkland (1998) put the *Nodosaurus textilis* horizon in the middle Cenomanian, citing Merewether and colleagues as "1978" (their reference list gives the same paper, *The Mountain Geologist* v. 16) and Merewether (1983). Singer and colleagues (2020) call the beds above the Mowry Shale the "Belle Fourche Shale Member of the Frontier Formation" and the Newcastle Sandstone below the Mowry lower Cenomanian. Three units carry the name: the member of the Frontier Formation in Wyoming; the Belle Fourche Shale, into which the member grades eastward in the Powder River Basin; and the Belle Fourche Formation of Canada that Pittman and colleagues (2020) tabulate. Governs the Turonian in `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian]` and the `dispute` on [Belle Fourche](../../../stratigraphy/b/belle-fourche.yml).

**Evidence.**
- merewether1979a, p. 67, Abstract: "the Belle Fourche Member of Cenomanian age, the unnamed member of early and middle Turonian age, and the Wall Creek Member of late Turonian and early Coniacian age"; "the shale, siltstone, and sandstone of the Belle Fourche Member grade eastward into shale of the Belle Fourche Shale and calcareous shale of the lower part of the Greenhorn Formation".
- merewether1996a, p. T28: "Ammonites and bivalves of middle and late Cenomanian and early Turonian age have been collected from outcrops and core of the middle and upper parts of the Belle Fourche"; a lower part "could be either early Cenomanian or middle Cenomanian"; the top "varies from early late Cenomanian (fig. 7, zone 13) in east-central Natrona County to early Turonian (fig. 7, zone 20 or 21) in southern and central Johnson County".
- merewether1996a, p. T30: "In Natrona and Converse Counties strata, of late Cenomanian age at the top of the Belle Fourche Member are disconformably overlain by beds of middle Turonian age in the Emigrant Gap Member".
- carpenter1998c, p. 251: "Belle Fourche Member (Middle Cenomanian: Merewether et al., 1978; Merewether, 1983), Frontier Formation"; p. 253: "Merewether et al. (1983) and Merewether (1983) revised the Frontier Formation in Wyoming, and reidentified Dunbar's Frontier Formation as the Belle Fourche Member of the Frontier Formation"; p. 270, References: "Merewether, E.A., Cobban, W.A. and Cavanaugh, E.T., 1978, Frontier Formation and equivalent rocks in eastern Wyoming: The Mountain Geologist, v.16, p. 67–101".
- singer2020a, p. 1672: the Clay Spur Bentonite "grades upward into as much as 2 m of the siliceous sediment characteristic of the Mowry Shale beneath the overlying dark, non-siliceous sediment of the Belle Fourche Shale Member of the Frontier Formation"; p. 1676: "The lower Cenomanian Newcastle Sandstone broadly overlaps in age with part of the Shell Creek Shale (100–99.3 Ma)".
- pittman2020c, p. 62, Table 5: "Belle Fourche Formation", Canada, Late Cretaceous, Cenomanian.

**Ruled out.**
- *A conflict between the Natrona County and Johnson County tops.* The late Cenomanian top that Cobban (1990) gives in Natrona County and the early Turonian top in Johnson County are the two ends of one eroded surface, whose age Merewether (1996) says "probably reflects differential erosion" (merewether1996a, p. T28). They are not rival ages for the member.

**Open.** Merewether and colleagues (2010) put the top "near Buffalo", in Johnson County, in "Zones 21 or 22 of the late late Cenomanian" (merewether2010a, p. 45), where Merewether (1996) has "early Turonian (fig. 7, zone 20 or 21)" in southern and central Johnson County. If the later zonation puts the same beds in the latest Cenomanian, the Turonian rests on a superseded zone assignment; comparing the zone tables of the two papers would settle it. Whether the Canadian Belle Fourche Formation is the same unit at another rank, as the `dispute` has it, or a separate unit like the Belle Fourche Shale, is not settled by the works cited. Carpenter and Kirkland's "Merewether et al. (1983)" (p. 253) has no entry in their reference list, which lists only the 1978 paper.

## Does the Belle Fourche Member, or the Frontier Formation, reach the Albian?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** No. The Albian–Cenomanian boundary lies below the Mowry Shale, which underlies the Frontier; the old "latest Albian" for the base of the Belle Fourche reflects a boundary once placed at the top of the Mowry. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian]` on Belle Fourche, the Frontier's Cenomanian base, and `stage: [Cenomanian]` on [*Stegopelta landerensis*](../../../genera/S/Stegopelta.yml) and [*Nodosaurus textilis*](../../../genera/N/Nodosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- singer2020a, p. 1665: "Topmost Bentonite, Mowry Shale, Kaycee, Wyoming, USA, 97.52 ± 0.09 Ma • Clay Spur Bentonite, Mowry Shale, Casper, Wyoming, 98.17 ± 0.11 Ma"; p. 1670: "historic usage of the bentonite atop the Mowry Shale … as demarcating the Albian–Cenomanian boundary", and "Only recently (Scott et al., 2018; Plint et. al., 2018) has the placement of the Albian–Cenomanian boundary below the Mowry Shale/Fish Scales Formation been widely accepted"; p. 1676: the Shell Creek Shale (100–99.3 Ma) and its equivalents "likely record the Albian–Cenomanian boundary".
- cobban1990a, p. B1: "the Belle Fourche Member of early to late Cenomanian age"; "Seven of the twelve established ammonite zones of middle and late Cenomanian age … can be recognized in the Belle Fourche Member".

**Ruled out.**
- *Latest Albian for the Stegopelta horizon.* Carpenter and Kirkland (1998) give "Base of Belle Fourche Member (latest Albian or earliest Cenomanian: Merewether, 1983), Frontier" (carpenter1998c, p. 263), on the old boundary.
- *An Early Cretaceous Frontier base in Utah.* Merewether and colleagues report that "the basal part of the Chalk Creek in some areas could be Early Cretaceous, as proposed by Ryer (1977b)", above "the Lower Cretaceous Aspen Shale" (merewether1984a, p. 5); a long-ranging ammonite "Metengonoceras sp. (Albian and early Cenomanian)" (merewether2010a, p. 14) does not fix the Albian. That the Aspen is the Mowry's equivalent is our inference.

**Open.** The Belle Fourche's top varies "from early late Cenomanian … to early Turonian … and the top of the member probably reflects differential erosion" (merewether1996a, p. T28).

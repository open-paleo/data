# Baruungoyot

## Do Shuvalov's basalt dates of 78–80 Myr belong to the svita?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Only as his text states them. Shuvalov (2000) gives K–Ar dates of 78–80 Myr from basalts "in the middle and upper parts of the svita", but his Table 14.2 lists two other figures under the Baruungoyot Svita: 75 ± 7 Myr from an upper-svita basalt at Chuluut Uul, and 80 Myr from a North Mongolian "basaltic sheet on the Lower Cretaceous" that the row does not place in the svita. All three are Campanian on the ICS chart v2024/12, so the stages are unaffected; the dispute summary now says what the table shows. Governs the `dispute` and the note on shuvalov2000a on [Baruungoyot](../../../stratigraphy/b/baruungoyot.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- shuvalov2000a, pp. 269–270: "K–Ar dates from basalts in the middle and upper parts of the svita (in the gorge Borzongiin Gobi and others) are 78–80 Myr (Campanian)."
- shuvalov2000a, Table 14.2, p. 261: under "Baruungoyot Svita", "1. South Gobi, Chuluut Uul mountain, upper part of svita, basalt", "75 ± 7 (IGEM)"; "2. North Mongolia, Tsagaan Nuur depression, basaltic sheet on the Lower Cretaceous", "80 (IG)".

**Ruled out.**
- *78–80 Myr as table dates from within the svita.* The table has neither figure for a sample placed in the svita.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Baruungoyot?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Santonian to Maastrichtian, with the older limit disputed. The Campanian is the reading of Gradziński and Jerzykiewicz (1974), who redefined the formation, of Kielan-Jaworowska (1974) and of Van Itterbeeck and colleagues (2005); Khand and colleagues (2000) give it in their table and mollusc scheme while calling the deposits Santonian–Campanian in their text, and concede that the formation's position remains unclear. The Santonian rests on Shuvalov's (2000) own dating of the svita and on Karczewska and Ziembinska-Tworzydlo's (1983) charophytes. The Maastrichtian comes from the top of the formation, which interfingers with the Nemegt Formation: Fanti and colleagues (2012) give the upper beds at Nemegt as Campanian to Maastrichtian, which matches the gradual passage Gradziński and Jerzykiewicz (1974) describe. Governs `stages: [Santonian, Campanian, Maastrichtian]` and `dispute` on [Baruungoyot](../../../stratigraphy/b/baruungoyot.yml).

**Evidence.**
- gradziński1974a, p. 111, Abstract: "The Barun Goyot Formation (previously termed Lower Nemegt Beds) is composed of clastic continental sediments of red-beds type; it is probably of Campanian age. The thickness of the formation exceeds 110 m. It is overlain by the Nemegt Formation (previously termed Upper Nemegt Beds), probably of Maastrichtian age; the passage between the two formations is gradual."
- kielan-jaworowska1974a, p. 234: "In the younger Asiatic Barun Goyot Formation, which is of Campanian age, the multituberculate fauna so far described consists of three genera"; "The post-Campanian multituberculate records are very scanty in Asia".
- kielan-jaworowska1974a, p. 233: the gap in the record depends "on whether the Djadokhta Formation of Asia is of Coniacian or Santonian age. In this discussion let us assume that the Djadokhta Formation is of Coniacian age".
- karczewska1983a, pp. 144–145: the charophytes indicate "that the Nemegt Formation is not younger than Lower Campanian. Accordingly, underlying Barun Goyot Formation and Djadokhta Formations must be older than the Lower Campanian"; "The Barun Goyot Formation (and its stratigraphic equivalent red beds of Khermeen Tsav) is probably of ?Late Santonian age. This agrees in general with the opinion of Kielan-Jaworowska (1974) based on the development of the multituberculates."
- shuvalov2000a, p. 268: the section heading "Baruungoyot Svita (Santonian–Campanian)"; "The Baruungoyot Svita unites the red sediments of the Djadokhta Formation" with the red and multicolored beds of many other Gobi localities.
- shuvalov2000a, pp. 269–270: "most researchers give a Senonian, most likely Campanian, age. Kielan-Jaworowska (1968, 1970) lowered the age of the Djadokhta to Coniacian–Santonian on the basis of mammal remains"; "molluscs, ostracods, and turtles do not allow an age greater than Santonian. In addition, K–Ar dates from basalts in the middle and upper parts of the svita (in the gorge Borzongiin Gobi and others) are 78–80 Myr (Campanian). Hence, we date the Baruungoyot Svita as Santonian–Campanian".
- shuvalov2000a, p. 261, Table 14.2: under the Baruungoyot Svita, "South Gobi, Chuluut Uul mountain, upper part of svita, basalt", "75 ± 7", and "North Mongolia, Tsagaan Nuur depression, basaltic sheet on the Lower Cretaceous", "80".
- vanitterbeeck2005b, p. 714: "Karczewska and Ziembinska-Tworzydlo (1983) proposed a Santonian age for the Barun Goyot Formation; however, radiometric calibrations (Shuvalov, 2000) have provided a Campanian age for this formation", and on that basis they "regard the age estimation for the Nemegt Formation as proposed by Karczewska and Ziembinska-Tworzydlo (1983) as incorrect".
- vanitterbeeck2005b, p. 701: "the Djadokhta and Barun Goyot formations are considered the equivalents of the Judithian Stage of North America, in which case they are respectively mid- and Late Campanian in age. This age estimation is supported by the radiometric ages of the volcanic layers within the Barun Goyot Formation".
- khand2000a, pp. 68–69: "The Santonian–Campanian deposits of the Baruungoyot Formation in the Altan uul-4 yield the prints of leaves of *Ginkgo, Platanus, Trochodendroides, Nelumbo* and *Quereuxia*."
- khand2000a, Table 2: the Baruungoyot is set against the Campanian row alone.
- khand2000a, p. 69: the mollusc assemblages of Martinson (1982) include "Baruungoyotian (Campanian) and Nemegtian (Maastrichtian)".
- khand2000a, p. 63: "The Javkhlant Formation (Baruungoyot Formation by Shuvalov and Stankevich [21])" of the eastern Gobi; "The fossil horizon of the Javkhlant Formation is correlative with the Santonian-Campanian Stage".
- khand2000a, p. 76: "The position of some stratigraphic divisions, for example, Bayanshiree, Baruungoyot and others, more lower divisions remain to be quite unclear."
- fanti2012a, p. 1, Abstract: the nest "was collected from the upper beds of the Baruungoyot Formation (Campanian-Maastrichtian)".
- fanti2012a, p. 2, Geology and Stratigraphy: the cuts at Nemegt "provide some of the best exposures of the upper Campanian - lower Maastrichtian Baruungoyot and Nemegt formations".
- fanti2012a, p. 13, Discussion: *Nemegtomaia* "is found in the typical upper Baruungoyot Formation facies, in the Baruungoyot-Nemegt interfingering interval, and also in the Nemegt Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Van Itterbeeck and colleagues' (2005) rejection of the Santonian.* They credit the Campanian to radiometric calibrations by Shuvalov (2000), but Shuvalov dates the svita Santonian–Campanian in his own voice, after weighing those same K–Ar dates against molluscs, ostracods and turtles. The source does not support the rejection, so it does not close the question.
- *Kielan-Jaworowska (1974) as support for a Santonian.* Karczewska and Ziembinska-Tworzydlo (1983) write that their "?Late Santonian" agrees with Kielan-Jaworowska (1974), and the work they list under that year is this paper. It gives the Barun Goyot Formation as "of Campanian age"; its only Santonian is an alternative for the Djadokhta, which it sets aside for the Coniacian. Kielan-Jaworowska (1974) counts for the Campanian only.

**Open.** Whether Shuvalov's Santonian dates the formation in the narrower sense of Gradziński and Jerzykiewicz (1974): his svita unites the Djadokhta Formation with red beds across the Gobi, and his evidence against an age greater than Santonian is not tied to a locality. Shuvalov's text gives the K–Ar dates as 78–80 Myr from the middle and upper svita, while his Table 14.2 lists 75 ± 7 from the upper svita at Chuluut Uul and 80 from a basaltic sheet in North Mongolia. A dated section within the formation in its type area would settle the older limit.

## What stages do the other Baruungoyot taxa take?

*2026-10-13*

**Conclusion.** The same rule as the entry below, applied to the rest. The two Khulsan taxa take the Campanian–Maastrichtian Napoli and colleagues (2021) give the Barun Goyot strata at Khulsan; *Natovenator*, with no statement about its horizon at Hermiin Tsav, takes the formation's range; *Ondogurvel* keeps the Campanian of its describing paper's horizon line. Alifanov and Barsbold's "?Lower Santonian" belongs to the *Ceratonykus* holotype's horizon and does not date the whole Hermiin Tsav locality. Governs `stage` on *Shri devi* in [*Shri*](../../../genera/S/Shri.yml) and on [*Parvicursor remotus*](../../../genera/P/Parvicursor.yml) (Campanian–Maastrichtian), [*Natovenator polydontus*](../../../genera/N/Natovenator.yml) (Santonian–Maastrichtian) and [*Ondogurvel alifanovi*](../../../genera/O/Ondogurvel.yml) (Campanian).

**Evidence.**

## What stages do the Baruungoyot taxa take?

*2026-10-13*

**Conclusion.** Each takes the statements about its own horizon, or the formation's Santonian–Maastrichtian where there are none. The earlier adjudication kept all four at the Campanian by counting the papers that give the formation that age, which counts repetition rather than evidence. Governs `stage` on [*Ceratonykus oculatus*](../../../genera/C/Ceratonykus.yml) (Santonian), [*Kuru kulla*](../../../genera/K/Kuru.yml) (Campanian–Maastrichtian), and [*Conchoraptor gracilis*](../../../genera/C/Conchoraptor.yml) and [*Breviceratops kozlowskii*](../../../genera/B/Breviceratops.yml) (Santonian–Maastrichtian).

**Evidence.**
- alifanov2009a, p. 97: "Upper Cretaceous, ?Lower Santonian, Baruungoyot Formation", for the *Ceratonykus* holotype.
- napoli2021a, p. 3: "All that can be said with certainty is that the Barun Goyot strata at Khulsan are Campanian to Maastrichtian in age."
- czepiński2019a: "the Campanian Baruungoyot Formation (Jerzykiewicz 2000)" for the *Conchoraptor* and *Breviceratops* localities, a relayed age for the formation.

**Ruled out.**
- *Campanian for all four, by count.* A tally of papers repeating an age is not a horizon statement.

**Open.** Horizon statements for *Conchoraptor* and *Breviceratops*.

## Why is the formation spelled Baruungoyot rather than Barun Goyot?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** Baruungoyot is Benton's (2000) form: *baruun* in his transliteration (older *barun*), written as one word because it names a formation. Most papers still print "Barun Goyot", the form under which the formation was formally defined, so the choice rests on adopting Benton's system, not on usage. Governs `name` and `variants` on [Baruungoyot](../../../stratigraphy/b/baruungoyot.yml) and `location.formation` on the records that name it.

**Evidence.**
- benton2000c, p. xxiii: "Baruungoyot Formation/ Svita"; "Baruun (barun), right"; "Barun (= Baruun)"; and "When these are converted into names of svitas or formations, the names are rendered as a single word".
- czepiński2019a, p. 4: "Transcriptions of the Mongolian locality names follow Benton (2000)", with the Hermiin Tsav specimens in the "Baruungoyot Formation" (Table 1).
- gradziński1974a, pp. 115–116: Martinson and colleagues "introduced the term "Barungoyotskaya svita" for the Lower Nemegt Beds"; "The present authors adopt the names: Barun Goyot Formation and Nemegt Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *A separate Khermeen Tsav formation.* Maryańska and Osmólska use "the informal lithostratigraphical unit "Khermeen Tsav formation" … (see Kielan-Jaworowska 1975a)" "for the purposes of simplicity", and call the Khermeen Tsav red beds and the Barun Goyot Formation "most probably contemporaneous" (maryańska1975a, p. 136; p. 148: "of the same age as Barun Goyot Formation"). Gradziński and colleagues treat the red beds as "biostratigraphic equivalents of the Barun Goyot Formation" (gradziński1977a, p. 302); Jerzykiewicz and Russell include Khermeen Tsav in the formation's outcrop (jerzykiewicz1991a, p. 353). The informal unit has no registry entry.

**Open.** Penkalski cites Carpenter and colleagues (2011) "for an explanation of why Barun Goyot is preferred over Baruungoyot" (penkalski2026a, p. 1). That paper has not been read.

## Do *Ondogurvel* and *Parvicursor* take the Maastrichtian?

*2026-08-05*

**Conclusion.** No. Both stay Campanian, which is the age each describing paper gives for its type locality. Governs `stage: [Campanian]` on [*Ondogurvel alifanovi*](../../../genera/O/Ondogurvel.yml) and [*Parvicursor remotus*](../../../genera/P/Parvicursor.yml).

**Evidence.**
- averianov2022a, p. 3: "Type locality and horizon. Nemegt, Mongolia; Barungoyot Formation, Upper Cretaceous (Campanian)."
- averianov2021d, p. 1097, Abstract: *Parvicursor remotus* is "based on a fragmentary skeleton from the Campanian Barungoyot Formation at Khulsan".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian.* Jones (2026) gives both species as "Barun Goyot Fm., Mongolia, Late Cretaceous, Maastrichtian" (jones2026c, Alvarezsauroidea systematics table). That is a compilation row, and it cites no primary source for the age. Fowler and colleagues (2020) list *Parvicursor* and *Ceratonykus* "from the Barun Goyot Formation (lower Maastrichtian)" (fowler2020b, Introduction), in a survey of the clade that gives no source for that age and dates the formation as a whole, not either horizon.

**Open.** Both Campanian statements date the formation as a whole ("the Campanian Barungoyot Formation"), not a level within it, and the unit's own stages run from Santonian to Maastrichtian. A dated section at Nemegt or Khulsan would settle whether either horizon reaches past the Campanian.

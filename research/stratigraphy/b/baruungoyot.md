# Baruungoyot

## Why is the formation spelled Baruungoyot rather than Barun Goyot?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** Baruungoyot is Benton's (2000) form: *baruun* in his transliteration (older *barun*), written as one word because it names a formation. Most papers still print "Barun Goyot", the form under which the formation was formally defined, so the choice rests on adopting Benton's system, not on usage. Governs `name` and `variants` on [Baruungoyot](../../../stratigraphy/b/baruungoyot.yml) and `location.formation` on the records that name it.

**Evidence.**
- benton2000c, p. xxiii: "Baruungoyot Formation/ Svita"; "Baruun (barun), right"; "Barun (= Baruun)"; and "When these are converted into names of svitas or formations, the names are rendered as a single word".
- czepiński2019a, p. 4: "Transcriptions of the Mongolian locality names follow Benton (2000)", with the Hermiin Tsav specimens in the "Baruungoyot Formation" (Table 1).
- gradziński1974a, pp. 115–116: Martinson and colleagues "introduced the term "Barungoyotskaya svita" for the Lower Nemegt Beds"; "The present authors adopt the names: Barun Goyot Formation and Nemegt Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *A separate Khermeen Tsav formation.* Maryańska and Osmólska use "the informal lithostratigraphical unit "Khermeen Tsav formation" … (see Kielan-Jaworowska 1975a)" "for the purposes of simplicity", and call the Khermeen Tsav red beds and the Barun Goyot Formation "most probably contemporaneous" (maryańska1975a, p. 136; p. 148: "of the same age as Barun Goyot Formation"). Gradziński and colleagues treat the red beds as "biostratigraphic equivalents of the Barun Goyot Formation" (gradziński1977a, p. 302); Jerzykiewicz and Russell include Khermeen Tsav in the formation's outcrop (jerzykiewicz1991a, p. 353). The informal unit has no registry entry.

**Open.** Penkalski cites Carpenter and colleagues (2011) "for an explanation of why Barun Goyot is preferred over Baruungoyot" (penkalski2026a, p. 1). That paper has not been read.

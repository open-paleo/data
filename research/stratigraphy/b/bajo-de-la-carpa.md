# Bajo de la Carpa

## Does the Bajo de la Carpa Formation reach the Campanian?

*2026-10-06*

**Conclusion.** No. The formation is Santonian. Governs `stages: [Santonian]` on Bajo de la Carpa. Supersedes the 2026-10-01 entry's `stages: [Santonian, Campanian]` and its **Open** line: Filippi (2015), the source that line said would settle the Campanian top, gives the formation as Santonian only.

**Evidence.**
- filippi2015a, p. 22: the *Rinconsaurus* site lies at "la base de la Formación Bajo de la Carpa (Santoniano inferior-medio; Garrido com. pers.)" ("the base of the Bajo de la Carpa Formation (lower–middle Santonian; Garrido pers. comm.)", translated).
- filippi2015a, p. 25: *Overosaurus*, which Coria and colleagues (2013) placed in the Anacleto, is reassigned to "la Formación Bajo de la Carpa (Santoniano; Garrido, com. pers.)" ("the Bajo de la Carpa Formation (Santonian; Garrido, pers. comm.)", translated).
- filippi2015a, p. 27, Figure 2, "Cronoestratigrafía y litoestratigrafía según Garrido (2010)" ("chronostratigraphy and lithostratigraphy after Garrido (2010)", translated): the Bajo de la Carpa sits wholly between the 85.8 and 83.5 Ma lines, in the Santonian, with the Anacleto above it in the Campanian. The chart is Garrido's (2010) scheme, already counted.

**Ruled out.**
- *Pérez-Moreno and colleagues' "middle Santonian – Campanian".* They attribute it to Filippi (2015) (pérez-moreno2023a, p. 5), who does not say it; his reading is the Santonian above, which the same paper gives on p. 2.
- *Filippi's Coloradoense association, "formaciones Bajo de la Carpa-Anacleto, Santoniano-Campaniano temprano"* ("Bajo de la Carpa–Anacleto formations, Santonian–early Campanian", translated; filippi2015a, p. 26). A range for the two formations together, after Leanza and colleagues (2004); its early Campanian is the Anacleto's. It does not date the Bajo de la Carpa by itself.
- *Hugo and Leanza's "Santonian-lower Campanian Rio Colorado Subgroup".* The parent subgroup's range, comprising this formation and the Anacleto (hugo2001a); a parent's range is not a dating of the child.
- *Coria and colleagues' "the Campanian Bajo de la Carpa and Anacleto formations"* (coria2013a, p. 372). A passing label for both formations, with no source given, in a paper that placed its own taxon in the Anacleto.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What is the age of the Bajo de la Carpa Formation?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Santonian, possibly reaching the early Campanian. Governs `stages: [Santonian, Campanian]` on Bajo de la Carpa. A species whose type horizon is in this formation takes the stage its own sources give, not the formation's full range.

**Evidence.**
- garrido2010a, p. 152: "entre los 86 y 83 Ma, situándola dentro del Santoniano … una edad santoniana también es atribuida por Bonaparte (1991) … también es adoptado en este trabajo" ("between 86 and 83 Ma, placing it within the Santonian … a Santonian age is also attributed by Bonaparte (1991) … also adopted in this work", translated). The 86–83 Ma figures are Legarreta and Gulisano's (1989).
- porfiri2018a, p. 303: the age "is widely regarded as Santonian (Bonaparte, 1991; Hugo and Leanza, 2001; Leanza et al., 2004; Rodríguez et al., 2007; Garrido, 2010)".
- pérez-moreno2023a, p. 5, Systematic paleontology: "base of the Bajo de la Carpa Formation (middle Santonian – Campanian; Filippi 2015)". On p. 2, Material and methods, the same paper gives "(Santonian; Filippi, 2015)".
- coria2013a, p. 372, Discussion: "the Campanian Bajo de la Carpa and Anacleto formations", with no source given.

**Ruled out.**
- *Coniacian.* The Coniacian comes from Cruz and colleagues' (1989) range for the whole Río Colorado Subgroup, "para la totalidad del Subgrupo Río Colorado … 88,5 a 80 Ma, correspondiendo al intervalo Coniaciano - Campaniano Temprano" ("for the entire Río Colorado Subgroup … 88.5 to 80 Ma, corresponding to the Coniacian – Early Campanian interval", translated; garrido2010a, pp. 151–152). That range covers the Anacleto Formation as well and does not date the Bajo de la Carpa by itself.
- *Recalibrating Legarreta and Gulisano's estimates.* Their 86–83 Ma figures are estimates tied to the timescale of 1989, not dates, and are not converted to a modern timescale stage by stage.

**Open.** The Campanian end rests on thin support: Filippi (2015) as reported by Pérez-Moreno and colleagues (2023), who give it two different ways in the same paper, and an unsourced phrase in Coria and colleagues (2013). Filippi (2015) itself would settle whether it argues for a Campanian top.

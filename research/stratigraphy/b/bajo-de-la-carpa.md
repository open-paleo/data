# Bajo de la Carpa

## Is the Balsa Formation a former name for the Bajo de la Carpa?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** Yes; "Balsa" is added to the variants. Hugo and Leanza (2001) assimilate Weber's (1968) Balsa Formation, south of Paso Córdova, to the Bajo de la Carpa on its lithology and position, and conclude that the name should be abandoned. Governs `variants` on [Bajo de la Carpa](../../../stratigraphy/b/bajo-de-la-carpa.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hugo2001a, p. 20: "debe ser asimilada, por sus características litológicas y su posición en la secuencia sedimentaria, a la Formación Bajo de la Carpa. De ello se desprende que el término Balsense (= Formación Balsa), a estar con los conocimientos actuales, debería ser abandonado" ["must be assimilated, by its lithological characteristics and its position in the sedimentary sequence, to the Bajo de la Carpa Formation. It follows that the term Balsense (= Balsa Formation), in the light of current knowledge, should be abandoned", translated]

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which of the cited ages are for the Bajo de la Carpa itself, and which for its subgroup?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Only the Santonian is given for the formation by itself. Garrido (2010) adopts it on two grounds, Legarreta and Gulisano's (1989) 86 to 83 Ma and Bonaparte's (1991) vertebrate fauna, and notes that no radiometric date exists for the unit; Hugo and Leanza (2001) give the formation the same Santonian after Bonaparte. Every other span in these works belongs to the Río Colorado Subgroup or Formation, which includes the Anacleto, or to the Neuquén Group. Cruz and colleagues (1989) rank the unit as a member of a Río Colorado Formation and give no age for it apart from the Anacleto. Governs `stages: [Santonian]` on [Bajo de la Carpa](../../../stratigraphy/b/bajo-de-la-carpa.yml).

**Evidence.**
- garrido2010a, p. 151: "No absolute datings have so far been carried out for this unit" (translated).
- garrido2010a, pp. 151–152: Cruz and colleagues (1989) give "the entire Río Colorado Subgroup (Bajo de la Carpa and Anacleto Fms.) an age of 88.5 to 80 Ma, corresponding to the Coniacian - Early Campanian interval (International Stratigraphic Chart, 2009)" (translated); Legarreta and Gulisano (1989) give the Bajo de la Carpa deposits "an age between 86 and 83 Ma, placing it within the Santonian (International Stratigraphic Chart, 2009)" (translated); "a Santonian age is also attributed by Bonaparte (1991) on the basis of its paleontological content, a criterion later followed by Hugo & Leanza (2001) and which is also adopted in this work" (translated).
- hugo2001a, p. 1, Resumen, and p. 2, Abstract: the group's three subgroups are "the Cenomanian-lower Turonian Limay Subgroup comprising the Candeleros, Huincul and Cerro Lisandro Formations; the upper Turonian-Coniacian Río Neuquén Subgroup including the Portezuelo and Plottier Formations; and the Santonian-lower Campanian Río Colorado Subgroup comprising the Bajo de la Carpa and Anacleto Formations" (p. 2), the same assignment in the Spanish of p. 1; the group's age "ranges from early Cenomanian (97 ± 3 Ma) to early Campanian (74 ± 3 Ma)" (p. 2).
- hugo2001a, p. 18: "The units that constitute the Río Colorado Subgroup are considered to have been deposited during the Santonian-Lower Campanian" (translated).
- hugo2001a, p. 20: "Bonaparte assigned the Bajo de la Carpa Formation a Santonian age based on the study of a fauna of vertebrates present in the badlands immediately north of the city of Neuquén" (translated).
- cruz1989a, p. 708: Gulisano and Legarreta set the limits of the Neuquén Group at "the Intracenomanian Discontinuity and the 80 m.a. Discontinuity, assigning it consequently a middle Cenomanian - lower Campanian age, which is the one used in the present work" (translated); "The nine sections or sequences into which the Neuquén Group has been divided could be grouped into three higher-order units", which "could be assimilated, with reservations, to the formations proposed by Cazau and Uliana" (translated).
- cruz1989a, pp. 708, 710: "The Portezuelo (sandstones) and Plottier (pelites) members of the Río Neuquén Formation"; "the members Bajo de la Carpa (sandstones) and Anacleto (pelites), of the Río Colorado Formation", for which "the same relationship is postulated, integrating Sequences VI, VII, VIII and IX" (translated).
- cruz1989a, p. 709, Fig. 9, "Cuadro estratigráfico comparativo para Grupo Neuquén": the Río Colorado Formation spans 88.5 to 80 Ma, with the Bajo de la Carpa and Anacleto members drawn as interfingering facies across Sequences VI to IX and no boundary age between them.

**Ruled out.**
- *A Coniacian or Campanian part from Hugo and Leanza (2001) or Cruz and colleagues (1989).* Their spans, Santonian to lower Campanian and 88.5 to 80 Ma, are the Río Colorado Subgroup's or Formation's, covering the Anacleto as well; each work gives the Bajo de la Carpa no other age of its own. Hugo and Leanza's age for the formation itself is Santonian.
- *Cruz and colleagues' (1989) member rank as a rival rank.* It is the older scheme of Cazau and Uliana (1973), in which the Río Colorado was a formation with members; Hugo and Leanza (2001) and Garrido (2010) make it a subgroup and its members formations, as the [Neuquén Group subgroups](../../topics/neuquén-group-subgroups.md) note records for the Candeleros.
- *Coniacian or Campanian from placing Legarreta and Gulisano's 86 to 83 Ma on the current chart.* On the ICS chart v2024/12, whose Campanian begins at 83.6 Ma, 83 Ma lies just inside the Campanian, and 86 Ma lies in the latest Coniacian below the Santonian base of 85.7 Ma. Garrido (2010) places the range on the 2009 chart as Santonian, and the 2026-10-01 entry rules out converting these figures stage by stage.

**Open.** Nothing beyond the 2026-10-06 entry.

## Does the Bajo de la Carpa Formation reach the Campanian?

*2026-10-06*

**Conclusion.** No. The formation is Santonian. Governs `stages: [Santonian]` on Bajo de la Carpa. Supersedes the 2026-10-01 entry's `stages: [Santonian, Campanian]` and its **Open** line: Filippi (2015), the source that line said would settle the Campanian top, gives the formation as Santonian only.

**Evidence.**
- filippi2015a, p. 22: the *Rinconsaurus* site lies at "la base de la Formación Bajo de la Carpa (Santoniano inferior-medio; Garrido com. pers.)" ("the base of the Bajo de la Carpa Formation (lower–middle Santonian; Garrido pers. comm.)", translated).
- filippi2015a, p. 25: *Overosaurus*, which Coria and colleagues (2013) placed in the Anacleto, is reassigned to "la Formación Bajo de la Carpa (Santoniano; Garrido, com. pers.)" ("the Bajo de la Carpa Formation (Santonian; Garrido, pers. comm.)", translated).
- filippi2015a, p. 27, Figure 2, "Cronoestratigrafía y litoestratigrafía según Garrido (2010)" ("chronostratigraphy and lithostratigraphy after Garrido (2010)", translated): the Bajo de la Carpa sits wholly between the 85.8 and 83.5 Ma lines, in the Santonian, with the Anacleto above it in the Campanian. The chart is Garrido's (2010) scheme, already counted.

**Ruled out.**
- *Pérez-Moreno and colleagues' "middle Santonian – Campanian".* They attribute it to Filippi (2015) (pérez-moreno2023a, p. 5), who does not say it; his reading is the Santonian above, which the same paper gives on p. 2.
- *Filippi's Coloradoense association, "formaciones Bajo de la Carpa-Anacleto, Santoniano-Campaniano temprano"* ("Bajo de la Carpa–Anacleto formations, Santonian–early Campanian", translated; filippi2015a, p. 26). A range for the two formations together, after Leanza and colleagues (2004); its early Campanian is the Anacleto's. It does not date the Bajo de la Carpa by itself.
- *Hugo and Leanza's "Santonian-lower Campanian Rio Colorado Subgroup".* The parent subgroup's range, comprising this formation and the Anacleto (hugo2001a); a parent's range is not a dating of the child.
- *Coria and colleagues' "the Campanian Bajo de la Carpa and Anacleto formations"* (coria2013a, p. 372). A passing label for both formations, with no source given, in a paper that placed its own taxon in the Anacleto.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What is the age of the Bajo de la Carpa Formation?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Santonian, possibly reaching the early Campanian. Governs `stages: [Santonian, Campanian]` on Bajo de la Carpa. A species whose type horizon is in this formation takes the stage its own sources give, not the formation's full range.

**Evidence.**
- garrido2010a, p. 152: "entre los 86 y 83 Ma, situándola dentro del Santoniano … una edad santoniana también es atribuida por Bonaparte (1991) … también es adoptado en este trabajo" ("between 86 and 83 Ma, placing it within the Santonian … a Santonian age is also attributed by Bonaparte (1991) … also adopted in this work", translated). The 86–83 Ma figures are Legarreta and Gulisano's (1989).
- porfiri2018a, p. 303: the age "is widely regarded as Santonian (Bonaparte, 1991; Hugo and Leanza, 2001; Leanza et al., 2004; Rodríguez et al., 2007; Garrido, 2010)".
- pérez-moreno2023a, p. 5, Systematic paleontology: "base of the Bajo de la Carpa Formation (middle Santonian – Campanian; Filippi 2015)". On p. 2, Material and methods, the same paper gives "(Santonian; Filippi, 2015)".
- coria2013a, p. 372, Discussion: "the Campanian Bajo de la Carpa and Anacleto formations", with no source given.

**Ruled out.**
- *Coniacian.* The Coniacian comes from Cruz and colleagues' (1989) range for the whole Río Colorado Subgroup, "para la totalidad del Subgrupo Río Colorado … 88,5 a 80 Ma, correspondiendo al intervalo Coniaciano - Campaniano Temprano" ("for the entire Río Colorado Subgroup … 88.5 to 80 Ma, corresponding to the Coniacian – Early Campanian interval", translated; garrido2010a, pp. 151–152). That range covers the Anacleto Formation as well and does not date the Bajo de la Carpa by itself.
- *Recalibrating Legarreta and Gulisano's estimates.* Their 86–83 Ma figures are estimates tied to the timescale of 1989, not dates, and are not converted to a modern timescale stage by stage.

**Open.** The Campanian end rests on thin support: Filippi (2015) as reported by Pérez-Moreno and colleagues (2023), who give it two different ways in the same paper, and an unsourced phrase in Coria and colleagues (2013). Filippi (2015) itself would settle whether it argues for a Campanian top.

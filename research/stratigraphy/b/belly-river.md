# Belly River

## Does the Belly River keep the Santonian?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No; the Santonian is dropped. Supersedes the 2026-11-25 entry on the stages. Its only sources are the CSPG lexicon, whose header field gives "Santonian - Campanian (85.8 - 70.6 ma)" and names no source, for a foothills unit that takes in Milk River equivalents, and Rozhdestvensky (1968), whose "has been dated to the Campanian and Upper Santonian" names none either; a reference work does not carry a value alone, and an unsourced relay adds nothing. Eberth (2005) and Brown and colleagues (2013) give the group wholly in the Campanian. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` and the note on cspg2010a on [Belly River](../../../stratigraphy/b/belly-river.yml); its genera, all Campanian, are unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- cspg2010a, p. ?: "Age Interval: Santonian - Campanian (85.8 - 70.6 ma)".
- rozhdestvensky1968a, p. ?: remains "were found in the Belly River Formation which has been dated to the Campanian and Upper Santonian".
- eberth2005a, p. 65: "Thus, in the Park the entire Belly River Group (outcrop and subsurface) ranges in age from 80.0 to 74.2 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *Santonian from the lexicon or Rozhdestvensky (1968).* Neither names a source.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What is the CSPG lexicon's 85.8 Ma, and does the Santonian stand?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** The lexicon's numbers are stage limits, and the Santonian stands. The lexicon gives the Belly River Formation "Santonian - Campanian (85.8 - 70.6 ma)"; 85.8 and 70.6 Ma are GTS2004's bases of the Santonian and Maastrichtian, so they restate the stages and date nothing. The lexicon's Belly River is the foothills unit running from the Wapiabi to the Bearpaw and taking in Milk River equivalents, and Rozhdestvensky (1968) dates the formation "to the Campanian and Upper Santonian"; both are published readings. Eberth (2005) and Brown and colleagues (2013) date the group in Dinosaur Provincial Park and the Red Deer valley to the Campanian only, which adds no stage and removes none. Governs `stages: [Santonian, Campanian]` and the note on cspg2010a on [Belly River](../../../stratigraphy/b/belly-river.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- cspg2010a, p. ?: "Age Interval: Santonian - Campanian (85.8 - 70.6 ma)"; "The name Belly River Formation has been applied in the southern Alberta Foothills to the undivided sequence from the Wapiabi Formation to the Bearpaw Formation, including Judith River, Pakowki and Milk River equivalents."
- ogg2004a, Table 19.4, p. 382: "Base of Santonian", 85.8; "Base of Maastrichtian", 70.6.
- rozhdestvensky1968a, p. ?: "the Belly River Formation which has been dated to the Campanian and Upper Santonian".

**Ruled out.**
- *85.8 Ma as a date for the base of the unit.* It is the GTS2004 base of the Santonian.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the rank, contents and age of the Belly River?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Dawson (1883) introduced the name as the Belly River "series" and gave it no stage: his correlation table, in North American series terms (Laramie with the Judith River series, Fox Hills, Pierre, Niobrara, and the local St. Mary River and Willow Creek series), puts the Belly River series below the Pierre, against a Niobrara marked with his own question mark. His series also took in the Pakowki marine shales below and beds now placed in the Milk River Group, so its contents are wider than the group's. Eberth (2005) treats the unit as a group of the Foremost, Oldman and Dinosaur Park formations, about 280 m thick at Dinosaur Provincial Park, and dates the whole group there at 80.0 to 74.2 Ma; that span is a measurement for the Park section, where the group is thicker and spans more time than to the east. Governs `rank: group` and bears on `stages` on [Belly River](../../../stratigraphy/b/belly-river.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dawson1883a, pamphlet p. 3: "Laramie, (including Judith River series.)"; "St. Mary River series"; "Willow Creek beds"; "Fox Hills".
- dawson1883a, pamphlet p. 4: "PIERRE. { Blackish and lead-coloured shales"; "NIOBRARA ? { Belly River series. Sandstones, shales and sandy clays. Upper part generally greyish; lower yellowish, and often banded by rapidly alternating beds. Fresh and brackish water molluscs."
- dawson1883a, pamphlet p. 4: "no reason has been found to suppose that any beds newer than the Laramie (including under this general name the Judith River and Fort Union series) have been found in this district".
- eberth2005a, p. 54: "first referred to as the Belly River "series" by Dawson (1883), who unintentionally included within it the underlying Pakowki marine shales, as well as sediments that are today included in the Milk River Group (Eagle Formation)".
- eberth2005a, p. 59: "the complete Belly River Group (outcrop and subsurface) is approximately 280 m thick and consists of the Foremost (170 m), Oldman (40 m), and Dinosaur Park (70 m) formations".
- eberth2005a, p. 65: "in the Park the entire Belly River Group (outcrop and subsurface) ranges in age from 80.0 to 74.2 Ma".
- eberth2005a, p. 67: "Eastward into Saskatchewan, the Belly River Group thins and becomes less time inclusive".

**Ruled out.**
- *Series as the unit's rank.* "Series" is Dawson's (1883) term for the beds before they were divided into formations; Eberth (2005) and Brown and colleagues (2013) treat the unit as a group.
- *A Niobrara age from Dawson (1883).* His table sets the Belly River series against "NIOBRARA ?", with his own question mark, and he gives no stage.
- *Dawson's (1883) series as the group's contents.* It took in the Pakowki marine shales and beds now in the Milk River Group (Eagle Formation), which the group excludes.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Bull Canyon

## Does the name Bull Canyon have priority over Cooper Canyon?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** For the interval the two names share, yes, on the dates Cather and colleagues (2013) give. Chatterjee (1986) named a Cooper Member in Texas, but the name was preoccupied (Lucas and colleagues, 1994), and Lehman and colleagues (1992) revised it to Cooper Canyon Formation only after Lucas and Hunt (1989) had applied Bull Canyon to the New Mexico interval. Priority does not merge the units: the Bull Canyon matches only the upper part of the Cooper (Martz, 2008), so the Texas name stays in use for the whole Texas formation, and the two units are kept apart as set out in [Bull Canyon and Cooper Canyon](../../topics/bull-canyon-and-cooper-canyon.md). Governs `name: Bull Canyon` on [Bull Canyon](../../../stratigraphy/b/bull-canyon.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cather2013a, p. 108: "A point of contention with this nomenclatural scheme is the term Bull Canyon Formation, first used by Lucas and Hunt (1989). The Bull Canyon interval is equivalent to the upper part (Martz, 2008) of the earlier-named Cooper Member of Chatterjee (1986) in Texas."
- cather2013a, p. 108: "As noted by Lucas et al. (1994), however, the term Cooper is preoccupied. Although rectified by Lehman et al. (1992) by revision of the name to Cooper Canyon Formation, Lucas and Hunt (1989) had, in the meantime, applied the term Bull Canyon to the interval. Thus, strictly speaking, the term Bull Canyon should have priority."
- cather2013a, p. 108: in east-central New Mexico the Dockum Group's five formations are, ascending, "the Santa Rosa Sandstone, the Tecovas Formation, the Trujillo Sandstone, the Bull Canyon Formation, and the Redonda Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Cooper Canyon replacing Bull Canyon in New Mexico on priority.* Lehman (1994) "argued that the Garita Creek and Bull Canyon Formations should be replaced by the Tecovas and Cooper Canyon Formations, respectively, on the basis of priority" (cather2013a, p. 107). The Cooper Member's name was preoccupied, and Cooper Canyon dates from 1992, after Bull Canyon (1989).
- *Bull Canyon replacing Cooper Canyon in Texas.* Cather and colleagues' priority covers only the interval the names share, and the Bull Canyon is equivalent to only the upper part of the Cooper Canyon.

**Open.** Nothing.

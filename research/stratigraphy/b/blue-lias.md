# Blue Lias

## Does the Blue Lias reach the Rhaetian? (revisited)

*2026-10-14*

**Conclusion.** Yes: Rhaetian, Hettangian and Sinemurian. Supersedes the 2026-09-20 entry below, which found no source giving the formation a Rhaetian in its own voice; three do. Cox and colleagues (1999), defining the formation, give it "Uppermost Triassic (Rhaetian) to Lower Jurassic, Sinemurian"; Simms and colleagues (2004) say it "encompasses the highest part of the Triassic Rhaetian Stage"; and Hillebrandt and colleagues (2013), defining the base of the Jurassic, expect that boundary "in the lowest few meters of the Blue Lias Formation", a boundary inside the unit, which gives it the stages on both sides. Simms and colleagues place the base of the Jurassic at the first *Psiloceras*, before the boundary was ratified; Hillebrandt and colleagues' statement is on the ratified definition. Governs `period: [Late Triassic, Early Jurassic]` and `stages: [Rhaetian, Hettangian, Sinemurian]` on [Blue Lias](../../../stratigraphy/b/blue-lias.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cox1999a, Blue Lias Formation, Chronostratigraphy: "Uppermost Triassic (Rhaetian) to Lower Jurassic, Sinemurian; upper boundary markedly diachronous".
- simms2004c, p. 55: the formation "encompasses the highest part of the Triassic Rhaetian Stage, the Hettangian Stage and the lowest part of the Sinemurian Stage"; p. 61: "the lowest, late Triassic part of the Blue Lias Formation".
- hillebrandt2013a, p. 189: "The boundary should be expected to occur in the lowest few meters of the Blue Lias Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Hettangian–Sinemurian alone.* The defining framework, the stratigraphic review and the boundary's definition all put the lowest beds in the Rhaetian.

**Open.** Where the boundary falls at a given section, which bears on the Bull Cliff Member and the *Dracoraptor* level at Lavernock.

## Does the Blue Lias reach the Rhaetian?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** No source read gives the formation a Rhaetian in its own voice. It is Hettangian–Sinemurian. Governs `period: [Early Jurassic]` and `stages: [Hettangian, Sinemurian]` on [Blue Lias](../../../stratigraphy/b/blue-lias.yml).

**Evidence.**
- baron2024a, p. 6: "Blue Lias Formation, Lower Lias Group, Charmouth, UK", "Hettangian-Sinemurian, Early Jurassic".
- martill2016a, p. 9, Latest Triassic or earliest Jurassic?: the theropod from the lower Bull Cliff Member "should be regarded as being of Early Hettangian age", and "The minor-nonsequence beneath the basal Paper Shale horizon at the base of the Blue Lias Formation would seem the most logical place to put the Triassic/Jurassic Boundary at Lavernock Point."

**Ruled out.**
- *Rhaetian, from the lithological base.* Martill and colleagues note that the change at the formation's base "historically was used by some workers as a lithological definition of the base of the Jurassic" (martill2016a, p. 8). That puts the boundary at the base of the formation, not inside it.
- *Rhaetian, from the Pre-Planorbis Beds.* The same paper reports that the beds below the first *Psiloceras planorbis* "have traditionally been regarded as Late Rhaetian age and are informally called the Pre-Planorbis Beds" (martill2016a, p. 7), citing Simms and colleagues (2004) and others. That is a relayed reading, and the paper relaying it argues against it for the bed it dates: the dinosaur lies above the carbon-isotope excursion, with a Jurassic shelly fauna and Jurassic-type pollen nearby (p. 9).

**Open.** Whether the Triassic–Jurassic boundary lies inside the formation. Martill and colleagues quote Hillebrandt and colleagues (2013), the paper defining the base of the Jurassic, as saying "the boundary should be expected to occur in the lowest few meters of the Blue Lias Formation" (martill2016a, p. 9), and the traditional Rhaetian rests on Simms and colleagues (2004). Neither has been read. If Hillebrandt and colleagues place the boundary inside the formation in their own voice, the Blue Lias takes the Rhaetian as a minimum extent under the rule on a stage boundary inside a unit ([methods](../../methods.md)).

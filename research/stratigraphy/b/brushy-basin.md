# Brushy Basin

## What do the argon dates say about the stages of the Brushy Basin?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Kimmeridgian and Tithonian. Kowallis and colleagues (1998) date the member by 40Ar/39Ar on sanidine from ash beds, from 150.3 ± 0.3 Ma near the base to 148.1 ± 0.5 Ma at the top. On the ICS chart v2024/12, with the base of the Tithonian at 149.2 Ma, the base is Kimmeridgian and the top Tithonian, the same two stages the paper reads on the time scales of its day. Kirkland and colleagues (2016) relay a recalibrated 150.00 ± 0.52 Ma from near the top of the member at little Cedar Mesa, which is Kimmeridgian (see the 2026-09-20 entry), but conclude from it and a younger U–Pb date that the top of the Morrison lies in the lower third of the Tithonian. They place the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary just above the highest smectitic mudstones of the member. Governs `stages: [Kimmeridgian, Tithonian]` on [Brushy Basin](../../../stratigraphy/b/brushy-basin.yml).

**Evidence.**
- kowallis1998a, p. 235, Abstract: the member gives "ages on sanidine that range systematically between 148.1 ± 0.5 (1 std. error of mean) at the top of the member to 150.3 ± 0.3 Ma near the bottom".
- kowallis1998a, p. 240: "The Brushy Basin Member consists largely of mudstone".
- kowallis1998a, p. 249: "The ages all fit into a consistent stratigraphic framework (Fig. 2) with the exception of MC-39 with a weighted mean age of 147.82 ± 0.63 Ma (1σ), which is about 1 Ma too young"; the sample has "the greatest variability of single-crystal ages", which "may indicate a greater degree of alteration".
- kowallis1998a, p. 256: the member's ages "suggest a Kimmeridgian–Tithonian age for this member based on the time scales" of three charts of the early 1990s.
- kirkland2016a, p. 115: the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary "appears to have stabilized just above the highest smectitic mudstone beds of the Brushy Basin Member of the Morrison Formation".
- kirkland2016a, p. 118: "Thus, the top of the Morrison Formation dates to the lower third of the Tithonian Stage near but not to the end of the Jurassic".

**Ruled out.**
- *147.82 ± 0.63 Ma as the member's youngest age.* Kowallis and colleagues (1998) give it for sample MC-39 from Montezuma Creek, call it "about 1 Ma too young", and leave it out of the range in their abstract. It would not change the stages, since 148.1 Ma is already Tithonian.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does Gillette (1991) date the Brushy Basin Member Early Cretaceous?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** No. The member is Kimmeridgian–Tithonian. Governs `period: [Late Jurassic]` and `stages: [Kimmeridgian, Tithonian]` on [Brushy Basin](../../../stratigraphy/b/brushy-basin.yml).

**Evidence.**
- gillette1991a, p. 418, Age and Horizon: "Upper part of the Brushy Basin Member of the Morrison Formation", followed by "radiometric dates for the upper part of the Morrison Formation elsewhere … and stratigraphic evidence (Bowman, 1986) indicate that at least the upper part of the formation may be wholly or partially Early Cretaceous in age. Della Valle (1980) established a minimum sedimentation age for the Brushy Basin Member of 124 ± 28 m.y."
- kirkland2016a, p. 118: "150.00 ± 0.52 Ma from near the top of the Brushy Basin Member, near little Cedar Mesa, Emery County, Utah, based on a recalibrated 40Ar/39Ar age (Trujillo and Kowallis, 2015)". The date is reported, not measured by the paper; it falls in the Kimmeridgian on the current chart (Tithonian base 149.2 Ma).

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Cretaceous (Gillette 1991).* The sentence concerns "the upper part of the Morrison Formation elsewhere", rests on dates the paper relays, and states a possibility ("may be wholly or partially"). The one figure for the member itself is a minimum relayed from Della Valle (1980), and a minimum of 124 ± 28 m.y. says the beds are at least that old, which a Jurassic age satisfies. The title's "(Upper Jurassic/Lower Cretaceous)" labels the formation, not the member.

**Open.** Nothing.

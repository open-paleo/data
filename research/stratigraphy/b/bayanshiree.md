# Bayanshiree

## What ages do Khand, Hicks and Eberth and their colleagues give the Bayanshiree?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian–Santonian, unchanged. Khand and colleagues (2000) give the formation four ranges in different parts of one chapter without reconciling them: Cenomanian–Santonian from its fossils in the eastern Gobi, Turonian–Santonian for the plant beds at Khar Khutul, late Cenomanian–Santonian for Martinson's (1982) mollusc assemblage, and Cenomanian–Turonian where its dinosaurs are discussed. All four lie inside Cenomanian–Santonian, and the chapter itself calls the formation's position "quite unclear". Hicks and colleagues (1999) report the biostratigraphic estimates in the literature as running from the Cenomanian to the early Campanian, then test them against their own magnetostratigraphy: both sections they sampled are normal throughout, which places the formation in chron C34n and so no later than the latest Santonian. Eberth and colleagues (2009) date the formation's fossils Cenomanian–Santonian; their Campanian covers the formation and the redbeds above it together, as the 2026-10-03 entry records. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian, Coniacian, Santonian]` on [Bayanshiree](../../../stratigraphy/b/bayanshiree.yml).

**Evidence.**
- khand2000a, p. 63: "Fossil evidence suggests that the Bayanshiree Formation is Cenomanian-Santonian in age".
- khand2000a, p. 64: once Shuvalov had referred the Baruunbayan Formation "to the upper part of the Lower Cretaceous", "the lowermost part of the Upper Cretaceous became the Bayanshiree Formation". The work cited is Shuvalov's 1980 chapter (khand2000a, p. 75, reference 3).
- khand2000a, p. 67: "In the locality Khar Khutul, sediments of the Bayanshiree Formation (Turonian–Santonian) contain *Bothrocaryum gobience* and *Nyssoidea mongolica*".
- khand2000a, p. 68: among Martinson's freshwater mollusc assemblages, "Bayanshireenian (late Cenomanian–Santonian)"; the work cited is Martinson's 1982 paper (p. 76, reference 33).
- khand2000a, p. 71: "Among the Bayanshiree Formation (Cenomanian-Turonian) discoveries the *Segnosaurus* and *Erlicosaurus* [41, 42] remains are interesting".
- khand2000a, p. 75: "The position of some stratigraphic divisions, for example, Bayanshiree, Baruungoyot and others, more lower divisions remain to be quite unclear".
- hicks1999a, p. 829, Abstract: the formation has "been assigned a 'Baynshirenian' biostratigraphic age that may range from Cenomanian to early Campanian"; it "was most likely deposited near the end of the Cretaceous Long Normal Interval, no later than the latest Santonian".
- hicks1999a, p. 835: "The lower Upper Cretaceous Bayn Shireh Formation of Martinson (1982)".
- hicks1999a, p. 837: the Baynshirenian age "has been cited in the literature as ranging from the late Cenomanian to early Santonian", from Martinson (1975) and Gradziński and colleagues (1977), "and more recently from the Turonian to lower Campanian", from Jerzykiewicz and Russell (1991).
- hicks1999a, p. 847: both sections "are entirely normal in polarity, which may suggest that the Baynshirenian age attributed to these units is no younger than the latest Santonian", "contra Jerzykiewicz & Russell (1991)"; but "the possibility of finding a reversal near the top of a more complete section of the Bayn Shireh Formation remains plausible".
- eberth2009a, p. 295: "Exposures of the 280 m thick, buff-colored Baynshiree Formation/Svita at each locality produce a rich assemblage of Cenomanian-Santonian vertebrate and invertebrate fossils".
- eberth2009a, p. 296: "we do not use 'Svita' (a chronostratigraphic term) and 'Formation' (a lithostratigraphic term) interchangeably", although p. 295 writes "Baynshiree Formation/Svita" throughout.

**Ruled out.**
- *Cenomanian–Turonian or Turonian–Santonian as the formation's whole range.* Khand and colleagues (2000) give each for one context, the dinosaur finds and the Khar Khutul plant beds, while the same chapter dates the formation Cenomanian–Santonian from its fossils. Neither is argued as the formation's age, and both fall inside the wider range.
- *Early Campanian.* Hicks and colleagues' "Cenomanian to early Campanian" (hicks1999a, p. 829) summarizes earlier biostratigraphic estimates, its Campanian end from Jerzykiewicz and Russell (1991), and their own magnetostratigraphy rejects that end (p. 847).

**Open.** Hicks and colleagues (1999) leave open a reversal near the top of a more complete section, which would carry the formation's top past the end of chron C34n, near the Santonian–Campanian boundary. A magnetostratigraphy of a complete section would settle it.

## Why is the formation spelled Bayanshiree?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** Bayanshiree is Benton's (2000) form: the place name Bayan Shiree in his transliteration, written as one word because it names a formation. The older forms Bayn Shire, Bayn Shireh and Baynshire are transliterations of the same name. Governs `name` and `variants` on [Bayanshiree](../../../stratigraphy/b/bayanshiree.yml) and `location.formation` on the records that name it.

**Evidence.**
- benton2000c, p. xxiii: "When these are converted into names of svitas or formations, the names are rendered as a single word"; "Bain Chire (= Bayan Shiree)"; p. xxiv: "Bayanshiree Formation/ Svita", "Bayn Shire (= Bayan Shiree)".
- jerzykiewicz2000a, p. 283, Fig. 15.3 caption: "Mongolian formation names are given traditional spellings (Barun Goyot = Baruungoyot; Bayn Shire = Bayanshiree; …".

**Ruled out.**
- *Baynshire.* Not a form Benton gives; it runs together the older transliteration.

**Open.** The erecting work. Jerzykiewicz and Russell credit Vasil'ev and colleagues (1959) with the "Bayn Shire Formation" (jerzykiewicz1991a, p. 352), but Gradziński and colleagues say that Vasil'ev and colleagues used a "Bayn Shireh Formation" "established previously in the Eastern Gobi" (gradziński1974a, p. 114). Vasil'ev and colleagues (1959) has not been read.

## Does the Bayanshiree Formation reach the Campanian?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** No: Cenomanian–Santonian. Each Campanian reading traces to an early reading by Jerzykiewicz or Shuvalov that a later paper by the same author narrows, or to a misrelay. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian, Coniacian, Santonian]` on Bayanshiree.

**Evidence.**
- jerzykiewicz2000a, p. 287: "Bayanshiree Formation (Late Cenomanian–Coniacian to ?Early Santonian)".
- shuvalov2000a, p. 266: "Bayanshiree (Cenomanian–Santonian), Baruungoyot (Santonian–Campanian)".
- hicks1999a, p. 829: the beds are "no later than the latest Santonian", from its own magnetostratigraphy.

**Ruled out.**
- *Turonian through early Campanian.* Jerzykiewicz and Russell's "Baynshirenian (Turonian through early Campanian) time" (jerzykiewicz1991a, p. 345) dates a faunal age, not the formation; that Jerzykiewicz's 2000 range revises it is our reading.
- *Shuvalov's Santonian–Campanian.* Maryańska (1977) tabulates Shuvalov's 1976 "Santonian–Campanian" for a Bayshin Tsav locality in the "Bayn Shireh or Barun Goyot svita" (maryańska1977a, p. 91), an undetermined unit, and herself prefers an Upper Cenomanian–Lower Santonian age (p. 133).
- *Cenomanian–Campanian.* Jin and colleagues give "Bayanshiree Svita (Cenomanian–Campanian: Makovicky et al., 2004)" (jin2012a, p. 482); Makovicky and colleagues tabulate Cenomanian–Santonian (makovicky2004a, p. 138, Table 6.1). Eberth (2009) gives "Cenomanian-Campanian" for the Bayanshiree together with the overlying Shine Us Khudag redbeds (eberth2009a, p. 295).

**Open.** Makovicky and colleagues are inconsistent: their text says "Cenomanian or Turonian" (p. 147).

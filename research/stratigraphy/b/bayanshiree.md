# Bayanshiree

## Why is the formation spelled Bayanshiree?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** Bayanshiree is Benton's (2000) form: the place name Bayan Shiree in his transliteration, written as one word because it names a formation. The older forms Bayn Shire, Bayn Shireh and Baynshire are transliterations of the same name. Governs `name` and `variants` on [Bayanshiree](../../../stratigraphy/b/bayanshiree.yml) and `location.formation` on the records that name it.

**Evidence.**
- benton2000c, p. xxiii: "When these are converted into names of svitas or formations, the names are rendered as a single word"; "Bain Chire (= Bayan Shiree)"; p. xxiv: "Bayanshiree Formation/ Svita", "Bayn Shire (= Bayan Shiree)".
- jerzykiewicz2000a, p. 283, Fig. 15.3 caption: "Mongolian formation names are given traditional spellings (Barun Goyot = Baruungoyot; Bayn Shire = Bayanshiree; …".

**Ruled out.**
- *Baynshire.* Not a form Benton gives; it runs together the older transliteration.

**Open.** The erecting work. Jerzykiewicz and Russell credit Vasil'ev and colleagues (1959) with the "Bayn Shire Formation" (jerzykiewicz1991a, p. 352), but Gradziński and colleagues say that Vasil'ev and colleagues used a "Bayn Shireh Formation" "established previously in the Eastern Gobi" (gradziński1974a, p. 114). Vasil'ev and colleagues (1959) has not been read.

## Does the Bayanshiree Formation reach the Campanian?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** No: Cenomanian–Santonian. Each Campanian reading traces to an early reading by Jerzykiewicz or Shuvalov that a later paper by the same author narrows, or to a misrelay. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian, Coniacian, Santonian]` on Bayanshiree.

**Evidence.**
- jerzykiewicz2000a, p. 287: "Bayanshiree Formation (Late Cenomanian–Coniacian to ?Early Santonian)".
- shuvalov2000a, p. 266: "Bayanshiree (Cenomanian–Santonian), Baruungoyot (Santonian–Campanian)".
- hicks1999a, p. 829: the beds are "no later than the latest Santonian", from its own magnetostratigraphy.

**Ruled out.**
- *Turonian through early Campanian.* Jerzykiewicz and Russell's "Baynshirenian (Turonian through early Campanian) time" (jerzykiewicz1991a, p. 345) dates a faunal age, not the formation; that Jerzykiewicz's 2000 range revises it is our reading.
- *Shuvalov's Santonian–Campanian.* Maryańska (1977) tabulates Shuvalov's 1976 "Santonian–Campanian" for a Bayshin Tsav locality in the "Bayn Shireh or Barun Goyot svita" (maryańska1977a, p. 91), an undetermined unit, and herself prefers an Upper Cenomanian–Lower Santonian age (p. 133).
- *Cenomanian–Campanian.* Jin and colleagues give "Bayanshiree Svita (Cenomanian–Campanian: Makovicky et al., 2004)" (jin2012a, p. 482); Makovicky and colleagues tabulate Cenomanian–Santonian (makovicky2004a, p. 138, Table 6.1). Eberth (2009) gives "Cenomanian-Campanian" for the Bayanshiree together with the overlying Shine Us Khudag redbeds (eberth2009a, p. 295).

**Open.** Makovicky and colleagues are inconsistent: their text says "Cenomanian or Turonian" (p. 147).

# Blackleaf

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Blackleaf Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Albian–Cenomanian. Ullmann and colleagues (2011) date the formation "Albian–Cenomanian" and, in the Lima Peaks area, divide the age by member: the upper Vaughn Member is early Cenomanian, and the lower Flood Member latest Albian. The latest Albian is their inference from U-Pb zircon dates of about 93–99 Ma in the middle of the overlying Vaughn Member, which they relay from Zartman and colleagues (1995). Their BL1 microsite lies 102 m above the base of the formation, in the Flood Member. Governs `stages: [Albian, Cenomanian]` on [Blackleaf](../../../stratigraphy/b/blackleaf.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ullmann2011a, p. 311: "Study of the Albian–Cenomanian Blackleaf Formation is significant for both geologic and geographic reasons."
- ullmann2011a, p. 311: "These outcrops primarily represent the early Cenomanian siliciclastic and volcaniclastic Vaughn Member", and "the lower Flood Member is also locally exposed".
- ullmann2011a, p. 311: "U-Pb zircon dates from the middle of the overlying Vaughn Member are approximately 93–99 Mya (Zartman et al. 1995), suggesting that the underlying Flood Member is the latest Albian in the Lima Peaks area".
- ullmann2011a, p. 312: "The BL1 locality occurs 102 m above the Kootenai–Blackleaf contact, a stratigraphic position within the Flood Member within this outcrop region".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Whether the Vaughn Member reaches the Turonian. The 93 Ma end of the zircon span that Ullmann and colleagues relay is younger than the base of the Turonian, 93.9 Ma on the current chart (Gale and colleagues, 2020), while they call the member early Cenomanian. Zartman and colleagues (1995), the source of the dates, has not been read; it would settle whether any date falls in the Turonian.

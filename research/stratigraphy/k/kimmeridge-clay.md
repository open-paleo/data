# Kimmeridge Clay

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Kimmeridge Clay?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Kimmeridgian and Tithonian. British usage long equated the "Kimmeridgian" with the whole Kimmeridge Clay, but the international Kimmeridgian–Tithonian boundary falls inside the formation, near the division between its lower and upper parts. Hesselbo and colleagues (2020) call the part of the Upper Kimmeridge Clay that Cope (1967) zoned by *Pectinatites* "lowermost Tithonian", and equate the British "Bolonian", the upper formation between that boundary and the Portland Sand, with the *Pectinatites elegans* through *Virgatopavlovia fittoni* zones, which their Fig. 26.9 draws in the Early Tithonian. The formation therefore runs from the Kimmeridgian into the lower Tithonian. Cope and colleagues (1980) describe the same British usage, and Benson (2008) independently places a specimen from the *Pectinatites pectinatus* Zone in the lower Tithonian. Governs `stages: [Kimmeridgian, Tithonian]` on [Kimmeridge Clay](../../../stratigraphy/k/kimmeridge-clay.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hesselbo2020a, p. 973: "a confusing equivalence of a "Kimmeridgian Stage" with the "Kimmeridge Clay" formation and an associated lifting the base of d'Orbigny's "Portlandian" Stage became common usage in England".
- hesselbo2020a, p. 974: "In Britain the Kimmeridge Clay Formation was arbitrarily subdivided into a lower and upper member at the approximate Kimmeridgian–Tithonian boundary level"; "Cope (1967) subdivided the lowermost Tithonian portion of the Upper Kimmeridge Clay into several ammonite zones based on successive species of his reconstituted Pectinatites genus".
- hesselbo2020a, p. 975: "The "Bolonian" of Britain is equivalent to the upper Kimmeridgian clay formation between the Kimmeridgian–Tithonian boundary and the Portland Sand, or the Pectinatites (Virgatosphinctoides) elegans through Virgatopavlovia fittoni ammonite zones".
- hesselbo2020a, Fig. 26.9 (p. 976): the Boreal ammonite zones from *Pectinatites elegans* up to *Virgatopavlovia fittoni* all lie in the Early Tithonian.
- cope1980a, p. 77: "In Britain it is customary to equate 'Kimmeridgian' with the Kimmeridge Clay"; "the British Upper Kimmeridgian (elegans Zone upwards) is already Portlandian in France".
- benson2008b, p. 734: "Pectinatites pectinatus ammonite Zone, P. eatlecottensis subzone, Kimmeridge Clay, Late Jurassic (lower Tithonian)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Kimmeridgian only.* Ősi and colleagues (2012) tabulate the formation as "Kimmeridge Clay; Kimmeridgian" (ősi2012a, Table 2). That is the British usage, which takes the stage name for the whole formation (cope1980a, p. 77; hesselbo2020a, p. 973), and it takes in the lower Tithonian upper part. Sánchez-Fenollosa and colleagues (2025) give "Kimmeridgian" only for the "Lower part of the Kimmeridge Clay Formation" (sánchez-fenollosa2025a, p. 170), which agrees with the international stage.
- *A Berriasian top.* Sánchez-Fenollosa and colleagues (2023) give "Kimmeridge Clay Formation (Kimmeridgian– Berriasian)" (sánchez-fenollosa2023a, p. 1013) with no basis for the Berriasian. The formation ends at the base of the Portland Sand, and the "Bolonian" below it reaches only the *V. fittoni* Zone of the Early Tithonian (hesselbo2020a, p. 975, Fig. 26.9).

**Open.** Nothing.

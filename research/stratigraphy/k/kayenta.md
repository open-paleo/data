# Kayenta

## On what evidence did Lewis and colleagues (1961) and Kermack (1982) date the Kayenta?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Lewis and colleagues (1961) proposed a Late Triassic(?) age from *Protosuchus* and a new tritylodont near the top of the formation on Comb Ridge, which they tied to the Stormberg of South Africa, then correlated with the Keuper, and from two freshwater gastropods. They set aside the Jurassic age that Welles (1954) drew from the limb proportions of the Kayenta dinosaur, and the Geologic Names Committee of the US Geological Survey approved their assignment. Kermack (1982) answered that *Kayentatherium*, though related to *Tritylodon* and *Bienotherium*, is considerably more advanced than either and need not be contemporaneous with them, and put the formation in the Lower or Middle Jurassic. Olsen and Galton (1984) explain the 1961 conclusion by the Stormberg's then being taken as typical Late Triassic, place the upper Stormberg in the Early Jurassic, and report Kermack's reading without adopting it. Marsh and Rowe (2020) cite both papers among those behind the "Early Jurassic or Late Triassic" age the formation carried around the collection of the *Dilophosaurus* holotype, and give the Silty Facies as Sinemurian–Pliensbachian; Nesbitt and colleagues (2007) give the formation as Sinemurian. This entry sets out the evidence behind the two readings that the 2026-10-03 entry rules out, and changes no value. Governs `period: [Early Jurassic]` on [Kayenta](../../../stratigraphy/k/kayenta.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lewis1961a, p. 1437: an appraisal "resulted in the proposal that the Kayenta formation and the lower part of the Navajo sandstone be assigned a Late Triassic (?) age"; "*Protosuchus richardsoni* and a new genus of tritylodont reptile are present from 8 to 10 feet below the top of the Kayenta formation on Comb Ridge"; the association "also suggests a correlation of the Kayenta and Moenave with the Stormberg series of South Africa"; "Welles believed the dinosaur to be Jurassic because of the relative proportions of the limb bones".
- lewis1961a, p. 1438: the limb-proportion comparisons "are not applicable for age determinations"; two freshwater gastropods, "*Valvata gregorii* and *Lymnaea hopii*", are assigned to the Late Triassic; "Therefore the evidence, although not conclusive, strongly suggests that the Kayenta formation is of Late Triassic age".
- lewis1961a, pp. 1439–1440: "the Geologic Names Committee of the U. S. Geological Survey has approved the following age assignments", among them "Kayenta formation—Late Triassic(?)".
- kermack1982a, p. 1: *Kayentatherium* "is related to the *Tritylodon Bienotherium* lineage, but is considerably more advanced than the latter, which is of Upper Triassic–Lower Jurassic age".
- kermack1982a, p. 15: the argument of Lewis and colleagues "ignores the fact that *Kayentatherium* is also considerably more advanced than either *Tritylodon* or *Bienotherium*, and need not be assumed to be contemporaneous with them"; for the Stormberg and Lufeng beds "a Rhaetian or early Jurassic age for both these formations is more probable".
- olsen1984a, p. 93: the *Tritylodon*-like tritylodontid "has been used as evidence by Lewis, Irwin, and Wilson (1961) to conclude that the age of the Glen Canyon Group is Late Triassic because the Stormberg was considered "typical" Late Triassic"; Kermack argues that *Kayentatherium* "actually suggests an Early to Middle Jurassic age for at least the Kayenta Formation"; "the upper Stormberg appears to be no older than very latest Triassic and the bulk of the evidence suggests an Early Jurassic age".
- marsh2020a, p. 1: the formation "was hypothesized to be Early Jurassic or Late Triassic in age, based predominantly on vertebrate biostratigraphy".
- marsh2020a, p. 6: "Silty Facies of the Kayenta Formation, Early Jurassic, Sinemurian–Pliensbachian".
- nesbitt2007a, p. 235: "the Kayenta Formation (Sinemurian)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Triassic, from the tritylodont's link to the Stormberg.* The correlation dates the Kayenta only as far as the Stormberg was then dated. Kermack (1982) counters that *Kayentatherium* is more advanced than the Stormberg and Lufeng tritylodonts and that a Rhaetian or Early Jurassic age is more probable for those beds, and Olsen and Galton (1984) put the upper Stormberg in the Early Jurassic. The gastropods are the only other evidence Lewis and colleagues give, and they call the whole "not conclusive".
- *Lower or Middle Jurassic, from Kayentatherium's advanced grade.* A reading from the grade of one tritylodontid, which Olsen and Galton (1984) report without adopting; the later works cited give Early Jurassic stages, Sinemurian and Sinemurian–Pliensbachian.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the Kayenta Formation Late Triassic or Middle Jurassic in part?

*2026-10-03*

**Conclusion.** Neither. The Kayenta is Early Jurassic; the Late Triassic and Middle Jurassic readings are superseded. Governs `period: [Early Jurassic]` on Kayenta.

**Evidence.**
- The record's Early Jurassic sources (not re-examined for this note) stand against the two readings below.

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Triassic.* Lewis and colleagues (1961) write that the fauna "strongly suggests … Late Triassic age" (lewis1961a, pp. 1437–1438), an early faunal reading.
- *Lower or Middle Jurassic.* Kermack (1982) concludes, in his own voice, that the beds "must be Lower or Middle Jurassic age" (kermack1982a, pp. 15–16); Olsen and Galton relay it (olsen1984a, p. 93). It is superseded, not merely relayed.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Kuwajima

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Kuwajima?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Berriasian to Barremian, the union of the readings. Ohashi and Barrett (2009) regard the formation "tentatively" as Valanginian–Hauterivian, bracketed by the ammonite-bearing Mitarai Formation of the Kuzuryu Subgroup below and the uppermost Barremian to Aptian Kitadani Formation above, and correlated by bivalves with the Hauterivian Ryoseki Formation. Sano (2015) places the formation in the Tetori Group *sensu stricto*, which the paper restricts to the Early Cretaceous; brackish bivalves occur only in its lower part, below the last brackish horizon, and the Kaseki-kabe vertebrate bed in its upper part is Barremian, younger than that horizon and older than the Barremian–Aptian Akaiwa Formation. It puts the whole formation "probably older than Aptian". Governs `stages: [Berriasian, Valanginian, Hauterivian, Barremian]` on [Kuwajima](../../../stratigraphy/k/kuwajima.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ohashi2009a, p. 748: the Kuwajima Formation "consists of alternating beds of sandstone, mudstone, and shale".
- ohashi2009a, p. 749: the Mitarai Formation of the underlying Kuzuryu Subgroup "yields ammonites"; the Kitadani Formation "is assigned an uppermost Barremian to Aptian age"; the Kuwajima "correlates with the Hauterivian-aged Ryoseki Formation of the Outer Zone of southwest Japan based on similarities in their bivalve faunas"; "Therefore, the upper part of the Itoshiro Subgroup is estimated to be of Early Cretaceous age, and the Kuwajima Formation is tentatively regarded as Valanginian–Hauterivian in age."
- ohashi2009a, p. 749, Type Locality and Horizon: "uppermost Kuwajima Formation, Lower Cretaceous (Valanginian–Hauterivian)".
- sano2015a, p. 25, Abstract: the Tetori Group *sensu lato* is divided into the "Middle–Late Jurassic Kuzuryu Group" and the "unconformably overlying Early Cretaceous Tetori Group (*sensu stricto*)".
- sano2015a, p. 33: "the Gomishima (or Gomijima), Kuwajima (or Kuwashima), Akaiwa and Kitadani formations, in ascending order"; brackish bivalves "are recovered only from the lower part of the Kuwajima Formation", and freshwater molluscs "have been known from the upper part".
- sano2015a, p. 35: the Akaiwa Formation's paleomagnetism and date "revealed that the Kuwajima Formation is probably older than Aptian".
- sano2015a, p. 38: the Kaseki-kabe vertebrate bed in the upper part of the formation "is assigned to Barremian, because it is younger than the age of brackish horizon, and earlier than Barremian–Aptian Akaiwa Formation".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Ohashi and Barrett (2009) give the *Albalophosaurus* horizon at Kaseki-kabe, in the uppermost formation, as Valanginian–Hauterivian, while Sano (2015) and Sano and Yabe (2017) assign the Kaseki-kabe bed to the Barremian on its position above the last brackish horizon. Both readings fall within the unit's stages; a direct date from the Kaseki-kabe bed would settle the horizon's stage.

# Kirtland

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Kirtland?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Hunt and Lucas (1992) divide the formation into the Bisti, Hunter Wash, Farmington, De-na-zin and Naashoibito Members, naming the Bisti, Hunter Wash and De-na-zin themselves, and date it by land-vertebrate biochrons: the members below the Naashoibito Edmontonian, the Naashoibito Lancian. The reading of each biochron as a stage is theirs, not a later conversion: they give the Aquilan, Judithian, Edmontonian and Lancian as early Campanian, late Campanian, early Maastrichtian and late Maastrichtian. They reject dating the whole Kirtland as late Campanian from the marine invertebrates that date the Fruitland below. Fassett and Steiner (1997) date a polarity reversal in the Farmington Member and doubt that the vertebrates near their section can fix its age, though they report Hunt and Lucas's Edmontonian, which those authors' figure 11 extends over all of the upper Kirtland Shale as Fassett and Steiner define it. Whether the Edmontonian of the lower members is Maastrichtian on the current chart is settled in [Maastrichtian in the lower Kirtland members](../../topics/lower-kirtland-maastrichtian.md). Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Kirtland](../../../stratigraphy/k/kirtland.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hunt1992a, p. 217, Abstract: "The Kirtland consists, in ascending order, of the Bisti, Hunter Wash, Farmington, De-na-zin and Naashoibito Members"; "Diverse evidence indicates that the Fruitland is of Judithian (late Campanian) age, the pre-Naashoibito Kirtland is of Edmontonian (early Maastrichtian) age and the Naashoibito is of Lancian (late Maastrichtian) age."
- hunt1992a, p. 217: "We formally propose adoption of the name Bisti Member"; p. 218: "We coin the name Hunter Wash Member"; p. 220: "We here name the upper shale member of Reeside (1924) the De-na-zin Member".
- hunt1992a, p. 220: Reeside's upper shale member "included what is now termed the Naashoibito Member of the Kirtland Formation (Baltz et al., 1966)"; p. 221: "Baltz et al. (1966) named the Naashoibito Member of the Fruitland Formation for strata that were generally included in the lower part of the Ojo Alamo Sandstone".
- hunt1992a, p. 232: the Aquilan, Judithian, Edmontonian and Lancian biochrons "are respectively of early Campanian, late Campanian, early Maastrictian and late Maastrichtian age".
- hunt1992a, p. 232: "it is reasonable to assume that the Fruitland can be approximately dated as late Campanian on the basis of the main invertebrates. However, it is not possible to further deduce that the marine invertebrates indicate that the entire Kirtland is also late Campanian in age".
- fassett1997a, p. 239, Abstract: ash beds bracketing the C33n-C32r reversal at Hunter Wash, in "the Upper Cretaceous continental Farmington Sandstone Member of the Kirtland Shale", "establish its age as 73.50 ± 0.18 Ma".
- fassett1997a, p. 244: the vertebrates' "value in fixing the biostratigraphic age of the rocks containing the polarity reversal (Kirtland Shale) is limited", because of "the endemic nature of taxa and uncertainties in differentiating between geographic and age differences in faunal assemblages"; "Hunt and Lucas (1992, p. 234) concluded that '... the lower Kirtland is Edmontonian (early Maestrichtian) in age'; moreover, their figure 11 shows *all* of the upper Kirtland Shale (of this report) to be Edmontonian."

**Ruled out.**
- *The whole Kirtland late Campanian, from the marine invertebrates.* Hunt and Lucas (1992, p. 232) accept the invertebrates as dating the Fruitland, but the Kirtland lies above it and was laid down in a different setting, so they do not carry that age up into it.
- *Maastrichtian for the members below the Naashoibito, from the Edmontonian.* Ruled out in [Maastrichtian in the lower Kirtland members](../../topics/lower-kirtland-maastrichtian.md).

**Open.** The Maastrichtian of the formation as a whole. With the lower members Campanian on the current chart, it rests on Hunt and Lucas's Lancian Naashoibito, which the registry places in the Ojo Alamo under a dispute (see [Naashoibito](../../../stratigraphy/n/naashoibito.yml)), and on Ford (2000), who gives a locality in the formation as Maastrichtian.

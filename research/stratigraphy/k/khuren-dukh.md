# Khuren Dukh

## Do Ito and colleagues and Nichols and colleagues agree on the Khuren Dukh's age?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No. Ito and colleagues (2006) credit Nichols and colleagues (2006) with refining the formation's age to the late Albian, but Nichols and colleagues give the formation "middle to late Albian" throughout and use "late Albian" only for the dinosaur fauna. The Ito note now says so, so the two notes do not read as agreeing. Governs the note on ito2006a on [Khuren Dukh](../../../stratigraphy/k/khuren-dukh.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- ito2006a, p. 229: "Nichols et al. (2006) have further refined the age of the Khuren Dukh Formation as late Albian, based on new palynological data."
- nichols2006a, p. 241, Abstract: "The Khuren Dukh Formation is determined to be middle to late Albian in age."
- nichols2006a, p. 250: "The palynological evidence presented in the foregoing indicates that this fauna is late Albian in age."

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What strata did Ito and colleagues (2006) erect the Khuren Dukh Formation for?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The infill of the Choyr Basin that Matsukawa and colleagues (1997) had mapped as the Shinekhudag Formation, and which earlier workers had assigned to the Zuunbayan Formation. Ito and colleagues (2006) define the Khalzan Uul and Khuren Dukh formations from their own mapping, and replace the older names because those came from type localities about 170 km away and carried ages they reject for the Choyr Basin. The Khuren Dukh is therefore a new unit for these strata, not a new name for the Shinekhudag, and the strata are no longer assigned to the Zuunbayan. For its age, Ito and colleagues relay earlier conclusions of Aptian–Albian or middle–late Albian, and a refinement to late Albian by Nichols and colleagues (2006), whose own wording is "middle to late Albian"; either way the age adds no stage beyond the Aptian and Albian. Governs the naming reference on [Khuren Dukh](../../../stratigraphy/k/khuren-dukh.yml), and its separation from the [Zuunbayan](../../../stratigraphy/z/zuunbayan.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ito2006a, p. 226, abstract: "The sedimentary infill of the Choyr Basin is newly defined as the Khalzan Uul and Khuren Dukh formations based on detailed mapping of lateral and vertical variations in component lithofacies assemblages."
- ito2006a, p. 227: the Shinekhudag and the other names "were adapted from units defined as 'svitas'", whose "type localities are about 170 km southeast of the Choyr Basin".
- ito2006a, p. 228: "we believe similar age assignments to strata in the Choyr Basin to be incorrect"; "the unit mapped as the Shinekhudag Formation is designated the Khuren Dukh Formation".
- ito2006a, pp. 228–229: "These studies concluded that the age of the Khuren Dukh Formation is either Aptian–Albian or middle-late Albian. Nichols et al. (2006) have further refined the age of the Khuren Dukh Formation as late Albian, based on new palynological data."
- nichols2006a, p. 241, abstract: "strata previously identified as either the Shinekhudag Formation or the Zuunbayan Formation but recently renamed the Khuren Dukh Formation".
- nichols2006a, p. 242: "none of these formation names is appropriate for the rocks in this isolated basin, because they are derived from distant localities and carry erroneous implications of geologic age".

**Ruled out.**
- *Khuren Dukh as a renaming of the Shinekhudag Formation.* Nichols and colleagues (2006) write "renamed", but Ito and colleagues apply the new name only to the Choyr Basin strata, and leave the Shinekhudag and the other older names to the units of their type areas about 170 km away.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does the Khuren Dukh Formation reach the Barremian?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** No. The only Barremian for the formation is Makovicky and Norell's attribution to Shuvalov (2000), whose paper dates Hüren Dukh Aptian–Albian. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on [Khuren Dukh](../../../stratigraphy/k/khuren-dukh.yml), replacing `[Barremian, Aptian, Albian]`.

**Evidence.**
- makovicky2006a, p. 2: the beds "have been interpreted as Aptian-early Albian in age … but have also been dated as Barremian (~128 MYA) by Shuvalov (2000)".
- shuvalov2000a, p. 264: "The Hüren Dukh succession is dated by molluscs and dinosaurs … The molluscs are characteristic of the Aptian-Albian … The spore-pollen assemblage … is also Aptian-Albian"; the Barremian in the paper belongs to the Shinekhudag.
- hicks1999a, p. 843: palynology gives "an Albian age—probably middle to late Albian" for the beds at Khuren Dukh.

**Ruled out.**
- *Barremian.* Makovicky and Norell's citation does not contain it. Hicks and colleagues (1999, p. 836) report a Hauterivian–Barremian ostracod age from Matsukawa and colleagues (1997) and do not adopt it; Matsukawa and colleagues have not been read.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which part of the Khuren Dukh Formation is *Choyrodon* from?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** The lower part, as the describing paper gives it: the Lower Member of Ito and colleagues' (2006) scheme. Governs `location.part: lower` on [*Choyrodon barsboldi*](../../../genera/C/Choyrodon.yml). Settles the **Open** line of the entry on *Zavacephale*.

**Evidence.**
- gates2018a, p. 4: the holotype quarry "is located within a thick brown–black organic rich siltstone at the Khuren Dukh locality of the Lower Member of the Khuren Dukh Formation (sensu Ito et al., 2006)".

**Ruled out.**
- *The middle unit, from Chinzorig and colleagues' Fig. 1a.* The figure sets a *Choyrodon* silhouette, credited to PhyloPic, beside the column at about the level of its "Middle Khuren Dukh" band, but the paper gives no text, data or source for *Choyrodon*'s level, and the same figure is inconsistent for its own taxon: its star sits in the "Lower Khuren Dukh" band, its caption says "upper part of the middle KDF", and its text "upper portion of the lower KDF" (chinzorig2025a, p. 1139). The two papers also use different schemes, two members against three units, which no source read relates.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which part of the Khuren Dukh Formation is Zavacephale from, and how is the name spelled?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** The upper portion of the lower Khuren Dukh Formation. "Khuren Dukh" is the majority spelling and the registry's headword; Hüren Dukh is the gazetteer's form. Governs `location.part: lower` on [*Zavacephale rinpoche*](../../../genera/Z/Zavacephale.yml) and the variants on Khuren Dukh.

**Evidence.**
- chinzorig2025a, p. 1139, Locality and horizon: "MPC-D 100/1209 was recovered from the upper portion of the lower KDF". In Fig. 1a the specimen's star sits in the bed colored "Lower Khuren Dukh" (our reading of the figure).
- chinzorig2025a, p. 1139: the Khuren Dukh faunas "differ from those of the potentially penecontemporaneous Khukhteeg Formation and warrant distinction".
- benton2000c, p. xxv: "Hüren Dukh [locality]", "Hürendukh Formation/ Svita", "Khooren Dookh (= Hüren Dukh)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Middle Khuren Dukh Formation.* The Fig. 1 caption says "the upper part of the middle KDF" (p. 1139); the paper's text and the figure itself say lower.

**Open.** The position of *Choyrodon*. Gates and colleagues (2018) place it "at the Khuren Dukh locality of the Lower Member of the Khuren Dukh Formation (Ito et al., 2006)", in the beds that yielded *Altirhinus*, "middle to late Albian (Nichols, Matsukawa & Ito, 2006)" (gates2018a, pp. 2, 4); Chinzorig and colleagues' Fig. 1a draws *Choyrodon* in the middle unit. Spellings in the literature also include Khuren-Dukh, Khoren Dukh and Khooren Dukh.

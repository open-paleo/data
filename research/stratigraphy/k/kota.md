# Kota

## Does the Kota keep its dispute, and does the Hettangian stay out?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** The Hettangian stays out, and the dispute is removed. Supersedes the ground of the 2026-09-20 entry. The only Hettangian is Bandyopadhyay and Sengupta's (2006) report of Bandyopadhyay and Roy Chowdhury (1996), not read; Bandyopadhyay's own later dating in the same paper and in 2010 gives the lower Kota Sinemurian to Pliensbachian and the upper Kota Toarcian to ?Aalenian, which supersedes it. The dispute set the 2010 abstract's "Sinemurian to Pliensbachian" for the whole formation against Kareem and colleagues' (2024) "Early to Middle Jurassic"; the abstract's line is the lower Kota's age, which the same paper's text gives as such, and Kareem and colleagues relay the same split, so no live disagreement remains. Governs the removed `dispute` and the note on bandyopadhyay2006a on [Kota](../../../stratigraphy/k/kota.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- bandyopadhyay2006a, p. 80: "Close scrutiny of the taxa of both the lower and the upper units of the Kota Formation led Bandyopadhyay and Roy Chowdhury (1996) to suggest that the Kota Formation has an age ranging from Hettangian to Toarcian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Hettangian.* A relay, superseded by the same author.

**Open.** Bandyopadhyay and Roy Chowdhury (1996) have not been read.

## Which part of the Kota does Bandyopadhyay and colleagues' (2010) "Sinemurian to Pliensbachian" date?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The lower Kota. The abstract of Bandyopadhyay and colleagues (2010) attaches "Sinemurian to Pliensbachian" to the Kota Formation as a whole (quoted in the 2026-09-20 entry), but the same paper gives the holotype's horizon as the "Early to early Middle Jurassic Kota Formation", and its section on age reports that Bandyopadhyay and Roychowdhury (1996) and Bandyopadhyay and Sengupta (2006) gave the lower Kota a Sinemurian to Pliensbachian age and the upper Kota a Toarcian, perhaps Aalenian, one. The same section records that the formation "has long been considered to be of Liassic age" on its fishes. Bandyopadhyay and Sengupta (2006), which is held, give the lower Kota Sinemurian to Pliensbachian, above the Hettangian Upper Dharmaram, and the upper Kota Toarcian to "probably ?Aalenian", and report that Bandyopadhyay and Roychowdhury (1996) had given the whole formation "an age ranging from Hettangian to Toarcian". Governs `stages: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian, Aalenian]` on [Kota](../../../stratigraphy/k/kota.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bandyopadhyay2010a, p. 538: "On the basis of fishes, the Kota Formation has long been considered to be of Liassic age"; "Bandyopadhyay and Roychowdhury (1996) and Bandyopadhyay and Sengupta (2006) suggested that the Lower Kota Formation has an age ranging from Sinemurian to Pliensbachian".
- bandyopadhyay2010a, p. 541, Locality and horizon: "Early to early Middle Jurassic Kota Formation, Pranhita-Godavari basin".
- bandyopadhyay2006a, p. 80: "Bandyopadhyay and Roy Chowdhury (1996) to suggest that the Kota Formation has an age ranging from Hettangian to Toarcian, and it may even extend into the Middle Jurassic".
- bandyopadhyay2006a, pp. 80–81: "the age of the Lower Kota with its sauropods and therian and non-therians, overlying the Hettangian Upper Dharmaram, ranges from Sinemurian to Pliensbachian, while the overlying Upper Kota, with its Toarcian fishes, crocodylomorph, and turtles, has a Toarcian to probably ?Aalenian age".

**Ruled out.**
- *"Sinemurian to Pliensbachian" as the age of the whole formation.* The abstract of Bandyopadhyay and colleagues (2010) words it so, but the same paper dates the formation Early to early Middle Jurassic and gives the Sinemurian to Pliensbachian to the lower Kota only, after the works it cites.
- *"Liassic" as an age for the formation.* Bandyopadhyay and colleagues (2010) report it as the long-standing reading from the fishes, and it gives no stage.

**Open.** Whether Bandyopadhyay and Roychowdhury (1996) gave the formation the Hettangian, as Bandyopadhyay and Sengupta (2006) report, or the lower Kota the Sinemurian to Pliensbachian, as Bandyopadhyay and colleagues (2010) report; the 1996 chapter would settle it. The 2026-09-20 entry rules out the Hettangian because no primary source read for the unit gives it, a reason this report bears on; its conclusion does not depend on it, since Bandyopadhyay and Sengupta (2006) place the lower Kota above the Hettangian Upper Dharmaram.

## Do the Hettangian, the Late Jurassic or the Early Cretaceous belong on the Kota?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** No. The formation is Sinemurian–Aalenian: Sinemurian–Pliensbachian for the lower Kota, and Toarcian, possibly Aalenian, for the upper. Governs `period: [Early Jurassic, Middle Jurassic]` and `stages: [Sinemurian, Pliensbachian, Toarcian, Aalenian]` on [Kota](../../../stratigraphy/k/kota.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bandyopadhyay2010a, p. 533, Abstract: "the Early Jurassic Kota Formation (Sinemurian to Pliensbachian)"; p. 538: "the age of the upper Kota is Toarcian and may even be extended to Middle Jurassic (?Aalenian)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Hettangian.* Weishampel and colleagues' distribution chapter gives "Age: Hettangian-Pliensbachian" for the Kota Formation, with no source for the age (weishampel2004b, p. 534). No primary source read for this unit gives the Hettangian, and a reference work does not carry a value alone ([methods](../../methods.md)).
- *Early Cretaceous, on palynology.* Buffetaut and colleagues write that the Kota "may in fact be as recent as Early Cretaceous on the basis of palynology; G. V. R. Prasad, personal communication" (buffetaut2000a, p. 72). A personal communication is not a published determination; this is the case behind that rule in [methods](../../methods.md).
- *Late Jurassic and Early Cretaceous, from Rincón and colleagues (2022).* Their "age estimate has varied from Early Jurassic to Early Cretaceous depending upon the biostratigraphic indicator (see summary in Chinnappa et al., 2019)" reports other authors' endpoints. The paper's own statement is that "An Early–Middle Jurassic age is currently accepted for the upper part of the Kota Formation" (rincón2022a, p. 13, India).

**Open.** Whether the palynological Early Cretaceous was ever published. Chinnappa and colleagues (2019), the summary Rincón and colleagues cite, has not been read.

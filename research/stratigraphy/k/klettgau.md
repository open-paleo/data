# Klettgau

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Klettgau?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Carnian to Rhaetian. Jordan and colleagues (2016), who formally define the formation, give it "Carnian to Rhaetian" after Kozur and Bachmann (2008), and date its three lowest members, the Ergolz, Gansingen and Berlingen, as Carnian; the Gansingen Member starts with dolomite bearing Carnian bivalves. The Rhaetian rests on the Belchen Member at the top. The same paper gives that member "(?) Norian to (at least middle) Rhaetian", and Rauhut and colleagues (2020) describe its sandy bonebeds in Canton Baselland and Solothurn as dated by palynology to lower, middle and upper Rhaetian. Sues and Schoch (2025) give the formation as lower to middle Rhaetian at Füllinsdorf-Schönthal near Basel, without naming a member. In Canton Schaffhausen no Rhaetian has been proved, on the account of Rauhut and colleagues (2020): they derive the type material of their sauropodiform from the uppermost Gruhalde Member, "uppermost Norian", after Jordan and colleagues (2016), and the Bratelen Bonebeds on top of that member record erosion at the end of or after its deposition. Governs `stages` on [Klettgau](../../../stratigraphy/k/klettgau.yml).

**Evidence.**
- jordan2016a, p. 268: the formation's chronostratigraphic age, "Carnian to Rhaetian (DSK 2002, 2005; Kozur and Bachmann 2008)".
- jordan2016a, p. 271: the Ergolz Member, "Carnian (DSK 2002, 2005; Kozur and Bachmann 2008)".
- jordan2016a, p. 257, Abstract: "the Norian to Rhaetian Klettgau Formation"; the Gansingen Member "starts with partly porous dolomite with Carnian bivalves".
- jordan2016a, p. 282: the Belchen Member, "(?) Norian to (at least middle) Rhaetian".
- rauhut2020a, p. 9, Systematic palaeontology: the type material "was most probably derived from the uppermost part of the 'Zanclodonmergel'(=Knollenmergel), now called Gruhalde Member of the Klettgau Formation, uppermost Norian (Jordan et al. 2016)".
- rauhut2020a, p. 8, Geology and stratigraphy: the Bratelen Bonebeds record "an important erosional event at the end or after the deposition of the Gruhalde Member (late Norian?)"; "Rhaetian sediments have never been proved by litho-, bio- or chronostratigraphy in the Canton Schaffhausen"; the bonebeds differ from sandy bonebeds "dated by palynostratigraphy to lower, middle and upper Rhaetian".
- rauhut2020a, p. 9, Geology and stratigraphy: those Rhaetian bonebeds include the "Belchen Member of the Klettgau Formation in Canton Baselland and Solothurn".
- sues2025a, p. 452: "the Klettgau Formation (lower to middle Rhaetian) of Füllinsdorf-Schönthal".

**Ruled out.**
- *Norian to Rhaetian, without the Carnian.* The abstract of Jordan and colleagues (2016) calls it "the Norian to Rhaetian Klettgau Formation", but the same paper's formal definition gives Carnian to Rhaetian, and its member descriptions date the Ergolz, Gansingen and Berlingen Members as Carnian. The formal definition is taken as the paper's reading.
- *A Rhaetian age for the Bratelen Bonebeds of Canton Schaffhausen.* Their older name, "Rhät-Bonebed", implies one, but Rauhut and colleagues (2020) place the erosional event they record at the end of or after the Gruhalde Member, "(late Norian?)", and set them apart from the Rhaetian sandy bonebeds of the Belchen Member. The Rhaetian of the formation rests on the Belchen Member, not on these beds.

**Open.** Nothing.

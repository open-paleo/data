# Kaiparowits

## Does the Maastrichtian stand on the originals Sullivan and Williamson relay?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No; the Maastrichtian is dropped. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry on the stages and answers its Open line. Gregory and Moore (1931) give the formation as "of very late Montana age", corresponding to the Fruitland, and name no international stage; a Montana age adds no stage, as in the Kirtland note (see [Kirtland](../k/kirtland.md)), and Sullivan and Williamson's (1999) "early Maastrichtian (Gregory and Moore, 1931)" is their gloss, not the 1931 text. Lohrengel (1969) reads the formation from its pollen as "upper Lancian to Hell Creekian, or upper Maestrichtian". Eaton (1991) re-examines those identifications one by one, finds the taxa Campanian or long-ranging and the assemblage lacking "virtually all" the Maastrichtian index taxa, and concludes that "no evidence suggests that the Kaiparowits Formation is younger than Campanian". An explicit supersession of the same evidence moves Lohrengel's reading to the reference notes. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` on [Kaiparowits](../../../stratigraphy/k/kaiparowits.yml) and the notes on gregory1931a, lohrengel1969a and eaton1991a; its genera, all Campanian, are unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- gregory1931a, p. 112: the Wahweap lies "beneath the Kaiparowits formation, of very late Montana age".
- sullivan1999b, p. 34: "The Kaiparowits Formation has been correlated to the early Maastrichtian (Gregory and Moore, 1931) and the late Maastrichtian (Lohrengel, 1969) on the basis of invertebrates and pollen, respectively."
- lohrengel1969a, p. 91: the Kaiparowits Formation "is upper Lancian to Hell Creekian, or upper Maestrichtian, in age".
- eaton1991a, p. 57: "The specimen figured by Lohrengel looks almost identical to A. turbidus"; p. 58: "Lohrengel's assemblage lacks virtually all the distinctive Maastrichtian taxa present in the Western Interior Wodehouseia spinata Assemblage Zone".
- eaton1991a, p. 47, Abstract: "The Kaiparowits Formation is middle to late Campanian in age, based on mammalian faunas and reevaluation of previously reported palynofloras"; p. 60: "no evidence suggests that the Kaiparowits Formation is younger than Campanian".
- loewen2013b, p. 492: "The Kaiparowits Formation, dating to ~76.6–74.5 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian from Gregory and Moore (1931).* They give a Montana age and no stage; the early Maastrichtian is a later gloss.
- *Maastrichtian from Lohrengel (1969).* Superseded by Eaton's re-examination of the same pollen.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do Sullivan and Williamson (1999) say about the age of the Kaiparowits?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** They treat the formation's age as unsettled and do not date it themselves. They report that Gregory and Moore (1931) and Lohrengel (1969) correlated it with the early and the late Maastrichtian, from invertebrates and pollen, and that Eaton (1991) found the palynomorph and mammal data to support a Campanian age, "late early(?) to late Campanian". They then reason from that Campanian reading on condition that it is correct, and say that the lower third of the formation, which yielded two referred specimens of *Parasaurolophus cyrtocristatus*, has been read as Judithian but "remains equivocal". The Maastrichtian in the formation's stages therefore traces to the two earlier correlations they relay, not to Sullivan and Williamson. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Kaiparowits](../../../stratigraphy/k/kaiparowits.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sullivan1999b, p. 34: "The Kaiparowits Formation has been correlated to the early Maastrichtian (Gregory and Moore, 1931) and the late Maastrichtian (Lohrengel, 1969) on the basis of invertebrates and pollen, respectively".
- sullivan1999b, p. 35: the palynomorph data with the fossil mammals "supported a Campanian age for this formation"; "the Kaiparowits is late early(?) to late Campanian (Eaton, 1991, p. 59)".
- sullivan1999b, p. 37: "the relative age of the lower third of the Kaiparowits Formation, which has yielded two referred specimens of *P. cyrtocristatus*, has been interpreted as Judithian age, but remains equivocal".
- sullivan1999b, p. 38: "If the interpretation of the age of the Kaiparowits Formation is correct (see above), the lower part of the Kaiparowits Formation is significantly older than the Dinosaur Park Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Sullivan and Williamson (1999) as a source of the Maastrichtian.* They relay the Maastrichtian correlations as earlier views and reason from Eaton's Campanian age; a relayed reading adds nothing to the original it relays.

**Open.** Whether the Maastrichtian stands on the originals Sullivan and Williamson relay. Reading the stage each of Gregory and Moore (1931) and Lohrengel (1969) gives, against Eaton's (1991) re-evaluation of Lohrengel's pollen, would settle it.

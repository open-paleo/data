# Kalazha

## Is the Kalazha one unit across the Turpan and Junggar basins, and what dates it?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** One entry, dated from the Junggar Basin, with the age disputed. Upchurch and colleagues (2021), who place [*Hudiesaurus*](../../../genera/H/Hudiesaurus.yml) and [*Rhomaleopakhus*](../../../genera/R/Rhomaleopakhus.yml) in the lower Kalazha at Qiketai in the Turpan Basin, note that the formation's correlation across the two basins has no explicit evidence behind it, yet date the Turpan beds from work on the Junggar Basin, Eberth and colleagues (2001), Deng and colleagues (2015) and Fang and colleagues (2016) among it. No work read separates the two, so the entry keeps both under one name, as the Shengjinkou does. Read first-hand, the Junggar works disagree. Deng and colleagues date the formation late Kimmeridgian, inferred from the dated top of the underlying Qigu, with the Tithonian missing above it. Eberth and colleagues report the basal conglomerate at Manas "regarded as latest Jurassic (Tithonian)" and put the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary at the base of or within the formation, which carries it into the Early Cretaceous, though they name no Cretaceous stage. Fang and colleagues (2016) give epochs only and disagree with themselves, and Upchurch and colleagues' (2004) "Tithonian" is a table entry with no stated ground. Governs `period: [Late Jurassic, Early Cretaceous]`, `stages: [Kimmeridgian, Tithonian]` and the dispute on [Kalazha](../../../stratigraphy/k/kalazha.yml), and the unchanged stages of *Hudiesaurus* and *Rhomaleopakhus*.

**Evidence.**
- upchurch2021a, p. e1994414-3: "there is currently no explicit evidence that the putative outcrops of the Qigu Formation in the Turpan Basin correlate with those in the Junggar Basin (N.B., the same issue also affects correlation of the Kalazha Formation across these two basins)".
- deng2015a, p. 570: "据下伏地层的时代推断喀拉扎组的时代应为晚侏罗世基默里奇期晚期" ["Judging from the age of the underlying strata, the age of the Kalaza Formation should be late Kimmeridgian of the Late Jurassic", translated].
- eberth2001a, p. 1630: the transition into the Qingshuihe Formation "suggests that the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary occurs at the base of or within the Kalaza Formation and that the Kalaza is not entirely Late Jurassic in age"; p. 1639: "At Manas, the basal conglomerate is regarded as latest Jurassic (Tithonian)".

**Ruled out.** - *Two units, one per basin.* No work read separates them; Upchurch and colleagues note only that the correlation is undemonstrated.
- *Upchurch and colleagues' (2004) Tithonian as a reading.* A table entry with no stated ground.

**Open.** A date or correlation for the Kalazha of the Turpan Basin.

## What do the cited works say about the Kalazha in the southern Junggar Basin?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Fang and colleagues (2015) list the Kalazha last in the Upper Jurassic of the southern Junggar Basin, above the Toutunhe and Qigu formations and below the Lower Cretaceous Tugulu Group, and give it no stage. Its thick, poorly sorted conglomerates are alluvial fan deposits that reworked the underlying sedimentary strata; they rest directly on fine-grained red beds and mark rapid tectonic uplift of the Tian Shan and rapid lake regression. Governs `period: [Late Jurassic]` on [Kalazha](../../../stratigraphy/k/kalazha.yml).

**Evidence.**
- fang2015a, p. 47: "By the time of deposition of the Kalazha Formation, Tian Shan experienced rapid tectonic uplift, leading to rapid lake regression."
- fang2015a, p. 50: the southern Junggar Basin Jurassic consists of "the Lower Jurassic Badaowan Formation, the Middle Jurassic Sangonghe and Xishanyao Foramtions, and the Upper Jurassic Toutunhe, Qigu and Kalazha Formations".
- fang2015a, p. 52: "Kalazha Formation is composed of thick, poorly sorted and matrix-supported conglomerates with a series of coarsening-upward sequences, consistent with multiple-phase alluvial fan deposits".
- fang2015a, p. 61: "alluvial fan conglomerates that reworked underlying sedimentary strata"; "The conglomerates directly overlie the fine-grained red beds, marking rapid tectonic uplift of the Tian Shan".

**Ruled out.** Nothing among the works cited here.

**Open.** Whether the Kalazha of the Junggar Basin, which Fang and colleagues describe, is the same unit as the Kalazha of the Turpan Basin that yielded *Hudiesaurus*. Upchurch and colleagues (2021) note that its correlation across the two basins has no explicit evidence behind it. A published correlation of the two basins' Upper Jurassic would settle it.

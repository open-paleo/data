# Kalazha

## What do the cited works say about the Kalazha in the southern Junggar Basin?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Fang and colleagues (2015) list the Kalazha last in the Upper Jurassic of the southern Junggar Basin, above the Toutunhe and Qigu formations and below the Lower Cretaceous Tugulu Group, and give it no stage. Its thick, poorly sorted conglomerates are alluvial fan deposits that reworked the underlying sedimentary strata; they rest directly on fine-grained red beds and mark rapid tectonic uplift of the Tian Shan and rapid lake regression. Governs `period: [Late Jurassic]` on [Kalazha](../../../stratigraphy/k/kalazha.yml).

**Evidence.**
- fang2015a, p. 47: "By the time of deposition of the Kalazha Formation, Tian Shan experienced rapid tectonic uplift, leading to rapid lake regression."
- fang2015a, p. 50: the southern Junggar Basin Jurassic consists of "the Lower Jurassic Badaowan Formation, the Middle Jurassic Sangonghe and Xishanyao Foramtions, and the Upper Jurassic Toutunhe, Qigu and Kalazha Formations".
- fang2015a, p. 52: "Kalazha Formation is composed of thick, poorly sorted and matrix-supported conglomerates with a series of coarsening-upward sequences, consistent with multiple-phase alluvial fan deposits".
- fang2015a, p. 61: "alluvial fan conglomerates that reworked underlying sedimentary strata"; "The conglomerates directly overlie the fine-grained red beds, marking rapid tectonic uplift of the Tian Shan".

**Ruled out.** Nothing among the works cited here.

**Open.** Whether the Kalazha of the Junggar Basin, which Fang and colleagues describe, is the same unit as the Kalazha of the Turpan Basin that yielded *Hudiesaurus*. Upchurch and colleagues (2021) note that its correlation across the two basins has no explicit evidence behind it. A published correlation of the two basins' Upper Jurassic would settle it.

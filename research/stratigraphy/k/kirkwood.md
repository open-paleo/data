# Kirkwood

## What do Muir and colleagues' Kirkwood lithostratigraphy (2017) and U–Pb study (2020) give for the age of the Kirkwood?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Aalenian to Early Cretaceous across its basins. Supersedes the Open line of the 2026-11-23 entry: the Tithonian–Valanginian comes from microfossils in the Algoa Basin, the Tithonian from microfossils in the Bethelsdorp and Colchester members, and the Lower Cretaceous top from a probable equivalence with the Sundays River Formation; the same paper says these ages may apply only to the type area. Muir and colleagues (2020) date nine pyroclastic beds in the Kirkwood Formation of the Robertson, Heidelberg, Mossel Bay, Vlees Bay, Oudtshoorn and Plettenberg Bay basins and read them as depositional ages. On the ICS chart v2024/12 their preferred ages fall in the Aalenian (172.6 Ma), Bajocian (170.5 Ma), Callovian (162.3 Ma), Oxfordian (156.4 Ma), Kimmeridgian (153.8 and 150.3 Ma) and Berriasian (139.8 Ma), and they describe deposition continuing "through the Jurassic and into the Early Cretaceous"; the Bathonian and Tithonian have no date but lie inside that span. The Hauterivian, from McPhee and colleagues (2016), is unchanged. Governs `period: [Middle Jurassic, Late Jurassic, Early Cretaceous]` and `stages`, Aalenian to Hauterivian, on [Kirkwood](../../../stratigraphy/k/kirkwood.yml).

**Evidence.**
- muir2017b, p. 281: "from the Tithonian to Valanginian. These microfossil-based ages may only apply to the unit in its type area in the Algoa Basin".
- muir2017b, p. 283: "Microfossil assemblages from the Bethelsdorp and Colchester Members in the lower part of the Kirkwood Formation of the Algoa Basin are dated as Tithonian (McMillan et al., 1997; McMillan, 2010)".
- muir2020a, p. 183: "Each are located within either the lacustrine or floodplain deposits of the Kirkwood Formation"; p. 184: "We therefore interpret ages extracted from these samples as direct depositional ages".
- muir2020a, Table 1, p. 183: preferred ages MATJ 172.6 +0.6/−0.9, HBMB 170.5 ± 0.6, HBUP 162.3 +0.7/−0.9, VOEL 156.4 +0.7/−0.9, CALI 153.8 ± 0.2, ROBE 150.3 ± 0.2 Ma.
- muir2020a, p. 186: "the Uitenhage Group deposition was well underway by the Aalenian"; "deposition continued from this time through the Jurassic and into the Early Cretaceous, with the PLET bentonite (139.8 ± 0.6 Ma)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Tithonian to Valanginian for the formation as a whole.* Muir and colleagues (2017) restrict it to the Algoa Basin, and their 2020 dates from the western basins are older.
- *Only the dated stages.* The paper describes continuous deposition across the span, so the undated Bathonian and Tithonian stay.

**Open.** Whether the Kirkwood of the Algoa Basin, where no pyroclastics have been dated, is older than the Tithonian. A radiometric date from the type area would settle it.

## What does the Enon Formation paper of Muir and colleagues (2017) say about the Kirkwood?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Muir and colleagues (2017) give the Kirkwood as "constrained to Tithonian – Valanginian in the Algoa Basin", without naming the work that constrains it. They use this age to infer a Cretaceous age for at least parts of the Enon Formation in that basin, because Dingle and colleagues (1983) hold the two formations to have been deposited at the same time. They report that preliminary U–Pb zircon ages from the Kirkwood's volcaniclastic layers range from Middle Jurassic to Early Cretaceous, and draw no age from them. Forster and colleagues (2023) repeat the Tithonian–Valanginian age, citing this paper, so it is one statement, not two. The paper also describes the contact: the mudstone- and sandstone-dominated Kirkwood conformably overlies the conglomeratic Enon, the contact is usually gradational, and the two interfinger locally. Governs the Tithonian and Valanginian in `stages` on [Kirkwood](../../../stratigraphy/k/kirkwood.yml).

**Evidence.**
- muir2017a, p. 274, Stratigraphic position and age: "Dingle et al. (1983) support the contemporaneity of deposition of the Enon and Kirkwood Formations, the latter of which has been constrained to Tithonian – Valanginian in the Algoa Basin and by inference suggests a Cretaceous age for at least parts of the Enon Formation in that area".
- muir2017a, p. 274, Stratigraphic position and age: "The Kirkwood Formation hosts datable volcaniclastic layers which may provide the best means to infer the age range of the Enon, but preliminary U-Pb analysis of zircon from these layers have yielded ages ranging from Middle Jurassic to Early Cretaceous".
- muir2017a, p. 278, Upper boundary: between the "generally resistant Enon and the overlying softer Kirkwood Formation", where it can be seen, "the contact is usually gradational".
- muir2017a, p. 278, Lateral boundary: "The Enon Formation, being diachronous, locally displays a laterally gradational, interfingering contact with the Kirkwood Formation"; the boundary is placed where "conglomerates, which are dominant in the Enon Formation, give way to sand- and mudstone dominated lithologies typical of the Kirkwood Formation".
- muir2017a, p. 278, Criteria for lateral extension: "The Enon Formation is conformably overlain by and interfingers with the Kirkwood Formation."
- forster2023a, p. 1764, Sedimentology and Stratigraphy of the Kirkwood Fm: "Muir et al. (2017b) assigned a Tithonian to Valanginian age for the entire Kirkwood Fm"; the paper's 2017b is the Enon Formation paper.

**Ruled out.**
- *Middle Jurassic, from the preliminary U–Pb zircon ages.* Muir and colleagues (2017) call the analyses preliminary, report them only as a span across the Kirkwood's volcaniclastic layers, and do not use them to date the formation. They add no epoch.

**Open.** The paper does not say which work constrained the Kirkwood to the Tithonian–Valanginian. The same authors' lithostratigraphy of the Kirkwood Formation, in the same journal issue, is the likely source and would show the basis of the Tithonian.

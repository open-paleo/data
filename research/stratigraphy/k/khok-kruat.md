# Khok Kruat

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Khok Kruat?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Early Cretaceous, with the Aptian as the stage the works most often give. Racey and colleagues (1996) hedge it as "?Aptian", resting on two abstracts by Sattayarak and colleagues (1991): unreleased Esso palynological data placing the upper part of the formation in the uppermost Aptian, and Albian–Cenomanian palynomorphs from the lower part of the overlying Maha Sarakham Formation. Shibata and colleagues (2011) consider the age undetermined, report the traditional Aptian–Albian from palynology, vertebrates and the age of the Maha Sarakham, and follow the Aptian. Jia and colleagues (2022) call the formation Upper Cretaceous once, in passing, while their stratigraphic summary gives it only as Cretaceous. Governs `period: [Early Cretaceous]` and the Aptian in `stages` on [Khok Kruat](../../../stratigraphy/k/khok-kruat.yml).

**Evidence.**
- racey1996a, p. 5, Abstract: the Phra Wihan, Sao Khua, Phu Phan and Khok Kruat formations "are reassigned to the Early Cretaceous (Berriasian to Aptian) on the basis of new palynological data".
- racey1996a, p. 29: "Sattayarak et al. (1991a) quoted unreleased Esso palynological data which assigned the upper part of the Khok Kruat Formation to the uppermost Aptian. The lower part of the overlying Maha Sarakham Fm has yielded Albian-Cenomanian palynomorphs (Sattayarak et al., 1991b)"; "the formation is suggested to be Lower Cretaceous (?Aptian) in age".
- shibata2011a, p. 970: "The age of this formation has not been determined yet because of lack of any crucial fossils to date. Hasegawa, et al. (2010) pointed out a possibility of Barremian age based on magnetostratigraphic data from the overlying Phu Thok Formation. However, no direct information from the Khok Kruat Formation was obtained for its age. Traditionally, a Aptian-Albian age is believed for the formation by means of palynology and vertebrate fossil records, and the age of overlying Maha Sarakham Formation"; "We follow the Aptian age determination in this study."
- hasegawa2010a, p. 607: in the Nakhon Thai sub-basin "the Phu Thok Formation overlies the fluvial-dominated Khok Kruat Formation"; p. 619: the Phu Thok is "from younger than late Barremian–early Aptian to older than early Cenomanian–middle Turonian".
- jia2022a, p. 3483: "the Cretaceous Sao Khua, Phu Phan and Khok Kruat formations develop in succession", the Maha Sarakham Formation lying on the Khok Kruat in angular unconformity, after Ridd and colleagues (2011) (translated).
- jia2022a, p. 3488: "the regional angular unconformity between the Upper Cretaceous Khok Kruat Formation and the Maha Sarakham Formation in the Nakhon Thai Basin" (translated), part of the argument that the remanence of the Nam Phong Formation is primary.

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Cretaceous.* Jia and colleagues' "Upper Cretaceous" (jia2022a, p. 3488) is a passing label in an argument about another formation's remanence; their stratigraphic summary gives only "Cretaceous" (p. 3483). A passing label is not a reading of the epoch (see [methods](../../methods.md)).

**Open.** Whether the Barremian is a reading. Shibata and colleagues relay it from the magnetostratigraphy of the overlying Phu Thok Formation as "a possibility" and add that it gives "no direct information" on the Khok Kruat (shibata2011a, p. 970); an age for the overlying unit is a limit on the Khok Kruat, not an age for it. Reading Hasegawa and colleagues (2010) in full, for whether they place the Khok Kruat in the Barremian themselves, would settle it.

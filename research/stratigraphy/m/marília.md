# Marília

## What do the cited works say about the definition and age of the Marília?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Maastrichtian, as the 2026-10-01 entry in [Marília and Serra da Galga formations](../../topics/marília-and-serra-da-galga.md) concludes. Soares and colleagues (1980) define the formation in the broad sense, with its type section at Marília and a reference section at Monte Alto, and Soares and colleagues (2020) restrict it to the southeastern and northwestern outcrops, the former Echaporã Member. Most of the material that dated the broad unit came from the northeastern outcrops now in the Serra da Galga, so the restricted Marília's Maastrichtian is carried over: Iori and colleagues (2021) assume it from the lateral relationship of the former members, and Arai and Dias-Brito (2023) infer it from the units' arrangement, the direct dating being from the Serra da Galga. Menegazzo and colleagues (2016) date the broad unit Maastrichtian on fish, charophytes and ostracods, after earlier ostracod and charophyte work, and call the *Aeolosaurus* evidence for a Campanian–Maastrichtian age inconclusive. Bertini and colleagues' (2001) late Campanian to early Maastrichtian rests on their own earlier abstracts reporting *Aeolosaurus*. Governs `stages: [Maastrichtian]` on [Marília](../../../stratigraphy/m/marília.yml).

**Evidence.**
- soares1980a, p. 182: "In this work the Marília Formation is defined as a unit composed of coarse to conglomeratic sandstones, with angular grains, variable matrix content, poor sorting" (translated); "The type section of the Marília Formation is located on the southern escarpment of the Marília Plateau, between the Rio do Peixe and the city of Marília", with "Another reference section presented is that of Monte Alto" (translated).
- soares2020a, p. 953 (early-view version, p. 3): "this work refers to the deposits of the Marília Formation exposed in the northeastern part of the Bauru Basin (traditionally subdivided in Serra da Galga and Ponte Alta members) as the Serra da Galga Formation".
- iori2021a, p. 2: "the Marília Formation was reduced to the area of occurrence of the Echaporã Member"; "the majority of species used to formerly establish the relative dating of Marília Formation comes from the deposits now recognized as the new unit Serra da Galga Formation. Hence, considering the lateral relationships among the three formerly known members, a similar age is assumed".
- iori2021a, p. 4, Locality and horizon: the Monte Alto site is in the "Marília Formation, Bauru Group, Bauru Basin, Upper Cretaceous (Maastrichtian)".
- arai2023a, p. 2, English abstract: "micropaleontology of charophytes and ostracods has indicated Maastrichtian (upper Maastrichtian?) strata in the Serra da Galga Formation".
- menegazzo2016a, p. 133: earlier work held that "the Marília Formation is Maastrichtian, considering the record of ostracods and charophytes", and gave a "Maastrichtian age for the Marília Formation by focusing on ostracods and charophytes".
- menegazzo2016a, p. 136: "The genus Aeolosaurus has been used to attribute Campanian–Maastrichtian to the Adamantina and Marília formations", but "the evidence of this genus in the Bauru Basin is inconclusive".
- bertini2001a, p. 93, Abstract: titanosaur remains from "the Marília Formation (Echaporã Member)" near Monte Alto "provide the possibility of correlations with Titanosauridae remains collected from the Serra da Galga Member of the Marília Formation".
- bertini2001a, p. 101: the caudal vertebrae "are contained in units of neo-Campanian/eo-Maastrichtian age" (translated), citing the authors' own 1999 and 2000 abstracts, which report *Aeolosaurus*.

**Ruled out.**
- *Bertini and colleagues' (2001) age as an independent dating.* It repeats the age the same authors had given in abstracts reporting *Aeolosaurus*, and so falls with the *Aeolosaurus* biochron.
- *The restricted Marília as dated on its own fossils.* None of the works cited dates the southeastern or northwestern outcrops on their own fossils or by radiometric means; Iori and colleagues assume the Serra da Galga's age, and Arai and Dias-Brito infer it from the units' arrangement.

**Open.** A biostratigraphic or radiometric age from the restricted Marília's own outcrops would test the Maastrichtian carried over from the Serra da Galga.

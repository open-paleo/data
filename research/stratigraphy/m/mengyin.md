# Mengyin

## How have the cited works dated the Mengyin?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The 2026-10-02 entry's Berriasian–Valanginian stands; this entry records the earlier readings the cited works report and how the zircon bracket falls on the chart. Wiman (1929) put the "Meng-Yin-Series" in the Lower Cretaceous, relaying an earlier survey's cautious estimate. Most authors then accepted an early Tithonian age from the dinosaurs and conchostracans, and Barrett and Wang (2007) inferred a possible Aptian age from teeth; Wilson and Upchurch (2009) and Moore and colleagues (2020) review that history. Xu and Li (2015) report that the biostratigraphic age was disputed among the Late Jurassic, the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary and the Early Cretaceous before the formation came to be regarded as Early Cretaceous, and call it lowermost Cretaceous. On ICS chart v2024/12 the central value of their 145 ± 4 Ma zircon floor falls just before the base of the Berriasian at 143.1 Ma, and the 135.6 ± 3.9 Ma volcanic age above is Valanginian, so the bracket runs from the Berriasian to the Valanginian. Tong and colleagues (2021) read it as "Berriasian–lower Valanginian" and correlate the Luohandong Formation of the Ordos Basin with the Mengyin on their turtles. Governs `stages: [Berriasian, Valanginian]` on [Mengyin](../../../stratigraphy/m/mengyin.yml).

**Evidence.**
- wiman1929a, p. ? (translated): the earlier survey "made only a very cautious estimate of age determinations"; "Following T'an, the dinosaurs come from three different formations: 1. The Meng-Yin-Series is from the Lower Cretaceous".
- wilson2009a, p. 203: the formation "was originally considered to be Early Cretaceous (?Neocomian) in age by Wiman (1929). Subsequently, a Late Jurassic age (early Tithonian) was suggested on the basis of the dinosaurian fauna" and conchostracans, "which has been accepted by most authors"; Barrett and Wang's teeth "led them to infer a possible Aptian age for the Mengyin Formation".
- xu2015b, in-press version, p. 1: "the lowermost Cretaceous Mengyin Formation".
- xu2015b, in-press version, p. 3: "disputed biostratigraphic ages of Late Jurassic …, Late Jurassic–Early Cretaceous …, and Early Cretaceous"; after the redefinition of the Eosestheria and Jehol Biota ages, the formation "is currently regarded to be deposited during the Early Cretaceous".
- moore2020a, accepted manuscript, p. 79: the formation "has historically been considered Late Jurassic (early Tithonian) to Early Cretaceous in age (as late as Aptian; see review in Wilson & Upchurch 2009)".
- tong2021a, p. 14, 5. Turtles from the Phu Kradung Formation and Their Implications for the Age of the Formation: the formation, "previously considered as Late Jurassic by some authors", "has been recently dated using detrital zircons as 145–136 Ma, corresponding to the Berriasian–lower Valanginian"; "the Luohandong Formation … is correlated with the Mengyin Formation"; "The Sinemys–Ordosemys assemblage seems to be typical of the basal Cretaceous formations such as the Mengyin and Luohandong Formations".
- tong2021a, Table 1: Ordosemys leios, "Mengyin Fm. Early Cretaceous (Berriasian–Valanginian)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Early Tithonian.* The age most authors once accepted, from the dinosaur fauna and conchostracans, and still cited from Dong (1992) by Tong and colleagues (2021) as an earlier view; Xu and Li (2015) report the biostratigraphy as since revised, and the zircon bracket postdates it.
- *A possible Aptian age.* Barrett and Wang (2007) inferred it from Euhelopus-like teeth in the Yixian Formation, a faunal comparison that the zircon bracket postdates.

**Open.** T'an (1923), the survey Wiman follows for the unit's name and its Lower Cretaceous age, has not been read.

## How old is the Mengyin Formation?

*2026-10-02*

**Conclusion.** Berriasian–Valanginian, between its youngest detrital zircons and the oldest overlying eruption age. Governs `stages: [Berriasian, Valanginian]` on Mengyin; the overlying [Qingshan Group](../../../stratigraphy/q/qingshan.yml) supplies the ceiling.

**Evidence.**
- xu2015b, in-press version, p. 3: youngest detrital zircons "weighted average age of 145 ± 4 Ma"; the overlying "maximum U–Pb zircon age … is 135.6 ± 3.9 Ma (Zhu et al., 2010)"; the formation "constrained to 145–136 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *144–130.7 Ma.* Moore and colleagues write "the Mengyin Formation is 144–130.7Ma (J. Xu & Li 2015)" (moore2020a, accepted manuscript, p. 79), which differs from Xu and Li's bracket; 130.7 Ma is probably 132.7 − 2.0, a detrital maximum of an overlying member (our inference).
- *Tithonian, Hauterivian, Barremian, Aptian.* These rested on readings superseded by the bracket above.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Mtuka

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Mtuka?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Aptian to Cenomanian, from the magnetostratigraphy of Widlansky and colleagues (2018). About 150 m of the member preserve dominantly normal polarity, consistent with deposition during the Cretaceous Normal Superchron, and the member lies below the Namba Member, which the same study places in the Cenomanian–Campanian. Gorscak and O'Connor (2019) repeat the Aptian–Cenomanian from that study, so it counts once. The Late Cretaceous in `period` comes from the Cenomanian end of that range, not from the paleomagnetic poles. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian, Cenomanian]` and `period: [Early Cretaceous, Late Cretaceous]` on [Mtuka](../../../stratigraphy/m/mtuka.yml).

**Evidence.**
- widlansky2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 2, Abstract: the Namba Member is "no older than Late Cretaceous (Cenomanian-Campanian), with the Mtuka Member less well constrained to the middle Cretaceous (Aptian-Cenomanian)".
- widlansky2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 27, 5.1 Correlation to the Geomagnetic Polarity Timescale (GPTS): "The relatively long (~150 m) exposure of the Mtuka Member sampled for this study preserves dominantly normal polarity throughout, and is consistent with deposition during the CNS, between approximately 121 Ma and 83 Ma"; slow accumulation "increases the chance of missing a reversed interval in a sampling gap".
- widlansky2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 35, 5.3 Cretaceous Paleobiogeography Implications: "The Mtuka Member is likely Aptian – Cenomanian given its normal polarity and stratigraphic position below the Namba Member".
- widlansky2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 36, 6. Conclusions: "The underlying Mtuka Member is dominated by normal polarity, consistent with deposition in the lower portion of the CNS (Aptian – Cenomanian)"; the poles calculated for the Namba and Mtuka members "are also most consistent with a Late Cretaceous (Cenomanian or younger) age".
- widlansky2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 26, 4.6 Paleomagnetic Pole: the poles for the Namba (α95 5.9°) and Mtuka (α95 11.1°) members "are most similar to the poles from the 60 – 70 Ma age range", but "there is considerable overlap with a wide range in ages between 100 Ma and 30 Ma"; "As a whole, the paleomagnetic poles are most consistent with a Late Cretaceous age for the Namba and Mtuka members" (continued on p. 27).
- gorscak2019a, p. 6, Age and distribution: "new paleomagnetic data place the Mtuka Member ... within the Cretaceous long normal with estimates of Aptian–Cenomanian for the unit", citing Widlansky and colleagues.

**Ruled out.**
- *A Late Cretaceous age for the whole member from the paleomagnetic poles.* Widlansky and colleagues compute a pole for each member and find both closest to the Late Cretaceous path, but they note overlap with ages from 100 to 30 Ma, the Mtuka pole has the wider confidence circle (α95 11.1°), and their own age for the member rests on polarity and position, Aptian–Cenomanian. The poles are the only place the study draws the Mtuka into a Late Cretaceous reading.
- *"No older than Late Cretaceous (Cenomanian–Campanian)" for the Mtuka.* The phrase belongs to the overlying Namba Member; the same sentence gives the Mtuka "middle Cretaceous (Aptian-Cenomanian)".

**Open.** The Aptian floor rests on normal polarity alone, and Widlansky and colleagues note that a reversed interval could fall in a sampling gap. A radioisotopic date from the member would settle it.

# Monmouth

## What do the cited works say about the age and rank of the Monmouth?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** A group, Maastrichtian, after Gallagher (1993). Gallagher's lithostratigraphy runs the Mount Laurel, Navesink, Red Bank, Tinton and New Egypt formations under a Monmouth Group heading, and its Table 1 sets the group against the Maastrichtian; its outcrop map calls it Upper Cretaceous. The older works cited use formation rank: White (1973) and Sohl (1960), and Prieto-Márquez and colleagues (2006) for the *Ornithotarsus* type. Governs `rank: group`, `period: [Late Cretaceous]` and `stages: [Maastrichtian]` on [Monmouth](../../../stratigraphy/m/monmouth.yml).

**Evidence.**
- gallagher1993a, p. 80, Table 1, "Chronostratigraphic and lithostratigraphic units of the New Jersey Coastal Plain": Upper Cretaceous, Maastrichtian, Monmouth Group, with the Tinton, Red Bank, New Egypt, Navesink and Mount Laurel formations; the Matawan Group below is Campanian.
- gallagher1993a, p. 81: under "MONMOUTH GROUP", the Mount Laurel Formation's "AGE—Late Campanian (Owens et al., 1970; Olsson, 1989); early Maastrichtian (Olsson, 1975; Petters, 1976; Jordan and Smith, 1983)".
- gallagher1993a, p. 82, Fig. 4: "Outcrop belt of Monmouth Group, Upper Cretaceous, New Jersey".
- prieto-márquez2006a, p. 79: *O. immanis* "was based on a partial tibia, fibula, astragalus, and calcaneum from the Monmouth Formation near Keyport, New Jersey".
- prieto-márquez2006a, p. 91: the type's horizon was questionably assigned "to the Campanian Woodbury Formation", while "both Cope (1869) and Lull and Wright (1942) stated that the bones were discovered in the shores of Raritan Bay ..., from clays belonging to the Lower Cretaceous".

**Ruled out.**
- *An Early Cretaceous Monmouth.* "Lower Cretaceous" is the age that nineteenth- and early twentieth-century authors gave the *Ornithotarsus* bones, as Prieto-Márquez and colleagues (2006) report it, not a dating of the unit, and no work cited dates the Monmouth to the Early Cretaceous. Gallagher (1993) places the group in the Upper Cretaceous. The Woodbury assignment concerns which formation the bones came from, not the Monmouth's age; it is weighed in [*Ornithotarsus*](../../genera/O/Ornithotarsus.md).

**Open.** Whether the group reaches the Campanian. Gallagher (1993) relays a late Campanian age for the Mount Laurel, its lowest formation, from Owens and colleagues (1970) and Olsson (1989), and gives the Navesink as "Late Campanian to early Maastrichtian (Owens et al., 1970)" (p. 82); in its own voice it puts the *Exogyra cancellata* zone of the Mount Laurel at "the Campanian-Maastrichtian boundary" (p. 86). Owens and colleagues (1970) and Olsson (1989) have not been read; they would show whether the late Campanian is a dating of the group's base.

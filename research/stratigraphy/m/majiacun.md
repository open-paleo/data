# Majiacun

## Does the Majiacun keep the Cenomanian?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No; the Cenomanian is dropped. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry on the stages and answers its Open line. Lü and colleagues (2010) credit "Cenomanian to Turonian" to Zhou and colleagues (1983), but Zhou and colleagues give the formation "相当于土仑期至赛诺早期" (Turonian to early Senonian) and never name the Cenomanian; traced to its original, the relay misreports its source and adds nothing. Zhou and colleagues' own reading keeps the Turonian, and their early Senonian needs no conversion, since later readings already cover the Coniacian onward. Wang and colleagues (2006), whom Lü and colleagues cite for a Coniacian–Santonian correlation, are now cited on the unit. Governs `stages: [Turonian, Coniacian, Santonian, Campanian, Maastrichtian]` and the notes on zhou1983a and wang2006a on [Majiacun](../../../stratigraphy/m/majiacun.yml); its genera, all Coniacian or younger, are unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- lü2010a, p. 387: "the Majiacun Formation was described as Cenoma− nian to Turonian (Zhou et al. 1983) in age on the bases of spores and pollens, and dinosaur egg fossils"; "Later, Wang et al. (2006) thought that the Majiacun Formation correlated with the Coniacian–Santonian."
- zhou1983a, p. 69: "该组时代以归入晚白垩世早—中期较为适宜，相当于土仑期至赛诺早期。" ["it is more appropriate to assign this formation to the early–middle Late Cretaceous, corresponding to the Turonian to early Senonian", translated].

**Ruled out.**
- *Cenomanian from Lü and colleagues (2010).* A misrelay of Zhou and colleagues (1983).

**Open.** Wang and colleagues (2006) has not been read.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Majiacun Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The Cenomanian–Maastrichtian in `stages` is the union of three readings that Lü and colleagues (2010) review: "Cenomanian to Turonian", which they attribute to Zhou and colleagues (1983) on spores, pollen and dinosaur eggs; Coniacian to Campanian from bivalves (Chen and colleagues 2007); and Santonian to Maastrichtian from gastropods (Pan and colleagues 2007). Lü and colleagues give the formation itself as Coniacian–Campanian, with the *Xixiasaurus* type horizon in its mid-lower part, and infer from the troodontid's affinity with *Byronosaurus* that the formation "may be Campanian". The bivalve and gastropod readings match their originals. Zhou and colleagues' own text does not match the relay: it places the Majiacun in the early–middle Late Cretaceous, "corresponding to the Turonian to early Senonian" (see Open). Governs `stages` on the [Majiacun Formation](../../../stratigraphy/m/majiacun.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lü2010a, p. 382, Type horizon: "Mid-lower part of the Majiacun Formation (Coniacian–Campanian)".
- lü2010a, p. 387, Geological age of the Majiacun Formation: "the Majiacun Formation was described as Cenomanian to Turonian (Zhou et al. 1983) in age on the bases of spores and pollens, and dinosaur egg fossils"; "Based on studies of bivalves and gastropods, the Majiacun Formation is regarded as Coniacian to Campanian (Chen et al. 2007), and Santonian to Maastrichtian, respectively (Pan et al. 2007)"; "Thus it is inferred that the geological age of the Majiacun Formation may be Campanian".
- zhou1983a, p. 69 (translated): "taking the stratigraphic position into account, it is more appropriate to assign this formation to the early–middle Late Cretaceous, corresponding to the Turonian to early Senonian".
- chen2007a, p. 305 (translated): "the dinosaur-egg-bearing middle-lower Gaogou Formation and middle Majiacun Formation of the Xixia Basin, Henan, cannot be Early Cretaceous but should be middle-late Late Cretaceous (Coniacian–Campanian)".
- pan2007a, p. 298: the Xixia Basin gastropods, including *Mesolanistes efremovi* from the Majiacun Formation, are "closely comparable to those from the Nemeget Formation and the Honglishan Formation, which is generally considered to be of Santonian—Maastrichitian stage".

**Ruled out.**
- *Campanian alone, from Lü and colleagues' inference.* It rests on the age of a related troodontid from elsewhere, not on fossils or dates from the Majiacun, and the same paper gives the formation as Coniacian–Campanian in its type-horizon line.

**Open.** The Cenomanian. Its only source among the works cited is Lü and colleagues' relay of Zhou and colleagues (1983), whose Majiacun section (pp. 68–69) gives Turonian to early Senonian. Whether the Cenomanian stays, and how far "early Senonian" reaches on the GTS2020 chapters, needs a decision.

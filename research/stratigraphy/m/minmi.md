# Minmi

## What is the age of the Minmi Member of the Bungil Formation?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Early Aptian; its base may reach the latest Neocomian. Governs `stages: [Aptian]` on Minmi, the Bungil Formation's Valanginian–Aptian range on [Bungil](../../../stratigraphy/b/bungil.yml), and `stage: [Aptian]` on [*Minmi paravertebra*](../../../genera/M/Minmi.yml).

**Evidence.**
- exon1976a, p. 110: the Bungil "includes the Kingull, Nullawurt Sandstone, and Minmi Members of Day (1964), and the Claravale Sandstone Member of Mollan et al. (1972)"; p. 112: the Minmi "rests on the Nullawurt Sandstone Member".
- exon1976a, p. 115: the Minmi "is of early Aptian age (Day, 1969; Appendix 1)"; "The age of the formation is thus, on fossil evidence, Neocomian to early Aptian."
- burger1980a, p. 11: "The Minmi Member, dated as lower Aptian, is restricted to the Surat Basin."

**Ruled out.**
- *Valanginian–Barremian.* Leahey and colleagues (2015) pair the Minmi Member with "(Valanginian–Barremian)" in their Fig. 1 caption (leahey2015a, p. 3), while their introduction gives "the lower Aptian (Burger, 1980 …) Minmi Member" (p. 2). Agnolín and colleagues (2010) give "Bungil Formation (Barremian-Valanginian: Early Cretaceous; Scheibner & Basden 1998)" in their systematics (agnolín2010a, p. 263) and "(Bungil Formation, Aptian)" in their Fig. 1 caption (p. 258). Both papers contradict themselves; the Barremian and Valanginian readings are formation-wide ranges, and the member-level primaries above give early Aptian.
- *116–135 Ma.* Arbour and colleagues (2015) write that "Raza et al. (2009) suggested an age of 116–135 Ma for the Bungil Formation" (arbour2015a, p. 41). Raza and colleagues give it in a sample table, "ca 116–135" for the Bungil Formation, with ages footnoted to Fielding and colleagues (1990) (raza2009a, p. 504, Table 1): a compiled stratigraphic age for a fission-track sample, not a new date.

**Open.** The base of the member. Its lower part falls in the *Murospora florida* Zone with the older members (exon1976a, p. 115, after Burger 1974), so the member's base may reach the latest Neocomian; that extension is an inference.

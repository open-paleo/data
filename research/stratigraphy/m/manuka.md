# Manuka

## What do the cited works say about the composition and age of the Manuka Subgroup?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Albian to Turonian. Vine and colleagues (1967) erect the subgroup for the Mackunda and Winton formations, the beds of labile sandstone above the Wilgunya Subgroup, and place the two subgroups together in the Rolling Downs Group; they give the subgroup no age. Gray and colleagues (2002) keep that composition and date both formations on spore-pollen assemblages of units APK6 and APK7: the Mackunda late Albian to early Cenomanian, the Winton late Albian(?) to Cenomanian. Tucker and colleagues (2013) repeat the composition after Gray and colleagues, and from detrital zircons place the base of the Winton no earlier than latest Albian and let its upper part continue into or past the early Turonian. The Turonian rests on maximum depositional ages from the upper Winton, a floor on its age, some of them from single grains. Governs `period` and `stages: [Albian, Cenomanian, Turonian]` on [Manuka](../../../stratigraphy/m/manuka.yml).

**Evidence.**
- vine1967a, p. 148: "The overlying Mackunda and Winton Formations are characterized by the presence of beds of labile sandstone, deposition of which resulted from a major influx of volcanic detritus. For this sequence we propose the term Manuka Sub-group. The name is derived from Manuka Station, 55 miles NNE of Winton"; "Together the Wilgunya and Manuka Sub-groups comprise the Rolling Downs Group of Whitehouse (1954)."
- gray2002a, p. 53: the Mackunda and Winton formations "were made part of the newly-proposed Manuka Subgroup (Vine & others, 1967)"; "The Wilgunyah and Manuka Subgroups constitute the Rolling Downs Group of Whitehouse (1955)."
- gray2002a, p. 54: "The Mackunda Formation embraces mid-Cretaceous (late Albian–early Cenomanian) spore-pollen assemblages".
- gray2002a, p. 55: "Mid-Cretaceous [late Albian(?)–Cenomanian] palynofloras of the Winton Formation encompass spore-pollen assemblages attributable to palynological units APK6 and APK7".
- tucker2013a, p. 768: Gray and colleagues (2002) "grouped the Winton together with the Mackunda Formation to form the Manuka Subgroup".
- tucker2013a, p. 767, Abstract: "sedimentation of the Winton Formation commenced no earlier than latest Albian (~103.0–100.5 Ma)".
- tucker2013a, p. 774: "the upper Winton Formation very likely continued into or past the early Turonian"; of the youngest single-grain ages, "these ages are based on single grains and may be erroneous".

**Ruled out.** None. No work cited gives the subgroup an age outside the Albian to Turonian.

**Open.** The Turonian depends on maximum depositional ages, some from single grains that Tucker and colleagues (2013) say "may be erroneous"; a younger population confirmed by further zircon analyses, or a direct date in the upper Winton, would settle it. Vine and colleagues (1967) give no age for the subgroup.

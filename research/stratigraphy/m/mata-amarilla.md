# Mata Amarilla

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Mata Amarilla?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian to Santonian, on the dating of Varela and colleagues (2012), which Sickmann and colleagues (2018) contest. Varela and colleagues date a tuff 29 m above the base of the middle section at 96.2 ± 0.7 Ma, middle Cenomanian (Cenomanian on the ICS chart v2024/12 as well), and give the formation as Cenomanian to Santonian; Novas and colleagues (2019) date it Cenomanian on that tuff and keep it apart from the Campanian Cerro Fortaleza. Sickmann and colleagues fold the outcrops mapped as Mata Amarilla between Lago Viedma, Lago Argentino and Tres Lagos into the Cerro Fortaleza Formation and date them Campanian to Maastrichtian. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian, Coniacian, Santonian]` on [Mata Amarilla](../../../stratigraphy/m/mata-amarilla.yml).

**Evidence.**
- varela2012a, p. 359, Abstract: "The chronological data provided by invertebrates, dinosaurs, fish, turtles, plesiosaurs and fossil flora are contradictory"; "twenty U-Pb spot analyses by laser ablation were carried out on the outer parts of the zoned zircon crystals from a tuff layer of the middle section of the Mata Amarilla Formation, yielding a U-Pb concordia age of 96.23±0.71 Ma, which corresponds to the middle Cenomanian".
- varela2012a, p. 364: the formation "was deposited during the early Upper Cretaceous, extending from the Cenomanian to the Santonian".
- varela2012a, p. 367: the dinosaurs "were assigned to the Maastrichtian following the stratigraphic criteria of Kraemer and Riccardi (1997)".
- varela2012a, p. 368: the sample came from "a primary tuff located 29 m above the point of contact between the lower and middle sections of the Mata Amarilla Formation, at the Estancia Mata Amarilla".
- varela2012a, p. 372: the onset of the foreland stage, with the lower section, "is located within the range between the lower Cenomanian and the upper Albian (~100 Ma), since the Piedra Clavada Formation, underlying the Mata Amarilla Formation, is lower Albian"; p. 374: "the lower section of the Mata Amarilla Formation (upper Albian-lower Cenomanian, ~100 Ma)".
- novas2019a, p. 274: "The stratigraphic correlations among these continental beds have been, and still are, a theme of intense debate among geologists"; "Available radimetric dating shows that the Mata Amarilla Formation is Cenomanian in age (approximately 97my; Varela et al., 2012), Cerro Fortaleza Formation is Campanian (around 76.2Ma, based on studies by Sickman et al., 2018)"; "Mata Amarilla, in proximities of the Sheuen River, afforded carcharodontosaurid remains" that "are congruent with a Cenomanian age obtained by radiometric studies".
- novas2019a, p. 275: of the dinosaur assemblages of southwestern Santa Cruz, "the oldest ones corresponding to the Cenomanian Mata Amarilla Formation".
- sickmann2018a, p. ? (accepted manuscript, p. 5): "This final delineation 'Cerro Fortaleza Formation' includes rocks that have sometimes been named 'Mata Amarilla Formation'".
- sickmann2018a, p. ? (accepted manuscript, p. 8): "Thirteen detrital zircon samples and new mapping from this study" provide "a multi-sample check on the terrestrial stratigraphy defined in the Fluvial Model as Cenomanian".
- sickmann2018a, p. ? (accepted manuscript, pp. 15–16): "the Cenomanian tuff age of Varela et al. (2012) is not viable for the terrestrial deposits of the eastern part of the Austral outcrop belt for four reasons", among them that "The presence of Cenomanian deep-marine facies between the terrestrial deposits in question and the southern Andes" would leave no route for rivers from the west.
- sickmann2018a, p. ? (accepted manuscript, p. 16): "this age range may approach Santonian to Coniacian, but likely does not approach Cenomanian anywhere in the study area".
- sickmann2018a, p. ? (accepted manuscript, p. 18): "Our redefinition of the age of the terrestrial deposits in question from Cenomanian—Santonian to Campanian—Maastrichtian".
- sickmann2018a, p. ? (accepted manuscript, p. 19): "the terrestrial deposits in the Austral Sector of the MAB in the vicinity of Lago Viedma and Lago Argentino east to the town of Tres Lagos are no older than Campanian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian, for the dinosaurs.* Varela and colleagues (2012) list it among the contradictory data that the tuff date was meant to resolve; it rests on Kraemer and Riccardi's (1997) stratigraphic criteria, not on a date, and the dinosaurs it dated are now placed in the Cerro Fortaleza (see [Cerro Fortaleza](../c/cerro-fortaleza.md)).

**Open.** Whether the rocks dated by Varela and colleagues (2012) are Cenomanian or no older than Campanian. Sickmann and colleagues (2018) reject the tuff age for the deposits east to Tres Lagos, the area of the dated tuff, while Novas and colleagues (2019) keep the Cenomanian for the Mata Amarilla and the Campanian for the Cerro Fortaleza; Novas and colleagues cite both papers without taking up Sickmann and colleagues' four reasons, and a new date from the type area would settle it. Varela and colleagues also put the start of the lower section in the "upper Albian-lower Cenomanian, ~100 Ma", between the lower Albian Piedra Clavada Formation below and the 96.2 Ma tuff above; no entry says whether that bracket adds the Albian.

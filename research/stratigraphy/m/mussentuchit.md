# Mussentuchit

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Mussentuchit Member?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Early Cenomanian. Every radiometric date on the member's own ashes falls between about 98.5 and 96.7 Ma, inside the Cenomanian on the ICS chart v2024/12 (base 100.5 Ma). Cifelli and colleagues (1997, 1999) give 98.37 ± 0.07 Ma, which Kirkland and Madsen (2007) and Sprinkel and colleagues (2012) relay. Garrison and colleagues (2007) give 98.2 ± 0.6 Ma for the base and 97.2 ± 0.6 Ma near the top. Kirkland and colleagues (2016) read the dates as "an interval of 1.5 Ma during the early Cenomanian". The Albian in older work is the same rocks under an older boundary. The palynomorph Albian predates the lower placement of the Albian–Cenomanian boundary (Kirkland and Madsen, 2007; Kirkland and colleagues, 2016). Sprinkel and colleagues' "latest Albian to earliest Cenomanian" and Garrison and colleagues' "at or near the Albian/Cenomanian boundary" are labels for dates that are Cenomanian on the current chart. Sprinkel and colleagues relay Garrison and colleagues' dates as 98.5 ± 0.06 and 97.2 ± 0.06 Ma on sanidine, where Garrison and colleagues print 98.2 ± 0.6 and 97.2 ± 0.6 Ma on plagioclase; either set is Cenomanian. Sprinkel and colleagues equate the member in its type area, on the southwest side of the San Rafael Swell, with the Mowry Shale of Wyoming, and would not apply the name to the top of the Cedar Mountain Formation in northeastern Utah. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian]` on [Mussentuchit](../../../stratigraphy/m/mussentuchit.yml).

**Evidence.**
- kirkland2007a, p. 17: "A radiometric age of 98.37 + 0.07 million years, obtained by the Oklahoma Museum of Natural History (OMNH) from volcanic ash within the Mussentuchit Member, indicates an early Cenomanian age (Cifelli and others, 1997, 1999)"; the palynomorph assemblage "was considered to indicate an Albian age (Tschudy and others, 1984), prior to the lower placement of the Albian-Cenomanian boundary based on ammonite correlations with the type Cenomanian in Europe".
- kirkland2016a, p. 155: "Additional ages by Garrison and others (2007) ranging from 96.7 ± 0.5 to 98.2 ±0.6 Ma indicate that the Mussentuchit Member was deposited over an interval of 1.5 Ma during the early Cenomanian"; p. 157 repeats the palynomorph sentence, "prior to the lower placement of the Albian-Cenomanian boundary based on ammonite correlations with the type Cenomanian in Europe"; p. 175: the faunal change in the member falls "at the base of the Cenomanian of Utah at about 100 to 98 Ma".
- garrison2007a, p. 476: "The new 40Ar/39Ar ages for plagioclase crystals range in age from 96.7 ± 0.5 to 98.2 ± 0.6 Ma"; "establishing the age of the base of the Mussentuchit Member as 98.2 ± 0.6 Ma"; "This establishes the upper age limit of the Mussentuchit Member as 97.2 ± 0.6 Ma, implying that the boundary between the Cedar Mountain Formation and the overlying Dakota Formation is at or near the Albian/Cenomanian boundary".
- sprinkel2012a, p. 2: "Radiometric dates indicate a latest Albian to earliest Cenomanian age for the capping Mussentuchit Member".
- sprinkel2012a, p. 12: sanidine ages "obtained from the lower part of the Mussentuchit Member ... are 98.37 ± 0.07 Ma (Cifelli and others, 1997; 1999) and 98.5 ± 0.06 Ma (Garrison and others, 2007)"; "A radiometric (40Ar/39Ar sanidine) age of 97.2 ± 0.06 Ma was obtained from near the top of the Mussentuchit Member (Garrison and others, 2007)".
- sprinkel2012a, p. 13: "the Mussentuchit Member in its type area on the southwest side of the San Rafael Swell is equivalent to the Mowry Shale in Wyoming"; "the Mussentuchit Member should not be applied to the uppermost part of the Cedar Mountain Formation in northeastern Utah".
- mori2009a, p. 7: the member's "age is reported to be early Cenomanian based on sanidine 40Ar/39Ar ages (Cifelli et al., 1997; Garrison et al., 2007)".
- mori2009a, p. 30: "the Mussentuchit Member ranges from about 109 - 105 Ma to 99 to 96 Ma (mid-Albian to mid-Cenomanian) and perhaps as young as 91 Ma (Turonian)"; from young detrital ages in the overlying Dakota Sandstone, "the Mussentuchit Member, if not the Mussentuchit fauna itself, may range to late Cenomanian or early Turonian. These anomalously young ages need to checked in a future study".
- kirkland2016a, pp. 118–119, on the Yellow Cat Member: "Mori (2009) reported a number of additional U-Pb ages from detrital zircons at several other sites, but stratigraphic misinterpretations relative to the stratigraphic framework presented herein and limited sample sizes raise some questions as to the accuracy of those results".

**Ruled out.**
- *Latest Albian.* Sprinkel and colleagues (2012) and Garrison and colleagues (2007) place the member, or its top, at the Albian–Cenomanian boundary on dates of 98.5 to 96.7 Ma. These are measured radiometric dates above the current base of the Cenomanian at 100.5 Ma, so the Albian label is an older chart's (see [methods](../../methods.md), on a 97–99.5 Ma "Albian"). The palynomorph Albian predates the lower placement of the boundary.
- *A mid-Albian base and a Turonian top (Mori, 2009).* The span rests on the youngest detrital zircon ages. Those are maximum depositional ages, which bound the member from below rather than date it, and the same thesis reports the sanidine dates as early Cenomanian. The Turonian rests on anomalously young detrital ages from the overlying Dakota that Mori says need checking. A thesis does not carry a value alone (see [methods](../../methods.md)). Kirkland and colleagues' (2016) doubts about Mori's results come in their discussion of the Yellow Cat Member and concern ages "at several other sites", so they are not counted against Mori's Mussentuchit sections; the reading fails on the grounds above.

**Open.** Why Sprinkel and colleagues' figures for Garrison and colleagues' dates (98.5 ± 0.06 and 97.2 ± 0.06 Ma, sanidine) differ from those Garrison and colleagues print (98.2 ± 0.6 and 97.2 ± 0.6 Ma, plagioclase). The difference does not change the stage.

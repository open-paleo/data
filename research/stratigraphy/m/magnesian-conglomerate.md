# Magnesian Conglomerate

## Is "Dolomitic Conglomerate" a name for the Magnesian Conglomerate?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** Yes; it is added to the variants. Galton (2007), relaying Moore (1881), and Foffa and colleagues (2014) print it for the Clifton breccia's host unit, as Owen (1842) and Etheridge (1870) did. ## What do Owen (1842) and Galton (2007) say about the Magnesian Conglomerate's names, age and outcrop at Clifton?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Adds to the 2026-10-14 entry without changing it. Owen (1842) uses two names for the one unit: "dolomitic conglomerate" where he describes *Thecodontosaurus* and the two *Palaeosaurus* teeth from the same formation, and "Magnesian conglomerate" where he discusses the order in which reptiles appear. He places it in the oldest division of the New Red Sandstone and ranks its thecodonts with the monitors of the Thuringian Zechstein as older than the Keuper labyrinthodonts. Galton (2007) explains how that age attached to the Clifton bones. The Durdham Down deposit was long equated with the nearby Magnesian Conglomerate and so taken for the "lower division of the New Red Sandstone", which he glosses as Permian; Etheridge (1870) equated both with the Muschelkalk or Lower Keuper. Galton follows the summary of Benton and colleagues (2000) in placing the bone bed outside the conglomerate (see the 2026-09-20 and 2026-10-14 entries). He also identifies the two outliers of the conglomerate that Sanders (1862) mapped, as N.R.S., in northern Clifton with the two quarries on Lavars's (1875) map, and puts the quarry in Clifton rather than in Redland, where Owen and others placed it. Governs `period: [Late Triassic]` and the absent `stages` on [Magnesian Conglomerate](../../../stratigraphy/m/magnesian-conglomerate.yml).

**Evidence.**
- owen1842a, p. 153: "In the dolomitic conglomerate at Redland near Bristol, a formation considered to belong to the oldest or lowest division of the new red sandstone series".
- owen1842a, p. 154: "In the formation which contained the jaw and teeth of the Thecodontosaurus, two other teeth were separately discovered", named *Palaeosaurus platyodon* and *P. cylindrodon*.
- owen1842a, pp. 197–198: "The Monitors of the Thuringian Zechstein are older than the Labyrinthodonts of the Keuper; and among British Reptiles, the thecodont Lizards of the Magnesian conglomerate have equal claims to a more ancient origin."
- galton2007a, p. 514: the Durdham Down deposit "was long equated with the Magnesian Conglomerate, which occurs very close by" and "originally thought to represent the 'lower division of the New Red Sandstone' (so Permian"; "Etheridge (1870) equated both with the German Muschelkalk or Lower Keuper (Middle Triassic)".
- galton2007a, p. 514: "Sanders (1862), showed two outliers of the Magnesian Conglomerate (as N.R.S., for New Red Sandstone) in the northern part of Clifton and these agree with the location of the two quarries shown by Lavars (1875"; the quarries are "in Clifton (then in Somersetshire), not in Redland", a mistake "repeated by subsequent authors (Owen, 1841, 1842a".
- galton2007a, p. 515: Benton and colleagues (2000) "conclude that it represent a Triassic (Rheatian) dolomitized cave breccia or fissure fill (not in Magnesian/ Dolomitic Conglomerate".

**Ruled out.**
- *Permian, from Owen's "oldest or lowest division of the new red sandstone series".* It is the age the Clifton bones took from their old equation with the conglomerate, which Galton (2007, p. 514) records as replaced by Etheridge's Triassic correlation. None of the later works cited dates the unit below the Late Triassic.
- *A Middle Triassic age from Etheridge (1870).* The 2026-10-14 entry rules out his Muschelkalk correlation; Galton (2007) gives it as Muschelkalk or Lower Keuper, and the same reasoning covers both.

**Open.** Nothing beyond the 2026-10-14 entry.

## What stages does the Magnesian Conglomerate take?

*2026-10-14*

**Conclusion.** None; the unit takes `period: [Late Triassic]` alone. Its former Norian–Rhaetian came from two works that date the *Thecodontosaurus* occurrence, which is the Durdham Down fissure fill and not this unit. What dates the conglomerate itself is an epoch from the survey map, an old correlation with the Muschelkalk, and a relayed Rhaetian whose two relayers read it oppositely. Governs `period` and the absent `stages` on [Magnesian Conglomerate](../../../stratigraphy/m/magnesian-conglomerate.yml).

**Evidence.**
- foffa2014a, p. 318, Fig. 1: "The Dolomitic conglomerate, Mercia Mudstone Group and Penarth Group are Late Triassic."
- galton2007a: the Durdham Down deposit is "a Triassic (Rhaetian) dolomitized cave breccia or fissure fill (not in Magnesian/Dolomitic Conglomerate … )".
- galton1990a and peyredefabrègues2015a: "?Norian-Rhaetian" and Norian to Rhaetian for the *Thecodontosaurus* occurrence.

**Ruled out.**
- *Norian–Rhaetian.* It dates the fissure fill.
- *The Muschelkalk.* Etheridge's (1870) correlation; a correlative's age does not date a unit.
- *Rhaetian from Moore (1867, 1881).* Benton and colleagues (2000) relay it as an age for the conglomerate (benton2000b, p. 80); Galton (2007) reads the same work as dating the fissures and leaving the conglomerate older.

**Open.** A stage-level dating of the conglomerate.

## Is the Magnesian Conglomerate Carnian?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** No source gives the unit a Carnian age in its own right. Governs `stages: [Norian, Rhaetian]` on [Magnesian Conglomerate](../../../stratigraphy/m/magnesian-conglomerate.yml), in that the Carnian is not added.

**Evidence.**
- vickers-rich1999a, p. 195: "the Late Triassic Magnesian Conglomerate fissure site in the Carboniferous Limestone from Durdham Down near Bristol, England … dated as Carnian to Rhaetian (Benton and Spencer 1995)".
- galton2007a, p. 515: the Clifton deposit is "a Triassic (Rheatian) dolomitized cave breccia or fissure fill (not in Magnesian/ Dolomitic Conglomerate …) in Lower Carboniferous (Dinantian) marine limestone".

**Ruled out.**
- *Carnian.* Vickers-Rich and colleagues (1999) report the Carnian–Rhaetian from Benton and Spencer (1995), as the age of the Durdham Down fissure. The date is second-hand, and it is for a fissure fill that Galton (2007) places outside the unit.

**Open.** Whether the Durdham Down ages date the unit at all. The record's Norian–Rhaetian also comes from statements about the Durdham Down occurrence (galton1990a, peyredefabrègues2015a).

# McNairy Sand

## What does Sohl (1960) say about the McNairy Sand's place in the Ripley and its rank?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** A member of the Ripley Formation where the Ripley can be divided, and a formation to the north where it cannot. Sohl (1960) recounts that Stephenson (1914) proposed the McNairy sand as a member of the Ripley, with its type section in a railway cut through "Big Hill" near Cypress Station, McNairy County, Tennessee. In northern Mississippi it is the third of the Ripley's five units, above the transitional clay and the Coon Creek tongue and below the sand of the upper Ripley and the Keownville limestone member. The member thickens northward into Tennessee and thins again in Kentucky, Illinois and Missouri, where it has been raised to formation rank after Stephenson (1955) and Grohskopf (1955), because the other Ripley members cannot be told apart there or are absent. Governs `rank: member`, `parent: Ripley` and the rank `dispute` on [McNairy Sand](../../../stratigraphy/m/mcnairy-sand.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sohl1960a, p. 1: "In northern Mississippi the formation can be divided into five units, which are, from oldest to youngest, transitional clay, Coon Creek tongue, McNairy sand member, sand of the upper part of the Ripley, and the Keownville limestone member (new name)."
- sohl1960a, p. 10: the same five units, numbered from the top, with "3. McNairy sand member" above "2. Coon Creek tongue" and "1. Transitional clay".
- sohl1960a, p. 15: "Stephenson (1914, p. 17, 18) originally proposed the McNairy sand as a member of the Ripley formation"; "the name McNairy sand member, derived from McNairy County, Tennessee is proposed. The type section of this member is exposed in a cut of the Southern Railway"; p. 17: "where the railroad passes a ridge known as "Big Hill"".
- sohl1960a, p. 17: "To the north in Tennessee the member thickens but then thins in Kentucky, Illinois, and Missouri. In these areas the McNairy has been raised to formation rank (Stephenson, 1955, p. 98; Grohskopf, 1955, p. 20), as the other members of the Ripley are impossible to distinguish or are absent."

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the McNairy Sand Member dated at its own level?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Yes, early Maastrichtian, stated for the member itself rather than relayed from the [Ripley Formation](../../../stratigraphy/r/ripley.yml)'s Campanian–Maastrichtian range. Governs `stages: [Maastrichtian]` on McNairy Sand and on [*Hypsibema crassicauda*](../../../genera/H/Hypsibema.yml).

**Evidence.**
- baird1979a, pp. 14–15: "the McNairy Sand Member of the Ripley Formation, considered early Maestrichtian in age".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Ripley Formation's Campanian.* That range belongs to the formation as a whole; Baird and Horner give the member its own age.

**Open.** Baird and Horner give no source for the age ("considered"), so it reports a received age rather than their own determination.

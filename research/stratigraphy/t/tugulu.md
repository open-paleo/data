# Tugulu

## What does Zheng and colleagues' (2023) age model give the Tugulu Group?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** An Early Cretaceous group whose formations run from the Berriasian to the Albian. Zheng and colleagues give the group's four formations in ascending order, Qingshuihe, Hutubihe, Shengjinkou and Lianmuqin, and date each in an age model they state for the group: the Qingshuihe Berriasian, the Hutubihe and Shengjinkou Valanginian, and the Lianmuqin Valanginian to Albian. The Valanginian rests on the Hutubihe sporopollen and on a 135.2 Ma zircon age from the top of the Shengjinkou, which places the upper Shengjinkou in the Valanginian and lets the Lianmuqin begin as early as that stage. Because the model is stated for the group, it dates the group from its own source; the group's range does not have to be widened to admit its formations. Governs the Valanginian, Hauterivian, Barremian, Aptian and Albian in `stages` on [Tugulu](../../../stratigraphy/t/tugulu.yml); the Berriasian is open.

**Evidence.**
- zheng2023a, p. 1, Abstract: fossils "recovered from the Lower Cretaceous Tugulu Group in Junggar Basin"; a U-Pb age "of 135.2 ± 0.9 Ma (2σ internal error) from a tuffaceous bed stratigraphically below the fauna-bearing layers, indicating a Valanginian maximum age for the Urho fauna".
- zheng2023a, p. 2, Geological Background: "Within the southern Junggar Basin proper, the Tugulu Group consists (in ascending order) of the Qingshuihe, Hutubihe, Shengjinkou, and Lianmuqin Formations".
- zheng2023a, p. 4, Biostratigraphic Age for the Tugulu Group: "The Qingshuihe Formation was interpreted as Berriasian in age based on clam shrimp, ostracod, and sporopollen fossils"; the Hutubihe sporopollen assemblage, "indicating a Valanginian age"; "Our ca. 135.2 Ma U-Pb age places the upper Shengjinkou Formation within the Valanginian Stage" (continued on p. 5).
- zheng2023a, p. 5, Biostratigraphic Age for the Tugulu Group: the fossils give "an Aptian–Albian age for the Lianmuqin Formation", but "our isotopic age from the top of the Shengjinkou Formation indicates that the Lianmuqin Formation could have been deposited as early as the Valanginian. The Tugulu Group thus formed during the Early Cretaceous"; "the following age model for the group: the Qingshuihe Formation as Berriasian, the Hutubihe and Shengjinkou Formations as Valanginian, and the Lianmuqin Formation as Valanginian to Albian".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Valanginian floor as the formations' ages carried up to the group.* A parent is dated from its own sources, and a child's age is never a reason to widen it (see [methods](../../methods.md#rules-stated-in-contributingmd)). The Valanginian stands because Zheng and colleagues state the age model for the group itself, not because the group must admit the ages of the units inside it.

**Open.** Whether the group takes the Berriasian. Zheng and colleagues' age model makes the Qingshuihe Berriasian, from earlier clam shrimp, ostracod and sporopollen work, and Dong's (1992) "Neocomian–Albian", as Tumanova (2018) relays it, also reaches into that stage; under the union rule either would add it. Dong (1992) and the Qingshuihe studies would show whether the Berriasian is a reading in its own right.

## Do Xu and colleagues (2018) date the upper Tugulu Group to the Late Cretaceous?

*2026-09-20*

**Conclusion.** No. Their horizon line pairs "Upper Cretaceous" with Barremian and Aptian, both Early Cretaceous stages, and their title says Early Cretaceous. Governs `period: [Early Cretaceous]` on [Tugulu](../../../stratigraphy/t/tugulu.yml).

**Evidence.**
- xu2018c, p. 2853, Locality and Horizon: "Upper Cretaceous (Barremian-Aptian?) upper part of the Tugulu Group"; title: "Two Early Cretaceous Fossils Document Transitional Stages in Alvarezsaurian Dinosaur Evolution".

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Cretaceous.* A slip that the same line and the title contradict (see [methods](../../methods.md#a-slip-that-its-own-document-contradicts-is-not-a-reading)).

**Open.** Nothing.

# Tendaguru

## Does Bussert and colleagues' "possibly Aptian" add the Aptian?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No; the stages stand, Oxfordian to Hauterivian. Bussert and colleagues (2009) commit to a range ending in the Hauterivian and name the Aptian only as a possible extension, which [methods](../../methods.md) excludes for stages as for epochs. Governs `stages` on [Tendaguru](../../../stratigraphy/t/tendaguru.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- bussert2009a, p. 170: "The age of the Tendaguru Formation ranges at least from the middle Oxfordian to the Hauterivian or possibly Aptian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian.* A possibility beyond the committed range.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Which parts of the Tendaguru Formation carry its Oxfordian and its Early Cretaceous stages?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Oxfordian through Hauterivian, as the 2026-09-30 entry gives it. Bussert and colleagues (2009) date the type area from the middle Oxfordian through the Tithonian for the Lower to Upper Dinosaur members, and from the Valanginian through the Hauterivian, or possibly the Aptian, for the Rutitrigonia bornhardti-schwarzi Member at the top, so the Valanginian and Hauterivian belong to the uppermost member. Schrank (2005) dates the Nerinea Bed Oxfordian–Kimmeridgian on dinoflagellate cysts, and a Muderongia–Oligosphaeridium assemblage late Valanginian–Hauterivian; that assemblage comes from the Namunda Plateau, 8 km south of Tendaguru, and only probably from the Trigonia schwarzi Bed. Mannion and colleagues (2019) call the formation Kimmeridgian–Tithonian, but they write only of its Late Jurassic, dinosaur-bearing part and place every sauropod they treat in the Upper Dinosaur Member. Governs `stages` (Oxfordian–Hauterivian) on [Tendaguru](../../../stratigraphy/t/tendaguru.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bussert2009a, p. 156: "the age of the Tendaguru Formation ranges in the type area from the middle Oxfordian through the Tithonian (Lower to Upper Dinosaur Member) and from the Valanginian through Hauterivian or possibly Aptian (Rutitrigonia bornhardti-schwarzi Member)".
- bussert2009a, p. 157: for the Lower Dinosaur Member, "Previous opinions had tentatively suggested an Oxfordian ... or even a Callovian age (Dietrich 1927a)".
- bussert2009a, p. 170: "The age of the Tendaguru Formation ranges at least from the middle Oxfordian to the Hauterivian or possibly Aptian, but the exact chronostratigraphy of its members still needs to be established."
- schrank2005a, p. 49: the cysts come "from the dinosaur-bearing Tendaguru Beds (Upper Jurassic-Lower Cretaceous)"; "the Rigaudella aemula-Chlamydophorella wallala assemblage from the Nerinea Bed (Oxfordian to Kimmeridgian)"; "the Muderongia-Oligosphaeridium assemblage probably from the Trigonia schwarzi Bed at Namunda Plateau, 8 km south of Tendaguru".
- schrank2005a, p. 78: "This indicates a late Valanginian to Hauterivian age for the Muderongia—Oligosphaeridium assemblage, which is supported by ammonite data from the Trigonia schwarzi Bed (Valanginian to Barremian; Zwierzycki, 1914)".
- mannion2019a, p. 785: "The Late Jurassic section of the Tendaguru Formation of south-eastern Tanzania"; p. 862: "The Late Jurassic (Kimmeridgian–Tithonian) Tendaguru Formation has yielded evidence for at least seven sauropod genera".
- mannion2019a, pp. 788, 812, 817, 822: "Upper Dinosaur Member (Upper Saurian Bed), Tendaguru Formation; Tithonian, Late Jurassic", the horizon line for each sauropod treated.

**Ruled out.**
- *Kimmeridgian–Tithonian as the formation's span.* Mannion and colleagues write of "The Late Jurassic section" and date the dinosaur-bearing interval, not the formation; the members that Bussert and colleagues and Schrank date run from the middle Oxfordian into the Early Cretaceous.
- *Callovian.* Bussert and colleagues give it only as an earlier, tentative opinion of Dietrich (1927) for the Lower Dinosaur Member, which they do not adopt. The many Callovian records in Schrank (2005) are previous records of individual cyst species in Australia, Europe and elsewhere, not ages for these beds.
- *Aptian.* Bussert and colleagues offer it as "possibly", a possibility beside their Valanginian through Hauterivian, not a reading of its own.

**Open.** Schrank relays an ammonite age of "Valanginian to Barremian" for the Trigonia schwarzi Bed from Zwierzycki (1914). If Zwierzycki dates the bed himself, it is an own reading that would add the Barremian; it has not been read. The Early Cretaceous cyst age rests on the Namunda Plateau samples, off the type area, and on a probable bed assignment.

## What is the age of the Tendaguru Formation, and of its dinosaurs?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** The formation runs from the middle Oxfordian into the Early Cretaceous (Valanginian–Hauterivian, possibly Aptian), the Cretaceous belonging at least to the top member; its dinosaurs are Kimmeridgian–Tithonian, with the Upper Dinosaur Member possibly reaching the Berriasian. Governs `stages` (Oxfordian–Hauterivian) on Tendaguru, the stages of the [Upper Dinosaur](../../../stratigraphy/u/upper-dinosaur.yml) and [Middle Dinosaur](../../../stratigraphy/m/middle-dinosaur.yml) members, and the Late Jurassic of taxa such as [*Giraffatitan brancai*](../../../genera/G/Giraffatitan.yml) and [*Kentrosaurus aethiopicus*](../../../genera/K/Kentrosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bussert2009a, p. 141: "we raise the Tendaguru Beds to formational rank and recognise six members", the top being the "Rutitrigonia bornhardti-schwarzi Member"; "The age of the whole formation apparently ranges at least from the middle Oxfordian to the Valanginian through Hauterivian or possibly Aptian." p. 149: the top member is Dietrich's "Early Cretaceous 'Schwarzistufe'".
- schrank2005a, p. 49: assemblages from "the Middle Saurian Bed (late Kimmeridgian)", "the Trigonia smeei Bed (Tithonian)" and "probably from the Trigonia schwarzi Bed … (late Valanginian to Hauterivian)".

**Ruled out.**
- *An Early Cretaceous age for the dinosaur-bearing members as established.* No source read for this note puts a dinosaur horizon in the Cretaceous; the records keep all thirteen species Late Jurassic.

**Open.** Where the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary falls. "The placement of the Jurassic/Cretaceous boundary within the Tendaguru Formation is as yet uncertain" (bussert2009a, p. 170), and "More work is needed to decide unambiguously whether the Upper Dinosaur Member is wholly Late Jurassic or, in part, Early Cretaceous" (p. 165). Schrank (2005, p. 78) does not exclude an Early Cretaceous age, "probably not younger than Berriasian", for the upper Trigonia smeei Bed.

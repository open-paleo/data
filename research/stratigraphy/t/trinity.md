# Trinity

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Trinity?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Aptian to Albian. Imlay (1939) correlates the Trinity of Texas with the upper Aptian and lower Albian: his Table 2 sets the Trinity against both the Aptian and Lower Albian rows, and he reads a Trinity section at Cañón Santa Rosa in Sonora as either early Albian or late Aptian on its ammonites and pelecypods. Stoyanow (1949) repeats Imlay's correlation of the Glance, Morita and lower Mural limestone of the Bisbee group with the Trinity as one of the earlier readings of the Bisbee group's age; the Trinity's age in it is Imlay's. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on [Trinity](../../../stratigraphy/t/trinity.yml).

**Evidence.**
- imlay1939a, p. 1734: "The faunal assemblage shows that the entire section is of Trinity age and, in terms of the European time scale, represents either early Albian or late Aptian".
- imlay1939a, p. 1738, Table 2: "Suggested correlation of Bisbee group", with the Texas "Trinity" set against the "Lower Albian" and "Aptian" rows.
- stoyanow1949a, p. 5: "Imlay (1939a, p. 1738, Table 2) tentatively correlated the Glance, Morita, and the lower member of the Mural limestone with the Trinity group of Texas (Upper Aptian-Lower Albian), assigning the upper member of the Mural and the Cintura formation to the Fredericksburg (Middle Albian)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Stoyanow (1949) as an independent date for the Trinity.* The memoir reports Imlay's correlation in its history of readings of the Bisbee group of southeastern Arizona, beside those of Stanton and Burckhardt, and does not argue the age from the Texas section.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Tuchengzi

*2026-11-26*

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Tuchengzi?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Tithonian to Berriasian. Liu and colleagues (2012) refer the formation to the latest Jurassic (Tithonian) to the earliest Cretaceous (Berriasian), on K–Ar, SHRIMP U–Pb and LA-ICP-MS ages of 156–139 Ma reported by earlier work, and place it above the Middle Jurassic Lanqi/Tiaojishan Formation and below the Lower Cretaceous Zhangjiakou Formation. Swisher and colleagues (2002) give the first isotopic date for the formation, 139.4 Ma on sanidine from a tuff in its upper part; on ICS chart v2024/12 (base Berriasian 143.1 Ma, base Valanginian 137.05 Ma) that is Berriasian. On that result they place the continental Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary of northeastern China in the lower part of the formation or below it. Butler and colleagues (2008) relay two readings for the *Chaoyangsaurus* horizon, Middle Jurassic from Zhao and colleagues (1999) and Tithonian from Weishampel and colleagues (2004), joined as "Middle or Late Jurassic", a hedge that crosses an epoch boundary. Governs `stages` and `period` on [Tuchengzi](../../../stratigraphy/t/tuchengzi.yml).

**Evidence.**
- liu2012a, p. 3: "The Tuchengzi/Houcheng Formation referred to the latest Jurassic (Tithonian) to the earliest Cretaceous"; "based on K–Ar, SHRIMP U–Pb and LA-ICP-MS dates, ages of 156–139 Ma of the Tuchengzi/Houcheng Formation were reported".
- liu2012a, p. 2: in ascending order, "the Middle Jurassic Haifanggou Formation (called the Jiulongshan Formation in Hebei Province) and Lanqi/Tiaojishan Formation, the Upper Jurassic-Lower Cretaceous Tuchengzi Formation (called the Houcheng Formation in Hebei Province), the Lower Cretaceous Zhangjiakou Formation".
- swisher2002a, p. 138: "The tuff from the upper part of the underlying Tuchengzi Formation gave a mean 40Ar/39Ar age of 139.4 ± 0.19 (1SD) ± 0.05 (SE) Ma, based on 19 single sanidine crystal analyses"; "This represents the first isotopic dating for the Tuchengzi Formation and although considered previously middle Jurassic in age, at least the upper part of the formation can be referred to the Early Cretaceous."
- swisher2002a, p. 138: "the stratigraphic location of the continental Jurassic-Cretaceous boundary in northeastern China must be sought for within the lower part of the Tuchengzi Formation or within underlying formations".
- butler2008b, p. 9: *Chaoyangsaurus youngi* is from "the Tuchengzi Formation of Liaoning Province, China (Middle or Late Jurassic: Middle Jurassic, Zhao *et al*. 1999; Tithonian, Weishampel *et al*. 2004*b*)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Middle Jurassic.* Butler and colleagues (2008) relay it from Zhao and colleagues (1999) beside a Tithonian reading. Swisher and colleagues (2002) describe the Middle Jurassic age as the view held before their date, which refers at least the upper part of the formation to the Early Cretaceous, and Liu and colleagues (2012) place the formation above the Middle Jurassic Lanqi/Tiaojishan Formation.

**Open.** Whether the lowest beds are older than the Tithonian. The 156 Ma end of the ages Liu and colleagues (2012) report would fall in the Oxfordian on the current chart (base Kimmeridgian 154.8 Ma), but it is a reported spread of other authors' dates, and they label the formation Tithonian at its base. Tracing the 156 Ma date to the work that measured it would settle whether it is a dated sample from the formation.

# Toutunhe

## What ages do Eberth and colleagues (2001) and Fang and colleagues (2015) give the parts of the Toutunhe Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Adds to the 2026-10-02 entry without changing it. In the composite section at Manas, Eberth and colleagues (2001) date the 200-m variegated interval at the base of the formation to the Bajocian, from a drying episode they correlate with the upper Xishanyao Formation, and hold a Bathonian age for it equally likely. The carbonaceous upper part they put at the end of the Middle Jurassic, in the Callovian. Their Bajocian base departs from earlier interpretations that gave the whole formation a late Middle Jurassic (Bathonian–Callovian) age. Fang and colleagues (2015) list the formation among the Upper Jurassic units of the southern Junggar Basin, as the first to carry abundant late Jurassic magmatic zircons, and infer large-scale volcanism during its deposition; they name no Late Jurassic stage. Governs `stages: [Bajocian, Bathonian, Callovian]` and the dispute on [Toutunhe](../../../stratigraphy/t/toutunhe.yml).

**Evidence.**
- eberth2001a, p. 1630, Manas section: "A 200-m-thick variegated mudstone and sandstone alluvial interval at the base of the Toutunhe Formation, however, suggests an episode of increasing seasonal dryness during the Middle Jurassic (Bajocian). Carbonaceous shales and carbonaceous roots and rootlets in the overlying portion of the Toutunhe Formation suggest a return to warm and humid conditions at the end of the Middle Jurassic (Callovian)."
- eberth2001a, p. 1637: "we correlate the basal Tountunhe Formation at Manas with the upper Xishanyao Formation"; the correlation "differs with all previous interpretations that claim a strictly early Middle Jurassic age (Aalenian–Bajocian) for the Xishanyao Formation and a late Middle Jurassic age (Bathonian–Callovian) for the Toutunhe Formation".
- eberth2001a, p. 1639: "a Bajocian or Bathonian age is equally likely for the lower redbeds section of the Toutunhe Formation at Manas".
- fang2015a, p. 50: "the Upper Jurassic Toutunhe, Qigu and Kalazha Formations".
- fang2015a, p. 59: "The Toutunhe Formation is the first to contain abundant late Jurassic magmatic zircons"; "These were formed nearly at the same time as the sedimentation age of the Toutunhe Formation (younger than 161.2 Ma)"; "relatively large scale volcanic activity occurred during the deposition period of the Toutunhe Formation (late Jurassic)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Bathonian–Callovian for the whole formation.* The age of the earlier interpretations that Eberth and colleagues (2001, p. 1637) set aside; their correlation of the basal redbeds with the upper Xishanyao makes a Bajocian base as likely as a Bathonian one (p. 1639).
- *An Oxfordian, Kimmeridgian or Tithonian stage from Fang and colleagues (2015).* The paper names no Late Jurassic stage. Its "late Jurassic" for the volcanism repeats the Upper Jurassic assignment that the 2026-10-02 entry shows is a premise of the argument, not a result.

**Open.** Whether the lower redbeds at Manas are Bajocian or Bathonian; Eberth and colleagues leave the two equally likely. The zircon question stays as the 2026-10-02 entry leaves it.

## Is the Toutunhe Formation Middle Jurassic or Oxfordian?

*2026-10-02*

**Conclusion.** Disputed between a Middle Jurassic (Bajocian–Callovian) age from fossils and palynology and a younger age implied by detrital zircons. Governs `stages: [Bajocian, Bathonian, Callovian]` and the dispute on Toutunhe.

**Evidence.**
- averianov2005a, p. 151: "dated on the basis of palynoflora as Bathonian", relayed from Wang (2001).
- eberth2001a, pp. 1630, 1639: "a Bajocian or Bathonian age is equally likely for the lower redbeds", on fossils and sequence correlation.
- fang2015a, p. 55, Table 1: the youngest zircon population in the Toutunhe runs about 153–161 Ma.

**Ruled out.**
- *A zircon floor at 161.2 Ma.* Fang and colleagues write "the sedimentation age of the Toutunhe Formation (younger than 161.2 Ma) (Fig. 2b), and thus we term them syndepositional zircons" (fang2015a, p. 59). The 161.2 Ma is the chart age of the formation's Upper Jurassic assignment in their Fig. 2b, "ages … from ICS (2013)" (p. 49), an assignment taken from unpublished regional mapping (p. 50); it is the premise of their reasoning, not a date.

**Open.** Whether the zircons, which reach about 153 Ma, are syndepositional. If they are, the Toutunhe would be Late Jurassic; Fang and colleagues assume so from the chart age, which makes the argument circular.

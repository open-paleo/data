# Turney Ranch

## Does the Turney Ranch take the Bisbee Group as parent?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** Held. Ratkevich (1998) and D'Emic and colleagues (2016) place the formation in the Bisbee Group in their own voice, but neither defines or dates the group, which they take from Dickinson and colleagues (1989). Governs nothing yet on [Turney Ranch](../../../stratigraphy/t/turney-ranch.yml).

**Evidence.** None.

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

## What does the K/Ar date near the Sonorasaurus quarry measure?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** A minimum age, not the age of the bone bed. Adds to the 2026-10-02 entry without changing it. The one date Ratkevich (1998) prints, 97.7 ± 2.2 Ma, is a K/Ar age on authigenic illite from a mudstone a short distance above the *Sonorasaurus* quarry, and he says such dates "must be considered as minimal ages". D'Emic and colleagues (2016) read the earlier Albian–Cenomanian assignments the same way, as resting on "a K/Ar minimum age of about 98 Ma", and use it as the young end of their 98 to 110 Ma bracket. The mudstone is therefore at least about 98 Ma old and the quarry below it older still, which leaves the bone bed in the Albian or the earliest Cenomanian (the Cenomanian begins at 100.5 Ma); D'Emic and colleagues' biostratigraphic evidence puts *Sonorasaurus* "just before or after the Albian-Cenomanian boundary". Both stages stay. Governs `stages: [Albian, Cenomanian]` on [Turney Ranch](../../../stratigraphy/t/turney-ranch.yml).

**Evidence.**
- ratkevich1998a, p. 72: "Distribution: Turney Ranch Formation, Bisbee Group, vicinity of Whetstone Mountains, Southeastern Arizona. Age: Middle Cretaceous (Albian) ± 97 Ma".
- ratkevich1998a, p. 79: "Two K/Ar ages have been reported … on authigenic illite from the Whetstone Mountains: 97.7±2.2 Ma from a mudstone layer in Turney Ranch beds a short stratigraphic distance above the dinosaur quarry"; "dates on authigenic minerals must be considered as minimal ages, based upon both their authigenic origin and the presence of abundant Laramide sills in the area".
- ratkevich1998a, p. 80: "Radioisotopic dates are suggesting that the Turney Ranch Formation does indeed span the Albian-Cenomanian boundary."
- d'emic2016a, p. 102, Abstract: "*Sonorasaurus thompsoni* from the Turney Ranch Formation of the Bisbee Group of Arizona, USA, potentially represents one of the youngest sauropods before their ca. 30-million-year-long hiatus from the record".
- d'emic2016a, p. 112: "The Turney Ranch Formation was regarded as Albian-Cenomanian by Ratkevitch (1998) and Scarbourough (2000) on the basis a K/Ar minimum age of about 98 Ma"; "radiometric dates bracket the age of *Sonorasaurus* between ca. 98 and ca. 110 Ma; biostratigraphic evidence suggests that it lived sometime just before or after the Albian-Cenomanian boundary".
- gale2020a, p. 1036: "The base of the Cenomanian is taken at 100.5 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *97.7 ± 2.2 Ma as the age of the bone bed, which would make it Cenomanian.* The date is on illite that formed after the mudstone was laid down, from a layer above the quarry; Ratkevich himself calls such dates minimal ages, and D'Emic and colleagues call it a minimum age.

**Open.** Ratkevich reports "Two K/Ar ages" but prints only one; the date compilation he cites would give the second.

## Does the Turney Ranch Formation keep its Albian?

*2026-10-02*

**Conclusion.** Yes. Unlike units whose "Albian" was only a pre-2012 label for dates near 97–99 Ma, the Turney Ranch is bracketed by dates reaching back to about 110 Ma. Governs `stages: [Albian, Cenomanian]` on Turney Ranch and on [*Sonorasaurus thompsoni*](../../../genera/S/Sonorasaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- d'emic2016a, p. 112: "radiometric dates bracket the age of Sonorasaurus between ca. 98 and ca. 110 Ma"; "the Turney Ranch Formation is likely younger than ~110 Ma"; "late Albian-early Cenomanian age estimates for the Cintura Formation based on fossil bivalves", a possibly coeval unit.

**Ruled out.**
- *An Albian resting on "(Albian) +. 97 Ma".* Ratkevich gives "Age: Middle Cretaceous (Albian) +. 97 Ma" (ratkevich1998a, p. 72); on the current chart 97 Ma is Cenomanian, so this label alone would not support the Albian. The same paper says the formation "does indeed span the Albian-Cenomanian boundary" (p. 80).

**Open.** Nothing.

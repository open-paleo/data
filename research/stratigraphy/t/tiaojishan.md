# Tiaojishan

## Does Yang and Li's dating add the Bathonian or the Tithonian?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** The Bathonian, not the Tithonian. Answers the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Yang and Li (2008) date a basalt from the "lower bed of the Lanqi Formation", the Tiaojishan under another name, by their own Ar–Ar to 166.1 ± 0.9 Ma, Bathonian on the ICS chart v2024/12 at its central value (base of the Callovian 165.3 Ma), with an error that reaches the Callovian. Their own dates have the lavas erupt "from 166 to 153 Ma", the 153 Ma from an intrusion; their "166–148 Ma" for the formation takes its younger end from published ages relayed by Davis (2005), a second-hand figure that adds no stage. Governs `stages: [Bathonian, Callovian, Oxfordian, Kimmeridgian]` and the note on yang2008a on [Tiaojishan](../../../stratigraphy/t/tiaojishan.yml), and the stages of [*Aurornis xui*](../../../genera/A/Aurornis.yml), which takes its unit's.

**Evidence.**
- yang2008a, p. 92: "HFG-13 and HFG-15 are basalts collected from the lower bed of the Lanqi Formation in Haifanggou of Beipiao".
- yang2008a, p. 101: basalt HFG-13 "yields the 40Ar/39Ar plateau age of 166.1 ± 0.9 Ma".
- yang2008a, p. 107: "Our results indicate that the Lanqi lavas erupted from 166 to 153 Ma"; "Collectively, these ages indicate an age range of ca. 166–148 Ma for the Tiaojishan and Lanqi Formations."

**Ruled out.**
- *Tithonian from the 148 Ma end.* Relayed from Davis (2005), unread.

**Open.** Davis (2005) has not been read.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Tiaojishan?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Callovian to Kimmeridgian. Godefroit and colleagues (2013) state that range from SHRIMP ages of 165 ± 1.2 to 153 ± 2 Ma, and the boundary dates of Zhang and colleagues (2008) that Sullivan and colleagues (2014) report, about 165 Ma at the base and 156 to 153 Ma at the top, give the same stages on the ICS chart v2024/12. Xu and colleagues (2016) date the formation "probably Oxfordian" on 40Ar/39Ar dates of 161.8 and 159.5 Ma from the base and top of the Lanqi Formation in western Liaoning, and Peng and colleagues (2012) place the fossil-bearing strata at Linglongta in the late Middle or early Late Jurassic; both fall inside the range. Governs `stages: [Callovian, Oxfordian, Kimmeridgian]` and `period` on [Tiaojishan](../../../stratigraphy/t/tiaojishan.yml).

**Evidence.**
- godefroit2013a, p. 6, Methods: "Ages between 165±1.2 Ma and 153±2 Ma were obtained by SHRIMP U-Th-Pb analysis of samples from the Tiaojishan (= Lanqi) Formation"; "These results indicate that the age of the Tiaojishan Formation is between the Callovian (Middle Jurassic) and the Kimmeridgian (Late Jurassic)".
- sullivan2014a, p. 248: Zhang and colleagues (2008) "place the upper boundary of the formation at 156–153 Ma and the lower boundary at about 165 Ma, values that comfortably span the Middle–Late Jurassic boundary"; Yang and Li (2008) suggest "that the Tiaojishan Formation was deposited between 166 and 148 Ma, whereas Chang et al. (2009) proposed that the lower bound of the Tiaojishan Formation was only 160.7 ± 0.4 Ma".
- sullivan2014a, p. 248: the Daohugou strata "might either extend across the Jiulongshan-Tiaojishan boundary or be entirely referable to the upper Jiulongshan or lower Tiaojishan formation".
- xu2016b, p. 2231: "The Lanqi/Tiaojishan Formation is probably Oxfordian. Two recent 40Ar/39Ar dates for the Lanqi Formation of western Liaoning are 159.5±0.6 Ma, based on samples from the top of the formation" and "161.8±0.4 Ma based on samples from the basal portion of the formation"; "The Daxishan Locality, another major Yanliao site, exposes the Oxfordian Lanqi/Tiaojishan Formation".
- peng2012a, p. 1326, Abstract: the fossil-bearing succession at Linglongta "correlates well with the Middle Jurassic Lanqi/Tiaojishan Formation in northeastern China".
- peng2012a, p. 1332: the radioisotopic ages for the formation "varied from 175 Myr to 152 Myr"; "Estimates of the Lanqi/Tiaojishan Formation have ranged from the Middle Jurassic (Toarcian) to the latest Jurassic (Tithonian)".
- peng2012a, p. 1334: the new dates match "the dating results of 152~165 Myr obtained from the Lanqi/Tiaojishan Formation in both northern Hebei and western Liaoning province regionally and locally in Daohugou".
- peng2012a, p. 1335, Conclusions: "A current SHRIMP U-Pb zircon date of 160.7 Myr for the basal andesites in the fossil-bearing succession at Linglongta"; the strata "are undeniably late Middle Jurassic or early Late Jurassic in age".

**Ruled out.**
- *Toarcian to Tithonian, from Peng and colleagues (2012).* The span is the range of earlier estimates that the paper reports, not its own reading, and it calls the Toarcian Middle Jurassic, though the Toarcian is the last stage of the Early Jurassic. The dating results the paper accepts run from 152 to 165 Myr in northern Hebei, western Liaoning and Daohugou.
- *Latest Bathonian, from the uncertainty of the 165 ± 1.2 Ma date.* On the ICS chart v2024/12 the older end of that uncertainty reaches the latest Bathonian, but the central value is Callovian, and Godefroit and colleagues (2013) themselves give the Callovian as the lower limit.
- *A date for the formation from the Daohugou strata.* Sullivan and colleagues (2014) leave open whether those strata belong to the Jiulongshan, the Tiaojishan or both, so what the paper says about their age does not date this unit. Their placement is settled in [Daohugou and Linglongta phases](../../topics/daohugou-and-linglongta-phases.md).

**Open.** Yang and Li's (2008) 166 to 148 Ma, which Sullivan and colleagues (2014) report, reaches the Bathonian and the Tithonian on the current chart, and no entry says why neither stage is carried; a reading of Yang and Li (2008) for whether that span is its own measured range or a compilation would settle it. Xu and colleagues (2016) give the basal Lanqi date as 161.8 ± 0.4 Ma, citing Chang and colleagues (2009) and a later paper by the same group, while Sullivan and colleagues (2014) and Godefroit and colleagues (2013) give Chang and colleagues' (2009) figure as 160.7 ± 0.4 Ma; both are Oxfordian, so the stage does not depend on which is right.

# Tecovas

## Do Cornet (1993) and Chatterjee (1986) give the Tecovas the Carnian?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** Yes, and as stated stages, so the Carnian does not depend on the older calibration of the Carnian–Norian boundary. Answers the 2026-11-23 entry's Open line. Chatterjee (1986), now read, puts the Tecovas in the *Parasuchus* zone by its vertebrates, and *Parasuchus* is "restricted to the Carnian"; Cornet (1993) recognizes two Carnian pollen zones in the Dockum Group, the older with taxa of a possible middle Carnian correlation in its basal samples, which his Fig. 5 places in the Tecovas. A stated stage is carried over; only a bare number is re-placed on the current chart. The Norian of Martz and colleagues (2013) and the `dispute` stand. Governs the Carnian in `stages` and the notes on chatterjee1986a and cornet1993a on [Tecovas](../../../stratigraphy/t/tecovas.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- chatterjee1986a, pp. 143–144: "Parasuchus is restricted to the Carnian".
- chatterjee1986a, p. 145: "The Parasuchus zone corresponds to the lower Dockum (Tecovas Member), and the Nicrosaurus zone to the upper Dockum (Cooper Member)."
- cornet1993a, p. 75: "Three zones ranging in age from middle Carnian to early Norian are recognized in the Chinle Formation, but only the older two zones are recognized in the Dockum Group."

**Ruled out.**
- *A Carnian that rests on the pre-2010 calibration.* Both authors name the stage; neither places a number on a chart.

**Open.** Unchanged: a radioisotopic date from the Tecovas itself.

## What age and extent do the cited works give the Tecovas Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Carnian and Norian, held as a dispute between two readings. Lehman and Chatterjee (2005) place the formation in the Carnian on their charts and in their text, relaying Chatterjee's (1986) tetrapod correlation and Cornet's (1993) palynology, and put the Carnian–Norian boundary at the Trujillo–Cooper Canyon contact above. Martz and colleagues (2013) assign the formation's fauna to the Adamanian, which they date to about 223–215 Ma only by correlation with the Chinle Formation, since no high-resolution dates had been published from the Dockum; they present this as revising the Carnian age earlier given to the lower Dockum from pollen and vertebrates, and allow that the lowermost Tecovas may be Otischalkian. Chatterjee (1985) gives the formation no age beyond the Late Triassic of the group, credits the name to Gould (1907) in Palo Duro Canyon, and doubts that the Dockum formation names can be used elsewhere before detailed mapping. Later work does carry the name into New Mexico: Martz and colleagues treat the Garita Creek as the same unit. Governs `stages: [Carnian, Norian]` and `dispute` on [Tecovas](../../../stratigraphy/t/tecovas.yml), and `variants: [Garita Creek]`.

**Evidence.**
- chatterjee1985a, p. 398: "Gould (1907) recognized two units within the Dockum Group. The basal unit, the Tecovas Formation, consists of variegated shales overlain by magenta shales"; "In the Palo Duro Canyon, this entire sequence is well exposed. This is not so in other areas"; "it is difficult to apply these formational names to other regions of the Dockum until detailed lithological mapping is completed".
- chatterjee1985a, p. 399: "*Horizon.* Dockum Group, late Triassic."
- lehman2005a, p. 325: the Dockum's two sequences "are represented by the (1) Santa Rosa–Tecovas, and (2) Trujillo–Cooper Canyon Formations".
- lehman2005a, p. 326: "The Dockum Group in its type area includes four mappable units: the Santa Rosa Sandstone, Tecovas Formation, Trujillo Sandstone, and Cooper Canyon Formation"; Fig. 1 sets the Tecovas against "CARNIAN".
- lehman2005a, p. 346: "Chatterjee (1986a) correlated the Tecovas and Trujillo with the Carnian Stage of Germany, and the overlying Cooper Canyon with the Norian Stage"; "Cornet (1993) studied the palynomorphs from the Tecovas and Trujillo Formations and inferred a Middle to Late Carnian age for them".
- lehman2005a, p. 348: "The boundary between the Trujillo and the Cooper Canyon Formations approximates the stage boundary."
- martz2013a, p. 341: the northern Dockum includes "a lower mudstone-dominated unit (the Tecovas Formation and/or Garita Creek Formation); a middle sandstone-dominated unit (the Trujillo Formation)"; Fig. 3 caption: units are tied to the numeric timescale "exclusively through the Chinle Formation", "because published high-resolution radioisotopic dates are unavailable from the Dockum Group".
- martz2013a, p. 347: "the Santa Rosa and Garita Creek (= Tecovas) Formations of New Mexico".
- martz2013a, p. 358: the lower Cooper Canyon "correlates lithostratigraphically with the Tecovas Formation (Martz 2008), which also contains a characteristically Adamanian vertebrate assemblage"; "the lowermost Tecovas Formation may fall within the Otischalkian biozone"; "known localities in the Tecovas Formation might be slightly older than the Post Quarry, and closer in age to localities in the Blue Mesa Member".
- martz2013a, p. 359: "the Chinle Formation is probably entirely post-Carnian"; "this revises previous interpretations of the lower part of the Chinle Formation and Dockum Group as Carnian based on pollen and vertebrate fossils"; "a minimum age range for the Adamanian biozone in Arizona of 223–215 Ma (late Lacian to earliest Alaunian)".

**Ruled out.** None. The Carnian and the Norian readings are both live, and the record holds them as a dispute rather than choosing one.

**Open.** Whether the formation reaches the Carnian. The Carnian rests on Cornet (1993) and Chatterjee (1986), neither of which has been read; they are known here only as Lehman and Chatterjee relay them. Cornet's palynology would show whether the Carnian depends on the older calibration of the Carnian–Norian boundary that Martz and colleagues revise. A radioisotopic date from the Tecovas itself would settle it.

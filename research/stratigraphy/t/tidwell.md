# Tidwell

## Does the Tidwell Member reach the Kimmeridgian?

*2026-10-21*

**Conclusion.** Yes. Kowallis and colleagues (1998) date an ash about 3 m above the member's base to about 154.8 Ma and describe the dated beds as "latest Oxfordian(?) to early Kimmeridgian", the query on the Oxfordian only, with Kimmeridgian charophytes and palynomorphs reported from higher in the member. Under the union rule the member takes the Kimmeridgian beside the Oxfordian it already holds. Governs `stages: [Oxfordian, Kimmeridgian]` on [Tidwell](../../../stratigraphy/t/tidwell.yml).

**Evidence.**
- kowallis1998a, p. 235: "one ash bed about 3 m above the J-5 unconformity", giving "154.75 ± 0.54 Ma", "154.82 ± 0.58 Ma", "154.87 ± 0.52 Ma" and "154.8 ± 1.4 Ma".
- kowallis1998a, p. 256: "The new ages reported here are the most precise isotopic age data yet published from latest Oxfordian(?) to early Kimmeridgian-age sedimentary rocks."
- kowallis1998a, p. 256: "Oxfordian(?) fossils were found just above the level of the dated bentonite bed in the Tidwell Member near Grand Junction, Colorado and Kimmeridgian charophytes and palynomorphs were recovered from higher strata in the Tidwell (Litwin and others, this volume; Schudack and others, this volume)."

**Ruled out.**
- *Placing the ash on the chart by its number alone.* The four ages have central values on both sides of the current Oxfordian–Kimmeridgian boundary (154.8 Ma), and each uncertainty spans it, so the number does not choose a stage. The Kimmeridgian rests on the paper's own stage reading, not on the number.
- *Oxfordian only.* The 158 ± 3 Ma U-Pb date that Foster and colleagues (2016) give for the *Dystrophaeus* site in the lower half of the member is preliminary, and the beds above the dated ash reach higher.

**Open.** Litwin and colleagues (1998) and Schudack and colleagues (1998) have not been read; they would show how high in the member the Kimmeridgian microfossils lie.

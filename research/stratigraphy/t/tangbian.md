# Tangbian

## Does any work date the Tangbian Campanian?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No; the Campanian is dropped, leaving the Late Cretaceous. Supersedes the 2026-11-23 entry on the stages and answers its Open line. Neither Xi paper dates the Tangbian in its text; Xi and colleagues (2018) date only the Guifeng Group as a whole, after Wu and colleagues (2002), and the Campanian comes from where their compiled correlation charts draw the Tangbian box, with no reason given. A chart position is a label, not a reading. Zhu and colleagues (2025) relay "deposited in Campanian" from those charts. The Hekou that Jin and colleagues (2022) date Maastrichtian is the Guifeng Group's lowest formation, below the Tangbian in the stacked order, not the Hekou Group of Gansu in the registry. Governs `period: [Late Cretaceous]` and the absent `stages` on [Tangbian](../../../stratigraphy/t/tangbian.yml), and the absent `stage` on [*Huaxiazhoulong shouwen*](../../../genera/H/Huaxiazhoulong.yml).

**Evidence.**
- xi2018a, p. 275: "two isotopic ages of 89–86 Ma from the basalt of the Hekou Formation imply that the age of the Guifeng Group might be early to mid-late Cretaceous (Wu et al., 2002)".
- xi2018a, p. 274, Fig. 9: "Tangbian Fm." drawn in the Campanian row of the Jiangxi column, with no age printed on the box.

**Ruled out.**
- *Campanian from Xi and colleagues' charts, and from Zhu and colleagues (2025) after them.* A chart position with no stated basis.

**Open.** A date or index fossil from the Tangbian itself.

## What do the cited works say about the definition and age of the Tangbian?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Ling (1996) erects the Tangbian from the middle part of the former Guifeng Formation, which the same paper raises to a group: red fine clastic rocks, 240.2 m thick at the stratotype on the Tangbian section in Lianhe Township, Hengfeng County, conformable on the Hekou Formation and below the Lianhe Formation. Ling dates it only to the epoch, from the egg *Oolithes elongatus*, ostracods and charophytes in the formation and from dinosaur eggs in the conformable Hekou and Lianhe. The Campanian is a position on a correlation chart: Xi and colleagues' (2021) chart of southeastern China, modified from the Chinese edition of Xi and colleagues (2018), draws the Tangbian at about 80.5 to 73.5 Ma, wholly within the Campanian, between a Hekou at about 89 to 80.5 Ma and a Lianhe from about 73.5 Ma upward. The boundaries are read from the chart and are approximate, and the text of Xi and colleagues (2021) does not name the formation. Governs `parent: Guifeng`, `lithology` and `stages: [Campanian]` on [Tangbian](../../../stratigraphy/t/tangbian.yml). The age of the group and the stacking of its formations are discussed in the entries on [Guifeng](../g/guifeng.md).

**Evidence.**
- ling1996a, p. 55, Abstract: "The former Ganzhou Formation and Guifeng Formation are both raised to group rank. The Maodian Formation is established in the lower part of the former, and the Hekou Formation and Tangbian Formation in the lower and middle parts of the latter, respectively" (translated).
- ling1996a, p. 58, 3.2 Stratotype: "The holotype section (stratotype) is the Tangbian section at Lianhe Township, Hengfeng County"; "Tangbian Formation (Kt) 240.2m", with conformable contacts above and below (translated).
- ling1996a, p. 58, 3.3 Current definition: "The Tangbian Formation is a set of red fine clastic rocks lying above the Hekou Formation and below the Lianhe Formation, and belongs to the middle part of the Guifeng Group" (translated).
- ling1996a, pp. 58–59, 3.4 Discussion of age: the formation "yields the dinosaur egg *Oolithes elongatus*" at Dongtuan and Xiaohan, with ostracods at Maodian and charophytes at Tankou; "Both the underlying Hekou Formation and the overlying Lianhe Formation, with which it is in conformable contact, yield dinosaur eggs. The Tangbian Formation is therefore undoubtedly Late Cretaceous" (translated).
- xi2021a, p. 386, Fig. 4: "Lithostratigraphic division and correlation of the Cretaceous in the Southeast China Stratigraphic Povince [Modified from Xi et al., 2019 (in Chinese)]"; the Jiangxi column stacks the Hekou, Tangbian and Lianhe, the Tangbian between about 80.5 and 73.5 Ma on the chart's scale, whose Campanian runs from 83.65 to 72.17 Ma.

**Ruled out.**
- *A Paleogene age.* Earlier surveys, as Ling (1996) relays, put these beds in the "Tertiary Yudu red sandy conglomerate" and in the Yangmeiling Formation "of age E1–2" (ling1996a, p. 58, translated). Ling places the formation in the Late Cretaceous from the eggs in it and in the formations above and below, and no work cited for the unit gives it a Paleogene age.

**Open.** None of the works cited dates the Tangbian itself. The Campanian rests on its position on the two correlation charts of Xi and colleagues (2018, 2021), which count as one source and give no reason for that position in their text; the basalt ages behind the group's age come from the Hekou (see [Guifeng](../g/guifeng.md)). A radiometric, magnetostratigraphic or biostratigraphic date from the formation would settle it.

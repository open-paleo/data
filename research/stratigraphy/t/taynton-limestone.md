# Taynton Limestone

## What was the Taynton Limestone first called, and is the Stonesfield Slate a unit within it?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Barron and colleagues (2012) give the formation's first name as "Taynton Stone", introduced by Woodward (1894) for a freestone of the Great Oolite sequence, and that name is the formation's variant. They place the Stonesfield Slate Beds at three levels within the formation's ooidal limestones, which they give, citing Boneham and Wyatt (1993), as the reason the beds remain informal, so the Stonesfield Slate is not a member or bed of the formation. They give the parent as the Great Oolite Group and the age as "Bathonian, Progracilis Zone to ?Subcontractus Zone". Governs `variants`, `parent` and `stages` on [Taynton Limestone](../../../stratigraphy/t/taynton-limestone.yml).

**Evidence.**
- barron2012a, p. 72: "Name introduced (as Taynton Stone) by Woodward (1894) for a 'freestone' within the Great Oolite sequence, and worked in quarries on Taynton Down, near Burford."
- barron2012a, p. 73: the Stonesfield Slate Beds "are now known to lie at three levels within the ooidal limestone beds of the Taynton Limestone Formation, necessitating their continued informal status".
- barron2012a, p. 74: "Parent Great Oolite Group"; "Age Bathonian, Progracilis Zone to ?Subcontractus Zone".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Stonesfield Slate as a formal member or bed of the Taynton Limestone.* The beds occur at three separate levels within the formation's ooidal limestones, which Barron and colleagues give as the reason they stay informal.

**Open.** Nothing.

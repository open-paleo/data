# Tiourarén

## On what evidence do the cited works date the Tiourarén?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** On the relationships of its dinosaurs alone, as the 2026-09-30 entry says; this entry records how each cited work reaches its age. Rauhut and López-Arbarello (2009) find nothing in the fish and invertebrates for the older Early Cretaceous age, and only an Aptian upper limit in the geological relations. Their Bathonian and Callovian come from the ages of Afrovenator's nearest relatives elsewhere, the Bathonian Dubreuillosaurus and the Callovian Eustreptospondylus, not from the rock. Rauhut and colleagues (2016) adopt the late Middle Jurassic and tabulate "?Bathonian-?Oxfordian"; their "earliest Late Jurassic", like the 2009 paper's pre-Kimmeridgian "Early Late Jurassic", is the Oxfordian. Remes and colleagues (2009) relay the 2009 reading and add no dating of their own. Rauhut and López-Arbarello spell the unit Tiouaren in all but two places. Governs `stages: [Bathonian, Callovian, Oxfordian]` on [Tiourarén](../../../stratigraphy/t/tiourarén.yml).

**Evidence.**
- rauhut2009a, p. 259: the fish and invertebrates do "not provide any evidence for such an age, and the geological relations only help to constrain the upper limit for the age of the formation (Aptian)"; "the described dinosaur taxa are phylogenetically nested with late Middle Jurassic to Early Late Jurassic taxa from other localities"; "Under the assumption of such an age, the dinosaur fauna of this formation provides new insights into dinosaur faunal provincialism during the latest Middle Jurassic".
- rauhut2009a, p. 262: "Afrovenator is grouped with taxa from the Middle Jurassic", in a polytomy "with the Bathonian Dubreuillosaurus and the Callovian Eustreptospondylus".
- rauhut2016a, p. 43: the formation "has traditionally been regarded as Early Cretaceous"; the Jurassic assignment has been followed "with most authors favouring a late Middle Jurassic age. This is the age assumed here as well".
- remes2009a, p. 2, Locality and horizon: the Spinophorosaurus site lies "at the base of the Irhazer Group ('Argiles de l'Irhazer' below the Tiourarén Formation)"; "the pre-Aptian and post-Triassic age of the Tiourarén Formation has been critically discussed [15], leading to the conclusion that the represented fauna fits more parsimoniously in a late Middle Jurassic to early Late Jurassic scene [16]"; Spinophorosaurus "is consistent with an even earlier age, presumably Middle Jurassic (Bajocian-Bathonian)".
- sereno1999a, p. 1342: "the Neocomian [~140 to 130 million years ago (Ma)] Tiourarén Formation", against "Aptian-Albian (~115 to 105 Ma) horizons of the Tegama Group in the Gadoufaoua region".
- upchurch2004a, Table 13.1: Jobaria tiguidensis, "Tiourarén Formation (Agadez), Farak Formation (Tahoua), Niger", "Hauterivian-Barremian, ?late Albian-early Cenomanian".
- upchurch2004a, p. 306: "Jobaria was found in the Tiourarén Formation of Niger (Sereno et al. 1999)", with no age.

**Ruled out.**
- *Upchurch and colleagues' (2004) age cell as the Tiourarén's.* The cell dates the Tiourarén and the Farak Formation together, so it cannot be read as this unit's age, and the chapter's text gives none. Its Hauterivian–Barremian is the Cretaceous reading the 2026-09-30 entry rules out.
- *A stage from Sereno and colleagues (1999).* The paper usually cited for the Cretaceous reading gives "Neocomian" with a numeric interval and names no stage for the unit; the reading itself is ruled out in the 2026-09-30 entry.
- *Remes and colleagues (2009) as a separate dating.* They relay Rauhut and López-Arbarello (2009). Their Bajocian–Bathonian concerns the Spinophorosaurus site in the Argiles de l'Irhazer below the formation, not the Tiourarén.

**Open.** A date from the rock, as the 2026-09-30 entry says. Until one exists the argument runs one way only: the fauna's relationships date the formation, and Rauhut and López-Arbarello (2009) then use that age to read the fauna's provincialism.

## What is the age of the Tiourarén Formation?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Middle to earliest Late Jurassic (about Bathonian–Oxfordian), but only by faunal comparison; there is no radiometric date. Governs `stages: [Bathonian, Callovian, Oxfordian]` on Tiourarén and the stages of its taxa, such as [*Jobaria tiguidensis*](../../../genera/J/Jobaria.yml) and [*Afrovenator abakensis*](../../../genera/A/Afrovenator.yml).

**Evidence.**
- rauhut2009a, p. 259: "pre-Kimmeridgian, probably late Middle Jurassic", argued from the phylogenetic position of its taxa (p. 263); p. 260: "no radiometric datings"; the fish are "of very limited utility" (p. 262) and "consistent with a Middle Jurassic age" (p. 263).
- rauhut2016a, p. 43: "probably Middle to earliest Late Jurassic … widely accepted since"; p. 45, Table 3: "?Bathonian-?Oxfordian". Same first author as the 2009 paper.

**Ruled out.**
- *Hauterivian–Barremian, or Neocomian.* Sereno and colleagues (1994) gave Hauterivian–Barremian; Sereno's later papers give "Neocomian" (130–140 Ma, about 135 Ma), as reported by Rauhut and López-Arbarello (rauhut2009a, p. 260). Sánchez-Hernández and Benton's "(?Hauterivian)" (sánchez-hernández2014a, p. 582) follows the older reading.

**Open.** A date. All readings rest on faunal comparison. Mannion and colleagues' "Bathonian–Callovian" (mannion2025a, p. 35) cites Rauhut and López-Arbarello together with Sereno and colleagues (1999); de Vries and colleagues say only "Jurassic horizons" (devries2022a, p. 3). Lasseron and colleagues (2019) have not been read.

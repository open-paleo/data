# Tugrugyin

## Is the Tugrugyin part of the Djadokhta, and what do the cited works say about its age?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** A member of the Djadokhta, as Dashzeveg and colleagues (2005) erect it, and Campanian. It overlies the Bayn Dzak Member at Tsonzh and Alag Teer, so it is "somewhat younger", by an amount they could not estimate. Its basal sands share the reversed polarity of the top of the Bayn Dzak, and if those reversed zones correlate, both lie above Chron 34n, in the Campanian or later. Gradziński and colleagues (1977) treated the same rocks, as the beds of Toogreeg, as a unit outside a Djadokhta Formation they knew only from Bayn Dzak and Khashaat, while already calling them its biostratigraphic equivalent and dating them with it. Governs `parent: Djadokhta`, the `Toogreeg` variant and `stages: [Campanian]` on [Tugrugyin](../../../stratigraphy/t/tugrugyin.yml).

**Evidence.**
- dashzeveg2005a, p. 2, Abstract: "Although the Bayn Dzak fauna lived somewhat earlier than that from Tugrugyin Shireh based on the superposition of the members, it is not clear how much earlier"; "New magnetostratigraphic data document a sequence of normal and reversed magnetozones through the Bayn Dzak Member up into the basal Tugrugyin Member".
- dashzeveg2005a, p. 25: "The oldest rocks in the mapped area consist of Late Cretaceous sands, primarily representing the Tugrugyin Member of the Djadokhta Formation"; "it is clear that the Tugrugyin Member overlies the Bayn Dzak Member, making it somewhat younger. However, it is not yet possible to estimate the precise temporal difference."
- dashzeveg2005a, p. 26: "The upper member is named the Tugrugyin Member and consists of very pale orange to light gray sands"; "the basal, light gray sands of the Tugrugyin Member in Units 4 and 5 are reversed"; "If the zones of reversed polarity at these localities correlate, it means that these units must be younger than Chron 34n".
- gradziński1977a, p. 296: the Djadokhta Formation "is known to be exposed only in a small area NE of the Bulgan settlement, in the localities of Bayn Dzak and Khashaat"; "the rocks at Toogreeg are distinctive because of very large-scale cross-laminations"; "these structures are typical of eolian dune slipfaces".
- gradziński1977a, p. 302: "the Toogreeg and Alag Teg beds are biostratigraphic equivalents of the Djadokhta Formation"; the ages of the formations are discussed with "their probable equivalents (i.e. beds of Toogreeg and Alag Teg, ...)"; "There are not radiometric age data available".

**Ruled out.**
- *The beds of Toogreeg as a unit outside the Djadokhta.* Gradziński and colleagues (1977) kept the Djadokhta to Bayn Dzak and Khashaat. Dashzeveg and colleagues (2005) trace the Tugrugyin sands above the Bayn Dzak Member at Tsonzh and Alag Teer and enlarge the formation to take both.
- *?Upper Santonian.* Gradziński and colleagues' "?upper Santonian and/or ?lower Campanian" for the Djadokhta covers the Toogreeg beds as its equivalent. It is ruled out for the formation in the 2026-11-23 entry in [Djadokhta](../d/djadokhta.md), and for this member the reversed basal sands, if correlated as Dashzeveg and colleagues propose, place it above Chron 34n.

**Open.** How much younger the member is than the Bayn Dzak Member, which Dashzeveg and colleagues (2005) could not estimate.

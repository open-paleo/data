# Tornhöj

## How do Christiansen and Bonde (2003) and Bonde (2012) tie the Tornhöj's fossil beds to the Berriasian?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Both place the same beds, the *Neomiodon* bed and the few meters of sand above it in Carl Nielsen's sandpit, in the Berriasian. Christiansen and Bonde's "?Early Valanginian" comes only from equating the Ryazanian with "Late Berriasian to ?Early Valanginian", and both works they cite against that equation keep the Ryazanian inside the Berriasian: Allen and Wimbledon (1991) as Mid–Late Berriasian, Horne (1995) as the whole stage. Bonde (2012) does not name the member but identifies the beds by the marker bed, the overlying sand and the pit, dates them late Berriasian, and correlates them with the Durlston Beds and the lower Ashdown Formation, the correlation Christiansen and Bonde make on condition that the beds are Berriasian. Both put the Valanginian higher in the Jydegaard Formation. This adds Horne's reading and the bed-level identification to the 2026-09-30 entry and does not change its conclusion. Governs `stages: [Berriasian]` on [Tornhöj](../../../stratigraphy/t/tornhöj.yml).

**Evidence.**
- christiansen2003a, p. 288: the basal part of the Tornhöj Member in Carl Nielsen's sandpit is "by far the richest locality of vertebrate fossils"; Fig. 1C is a "geological profile of the lower 8 meters of the Tornhöj Member, including the *Neomiodon* Bed (cross hatched) at the base".
- christiansen2003a, p. 288: "The basal 2-3 meters of the Jydegaard Formation are considered Late Berriasian to ?Early Valanginian, i.e. Ryazanian in age (…; in ALLEN & WIMBLEDON [1991: Fig. 2] Ryazanian corresponds, however, to Mid-Late Berriasian; HORNE [1995: Fig. 5] uses Ryazanian for the entire Berriasian)".
- christiansen2003a, p. 290: "providing the basal layers of the Jydegaard Formation are Berriasian, they correspond to the English sub-Wealden deposits, i.e. Durlston Beds, Upper Purbeck … and to the Lower Ashdown Formation".
- bonde2012a, p. 441: "The vertebrate fossils were found at the base of the Jydegaard Formation … in rusty, sideritic claystones, locally named the *Neomiodon* bed, and in the overlying 2–3 m of sand"; "The age of these sediments is Late Berriasian, probably corresponding to the middle part of the German Wealden (Middle Bückeberg Formation …), to the Durlston Beds, Upper Purbeck …, and to the lower Ashdown Formation at the base of the Hastings Beds"; "The remaining higher part of Jydegaard Formation might be of Valanginian age (Gravesen et al., 1982)".

**Ruled out.**
- *A Ryazanian that reaches the Valanginian.* The "?Early Valanginian" is Christiansen and Bonde's gloss on "Ryazanian", which both authorities they cite place inside the Berriasian (christiansen2003a, p. 288).

**Open.** Nothing.

## Where is the base of the Tornhöj Member, and how old is it?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** The base is the *Neomiodon* bed in Carl Nielsen's sandpit at Robbedale, Bornholm, and it is late Berriasian. The Valanginian belongs to the [Jydegaard Formation](../../../stratigraphy/j/jydegaard.yml) higher up, not to this member's base. Governs `stages: [Berriasian]` on Tornhöj and the stage of [*Dromaeosauroides bornholmensis*](../../../genera/D/Dromaeosauroides.yml).

**Evidence.**
- christiansen2003a, p. 288: "the basal part of the sandy Tornhöj Member, which rests directly on the Robbedale Formation, in the large 'Carl Nielsen's sandpit' at Robbedale"; "the one half meter thick *Neomiodon* Bed … is basal in the formation".
- bonde2012a, p. 441: "The age of these sediments is Late Berriasian"; p. 445, Fig. 24.4: "Late Berriasian dinosaurs from Robbedale. A, C. Nielsen's sandpit". Bonde does not use the name Tornhöj.

**Ruled out.**
- *Fossils from below the Neomiodon bed.* Both papers reject Rees (2000): "contra REES (2000) who suggests fossils are present below the *Neomiodon* bed" (christiansen2003a, p. 290); the fossils "could not have been collected from the few decimeters of sand below the *Neomiodon* bed, as claimed by Rees (2000)" (bonde2012a, p. 441).
- *Valanginian for the member's base.* Christiansen and Bonde (2003, p. 288) give "Late Berriasian to ?Early Valanginian, i.e. Ryazanian", then note that the Ryazanian "corresponds, however, to Mid-Late Berriasian" in Allen and Wimbledon (1991). The Valanginian is the formation's: it "supposedly includes most or all of the Valanginian" (christiansen2003a, p. 288), relaying Gravesen and colleagues (1982).

**Open.** The two accounts are not independent: Bonde co-authored the 2003 paper, which cites his review, and each cites the other. Bonde's section heading on p. 441 reads "(Early Berriasian)", against "Late Berriasian" in its text.

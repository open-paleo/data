# Tornhöj

## Where is the base of the Tornhöj Member, and how old is it?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** The base is the *Neomiodon* bed in Carl Nielsen's sandpit at Robbedale, Bornholm, and it is late Berriasian. The Valanginian belongs to the [Jydegaard Formation](../../../stratigraphy/j/jydegaard.yml) higher up, not to this member's base. Governs `stages: [Berriasian]` on Tornhöj and the stage of [*Dromaeosauroides bornholmensis*](../../../genera/D/Dromaeosauroides.yml).

**Evidence.**
- christiansen2003a, p. 288: "the basal part of the sandy Tornhöj Member, which rests directly on the Robbedale Formation, in the large 'Carl Nielsen's sandpit' at Robbedale"; "the one half meter thick *Neomiodon* Bed … is basal in the formation".
- bonde2012a, p. 441: "The age of these sediments is Late Berriasian"; p. 445, Fig. 24.4: "Late Berriasian dinosaurs from Robbedale. A, C. Nielsen's sandpit". Bonde does not use the name Tornhöj.

**Ruled out.**
- *Fossils from below the Neomiodon bed.* Both papers reject Rees (2000): "contra REES (2000) who suggests fossils are present below the *Neomiodon* bed" (christiansen2003a, p. 290); the fossils "could not have been collected from the few decimeters of sand below the *Neomiodon* bed, as claimed by Rees (2000)" (bonde2012a, p. 441).
- *Valanginian for the member's base.* Christiansen and Bonde (2003, p. 288) give "Late Berriasian to ?Early Valanginian, i.e. Ryazanian", then note that the Ryazanian "corresponds, however, to Mid-Late Berriasian" in Allen and Wimbledon (1991). The Valanginian is the formation's: it "supposedly includes most or all of the Valanginian" (christiansen2003a, p. 288), relaying Gravesen and colleagues (1982).

**Open.** The two accounts are not independent: Bonde co-authored the 2003 paper, which cites his review, and each cites the other. Bonde's section heading on p. 441 reads "(Early Berriasian)", against "Late Berriasian" in its text.

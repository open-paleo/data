# Terra Cotta Clay

## Is the Terra Cotta Clay Member Albian, Cenomanian, or both?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian on the current chart. Hamilton (1994) split the member across an Albian–Cenomanian unconformity on the older boundary placement, but his own maximum age for the whole Dakota Formation (97–99 Ma) falls in the Cenomanian on the ICS chart (base 100.5 Ma). Governs `stages: [Cenomanian]` on Terra Cotta Clay and on [*Silvisaurus condrayi*](../../../genera/S/Silvisaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hamilton1994a, p. 80: "the upper one-half of the Dakota Formation in Kansas, including all of the Janssen Clay Member and the upper part of the Terra Cotta Clay Member, is Upper Cretaceous", by correlation of unconformities from Colorado (Weimer 1984); p. 92: "The lower part of the formation is in the J Sequence and is late Albian".
- hamilton1994a, p. 90: a "maximum age … of 97 Ma (Weimer, 1984) … confirmed by Bergen et al. (1990) as 97 Ma to 99 Ma" for the Dakota.
- eaton1960a, p. 5: the *Silvisaurus* site "stands midway in the Terra Cotta clay member of the Dakota Formation, hence about one third of the way up in the Dakota".

**Ruled out.**
- *Albian for Silvisaurus.* Carpenter and colleagues (1998) give "Terra Cotta Clay (Albian: Hamilton, 1994)" (carpenter1998c, p. 258), a relay that does not weigh Hamilton's split or his maximum age.
- *The member straddling the stage boundary.* An earlier reading of Hamilton's split took the lower Terra Cotta as Albian; that rests on the late Albian of the boundary placement Hamilton used, not on a date.

**Open.** Where the *Silvisaurus* site lies relative to Hamilton's mid-Dakota unconformity: "midway" in the member does not decide it, because the unconformity also falls within the Terra Cotta. Carpenter (1999) gives "*Silvisaurus* from the Early Cenomanian of Kansas" (carpenter1999a, p. 250) without a source.

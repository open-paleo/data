# Terra Cotta Clay

## Does the Terra Cotta Clay Member span the Albian–Cenomanian boundary?

*2026-10-07*

**Conclusion.** Yes. Palynology tied to the members dates the lower Terra Cotta late Albian and the upper Terra Cotta latest Albian, locally early Cenomanian. Hamilton's (1994) 97 Ma maximum, which the 2026-09-30 entry placed in the Cenomanian, is an estimate attached to an Albian unconformity, not a measured date. Supersedes the 2026-09-30 entry. Governs `period: [Early Cretaceous, Late Cretaceous]` and `stages: [Albian, Cenomanian]` on [Terra Cotta Clay](../../../stratigraphy/t/terra-cotta-clay.yml), and the same on [*Silvisaurus condrayi*](../../../genera/S/Silvisaurus.yml), whose site cannot be placed within the member.

**Evidence.**
- ludvigson2010a, p. 9: palynostratigraphic Unit 1, with taxa "not known to range above the early Late Albian Kiowa–Skull Creek depositional cycle", includes strata "variously referred to the Nishnabotna Member, lower Terra Cotta Member, and Longford Member"; Unit 2, "variously assigned to the lower Woodbury and upper Terra Cotta Members", has "No Cenomanian indicators"; pp. 10–11: the Cenomanian Unit 3 "locally includes facies assigned to the upper Terra Cotta Member in areas of Nebraska and Kansas".
- brenner2000a, p. 875: Unit 1 "is recognized in lower Nishnabotna strata and lower Terra Cotta Clay Member units in the region", including a core in Lincoln County, Kansas; p. 876: "the Albian–Cenomanian boundary lies above the top of the Kiowa Formation in Kansas".
- weimer1986a, p. 397: unconformity "(3) Albian, ~97 m.y.", among dates that "are estimated", limited "because of problems in accurately defining the biostratigraphic level of the breaks and in the precision of radiometric dates". Weimer adapts his 1984 paper, the source Hamilton cites for 97 Ma.
- eaton1960a, p. 5: the *Silvisaurus* site "stands midway in the Terra Cotta clay member".

**Ruled out.**
- *Cenomanian only.* It rested on re-placing Hamilton's 97 Ma on the current chart; that number carries the Albian stage it was fitted to (see [methods](../../methods.md)).
- *A stage for Silvisaurus from Carpenter.* Carpenter and colleagues (1998) give "Albian: Hamilton, 1994" and Carpenter and colleagues (1999) "Early Cenomanian", each without placing the site within the member.

**Open.** Where the *Silvisaurus* site lies among the palynostratigraphic units: no source cited here correlates its Ottawa County site.

## Is the Terra Cotta Clay Member Albian, Cenomanian, or both?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian on the current chart. Hamilton (1994) split the member across an Albian–Cenomanian unconformity on the older boundary placement, but his own maximum age for the whole Dakota Formation (97–99 Ma) falls in the Cenomanian on the ICS chart (base 100.5 Ma). Governs `stages: [Cenomanian]` on Terra Cotta Clay and on [*Silvisaurus condrayi*](../../../genera/S/Silvisaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hamilton1994a, p. 80: "the upper one-half of the Dakota Formation in Kansas, including all of the Janssen Clay Member and the upper part of the Terra Cotta Clay Member, is Upper Cretaceous", by correlation of unconformities from Colorado (Weimer 1984); p. 92: "The lower part of the formation is in the J Sequence and is late Albian".
- hamilton1994a, p. 90: a "maximum age … of 97 Ma (Weimer, 1984) … confirmed by Bergen et al. (1990) as 97 Ma to 99 Ma" for the Dakota.
- eaton1960a, p. 5: the *Silvisaurus* site "stands midway in the Terra Cotta clay member of the Dakota Formation, hence about one third of the way up in the Dakota".

**Ruled out.**
- *Albian for Silvisaurus.* Carpenter and colleagues (1998) give "Terra Cotta Clay (Albian: Hamilton, 1994)" (carpenter1998c, p. 258), a relay that does not weigh Hamilton's split or his maximum age.
- *The member straddling the stage boundary.* An earlier reading of Hamilton's split took the lower Terra Cotta as Albian; that rests on the late Albian of the boundary placement Hamilton used, not on a date.

**Open.** Where the *Silvisaurus* site lies relative to Hamilton's mid-Dakota unconformity: "midway" in the member does not decide it, because the unconformity also falls within the Terra Cotta. Carpenter (1999) gives "*Silvisaurus* from the Early Cenomanian of Kansas" (carpenter1999a, p. 250) without a source.

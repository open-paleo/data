# Altmühltal

## What do Niebuhr and Pürner (2014) and Foth and colleagues (2025) say about the Altmühltal's age and its neighbors?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Niebuhr and Pürner (2014) give the formation as Upper Kimmeridgian to Lower Tithonian and date it by ammonites from the uppermost *beckeri* zone to the middle *hybonotum* zone. Its plattenkalks are diachronous: after Schweigert (2007) the plate limestones of Solnhofen, Eichstätt and Pfalzpaint are not contemporaneous, the Lower Pfalzpaint Subformation is the oldest and the Upper Solnhofen Subformation the youngest, and the proposed Kimmeridgian–Tithonian boundary runs through the Walting–Pfalzpaint occurrence. The formation is 40–90 m thick at Solnhofen. Foth and colleagues (2025) follow that scheme: the Painten Formation holds the contemporaneous plattenkalks farther east, the Torleite Formation underlies the Painten, and the Mörnsheim Formation lies on top of both the Altmühltal and the Painten. Of the eleven *Archaeopteryx* specimens they count, nine come from the Altmühltal, one from the Kimmeridgian–Tithonian boundary in the lowermost Painten and one from the Mörnsheim. Governs `stages: [Kimmeridgian, Tithonian]` on [Altmühltal](../../../stratigraphy/a/altmühltal.yml), unchanged; the variants and the Kimmeridgian are settled in the 2026-11-18 and 2026-10-28 entries.

**Evidence.**
- niebuhr2014a, p. 40: "Chronostratigraphy: Upper Kimmeridgian, Lower Tithonian" (translated).
- niebuhr2014a, pp. 41–42, Biostratigraphy: "Determination by ammonites yields the uppermost Upper Kimmeridgian (uppermost beckeri zone) to lower Lower Tithonian (middle hybonotum zone)"; "Schweigert (2007) was able for the first time to faunistically prove that the plate limestones of Solnhofen, Eichstätt, and Pfalzpaint ... are not contemporaneous"; the plattenkalks are "clearly diachronous and become progressively younger from east (Pfalzpaint) to west (Solnhofen)" (translated).
- niebuhr2014a, p. 41: "Thickness: 40-90 m at Solnhofen" (translated).
- niebuhr2014a, p. 41, Contemporaneous units: "On the eastern Southern Frankenalb, the Tangrintel and Painten Formations are contemporaneous" (translated).
- foth2025a, p. 18: *Archaeopteryx* "is only known from the lower Tithonian of Bavaria, Germany, with nine out of eleven specimens currently referred to this genus having been unearthed from the Altmühltal Formation"; the Schamhaupten specimen "comes from the Kimmeridgian/Tithonian boundary in the lowermost part of the Painten Formation"; the Daiting specimen "comes from the Mörnsheim Formation that overlies the Altmühltal Formation".
- foth2025a, pp. 18–19: "The geologically younger Mörnsheim Formation lies on top of both these formations"; "In more eastern areas, the upper part of the Torleite Formation, which underlies the Painten Formation, yielded important vertebrate fossils as well".

**Ruled out.**
- *The Painten Formation as the unit beneath the Altmühltal.* The Schamhaupten specimen's place at the Kimmeridgian–Tithonian boundary "in the lowermost part of the Painten Formation" can be read as putting the Painten below. Foth and colleagues give the Painten as the contemporaneous plattenkalks east of Ingolstadt, with the Mörnsheim above both, and Niebuhr and Pürner list it among the Altmühltal's contemporaneous units. The two formations are lateral equivalents.
- *The lower Tithonian as the formation's whole age.* It is the age Foth and colleagues give for *Archaeopteryx*, not for the formation, which by their own account and Niebuhr and Pürner's begins in the latest Kimmeridgian.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the Solnhofen Lithographic Limestone the Altmühltal Formation?

*2026-11-18*

**Conclusion.** Yes; the Solnhofen Lithographic Limestone is the traditional name for the plattenkalks Niebuhr and Pürner (2014) formalized as the Altmühltal Formation, and the registry carries it as a variant. Its separate entry, which listed Altmühltal among its own variants, is retired, since no record or unit names it. Governs `variants` on [Altmühltal](../../../stratigraphy/a/altmühltal.yml), and supersedes the 2026-10-28 entry below as a ruling on a unit of its own.

**Evidence.**
- niebuhr2014a, p. 7: "The Altmühltal Formation contains the typical lithographic plattenkalks of the western Southern Franconian Alb, which formerly has been termed 'Solnhofener Schichten'".
- foth2025a, p. 18: "the 'Solnhofen limestone' *sensu stricto* is part of the Altmühltal Formation and restricted to the area northwest of Ingolstadt, whereas the contemporaneous plattenkalks more to the east belong to the Painten Formation".
- ding2020a, appendix table: Archaeopteryx horizons given as "Solnhofen Limestone (Altmühltal Formation)".

**Ruled out.**
- *A separate entry.* No record has named the Solnhofen Lithographic Limestone since *Wellnhoferia* was retired into *Archaeopteryx*, and no unit gives it as parent, so it fails the registry rule in research/methods.md; it was also a second entry for one unit.
- *The broad sense as the Altmühltal.* The name has also covered the contemporaneous eastern plattenkalks, now the Painten Formation: Holtz and colleagues (2004) use it for *Compsognathus* at Jachenhausen, which the *Compsognathus* note places in the Painten. A record meeting the name in that sense takes the Painten, not this variant.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does Holtz's (2004) "Kimmeridgian or basal Tithonian" change the unit's stages?

*2026-10-28*

**Conclusion.** No. A paper's undecided dating between two readings contributes every stage it allows, so Holtz's reading gives the Kimmeridgian and the Tithonian, and both are already in the unit's stages from Foth and colleagues' (2025) late Kimmeridgian to early Tithonian. The record note once said the hedge was not added because it falls at a stage boundary; that is not the rule. Governed `stages: [Kimmeridgian, Tithonian]` on the Solnhofen Lithographic Limestone entry, now a variant of [Altmühltal](../../../stratigraphy/a/altmühltal.yml).

**Evidence.**
- holtz2004a, p. 105: "the Kimmeridgian or basal Tithonian Solnhofen Lithographic Limestone of Jachenhausen".
- foth2025a, p. 18: "partially laminated, partially silty limestones of late Kimmeridgian to early Tithonian age, commonly referred to as the 'Solnhofen limestone'".
- foth2025a, p. 19: the Altmühltal Formation "begins with the latest Kimmeridgian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Leaving Holtz's reading out because it hedges at a boundary.* CONTRIBUTING ([Age](../../../CONTRIBUTING.md#age-period-and-stages)) has an undecided dating contribute every alternative it allows.

**Open.** Nothing.

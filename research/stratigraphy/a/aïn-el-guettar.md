# Aïn el Guettar

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Aïn el Guettar?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Aptian and Albian. Fanti and colleagues (2012), the stratigraphic revision of the Tataouine basin, date the formation from its own sections as Albian throughout: they refer the Chenini Member to the lower Albian, the Oum ed Diab Member below the "Barre Vraconnienne" to the middle Albian, and the "Barre Vraconnienne" of the Rhadouane Member, which they make the formation's top, to the late Albian. The Aptian comes from a palynological "upper Aptian–lower Albian" age that they report from two earlier studies and call the only direct evidence, from Fanti and colleagues (2014), who give "upper Aptian–Albian", and from Ibrahim and colleagues (2020), who regard "the Aptian-Albian age as most likely" on its crocodyliforms. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on [Aïn el Guettar](../../../stratigraphy/a/aïn-el-guettar.yml).

**Evidence.**
- fanti2012b, p. 2: "the Aïn El Guettar Formation (Chenini and Oum ed Diab members)", above the Merbah el Asfer Group.
- fanti2012b, p. 6: Busson "considered this stratigraphic interval as Barremian–Aptian in age"; palynomorphs "suggest an upper Aptian–lower Albian age for the Aïn El Guettar Formation".
- fanti2012b, p. 7: "we refer the Chenini Member to the lower Albian"; "In this study the 'Barre Vraconnienne' is considered as late Albian in age: therefore, the Oum ed Diab member and the vertebrate fauna recovered in this unit ... is middle Albian in age".
- fanti2012b, p. 14: "we include the Rhadouane Member within the Aïn El Guettar Formation rather than the Cenomanian Zebbag Formation".
- ibrahim2020a, p. 183: "Its age has been variously estimated to be as old as Barremian (Busson 1967, 1970) or as young as late Albian (Fanti et al. 2015). We regard the Aptian-Albian age as most likely, given the recent discovery of diagnostic remains of the giant crocodyliform *Sarcosuchus* ... and the smaller notosuchian *Araripesuchus*".
- fanti2015a, p. 1, Abstract: "The type specimen was collected from the lower Albian, fluvio-estuarine deposits of the Ain el Guettar Formation".
- fanti2015a, p. 9, Type locality and horizon: "Ain el Guettar Formation, Oum ed Diab Member, Jebel El Mra, Tataouine Governorate, southern Tunisia; early Albian".

**Ruled out.**
- *A Barremian base.* Busson (1967) is the source: Fanti and colleagues (2012) relay its "Barremian–Aptian" for the interval, and Ibrahim and colleagues (2020) its "as old as Barremian". Both report it among older estimates and set it aside, Fanti and colleagues for their Albian dating and Ibrahim and colleagues for an Aptian–Albian age. No work cited dates the formation Barremian in its own voice.
- *A late Albian age attributed to Fanti and colleagues (2015).* Ibrahim and colleagues (2020) cite that paper for the young end of the range, but it gives the early Albian, in its abstract and in its type horizon, and its text has no late Albian. The late Albian stands on Fanti and colleagues (2012), whose formation top, the "Barre Vraconnienne", is late Albian; it adds no stage beyond the Albian.

**Open.** Busson (1967) has not been read; it would show what the Barremian–Aptian dating rests on.

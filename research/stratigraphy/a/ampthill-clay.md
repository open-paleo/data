# Ampthill Clay

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Ampthill Clay, and which Yorkshire clay is it?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Oxfordian, Middle to Late. Cope and colleagues (1980) chart the Ampthill Clay of South Cambridgeshire from the tenuiserratum Zone, the highest zone of their Middle Oxfordian, through the glosense and serratum zones to the regulare Zone of the Upper Oxfordian, and give the lowest Ampthill Clay tenuiserratum Zone ammonites. In north-east Cambridgeshire the succession lacks only the rosenkrantzi Zone, the last of the Oxfordian, which they attribute to erosion beneath the Kimmeridge Clay. At the Ampthill Cutting, the type locality, the only ammonites found suggest the ilovaiskii Subzone of the glosense Zone. In Yorkshire, Cope and colleagues keep the name Ampthill Clay for the Middle to Upper Oxfordian clay above the Corallian Beds, which Wright (1972) had identified as Kimmeridge Clay, and rename the clay below the North Grimston Cementstone, reported as "Ampthill Clay" by Wright (1976), the Langton Clay. Governs `stages` on [Ampthill Clay](../../../stratigraphy/a/ampthill-clay.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cope1980a, Fig. 11b, column O11 (South Cambridgeshire): "AMPTHILL CLAY 52 m" runs from the C. tenuiserratum Zone (Middle Oxfordian) to the A. regulare Zone (Upper Oxfordian), with the "Phosphate beds" at its top.
- cope1980a, p. 71: "The lowest Ampthill Clay contains Cardioceras spp. of the tenuiserratum Zone".
- cope1980a, p. 73: "The Ampthill Clay succession is almost complete except for the rosenkrantzi Zone at the top; this is almost certainly absent due to erosion beneath the Kimmeridge Clay."
- cope1980a, p. 70: "The succession in the Ampthill Cutting—the type locality of the Ampthill Clay—has never been measured adequately"; "The only ammonites found suggest the ilovaiskii Subzone of the glosense Zone."
- cope1980a, p. 74: "Previously, this clay had been identified as Kimmeridge Clay"; "Glosense and serratum Zone ammonites were found in the Acklam area boreholes".
- cope1980a, p. 74: "it is inadvisable to have two Ampthill Clays in Yorkshire, one above and one below the North Grimston Cementstone"; the Ampthill Clay is kept for the upper clay because "it has priority and it is the most widespread Middle/Upper Oxfordian clay formation in Yorkshire", and the lower clay "is here named the Langton Clay".

**Ruled out.**
- *Kimmeridge Clay for the Yorkshire clay above the Corallian Beds.* Wright (1972) identified it so; Cope and colleagues report glosense and serratum Zone ammonites from it in the Acklam boreholes and treat it as Ampthill Clay.
- *The clay below the North Grimston Cementstone south of Malton as Ampthill Clay.* Wright (1976) reported it as "Ampthill Clay"; Cope and colleagues rename it the Langton Clay to avoid two Ampthill Clays in Yorkshire, keeping the name for the more widespread upper clay.

**Open.** Nothing.

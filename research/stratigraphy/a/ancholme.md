# Ancholme

## Does the Ancholme Group reach the Tithonian?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Yes: the group runs from the Callovian to the early Tithonian. Barron and colleagues (2012) give it as "Callovian to Kimmeridgian", with the Kimmeridge Clay as its youngest formation. That is the British Kimmeridgian, which Cope and colleagues (1980) describe as customarily equated with the Kimmeridge Clay and running up to the Portland Beds. Hesselbo and colleagues (2020) equate the British "Bolonian", the upper Kimmeridge Clay above the international Kimmeridgian–Tithonian boundary, with what is Tithonian internationally, so the top of the group is early Tithonian on the current chart. Governs the Tithonian in `stages` on [Ancholme](../../../stratigraphy/a/ancholme.yml).

**Evidence.**
- barron2012a, p. 18: the Kellaways, Oxford Clay, West Walton, Ampthill Clay and Kimmeridge Clay formations "are assembled into the Middle to Upper Jurassic (Callovian to Kimmeridgian) Ancholme Group".
- cope1980a, p. 77: "In Britain it is customary to equate 'Kimmeridgian' with the Kimmeridge Clay and 'Portlandian' with the Portland Beds", so that "the British Upper Kimmeridgian (elegans Zone upwards) is already Portlandian in France".
- hesselbo2020a, pp. 974–975: "The “Bolonian” of Britain is equivalent to the upper Kimmeridgian clay formation between the Kimmeridgian–Tithonian boundary and the Portland Sand".
- gaunt1992a, p. 30: "The open-marine environment was brought to an end in Portlandian times by widespread uplift, and considerable erosion followed."

**Ruled out.**
- *Callovian to Kimmeridgian, read on the international chart.* Barron and colleagues' "Kimmeridgian" is the British stage that takes in the whole Kimmeridge Clay; read as the international stage it would leave out the upper Kimmeridge Clay, which is Tithonian.
- *Berriasian.* None of the works cited gives the group a Berriasian. Gaunt and colleagues (1992) end its open-marine deposition in Portlandian times, with uplift and erosion following.

**Open.** Nothing.

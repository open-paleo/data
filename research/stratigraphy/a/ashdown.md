# Ashdown

## Does the Ashdown reach the Hauterivian?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** No. The Hauterivian rests on one spinosaurid crown from near Bexhill that Charig and Milner (1997) give as from the "Ashdown Sand, (Hauterivian)", a provenance that Naish and Martill (2007) relay. Barker and colleagues (2023) trace the crown to Ashdown Brickworks on Turkey Road, the site Charig and Milner name, and report that the only beds exposed there are the Valanginian Wadhurst Clay Formation, after Turmine-Juhel and colleagues (2019): the tooth is neither from the Ashdown nor Hauterivian. The formation itself they place as "late Berriasian–early Valanginian". Governs `stages: [Berriasian, Valanginian]` on [Ashdown](../../../stratigraphy/a/ashdown.yml).

**Evidence.**
- charig1997a, p. 54: "Ashdown Sand, (Hauterivian) at Redlands Bricks, Ashdown Works, Scallets Wood, Turkey Road, Bexhill-on-Sea, East Sussex", a single crown "(Bexhill Museum BEXHM:1993.485), collected by Mr D. Brockhurst in 1993".
- naish2007a, p. 502: "Isolated British baryonychine material is known from the Hauterivian Ashdown Beds Formation of East Sussex".
- barker2023a, p. 6, Geological context and provenance of HASMG G369a: the Hastings Group "is comprised of the older (late Berriasian–early Valanginian) Ashdown Formation, followed by the Wadhurst Clay Formation (Valanginian) and Tunbridge Wells Formation (late Valanginian ...)".
- barker2023a, p. 18, Comparative anatomy: "BEXHM 1995.485 ... Charig & Milner (1997) misreported the accession number of this specimen as ''BEXHM 1993.485''".
- barker2023a, p. 24, The British spinosaurid record and biogeography of early spinosaurids: "The term ''Ashdown Sands'' is now defunct ..., having been introduced by Drew (1861) before being formalised to Ashdown Formation by Rawson (1992)"; the find site is Ashdown Brickworks, "where the only exposures are of the Valanginian Wadhurst Clay Formation ... BEXHM 1995.485 therefore cannot be Hauterivian or from the Ashdown Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *A Hauterivian Ashdown, from the Bexhill crown.* Charig and Milner (1997) give the stage in a locality line, and Naish and Martill (2007) repeat it with Charig and Milner among their sources. Barker and colleagues (2023) show the site exposes only Valanginian Wadhurst Clay, so the crown dates neither the Ashdown nor any Hauterivian bed. The site's name, Ashdown Works or Ashdown Brickworks, is no evidence that the beds there are the Ashdown Formation. Barker and colleagues identify the site partly from personal communications, but Charig and Milner name the same place, Turkey Road at Bexhill, themselves.

**Open.** Turmine-Juhel and colleagues (2019), the source of the Wadhurst-only exposure at Ashdown Brickworks, have not been read.

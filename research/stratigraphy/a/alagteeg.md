# Alagteeg

## Is Alag Teeg in the Djadokhta Formation or the Alagteeg Formation, and how old is it?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** The Alagteeg Formation, the fluvial lower part split from the Djadokhta Formation by Hasegawa and colleagues (2009). Its age is poorly constrained, probably Campanian and possibly older. Governs `location.formation: Alagteeg` on [*Plesiohadros djadokhtaensis*](../../../genera/P/Plesiohadros.yml) and [*Abdarainurus barsboldi*](../../../genera/A/Abdarainurus.yml), and the stages on Alagteeg.

**Evidence.**
- averianov2020a, p. 2 (online-first): Hasegawa and colleagues distinguish "sub-humid fluvial lithofacies in the lower part (Alagteeg Formation) and semi-arid aeolian lithofacies in the upper part (Djadokhta Formation)"; "The geological age of the Alagteeg Formation is not well constrained".
- arbour2015a, p. 27 (online-first): "The Alagteeg Formation represents the fluvially deposited sediments previously considered the lower part of the Djadokhta Formation".
- penkalski2026a, p. 3 (online-first): Hasegawa and colleagues "referred … much of the section at Alag Teeg to the new Alagteeg Formation" and "tentatively correlated it with the upper part of the Baynshiree Formation (although see below)"; the beds "are roughly coeval with the lowermost beds at Bayn Dzak", reflecting "this time-transgressive change".

**Ruled out.**
- *Djadokhta Formation.* Older papers, and some later ones, place Alag Teeg in the Djadokhta, for example Dashzeveg and colleagues' section at "Aleg Teer" with the Bayn Dzak and Tugrugyin members (dashzeveg2005a, p. 22, Fig. 11) and Tsogtbaatar and colleagues (2014), who still write Djadokhta but describe "Alluvial and paludal facies" with the holotype "in mudstone beds in the lowermost part of the geological section" (tsogtbaatar2014a, p. 109), which matches the fluvial Alagteeg (our inference).

**Open.** The age. Hasegawa and colleagues' tentative correlation with the upper Baynshiree would make it pre-Campanian; Penkalski and colleagues' correlation with the lowest Bayn Dzak beds points to the Campanian. Jones (2026) gives "85 to 72 million years" (jones2026b, p. 258), consistent with Santonian–Campanian, but no primary source states a Santonian age. Hasegawa and colleagues (2009) have not been read.

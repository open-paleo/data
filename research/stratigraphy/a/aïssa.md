# Aïssa

## Whose determination is the Middle Jurassic of the lower member, and is the dispute a difference between troughs?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** The Middle Jurassic reading is Kacemi and colleagues' (2026), published by the author of the 2005 thesis it cites, and the dispute is a disagreement about the same beds. Kacemi and colleagues state in their own text that the Lophinae oysters of member MI are characteristic of "the Middle Jurassic (Bajocian to Callovian)", crediting Kacemi (2005), a master's thesis; a thesis never carries an age alone, but where a published version exists it is cited instead, and the 2026 paper is that version. Within the pre-Atlas trough the same paper also labels member MI Callovo-Oxfordian–Kimmeridgian in its Fig. 4, and it gives the Atlas trough a Kimmeridgian–Portlandian age only by regional correlation, credited to Bassoullet (1973), an unpublished dissertation. The conflict is therefore within one trough and one member, not between troughs. Governs `stages`, Bajocian to Tithonian, and the `dispute` on [Aïssa](../../../stratigraphy/a/aïssa.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- kacemi2026a, p. 9: "The presence of Lophinae in member MI is characteristic of the Middle Jurassic (Bajocian to Callovian) (Kacemi, 2005)" (translated).
- kacemi2026a, Fig. 4, p. 6: member MI labelled "Callovo-Oxfordien-Kimmeridgien" and members MII and MIII "Kimmeridgien - Portlandien".
- kacemi2026a, p. 14: in the Atlas trough, "all relative dating remains impossible in the absence of fossils. This formation, by regional correlation, can be attributed to the Late Jurassic (Kimmeridgian-Portlandian) (Bassoullet, 1973)" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *Dropping the Bajocian and Bathonian as thesis-only.* The reading is published in Kacemi and colleagues (2026), in their own text.
- *A lateral age difference between troughs.* The two ages for member MI are given for the same trough.

**Open.** What the Lophinae are and where they sit in member MI, which the 2005 thesis reports in detail.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Aïssa Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Bajocian to Tithonian, unchanged. The Late Jurassic reading is Kimmeridgian–Portlandian: Kacemi and colleagues (2026) give it "by regional correlation" for the section in the Atlas trough, where they find no fossils, and Amar-Bekada and Si Ali (2023) follow Kacemi's (2005) scheme, which puts the Kimmeridgian in the lower member, the Portlandian in the upper member, and the Callovian–Oxfordian in the Djara Formation beneath. The Portlandian is Tithonian in international stages, since Hesselbo and colleagues (2020) place the base of the Tithonian close to the base of d'Orbigny's Portlandian. The Middle Jurassic reading comes from the lower member in the pre-Atlas trough, the Rouis el Djir Alternations, whose Lophinae oysters Kacemi and colleagues (2026) take as "characteristic of the Middle Jurassic (Bajocian to Callovian)"; the same paper gives the dinosaur biotope of that trough as Callovian–Kimmeridgian. Läng and Mahammed (2010) call the formation Middle Jurassic and suppose the *Chebsaurus* site Callovian, after Kacemi (2005), while listing earlier datings of the site from "indeterminate Dogger" to Oxfordian–Portlandian. The Oxfordian is also named by Amar-Bekada and Si Ali where they introduce the Malm, more loosely than in their scheme. Governs `stages` on [Aïssa](../../../stratigraphy/a/aïssa.yml); the conflict between the readings is carried in its `dispute`.

**Evidence.**
- amar-bekada2023a, p. 9: the Malm "constitutes the Oxfordian, Kimmeridgian, and Portlandian. It is represented largely by the 'Formation of Aïssa' and occupies large anticlines such as Dj. Aïssa, Dj. Mekther, Dj. Morghad" (translated).
- amar-bekada2023a, p. 21: of the formations of Aïssa, Tiloula and Tiout, "the first formation constitutes the anticlines" (translated).
- amar-bekada2023a, p. 22: at Djebel Djeraouine "a formation 625 m thick" whose layers follow "the layers of the previous formation; the 'Djara Formation'"; at its top the green clays and dolomite beds "of the 'Tiloula Formation'" appear (translated).
- amar-bekada2023a, p. 23: "The 'Aïssa Formation' of approximately 625 m thickness constitutes a filling megasequence (4th order), with a general regressive tendency", "a succession of deltaic sequences" (translated).
- amar-bekada2023a, p. 43: after Kacemi (2005), "The Djara Formation: Lower Bathonian to Callovian Oxfordian, units 10 and 11" and "The Aïssa Formation: Kimmeridgian-Portlandian, units 12, 13 and 14" (translated).
- amar-bekada2023a, p. 56: unit 12 "encompasses the 'Lower Member' of the 'Aïssa Formation'; the appropriate age for this unit is Kimmeridgian (upper Jurassic)", and the "Upper Member" is "of Portlandian age" (translated).
- hesselbo2020a, p. 973: Neumayr's placement of the Kimmeridgian–Tithonian boundary "corresponded closely with the boundary between the 'Portlandian' and Kimmeridgian Stages as initially assigned by d'Orbigny".
- kacemi2026a, p. 4, Geological framework: below the Aïssa lies "the Djara Formation (Bathonian to Callovian-Oxfordian)", and the Larouia-Aourak-Oum El Arar syncline "consists of a part of the upper member of the Djara Formation and the Aïssa Formation" (translated).
- kacemi2026a, p. 5, Materials and methods: "It was in the Aïssa Formation that the remains of the Ksour dinosaur (*Chebsaurus*) were discovered in 2001" (translated).
- kacemi2026a, p. 9, Aïssa Formation in the pre-Atlas trough: "The presence of Lophinae in member MI is characteristic of the Middle Jurassic (Bajocian to Callovian)" (translated).
- kacemi2026a, p. 14, Aïssa Formation in the Atlas trough: "all relative dating remains impossible in the absence of fossils. This formation, by regional correlation, can be attributed to the Late Jurassic (Kimmeridgian-Portlandian)" (translated).
- kacemi2026a, p. 18, Discussion: the pre-Atlas trough "probably served as a biotope for dinosaurs and crocodilians during the Callovian-Kimmeridgian (Upper Jurassic)" (translated).
- läng2010a, p. 142: *Chebsaurus algeriensis* "from the Middle Jurassic Aïssa formation of the Naama Province, Algeria".
- läng2010a, p. 143: past studies of the site suggest "'indeterminate Dogger'", "Bathonian", "Callovian (Kacemi 2005) or Oxfordian to Portlandian", and "it is not so easy to determine clearly the age of the dinosaur site".
- läng2010a, p. 144: "the most recently published and soundly argued study of Kacemi (2005) led us to suppose that *Chebsaurus* should be dated from the Callovian".

**Ruled out.** None. The Middle and Late Jurassic readings are not reconciled by any work cited, and the record carries both.

**Open.** Whether the Middle Jurassic oysters and the Kimmeridgian–Portlandian correlation describe the same beds: the correlation is given for the Atlas-trough section, the oysters for the pre-Atlas trough. Amar-Bekada and Si Ali's lithological table (p. 54) gives the lower member as Portlandian and the upper as Kimmeridgian, the reverse of their text (p. 56); the text agrees with the equivalence scheme on p. 43. They also give the overlying Tiloula Formation's lower member as Tithonian (p. 54), so on their usage the Aïssa's Portlandian would be at most early Tithonian; this is an inference, not a statement in the thesis. Kacemi (2005), the source of the scheme and of the Lophinae reading, would settle both, but is an unpublished thesis.

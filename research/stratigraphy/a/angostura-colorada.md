# Angostura Colorada

## Does the Allenian age of Leanza and colleagues (2004) date the Angostura Colorada Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Only by correlation with the Allen Formation. The Late Campanian–Early Maastrichtian that Leanza and colleagues (2004) print is the age of the Allenian tetrapod assemblage, whose main unit in the Neuquén Basin is the Allen Formation; they include the Angostura Colorada and Los Alamitos formations of northeastern Patagonia because these "represent similar environments, age and faunas", and they report Hugo and Leanza's (2001a) correlation of the Angostura Colorada with the basal member of the Allen. The age heads a table that names five formations at once, and the paper warns that a tetrapod assemblage cannot date a unit. Its age for this formation is therefore the Allen Formation's, not one drawn from the Angostura Colorada's own fossils or rocks. Governs the weight given to leanza2004a in `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Angostura Colorada](../../../stratigraphy/a/angostura-colorada.yml).

**Evidence.**
- leanza2004a, pp. 71–72: "Hugo and Leanza (2001a) proposed a correlation of the basal member with the Angostura Colorada Formation".
- leanza2004a, p. 72: hadrosaurids such as *Kritosaurus australis* "are common in the Los Alamitos Formation at Los Alamitos, the Angostura Colorada Formation at Laguna Carri-Laufquen and the Allen Formation at Salitral Moreno".
- leanza2004a, p. 76, Allenian table: "Age: Late Campanian–Early Maastrichtian"; "Main stratigraphic units: Allen, Angostura Colorada, Loncoche, Los Alamitos and La Colonia formations"; "Aeolosaurus sp. ... and related species / Angostura Colorada Formation".
- leanza2004a, p. 79: "The main stratigraphic unit of the Neuquén Basin yielding this tetrapod assemblage is the Allen Formation. However, the equivalent units in north-eastern Patagonia (the Angostura Colorada and Los Alamitos formations) represent similar environments, age and faunas, so their taxa are also included".
- leanza2004a, pp. 81–82: "tetrapod assemblages cannot be used in order to determine the age of a certain stratigraphic unit".

**Ruled out.**
- *Leanza and colleagues (2004) date the Angostura Colorada Formation to the Late Campanian–Early Maastrichtian on its own evidence.* The range is the Allenian assemblage's, given once for five formations, and the authors state that tetrapod assemblages cannot determine the age of a unit; the paper's link between this formation and that age is its equivalence with the Allen Formation.

**Open.** Nothing.

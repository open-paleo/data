# Adamantina

## What do the cited works say about the age and the name of the Adamantina?

*2026-11-22*

**Conclusion.** Turonian to Maastrichtian, the union of the published readings, and the age stays disputed. The two original readings rest on the same evidence, ostracods, were published the same year, and do not overlap: Turonian–Santonian from Dias-Brito and colleagues (2001), and Campanian–Maastrichtian from Gobbo-Rodrigues (2001) as Batezelli (2015) reports it. Pinheiro and colleagues (2018) give Turonian–Early Maastrichtian in a synthesis of earlier studies, and Arai and Dias-Brito (2023) infer late Santonian to early Campanian from the palynology of the São Carlos and Araçatuba formations and the arrangement of the units, not from fossils of the Adamantina itself. The only radioisotopic date, from Castro and colleagues (2018), is a maximum depositional age for one bed at General Salgado, São Paulo: a floor for that bed, not an age for the base or the whole of the formation. The name stands: Fernandes and Coimbra (2000) divided the formation of Soares and colleagues (1980) among new units, and Arai and Dias-Brito (2023) keep Adamantina as the senior name over the Vale do Rio do Peixe Formation. Governs `stages`, `dispute` and the unit's name on [Adamantina](../../../stratigraphy/a/adamantina.yml).

**Evidence.**
- soares1980a, p. 177, Abstract: "is proposed for them the term Bauru Group which is constituted by the following four Formations", the Adamantina the third; the earlier surveys had achieved "the division of what was then considered the Bauru Formation into units mappable at a scale of 1:250,000" (Introduction, translated).
- soares1980a, p. 180: earlier workers proposed "informal designations such as member, facies, or lithofacies for this set of rocks", among them "the São José do Rio Preto lithofacies" and "the Taciba and Ubirajara facies" (translated).
- soares1980a, p. 182: "the presence of a titanosaurid allowed Huene (1933, in Price, 1950), by correlation with similar fossils in sediments of Patagonia, to assign a Senonian (Upper Cretaceous) age to this Formation" (translated).
- fernandes2000a, p. 720: the Vale do Rio do Peixe Formation "corresponds to much of the former Adamantina Formation defined by Soares et al. (1980)"; the Bauru Group "is composed of the Uberaba …, Vale do Rio do Peixe, Araçatuba, São José do Rio Preto, Presidente Prudente … and Marília … formations" (translated), with no Adamantina.
- dias-brito2001a, p. 246, Abstract: the associations of *"Chara" barbosai* - *Ilyocypris* cf. *riograndensis*, "found in the Adamantina Formation", and *Amblyochara* sp. - *Neuquenocypris minor mineira*, "found in the Marília Formation, Ponte Alta Member, represent two distinct groups that are respectively Turonian-Santonian and Maastrichtian"; "a hiatus, encompassing more than 11 Ma, separates those two formations".
- dias-brito2001a, p. 282: the hiatus would mark "the boundary between two sequences: the lower, which has its upper boundary equivalent to the top of the Adamantina Formation (Turonian-Santonian), and the upper, represented by the Marília Formation" (translated).
- batezelli2015a, p. 4, Chronostratigraphy: "Gobbo-Rodrigues (2001) identified" five ostracod species, which it names, "in the Adamantina and Marília formations. According to this author, the fossil assemblage can be compared to the Neuquén (Argentina) and Republic of Congo assemblages, with Campanian-Maastrichtian ages."
- pinheiro2018a, p. 6, Historical background: following Soares and colleagues, the formations of the Bauru Group "have the following ages based on the integration of all studies done so far here analyzed", among them "Adamantina (Turonian–Early Maastrichtian)".
- pinheiro2018a, p. 8, Stratigraphic analysis: authors usually assign "Turonian-Santonian age, as proposed by Dias-Brito et al. …, an interval of more than 10 Ma …, for the single horizon that provided the holotype specimen".
- pinheiro2018a, p. 10, Stratigraphic analysis: the unconformity at the top of the Presidente Prudente Formation "is correlated with the upper unconformity of the Adamantina Formation in Ibirá, and São José do Rio Preto regions, as well as with the subaerial unconformity of the contact between the Adamantina and Marília formations"; "Based on the Maastrichtian age for the Marília Formation, we consider this subaerial unconformity to be the boundary between the Campanian and Maastrichtian in the Bauru Group."
- arai2023a, p. 2, Abstract: "Palynological studies have identified upper Santonian and lower Campanian sediments in the São Carlos and Araçatuba formations, respectively"; "The combination of these data with the stratigraphic-spatial arrangement of the units allows us to infer a late Santonian to early Campanian age for the Adamantina/Uberaba formations"; "It is not ruled out that older Santonian or even pre-Santonian strata compose the Adamantina/Araçatuba formations."
- arai2023a, p. 11, 2.2.3 Formação Adamantina: "the terms Adamantina and Vale do Rio do Peixe are synonymous, with the former being senior and having priority of use" (translated).
- castro2018a, p. 2, 2.1. U-Pb geochronology: zircons were isolated from a sample of "the bed where the mammalian remain was collected", at Buriti Farm, General Salgado, São Paulo (p. 3, Type locality and horizon). The paper's text does not place the bed at the base of the formation, and its Fig. 1c column covers 31 m at the site alone.
- castro2018a, p. 3, 3.1. U-Pb geochronology: the LA-ICP-MS dates "range from approximately 3.3 Ga to 85.2 ± 2.7 Ma"; "the youngest ID-TIMS date of 87.782 ± 0.062 Ma (±0.12 Ma including total propagated uncertainties) best represents the maximum age of deposition of the fossil-bearing bed".
- castro2018a, p. 2, Abstract: "constraining the age of the Adamantina Formation at the site to late Coniacian–late Maastrichtian".
- castro2018a, p. 6, 4.1. Age of the fossil bed: the date "rules out any pre-Coniacian age for the *B. stardusti* fossil bed", but "there is presently no evidence that these stratigraphic units could not actually span a significant portion of their assigned age bracket".
- dias2020a, p. 2, 2. Geologic context of the Bauru Basin in Triângulo Mineiro: the Uberaba Formation "is considered to have been deposited in the Campanian, based on lithostratigraphic relationships with the Adamantina and Marília formations"; the Adamantina is the "Vale do Rio do Peixe Formation in Paula e Silva et al., 2009".
- dias2020a, p. 2, 2. Geologic context of the Bauru Basin in Triângulo Mineiro: the Marília "is the youngest among the Cretaceous formations of the region, with a Maastrichtian age".
- dias2020a, p. 11, 5. Discussion: "Castro et al. (2018) dated the youngest detrital zircon grain of the basal section of the Adamantina Formation at 87.78 ± 0.06 Ma (CA-ID-TIMS), giving the maximum depositional age for Adamantina Formation in the Triângulo Mineiro region".
- aureliano2021a, p. 3: a titanosaur fibula from "Ibirá municipality, Adamantina Formation (ca. 85.2 ± 2.7 Ma, Santonian; Castro et al., 2018)".

**Ruled out.**
- *A Campanian or Maastrichtian for the Adamantina from Dias and colleagues (2020).* The paper gives the formation no stage of its own. Its one Campanian belongs to the Uberaba, reached through that unit's relationships with the Adamantina and Marília, and its one Maastrichtian to the Marília.
- *87.78 Ma as the age of the base of the Adamantina in the Triângulo Mineiro.* Dias and colleagues (2020) give Castro and colleagues' date this scope, but it is not their own result. Castro and colleagues dated one fossil bed at General Salgado, in São Paulo rather than the Triângulo Mineiro, and do not place it at the base of the formation. A maximum depositional age bounds only the old end in any case.
- *Castro and colleagues' (2018) date as a floor for the whole formation, which would cut the Turonian.* Castro and colleagues limit the constraint to "the Adamantina Formation at the site" and the pre-Coniacian exclusion to the fossil bed, and they say the units may span much of their assigned age bracket.
- *"ca. 85.2 ± 2.7 Ma, Santonian" as Castro and colleagues' age for the formation.* Aureliano and colleagues (2021) give it, for a fibula from Ibirá, with Castro and colleagues (2018) as the source. Castro and colleagues give no such age: 85.2 ± 2.7 Ma is the youngest of their LA-ICP-MS dates on detrital zircons from the General Salgado bed, and they take the youngest ID-TIMS date, 87.782 ± 0.062 Ma, as that bed's maximum age of deposition. Neither is an age for the formation, nor for Ibirá.
- *The two ostracod readings as two kinds of evidence in conflict.* Both rest on ostracods and appeared in 2001, Dias-Brito and colleagues' with charophytes as well; the dispute is one kind of evidence read two ways, Turonian–Santonian against Campanian–Maastrichtian.

**Open.** Gobbo-Rodrigues (2001), a master's thesis, would show what its comparison with the Neuquén and Congo assemblages rests on; Batezelli (2015) reports only the conclusion.

## Does the "Cenomanian to Santonian" that Soares and colleagues (1980) relay put the Adamantina in the Cenomanian?

*2026-10-21*

**Conclusion.** No. The range is Soares and Landim's (1975), and its grounds as relayed are a comparison with the evolution of the Santos Basin and the formation's relation to alkaline rocks associated with the overlying unit. Neither dates the Adamantina itself, so the range adds no stage even before Soares and Landim (1975) is read. Governs `stages` on [Adamantina](../../../stratigraphy/a/adamantina.yml), which stay Turonian to Maastrichtian.

**Evidence.**
- soares1980a, p. 182: "Soares and Landim (1975), by comparison with the evolution of the Santos Basin and owing to the contemporaneity with alkaline rocks of the overlying Formation in the Triângulo Mineiro region, assigned a Middle Cretaceous age, Cenomanian to Santonian, to the interval corresponding to the Adamantina Formation" (translated).
- soares1980a, p. 182: "This age range is consistent with the Senonian age assigned to the fossils, since these are found in the upper part of the Formation" (translated). The fossil age is Huene's (1933), relayed through Price (1950).

**Ruled out.**
- *A Cenomanian for the Adamantina.* CONTRIBUTING does not let the age of a different unit or a correlation with another basin date a unit. Soares and colleagues reconcile the range with a Senonian fossil age, but do not argue for its Cenomanian end. The Adamantina of 1980 also took in beds that Fernandes and Coimbra (2000) later assigned to the Vale do Rio do Peixe, Araçatuba and Presidente Prudente formations (fernandes2000a, p. 719, Table 1), so the 1980 range covers more rock than the unit in later use.

**Open.** Soares and Landim (1975) has not been read; it would show whether the comparison carries any evidence beyond what is relayed.

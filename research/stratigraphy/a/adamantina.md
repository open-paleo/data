# Adamantina

## Does the "Cenomanian to Santonian" that Soares and colleagues (1980) relay put the Adamantina in the Cenomanian?

*2026-10-21*

**Conclusion.** No. The range is Soares and Landim's (1975), and its grounds as relayed are a comparison with the evolution of the Santos Basin and the formation's relation to alkaline rocks associated with the overlying unit. Neither dates the Adamantina itself, so the range adds no stage even before Soares and Landim (1975) is read. Governs `stages` on [Adamantina](../../../stratigraphy/a/adamantina.yml), which stay Turonian to Maastrichtian.

**Evidence.**
- soares1980a, p. 182: "Soares and Landim (1975), by comparison with the evolution of the Santos Basin and owing to the contemporaneity with alkaline rocks of the overlying Formation in the Triângulo Mineiro region, assigned a Middle Cretaceous age, Cenomanian to Santonian, to the interval corresponding to the Adamantina Formation" (translated).
- soares1980a, p. 182: "This age range is consistent with the Senonian age assigned to the fossils, since these are found in the upper part of the Formation" (translated). The fossil age is Huene's (1933), relayed through Price (1950).

**Ruled out.**
- *A Cenomanian for the Adamantina.* CONTRIBUTING does not let the age of a different unit or a correlation with another basin date a unit. Soares and colleagues reconcile the range with a Senonian fossil age, but do not argue for its Cenomanian end. The Adamantina of 1980 also took in beds that Fernandes and Coimbra (2000) later assigned to the Vale do Rio do Peixe, Araçatuba and Presidente Prudente formations (fernandes2000a, p. 719, Table 1), so the 1980 range covers more rock than the unit in later use.

**Open.** Soares and Landim (1975) has not been read; it would show whether the comparison carries any evidence beyond what is relayed.

# Aguja

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Aguja?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian, with the uppermost part possibly reaching the early Maastrichtian. Befus and colleagues (2008) place the Peña Mountain basaltic pyroclastic deposits in Upper Cretaceous floodplain deposits of the upper shale member. The lower part of that member yields a vertebrate fauna, dinosaurs included, that they call Late Campanian; the upper part, where the pyroclastic deposits lie, has fewer and less diagnostic fossils and "could extend into the Early Maastrichtian". The youngest concordant U-Pb zircon analyses from three basaltic bombs give a weighted mean of 76.9 ± 1.2 Ma, which they read as the basalt's crystallization age and as consistent with the Late Campanian fossils. Lehman and colleagues (2024) likewise carry the post-pyroclastic interval at the top of the formation into the early Maastrichtian. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Aguja](../../../stratigraphy/a/aguja.yml).

**Evidence.**
- befus2008a, p. 245: basaltic pyroclastic deposits "within Upper Cretaceous floodplain deposits in the Aguja Formation"; "U–Pb isotopic analyses of zircon grains from three basaltic bombs"; "The youngest concordant analyses for all three samples yield a weighted mean age of 76.9 ± 1.2 Ma, consistent with the presence of Late Campanian vertebrate fossils in the upper Aguja Formation".
- befus2008a, p. 255: "The lower part of the upper shale member of the Aguja Formation contains a diverse assemblage of fossil mammals and reptiles (including dinosaurs) that indicate a Late Campanian age"; "Fossils are generally less abundant and diagnostic in the upper part of the upper shale member", "and that part of the member could extend into the Early Maastrichtian".
- befus2008a, p. 256: "the weighted mean age for the distinct population of youngest zircon grains represents the crystallization age of the basalt".
- lehman2024a, p. 870: "the post-pyroclastic interval of the Aguja likely extends into early Maastrichtian time".

**Ruled out.**
- *The 76.9 Ma zircons as xenocrysts.* Befus and colleagues (2008, p. 256) raise it, which would make the deposits younger than about 77 Ma, and prefer the reading of the date as the basalt's crystallization age. Either way the deposits lie within the Campanian–Maastrichtian span the formation carries.

**Open.** Nothing.

# Arlington

## Who named the Arlington, and in which unit?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** Dodge (1969), as a member of the Woodbine Formation. He proposes that the uppermost fossiliferous sandstone of Hill's (1901) Lewisville Beds be the Arlington Member, above his restricted Lewisville Member, into which it grades downward, with a type section in a roadcut in the 2000 block of West Park Row Street, Arlington. He gives it no stage. The record keeps `parent: Lewisville`, after Denne and colleagues (2016), who place the member "at the top of the Lewisville"; Dodge's scheme is recorded in the note. Governs the pointers to dodge1969a and hill1901a on [Arlington](../../../stratigraphy/a/arlington.yml); `parent` and `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- dodge1969a, p. 54: "It is also proposed here that the uppermost fossiliferous sandstone unit of Hill’s (1901) Lewisville Beds be designated the “Arlington Member” of the Woodbine Formation."
- dodge1969a, p. 57: "The Lewisville Member is gradational upward into the Arlington Member."

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Whether the parent should follow Dodge's Woodbine Formation or Denne and colleagues' Lewisville; it changes no stage.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Arlington?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian. Head (1998) dates the member by the unit that caps it: the Tarrant Member lies in the *Conlinoceras tarrantense* zone at the base of the middle Cenomanian, so the Arlington is 95.78 ± 0.61 Ma at youngest. That figure bounds the top of the member and is not an age for it. For the type locality of his hadrosaurid, inside the member, Head gives "Mid-Cenomanian, 95 Ma". Denne and colleagues (2016) report the zonal ammonite within the Arlington itself, which puts at least its upper part in the same zone as the Tarrant. Every reading falls inside the Cenomanian, which runs from 100.5 to 93.9 Ma on the current chart. Head describes the 6 m section at the locality as mainly mudstone, grading up into more massive sandstone with two lignitic layers; the lithology recorded for the member, a sandstone, is that of the type section in Denne and colleagues' account. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian]` and `lithology` on [Arlington](../../../stratigraphy/a/arlington.yml).

**Evidence.**
- head1998a, p. 718: "The Arlington Member has been correlated using ammonite biostratigraphy because it is capped by the Tarrant Member of the Eagleford Group, which is within the *Conlinoceras tarrantense* zone, at the base of the middle Cenomanian. Therefore the Arlington Member is 95.78 ± 0.61 Ma, at youngest".
- head1998a, p. 718: "The section is predominantly mudstone with thin sandstone layers, grading upwards to more massive sandstones. Two lignitic layers are present at approximately three and four meters above the base of the section."
- head1998a, p. 720, Type Locality and Age: "Woodbine Formation, early Late Cretaceous, Mid-Cenomanian, 95 Ma".
- denne2016a, p. 41: the Arlington is "a fossiliferous sandstone at the top of the Lewisville", its type section "10.8 m (35.4 ft) of fine-grained sandstones"; *Conlinoceras tarrantense* "is found within the underlying Arlington Member".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Whether the member reaches below the middle Cenomanian. Head's capping argument sets only its youngest age, and none of the works cited dates its base.

# Andakhuduk

## Which of the Andakhuduk's stages survive?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** Hauterivian–Barremian; the Aptian is dropped. Supersedes the 2026-11-25 entry on the stages. Jerzykiewicz and Russell (1991) accept the Aptian that Krassilov (1982) gives from plant megafossils, which has not been read. Khand and colleagues (2000) write "This fossil assemblage suggests the Hauterivian-Barremian age" after listing the unit's molluscs, ostracods, conchostracans, fish and insects, in a chapter that summarizes published data; the sentence weighs the assemblage in their own voice, and Shuvalov (1980), whom they credit for the formation scheme, does not name the unit. Napoli and colleagues' (2019) "generally considered to be Barremian" credits Jerzykiewicz and Russell, who accept the Aptian instead. Governs `stages: [Hauterivian, Barremian]` and the notes on khand2000a and jerzykiewicz1991a on [Andakhuduk](../../../stratigraphy/a/andakhuduk.yml), and the stages of [*Psittacosaurus amitabha*](../../../genera/P/Psittacosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- jerzykiewicz1991a, p. 350: "an Aptian age, suggested by similarities in plant megafossils to dated remains in eastern Siberia (Krassilov, 1982), is accepted here".
- khand2000a, p. 54: "This fossil assemblage suggests the Hauterivian-Barremian age."
- khand2000a, p. 50: "This paper summarizes published data on the Cretaceous System in Mongolia".
- napoli2019a, p. 6: "The age of the locality is generally considered to be Barremian (Jerzykiewicz and Russell, 1991)."

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian.* Adopted from Krassilov (1982), unread.
- *Barremian from Napoli and colleagues (2019).* A misrelay.

**Open.** Krassilov (1982) has not been read.

## Where does Napoli and colleagues' (2019) Barremian come from?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** It is relayed, and misattributed. Napoli and colleagues (2019) give the Ondai Sayr locality as "generally considered to be Barremian" and credit Jerzykiewicz and Russell (1991), who name a Barremian to Hauterivian age only as the reading of earlier mollusc and ostracod work and accept an Aptian age from plant megafossils. Napoli and colleagues make no dating of their own. The stages are unaffected: the Hauterivian–Barremian rests on Khand and colleagues (2000) and the Aptian on Jerzykiewicz and Russell. Governs the note on napoli2019a on [Andakhuduk](../../../stratigraphy/a/andakhuduk.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- napoli2019a, p. 6: "The age of the locality is generally considered to be Barremian (Jerzykiewicz and Russell, 1991)."
- jerzykiewicz1991a, p. 350: "Although abundant molluscan and ostracod fossils have been cited as evidence of a Barremian to Hauterivian age (Vasiliev et al., 1959; Shuvalov, 1975a), an Aptian age, suggested by similarities in plant megafossils to dated remains in eastern Siberia (Krassilov, 1982), is accepted here."

**Ruled out.**
- *Barremian from Jerzykiewicz and Russell.* They report it as an earlier reading and do not adopt it.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Andakhuduk Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Hauterivian to Aptian, from two datings of the same beds. Khand and colleagues (2000) describe the Andaikhudag, which they equate with the Ondai Sayr of Berkey and Morris (1927), as 150–700 m of bituminous paper shale, siltstone and aleurite resting conformably on the Undurukhaa Formation and overlain disconformably by the Khulsangol, and date it to the Hauterivian–Barremian on its molluscs, ostracods, conchostracans, fish, insects and plants. Jerzykiewicz and Russell (1991) note that molluscs and ostracods had been cited for a Barremian to Hauterivian age, but accept an Aptian age from the similarity of its plant megafossils to dated floras in eastern Siberia. Governs `stages: [Hauterivian, Barremian, Aptian]` and `period: [Early Cretaceous]` on [Andakhuduk](../../../stratigraphy/a/andakhuduk.yml).

**Evidence.**
- khand2000a, p. 54: "The Andaikhudag (= Ondai Sayr by Berkey and Morris [4]) Formation (150-700 m) overlies conformably beds of the Undurukhaa Formation"; "This fossil assemblage suggests the Hauterivian-Barremian age"; "The Khulsangol Formation (600-700 m) overlies the Andaikhudag Formation with erosional and stratigraphic disconformity".
- khand2000a, p. 54: "On the base of the bio- and lithostratigraphical studies the Andaikhudag Formation in the Toromkhon, Jargalant, Bakhar, Kholboot, Khukhtolgoi, Khulsyn gol and Andai khudag depressions were referred to the Upper Jurassic."
- jerzykiewicz1991a, p. 346: "an offshore lacustrine facies (Andakhuduk Formation) and sublittoral lacustrine and fluviolacustrine facies (Dushilin and Khulsyngol formations)".
- jerzykiewicz1991a, p. 350: "Although abundant molluscan and ostracod fossils have been cited as evidence of a Barremian to Hauterivian age (Vasiliev *et al.*, 1959; Shuvalov, 1975a), an Aptian age, suggested by similarities in plant megafossils to dated remains in eastern Siberia (Krassilov, 1982), is accepted here"; "The Khulsyngol (or Dushilin) Formation (Figure 2) overlies the Andakhuduk".
- napoli2019a, p. 6: "The age of the locality is generally considered to be Barremian (Jerzykiewicz and Russell, 1991)."
- sinitsa1993a, p. 63: "We accept the age of the Anda-Khuduk Formation as Late Jurassic" (translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *An Aptian age alone.* Jerzykiewicz and Russell (1991) prefer the plant-megafossil comparison to the molluscan and ostracod dating but give no evidence against the latter, and Khand and colleagues (2000) date the formation from its own fossil assemblage to the Hauterivian–Barremian.
- *Napoli and colleagues' (2019) Barremian as a separate reading.* They credit it to Jerzykiewicz and Russell (1991), who accept an Aptian age and mention the Barremian only as part of the older Barremian–Hauterivian dating.

**Open.** The Late Jurassic. Khand and colleagues (2000) report, in the paragraph that cites Sinitsa (1993), that the Andaikhudag in seven Valley of Lakes depressions was referred to the Upper Jurassic, and neither adopt nor reject it; Sinitsa (1993) dates the formation at its type depression to the Late Jurassic on ostracods. Whether that dating stands beside the Early Cretaceous ones is undecided.

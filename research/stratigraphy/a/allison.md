# Allison

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Juans Lake Beds?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian. McDonald and Wolfe (2018) give the beds, one of the three divisions into which Miller and colleagues (1991) split the Allison Member, as "lower Campanian, Upper Cretaceous", crediting Molenaar and colleagues (2002) and Lucas and colleagues (2005). McDonald and colleagues (2018) repeat that reading for a second Juans Lake Beds specimen, and McDonald and colleagues (2021) give "middle Campanian" for a locality about 120 m below the Cliff House Sandstone. All three readings fall in the Campanian. None of them dates the beds directly: the age is bracketed by the Menefee's span of about 84.0–78.5 Ma on the correlation chart of Molenaar and colleagues (2002), by *Baculites perplexus* in the overlying Cliff House Sandstone, at 78.0–78.5 Ma, where the fossils were collected, and by an Ar/Ar date of 78.22 Ma that Lucas and colleagues (2005) obtained from a bentonite near the top of the formation in the Gallina hogback, in the eastern part of the San Juan Basin. Governs `period: [Late Cretaceous]` and `stages: [Campanian]` on the Juans Lake bed of [Allison](../../../stratigraphy/a/allison.yml).

**Evidence.**
- mcdonald2018a, p. 1, Introduction: "Lucas et al. (2005) obtained an Ar/Ar radioisotopic date of 78.22 ± 0.26 Ma from a bentonite layer near the top of the formation in the Gallina hogback in the eastern part of the San Juan Basin"; where the fossils were collected, the overlying Cliff House Sandstone "contains fossils of the ammonoid Baculites perplexus", "corresponding to an age of between 78.0 and 78.5 Ma".
- mcdonald2018a, p. 2, Introduction: on the chart of Molenaar and colleagues, "the Menefee Formation spans approximately 84.0–78.5 Ma, based upon correlations with marine biostratigraphic zones. This age range corresponds to uppermost Santonian—middle Campanian".
- mcdonald2018a, pp. 2–3, Introduction: Miller, Carey and Thompson-Rizer "further divided the Allison Member into the Lower Beds, Juans Lake Beds, and La Vida Beds"; p. 3: the field area "consists predominantly of exposures of fluvial mudstones and sandstones in the Juans Lake Beds", and finds from the Allison Member include "three types of turtles; neosuchians distinct from Brachychampsa sealeyi; and hadrosaurid, ceratopsid, and theropod dinosaurs".
- mcdonald2018a, p. 4, Systematic paleontology: "All specimens were collected from outcrops of the Juans Lake Beds …, upper part of the Allison Member, Menefee Formation; lower Campanian, Upper Cretaceous".
- mcdonald2018b, p. 4, Systematic paleontology: "Juans Lake Beds …, upper part of the Allison Member, Menefee Formation; lower Campanian, Upper Cretaceous", followed by the same Ar/Ar date, *Baculites perplexus* bracket and 84.0–78.5 Ma span.
- mcdonald2021a, p. 5, Systematic paleontology: "Juans Lake Beds …, upper part of the Allison Member, Menefee Formation, approximately 120 meters below the overlying Cliff House Sandstone; middle Campanian, Upper Cretaceous, older than ~78.5 Ma based upon the occurrence of the ammonite index fossil Baculites perplexus in the overlying Cliff House Sandstone".

**Ruled out.**
- *Santonian for the Juans Lake Beds.* The "uppermost Santonian—middle Campanian" is the conversion of the whole Menefee Formation's 84.0–78.5 Ma span, a statement about the formation (see [methods](../../methods.md#a-parent-units-range-repeated-for-a-child-unit-is-the-parents-statement)). For the beds, all three papers give the Campanian.

**Open.** No radiometric date comes from the Juans Lake Beds themselves; the one Ar/Ar date is from near the top of the Menefee in the Gallina hogback, elsewhere in the basin. Whether the beds are early or middle Campanian is unresolved and does not change the stage.

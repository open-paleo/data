# Alemoa

## What do the cited works say about the age and extent of the Alemoa Member?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Late Carnian. Marsola and colleagues (2018) place the beds at Cerro da Alemoa in the Candelária Sequence and relate them to the *Hyperodapedon* Assemblage Zone on their rhynchosaurs. They call the zone late Carnian by correlation with the dated Ischigualasto Formation, which the maximum depositional age of about 233 Ma that Langer and colleagues (2018) obtained from mudstone 5 m below the top of the member, at its type section, corroborates. Colbert (1970) describes the same beds as the fossiliferous zone of the Santa Maria Formation, without naming the member, and already preferred a Carnian age to a Ladinian one. Da Rosa and Faccini (2005) date only the Rosário do Sul Group as a whole, as Middle to Late Triassic. At the type section the member is about 20 m of massive reddish mudstones with fine sandstones and calcareous concretions. Marsola and colleagues, after Da Rosa (2005, 2015), divide the exposure into lower and intermediate distal-floodplain levels and an upper proximal-floodplain level. Its contact with the Caturrita Formation above is offset across fault blocks by up to about 90 m, and Da Rosa and Faccini call it hard to fix. In the Candelária region they trace the member with the Therapsida Cenozone at Sanga de Pinheiros and the Rhynchosauria Cenozone at Cipriano. Governs `stages: [Carnian]` on [Alemoa](../../../stratigraphy/a/alemoa.yml).

**Evidence.**
- marsola2018a, p. 2, Geological Setting: "The lower and intermediate levels of the exposed Alemoa Member represent distal floodplain deposits, whereas the upper level represents a proximal floodplain (Da Rosa, 2005, 2015)"; "The occurrence of Hyperodapedon rhynchosaurs justifies relating the site to the Hyperodapedon AZ"; correlations with the Ischigualasto Formation "indicate that the Hyperodapedon AZ is late Carnian in age", corroborated by dating at the *Saturnalia tupiniquim* level "which has yielded a maximum age of ca. 233 Ma (Langer et al., 2018)".
- marsola2018a, p. 2, Type Locality and Horizon: "the upper levels of the Alemoa Member of the Santa Maria Formation, 1–1.5 m below the contact with the overlying Caturrita Formation".
- langer2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 3: Cerro da Alemoa is "the type-section of the Alemoa Member of the Santa Maria Formation (Andreis et al., 1980), with a 20 meters-thick sequence of massive reddish mudstones, intercalated with fine sandstones"; "The presence of calcareous concretions is the most remarkable feature of the Alemoa Member at this site, sometimes forming carbonaceous crusts of restricted lateral expression"; "fossils are better preserved in the upper 10 m of the Alemoa Member at Cerro da Alemoa; below which they are heavily distorted by calcitic cementation".
- langer2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 4: "The collected geochronology sample represents the typical Alemoa Member mudstone and comes from 5 m below its top"; near Cerro da Alemoa "about 3 m of the Alemoa Member" crop out at what is probably the type locality of *Staurikosaurus pricei* (Colbert, 1970), "correlating with the lower portion of the unit at Cerro da Alemoa".
- darosa2005a, p. 17: the mapped deposits, "from the Middle to Late Triassic", "include the Rosário do Sul Group, more specifically pelites of the Alemoa Member of the Santa Maria Formation" (translated).
- darosa2005a, p. 18: "The determination of the contact between the Alemoa Member and the Caturrita Formation is not secure, due to its recurrent character" (translated).
- darosa2005a, p. 20: "the Vacacaí Mirim lineament produces a vertical displacement on the order of 90 m" (translated); the Alemoa–Caturrita contact lies at 120 m northeast of Santa Maria and at 210 m in the São João do Polêsine block.
- darosa2005a, p. 21: across the Rio Botucaraí lineament the displacement "is at least 20 m, measured at the Alemoa-Caturrita contact" (translated).
- darosa2005a, p. 23: in the Candelária region, "the Alemoa Member in Sanga de Pinheiros, with the Therapsida Cenozone, the Alemoa Member in the Cipriano region, with the Rhynchosauria Cenozone" (translated).
- colbert1970a, p. 3: the bones "are found only in the middle part of the Santa Maria Formation, a zone some 30 meters in thickness, the base of which is about 70 meters above the basal beds"; p. 4: "The sediments at Alemõa, a brilliant orange-red in color, represent the fossiliferous zone of the formation"; p. 6, Horizon: "Santa Maria Formation, within the fossiliferous zone of the middle part of the formation".
- colbert1970a, p. 34: the cynodonts and pseudosuchians favor "a possible late Middle Triassic or Ladinian age", the rhynchosaurs and dinosaurs "a possible Upper Triassic or Carnian age"; p. 36: "the Carnian Ischigualasto fauna of Argentina is slightly later than the Santa Maria fauna", but "both assemblages can very logically be placed within the lower or Carnian phase of the Upper Triassic".

**Ruled out.**
- *Ladinian.* Colbert (1970) sets out the case for it from the cynodonts and pseudosuchians (p. 34) and then places the fauna in the Carnian (p. 36). None of the later works cited gives the member a Ladinian age.
- *Middle Triassic from Da Rosa and Faccini (2005).* Their "Middle to Late Triassic" is the age of the Rosário do Sul Group deposits they map, taken as a whole. A parent unit's range repeated for a child unit is the parent's statement, not a dating of the member (see [methods](../../methods.md)).

**Open.** The base of the member is not dated directly. The 233.23 Ma maximum comes from 5 m below its top, and the lower part at Cerro da Alemoa is dated only through the *Hyperodapedon* Assemblage Zone. Da Rosa's (2005) dissertation and Da Rosa (2015), which Marsola and colleagues follow for the floodplain levels, have not been read.

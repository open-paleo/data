# Alto Shale

## Which Lehman works do Breyer and colleagues (2007) credit for the upper shale member's age?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Two of the three are identified and read. Lehman (1989), on the Maastrichtian paleosols of Trans-Pecos Texas, puts the upper part of the Aguja Formation in the Maastrichtian; Lehman (1991), on the Tornillo Basin, puts the upper shale member above a Late Campanian marine sandstone. Between them they give the "Upper Campanian/Lower Maastrichtian" that Breyer and colleagues credit, so that span is Lehman's, not Breyer and colleagues' own (see the earlier entry). The 1986 work is still unidentified and unread. Answers the earlier entry's Open line for 1989 and 1991; whether Lehman's Maastrichtian for the upper Aguja holds against Lehman and colleagues' (2024) age model is not settled here.

**Evidence.**
- breyer2007a, p. 246: the strata "must represent a previously unrecognized facies of the upper shale member, which is Upper Campanian/Lower Maastrichtian (Lehman 1986, 1989, 1991)".
- lehman1989b, p. 188: "Fluvial flood-plain deposits of the upper Aguja, Javelina, and El Picacho Formations in Brewster and Presidio Counties of West Texas record multiple episodes of soil formation during Late Cretaceous (Maastrichtian) time."
- lehman1991a, p. 18: the last regressive marine sandstone of the Aguja is "of Late Campanian age (ca. 81 to 78 Ma"; "Paleocurrent data from this unit, and the overlying upper shale member of the Aguja, indicate that the Late Campanian strandline prograded eastward across the Big Bend region".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Lehman (1986), which the citing works give either as a West Texas Geological Society guidebook paper or as a Bulletin item, is unidentified and unheld.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Alto Shale?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian and Maastrichtian. Lehman and colleagues (2024), who formally name the member, date its lower 30 m to about 80.2–78.6 Ma and its post-pyroclastic top to a span that "likely extends into early Maastrichtian time". The vertebrate fauna, collected almost entirely below the pyroclastic deposits, is middle Campanian, 80 to 77 Ma (Lehman and colleagues 2019; Prieto-Márquez and colleagues 2020). The pyroclastic deposits in the upper part are dated by U-Pb to 76.9 ± 1.2 Ma at Peña Mountain (Befus and colleagues 2008) and 72.6 ± 1.5 Ma in the Rosillos Mountains (Breyer and colleagues 2007). Befus and colleagues allow that the top "could extend into the Early Maastrichtian", and Breyer and colleagues' "Upper Campanian/Lower Maastrichtian" is credited to Lehman. Both papers name the unit the "upper shale member", the informal name it carried before 2024. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Alto Shale](../../../stratigraphy/a/alto-shale.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lehman2024a, p. 829: the intervals "previously referred to informally as the "lower shale" and "upper shale", are herein formally designated the Abajo Shale Member and Alto Shale Member"; p. 849: the "upper shale member" (Lehman, 1985a, p. 60) "is herein formally recognized as the Alto Shale Member".
- lehman2024a, p. 825, Abstract: a progradational deltaic succession "includes the upper part of the McKinney Springs Tongue, Terlingua Creek Sandstone Member of the Aguja Formation, and lower part of the Alto Shale Member of the Aguja Formation".
- lehman2024a, p. 869: "the lower 30 m of the Alto Shale was deposited in a time span of ~1.6 m.y. (80.2–78.6 Ma)"; most of the upper Alto Shale "lies below the Peña Mountain pyroclastic deposits (ca. 76.9 Ma)".
- lehman2024a, p. 870: an MDA of ca. 72 Ma "from 20 m below the top of the Alto Shale on Dawson Creek", a polarity reversal 35 m from the top correlated "with the end of polarity subchron C32n (71.6 Ma)", and the base of the Javelina Formation "at ca. 69.5 Ma" together "indicate that the post-pyroclastic interval of the Aguja likely extends into early Maastrichtian time (Campanian-Maastrichtian boundary at 72.1 Ma; Gale et al., 2020)".
- lehman2024a, p. 873: the southeastward stream flow "evident in the upper Alto Shale persisted through Maastrichtian time during deposition of the Javelina Formation".
- lehman2019a, p. 292: "The uppermost part of the upper shale member includes intercalated pyroclastic deposits that yield U/Pb ages ranging from c. 77 Ma (Befus et al., 2008) to c. 73 Ma (Breyer et al., 2007)"; p. 293: its vertebrate fauna "has been collected almost exclusively from levels well below the pyroclastic deposits, and so must have an age between 80 and 77 Ma (middle Campanian)".
- prieto-márquez2020a, p. 463 (online version, p. 3): "Nearly the entire fauna, however, is known from the uppermost part of the formation – the upper shale member of Lehman (1985)"; p. 469 (online, p. 9): *Baculites haresi* "is found in paralic strata (the Rattlesnake Mountain and Terlingua Creek sandstone members)" (Waggoner 2006); pp. 469–470 (online, pp. 9–10): the fauna "must have an age between 80 and 77 Ma (middle Campanian)".
- befus2008a, p. 245, Abstract: "a weighted mean age of 76.9 ± 1.2 Ma, consistent with the presence of Late Campanian vertebrate fossils in the upper Aguja Formation".
- befus2008a, p. 255: fossils "are generally less abundant and diagnostic in the upper part of the upper shale member (Lehman, 1985), where the pyroclastic deposits described herein occur, and that part of the member could extend into the Early Maastrichtian".
- befus2008a, p. 256: the results are consistent with "the time scale of Ogg et al. (2004), who place the upper boundary of the Campanian at 70.6 ± 0.6 Ma".
- befus2008a, p. 258: "the available paleontological evidence indicates a Late Campanian (to possibly Early Maastrichtian) age for the pyroclastic deposits at both localities".
- breyer2007a, p. 243, Abstract: "The fauna indicate a Late Cretaceous, probably Campanian age"; a normal fault "juxtaposes sediments of the Upper Cretaceous Javelina Formation in the hanging wall with pyroclastic rocks in the footwall, indicating the pyroclastic strata must be Maastrichtian or older".
- breyer2007a, p. 246: the strata "must represent a previously unrecognized facies of the upper shale member, which is Upper Campanian/Lower Maastrichtian (Lehman 1986, 1989, 1991)".
- breyer2007a, p. 248: "a weighted mean concordia age of 72.6 ± 1.5 Ma", with the alternative that all the grains are xenocrysts, "which would make the basalt younger than 72.6 ± 1.5 Ma"; "Ogg et al. (2004) place the Campanian-Maastrichtian boundary at 70.6 ± 0.6 Ma". The Fig. 7 caption gives the calculated age "with 2σ error".
- gale2020a, p. 1041: the base of the Maastrichtian at "approximately 72.2 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian from Breyer and colleagues' 72.6 ± 1.5 Ma.* On Ogg and colleagues' (2004) chart the age sat well inside the Campanian; on the current chart (base of the Maastrichtian 72.2 Ma) the number is still Campanian, though its 2σ range, 71.1 to 74.1 Ma, straddles the boundary (our arithmetic). The stage name moved beneath the measurement, which did not change, and the measurement may itself be a maximum if the grains are xenocrysts.
- *Maastrichtian from Breyer and colleagues' "Maastrichtian or older".* It is a bound from a faulted contact with the Javelina, and a bound licenses no stage.
- *Breyer and colleagues' "Upper Campanian/Lower Maastrichtian" as their own dating.* The sentence credits it to Lehman (1986, 1989, 1991); the paper's own reading of the fauna is "probably Campanian".
- *Befus and colleagues' "possibly Early Maastrichtian" as a reading.* It is a possibility, hedged twice. The Maastrichtian rests on Lehman and colleagues' (2024) age model for the post-pyroclastic interval.

**Open.** Breyer and colleagues' reference list gives only Lehman (1985) of the three Lehman works they credit; Lehman (1986, 1989, 1991) have not been identified against the store or read.

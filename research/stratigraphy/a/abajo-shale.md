# Abajo Shale

## What do the cited works say about the definition and age of the Abajo Shale?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Lehman and colleagues (2024) formally name the member for the unit earlier called informally the "lower shale member", designate its type section in the southwestern foothills of Rattlesnake Mountain, and place it between the La Basa Sandstone Member below and the Rattlesnake Mountain Sandstone Member above. It is recessive and slope-forming, 70 to nearly 100 m thick in the western outcrop belt and thinning eastward, and divisible into three units where it is thickest. Its age is Campanian, bracketed by ammonites in the marine beds below and above. Lehman and colleagues (2024) place the base of the Aguja Formation in the upper part of the lower Campanian, about 82–81 Ma, and allow the overlying Rattlesnake Mountain Sandstone to reach the lowermost middle Campanian, about 81–80 Ma. Prieto-Márquez and colleagues (2020) date the vertebrate fauna of the concretionary interval in the middle of the member "between 81 and 80 Ma (early Campanian)" from *Scaphites hippocrepis* III in the underlying Pen Formation and *Baculites haresi* in the overlying Rattlesnake Mountain and Terlingua Creek sandstone members, both after Waggoner (2006). The two papers differ only on whether 81–80 Ma is early or earliest middle Campanian, which leaves the stage unchanged. Waggoner (2006) is a thesis; the ammonite occurrences it reports are carried here by the two published papers. Governs `lithology`, `parent: Aguja` and `stages: [Campanian]` on [Abajo Shale](../../../stratigraphy/a/abajo-shale.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lehman2024a, p. 841: "The unit previously referred to informally as the 'lower shale member' (Lehman, 1985a, p. 46) is herein formally designated the Abajo Shale Member"; "A type section is designated for the Abajo Shale Member in the southwestern foothills of Rattlesnake Mountain"; it "consists of dark gray and olive carbonaceous mudstone with beds of lignite and coal near the base"; "the Abajo Shale is a recessive, slope-forming unit"; "Where the Abajo Shale Member is thickest, it can generally be subdivided into three units"; it is thickest along the western outcrop belt, "where it ranges from 70 to nearly 100 m thick near Sierra Aguja and Rattlesnake Mountain to 40 or 30 m along Dawson Creek", reflecting "regional thinning from west to east".
- lehman2024a, p. 840, Fig. 11 caption: "contact between Abajo Shale and overlying Rattlesnake Mountain Sandstone"; p. 841: "The contact between the La Basa Sandstone Member and the recessively weathering Abajo Shale is placed at the top of the uppermost prominent sandstone bed in the La Basa Sandstone".
- lehman2024a, p. 865: the overlapping ammonite range zones place "the base of the Aguja Formation in the upper part of the lower Campanian (ca. 82–81 Ma; Gale et al., 2020)"; the Rattlesnake Mountain Sandstone "could extend into the lowermost part of the middle Campanian (ca. 81–80 Ma; Gale et al., 2020)".
- lehman2024a, p. 868: "Zone I is within the middle part (unit 2) of the Abajo Shale".
- prieto-márquez2020a, p. ?, Geological Setting: "This concretionary interval is the only part of the lower shale member that yields significant vertebrate fossils".
- prieto-márquez2020a, p. ?, Occurrence: "The uppermost biostratigraphically significant ammonite found in the Pen Formation below the Aguja is *Scaphites hippocrepis* III (Waggoner 2006)"; "*Baculites haresi* is found in paralic strata (the Rattlesnake Mountain and Terlingua Creek sandstone members) in the Aguja Formation above the lower shale member (Waggoner 2006)"; "The vertebrate fauna from the lower shale member is older, between 81 and 80 Ma (early Campanian)".

**Ruled out.**
- *"Lower shale member" as the unit's name.* It is the informal name that Prieto-Márquez and colleagues (2020) and Beveridge and colleagues (2022) use; Lehman and colleagues (2024) replace it with the formal Abajo Shale Member.

**Open.** Nothing.

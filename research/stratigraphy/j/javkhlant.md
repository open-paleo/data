# Javkhlant

## Is "Jibhalanta Suite" a variant of the Javkhlant?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No. Eberth and colleagues (2009) list the Jibhalanta Suite and the Javkhlant Formation side by side among the units the Shine Us Khudag red beds have been assigned to, which does not show that they are one unit. Governs the absent `variants` on [Javkhlant](../../../stratigraphy/j/javkhlant.yml).

**Evidence.**
- eberth2009a, p. 295: "The Shine Us Khudag redbeds have variously been assigned to the Jibhalanta Suite, Javkhlant Formation, Baruungoyot Svita, Djadokhta Formation"

**Ruled out.** - *Jibhalanta.* Listed as a separate assignment.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Does any work give the Javkhlant the Maastrichtian?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No; the Maastrichtian is dropped. Settles the question the 2026-11-23 entry left. Fowler and colleagues (2020) give "Campanian–Maastrichtian" after Nesbitt and colleagues (2011), but Nesbitt and colleagues give "?Santonian–Campanian", so the relay misreports its source and adds nothing. Khand and colleagues' (2000) Table 1 draws "Javkhlant" in the column it credits to Martinson and colleagues (1969) as an open-topped cell running up through the Maastrichtian and Upper Paleocene rows; a table's layout is not a stated age, Martinson and colleagues (1969) has not been read, and Eberth and colleagues (2009) report its age as "Santonian-Campanian?". Khand and colleagues' own text gives the Santonian–Campanian, with the Maastrichtian Nemegt above. The same table layout does not add the Coniacian either. Governs `stages: [Santonian, Campanian]` on [Javkhlant](../../../stratigraphy/j/javkhlant.yml); its genera, all Santonian–Campanian, are unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- fowler2020b, accepted manuscript, p. 5: "Albinykus (Javkhlant Formation; Campanian–Maastrichtian; Nesbitt et al., 2011, or Santonian–Campanian; Norell & Barta, 2016)".
- nesbitt2011a, p. 146: the material was collected from beds "considered to be ?Santonian–Campanian in age (Eberth et al., 2009)".
- khand2000a, p. 63: "The fossil horizon of the Javkhlant Formation is correlative with the Santonian-Campanian Stage"; the Nemegt Formation "overlies the Javkhlant Formation" and "appears to be of the Maastrichtian age".
- eberth2009a, p. 298: Martinson and colleagues (1969) assigned the beds "to a new stratigraphic unit – the Javkhlant Svita – whose age was assessed as "Santonian-Campanian?"".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian from Fowler and colleagues (2020).* A misrelay of Nesbitt and colleagues (2011).
- *Maastrichtian or Coniacian from Khand and colleagues' Table 1.* The layout of a cell credited to an unread work.

**Open.** Unchanged: no chronostratigraphic date from the formation itself.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Javkhlant?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Santonian to Campanian, from one original statement. Khand and colleagues (2000) list the formation's ostracods and correlate its fossil horizon with the Santonian–Campanian. Eberth and colleagues (2009) report that most stratigraphers have given the redbeds a Santonian–Campanian age since 1969, accept only that the formation is Late Cretaceous, and take the Santonian–Campanian tentatively, for want of chronostratigraphic data from the beds themselves. Norell and Barta (2016) relay the Santonian–Campanian to Khand and colleagues, Jerzykiewicz and Russell (1991) and Jerzykiewicz (2000). This entry does not settle the record's Maastrichtian, which none of these works gives. Governs the Santonian and Campanian in `stages` on [Javkhlant](../../../stratigraphy/j/javkhlant.yml).

**Evidence.**
- khand2000a, p. 63, 2.3.3. Basins of Dornogobi (Eastern Gobi): "The Javkhlant Formation (Baruungoyot Formation by Shuvalov and Stankevich [21]), 15-60 m thick, occurs on the Bayanshiree Formation, and is represented by red sandstones and sandy clay, alternating mainly with gravelite, and sometimes with conglomerate and sandstone concretion"; after a list of ostracods and Ornithomimidae, "The fossil horizon of the Javkhlant Formation is correlative with the Santonian-Campanian Stage." The same page: "The Nemegt Formation (up to 70 m) overlies the Javkhlant Formation".
- eberth2009a, p. 295, Introduction: "The Shine Us Khudag redbeds have variously been assigned to the Jibhalanta Suite, Javkhlant Formation, Baruungoyot Svita, Djadokhta Formation, and the 'Barunbayan' (Table 1), and since 1969 have been regarded by most stratigraphers working in the area as having a Santonian-Campanian age".
- eberth2009a, p. 300, Assignment of the Shine Us Khudag redbeds to the Javkhlant Formation: "we accept that the rocks and fossils of the Javkhlant Formation at Shine Us Khudag are Late Cretaceous in age (Table 1). However, in the absence of independent chronostratigraphic data from these beds, we can only tentatively accept the previously proposed biostratigraphically derived Santonian-Campanian age range for this formation at Shine Us Khudag."
- norell2016a, p. 2, Introduction: the expeditions "traveled to the white to reddish-brown beds of the Upper Cretaceous Javkhlant Formation in the eastern Gobi Desert"; Shine Us Khudag lies "~600 km away from the Zos Canyon locality".
- norell2016a, p. 17, Discussion: "The Javkhlant beds at Shine Us Khudug that produced the Haya griva type material have been assigned a Santonian-Campanian age based on, among other things, ostracods (Jerzykiewicz and Russell, 1991; Khand et al., 2000; Jerzykiewicz, 2001). Therefore, because of the occurrence of the basal ornithopod Haya griva at both sites we recognize the Zos Canyon beds as coeval with the Javkhlant beds at Shine Us Khudug and temporally correspondant in Santonian-Campanian age." The paper's Jerzykiewicz 2001 is jerzykiewicz2000a.
- jerzykiewicz1991a, p. 365, 7.5. Baynshirenian "Age": the locality appears as "Sheeneh Usu Khuduk* (Shine Usu Khuduk), (Martinson, 1982, loc. 19)" among the localities of the "Bayn Shiren Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Norell and Barta (2016) as a reading of their own.* They relay the age to earlier works, among them Khand and colleagues (2000), and date the Zos Canyon beds from it rather than the other way round; their Santonian–Campanian counts once, with Khand and colleagues'.
- *Eberth and colleagues' (2009) "since 1969" as a determination.* It reports the readings of earlier stratigraphers, and their own conclusion is only that the formation is Late Cretaceous.

**Open.** No chronostratigraphic date from the formation itself, as Eberth and colleagues (2009) note. Which of the works Norell and Barta (2016) cite carries the ostracods is not settled: Khand and colleagues (2000) list them, while Jerzykiewicz and Russell (1991) list the locality only under the Baynshirenian and do not name this formation.

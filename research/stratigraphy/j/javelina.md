# Javelina

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Javelina Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian and Maastrichtian, unchanged. The Maastrichtian rests on one date: Lehman and colleagues' (2006) U-Pb age of 69.0 ± 0.9 Ma on monazite from a tuff 60 m above the base of the formation and about 90 m below the Cretaceous–Tertiary boundary. That paper is the full account of the determination McDowell and colleagues (2004) reported in an abstract as 69 ± 1 Ma, which Breyer and colleagues (2007) relay, so the three count as one source. Lehman and colleagues read the date against a Campanian–Maastrichtian boundary of 70.6 Ma (Ogg and colleagues 2004) and conclude that "most or all" of the formation is Maastrichtian; on the current chart the boundary lies at about 72.2 Ma (Gale and colleagues 2020), so the date stays Maastrichtian and the boundary moves further below the tuff. Breyer and colleagues' "Middle to Upper Maastrichtian" labels the beds at one faulted contact and falls inside the range. The Campanian rests on Wick and Lehman's (2013) "terminal Campanian to early Maastrichtian" for the base of the formation. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Javelina](../../../stratigraphy/j/javelina.yml).

**Evidence.**
- lehman2006a, p. 922, Abstract: the tuff "contains monazite with a U-Pb age of 69.0 ± 0.9 Ma (2 sigma)"; "The age is from a 207Pb/204Pb vs. 235U/204Pb isochron, an approach chosen to avoid the effects of 230Th-derived excess 206Pb"; "The tuff bed occurs approximately in the middle of the fluvio-lacustrine Javelina Formation, about 90 m stratigraphically below the position of the Cretaceous–Tertiary boundary".
- lehman2006a, p. 922: the formation "consists of fluvial and lacustrine mudstones and sandstones"; later authors "have referred to our preliminary report on the age of the Alamosaurus fauna (McDowell et al., 2004), the full data and basis for the age assessment have not been presented and so are subject to misinterpretation. Here, we provide a thorough account of our suggested age determination"; the tuff is "exposed on the south side of Tornillo Creek" in Big Bend National Park and "occurs 60 m above the base of the Javelina Formation".
- lehman2006a, p. 923: "Five multigrain fractions of various sizes define a collinear array in 207Pb/204Pb vs. 235U/204Pb space corresponding to an age of 69.0 ± 0.9 Ma".
- lehman2006a, p. 925: Ogg and colleagues (2004) estimated the Campanian/Maastrichtian boundary "as 70.6 Ma"; "most or all of the Javelina Formation accumulated during Maastrichtian time".
- lehman2006a, p. 926: much of the formation "appears to have accumulated during an interval of reversed polarity initially correlated with C29r ..., but the 69.0 Ma age for the tuff bed requires correlation with C31r instead"; "a hiatus of as much as 2 Ma may exist within the Javelina Formation"; Conclusions: "A U-Pb age of 69.0 ± 0.9 Ma obtained from monazite in a tuff bed in the Javelina Formation confirms that these strata are of latest Cretaceous (Maastrichtian) age".
- mcdowell2004a, p. 6: the tuff "contains monazite with a U-Pb age of 69 ± 1 Ma", from the same isochron, "approximately in the middle of the fluvio-lacustrine Javelina Formation".
- breyer2007a, p. 243: "McDowell et al. (2004) reported a U-Pb monazite age of 69 ± 1 Ma from a tuff in the Upper Cretaceous Javelina Formation"; Abstract: a normal fault "juxtaposes sediments of the Upper Cretaceous Javelina Formation in the hanging wall with pyroclastic rocks in the footwall, indicating the pyroclastic strata must be Maastrichtian or older".
- breyer2007a, p. 247: "The southeastern boundary of the block is an outward-dipping normal fault that juxtaposes sediments of the Javelina Formation (Middle to Upper Maastrichtian) in the hanging wall of the fault with the pyroclastic strata in the footwall"; "At a minimum, the deposits in the fault block must be older than Middle to Late Maastrichtian."
- gale2020a, p. 1041: the base of the Maastrichtian "is approximately 72.2 Ma".
- lehman2024a, p. 870: "the estimated age of the base of the Javelina Formation at ca. 69.5 Ma (Leslie et al., 2018)", with the "Campanian-Maastrichtian boundary at 72.1 Ma; Gale et al., 2020".

**Ruled out.**
- *McDowell and colleagues' (2004) 69 ± 1 Ma as a second date.* It is the preliminary abstract of the same determination, which Lehman and colleagues (2006) replace with the full account; Breyer and colleagues (2007) relay the abstract. One date.
- *The formation's reversed-polarity interval as chron C29r.* The correlation Lehman and colleagues (2006) credit to earlier work; the 69.0 Ma tuff, they write, requires C31r instead.
- *Middle to Upper Maastrichtian only, after Breyer and colleagues (2007).* The label describes the beds at one faulted contact, in an argument about the age of the rocks beneath the fault, and the paper dates nothing in the formation itself.

**Open.** Whether the base of the formation reaches the Campanian. Wick and Lehman's (2013) "terminal Campanian" comes from an Edmontonian land-mammal age inferred for a site 57 m or more below the tuff, while Lehman and colleagues (2024) relay an estimated age of about 69.5 Ma for the base of the formation from Leslie and colleagues (2018), which would make the whole formation Maastrichtian. Leslie and colleagues (2018) would settle it.

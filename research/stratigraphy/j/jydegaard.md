# Jydegaard

## What age do the cited works give the Jydegaard as a whole?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Berriasian–Valanginian. Christiansen and Bonde (2003) place the formation as the youngest of the Nyker Group, which they date "(Tithonian?) Berriasian to Valanginian", and note that its facies and fauna have been reported as "Wealden-like". They give the dromaeosaurid tooth horizon as "Lower Cretaceous (Berriasian-Valanginian)" and write that the whole formation "supposedly includes most or all of the Valanginian, perhaps even younger stages". Bonde (2012) dates the basal beds late Berriasian and the remaining higher part of the formation possibly Valanginian. The age of the basal beds is treated in [Tornhöj](../t/tornhöj.md). Governs `stages: [Berriasian, Valanginian]` on [Jydegaard](../../../stratigraphy/j/jydegaard.yml).

**Evidence.**
- christiansen2003a, p. 288: "The Nyker Group … spans from (Tithonian?) Berriasian to Valanginian in age, and has previously yielded vertebrate remains from particularly its youngest Formation, the Jydegaard Formation. The sedimentary facies and fauna has previously been reported as 'Wealden-like'".
- christiansen2003a, p. 288: "The entire formation is 60-100 meters thick, and supposedly includes most or all of the Valanginian, perhaps even younger stages".
- christiansen2003a, p. 291: "Locality and horizon: Lower Cretaceous (Berriasian-Valanginian), Jydegaard Formation, Isle of Bornholm, Denmark".
- bonde2012a, p. 441: "The remaining higher part of Jydegaard Formation might be of Valanginian age".

**Ruled out.**
- *Stages above the Valanginian.* Christiansen and Bonde's (2003) "perhaps even younger stages" names no stage and is hedged and relayed from earlier stratigraphic work; the same paragraph closes the Nyker Group, of which the Jydegaard is the youngest formation, at the Valanginian, and Bonde (2012) goes no higher than "might be of Valanginian age" (christiansen2003a, p. 288; bonde2012a, p. 441).

**Open.** Nothing.

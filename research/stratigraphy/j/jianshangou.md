# Jianshangou

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Jianshangou, and which beds does the name cover?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The member is the broad lacustrine interval of the lower Yixian at Sihetun, and its stage is the Barremian that the U-Pb bracket of the formation sets (see [Yixian](../y/yixian.md)). Chen and colleagues (1980) gave the name to an interval that Wang and colleagues (1998) equate with their member 3, beds 5–9; Wang and colleagues keep "upper Jianshangou vertebrate fossil bed" for bed 8 at the top of the second cycle, above the lower Sihetun fossil bed (bed 6) that yielded *Sinosauropteryx*, and give no age of their own. The formal member of Wang and colleagues (2016) takes the broad sense: it runs from the Xiatulaigou Member to the overlying volcaniclastic beds and holds the Anjiagou Bed, whose type section is at the Sihetun excavation. Chen and colleagues (1998) also use the broad sense, a 60 m interbed at the base of the formation, and weigh an Early Cretaceous age against older suggestions. The argon ages that He and colleagues (2006) relay for "the Jianshangou bed", 124.6 and 125.0 Ma, come from the bed 6 tuff at Sihetun, as Wang and colleagues (2001) and Swisher and colleagues (2002) report; that these date the member, and not bed 8 alone, follows from the broad sense and is our inference. Zhong and colleagues (2021) also draw their Jianshangou Unit broadly, with bed 6 inside it, and date a tuff in it by U-Pb at 125.457 ± 0.051 Ma, which is Barremian on the ICS chart v2024/12 (Barremian base 125.77 Ma). Wang and colleagues (2001) bracket beds 2–9 between their own Ar-Ar ages of 128 and 122 Ma. He and colleagues' contemporaneity of the Lujiatun and Jianshangou, and the later recalculation of their Lujiatun date, are weighed in [Lujiatun](../l/lujiatun.md). Governs `stages: [Barremian]` on [Jianshangou](../../../stratigraphy/j/jianshangou.yml).

**Evidence.**
- wang1998a, p. 85 (translated): bed 8, 10–15 m of sandstone, shale, siltstone and mudstone with "multiple beds of grayish-white and light yellow bedded tuff and tuffaceous sandstone": "This bed is the upper Jianshangou vertebrate fossil bed", with *Zhangheotherium quinquecuspidens*; bed 6 is the lower Sihetun vertebrate fossil bed, with *Sinosauropteryx prima*.
- wang1998a, p. 87 (translated): Member 3 (Beds 5–9) "is roughly equivalent to the Jianshangou sedimentary interbed of the Yixian Formation of" Chen and colleagues (1980); "The upper part of the first cycle is the lower Sihetun vertebrate fossil bed, and the upper part of the second cycle is the upper Jianshangou vertebrate fossil bed".
- wang1998a, p. 92 (translated): beside Jianshangou village "4–5 interbedded layers of tuff deposits can be seen; about 20cm below the 2nd tuff layer is the burial horizon of the holotype of the mammal *Zhangheotherium quinquecuspidens*".
- wang2016a, p. 110, Abstract: the Jianshangou Member "is divided into 4 units", among them "the Anjiagou Bed, the main tetrapod-bearing unit".
- wang2016a, p. 112: exposures in the geopark museum "comprise the type section of the new Anjiagou Bed", and those immediately south "are parts of a larger excavation that include the type locality for *Sinosauropteryx*".
- wang2016a, p. 117: "We propose to restrict the Jianshangou bed to the strata between the Xiatulaigou Member and the overlying volcaniclastic beds that lack laminated mudstones as seen at Huangbanjigou … and formally designate it a member".
- chen1998a, p. 147: "The Jianshangou (formerly Jianshan) intercalated bed (60 m thick) is the basal part of this volcanic sedimentary formation"; the birds' primitive nature "has led to suggestions that the beds could be as early as Tithonian in age", but "we can only conclude that the beds that the fossils came from are probably younger than the Solnhofen Lithographic Limestones (Early Tithonian)"; "The presence of *Psittacosaurus* in the same beds is more consistent with an Early Cretaceous age, as are the palynomorphs and a recent radiometric date of the formation, but other radiometric dating attempts have indicated older ages"; the horizon line reads "Yixian Formation, Jehol Group, Upper Jurassic or Lower Cretaceous".
- zhong2021a, p. 2, Fig. 1C–E: sample CY17-17, "125.457±0.051/0.064/0.27 Ma", sits in the "Jianshangou Unit" of the composite column (80–120 m and 150–300 m thick), and the literature-age plot marks "Bed 6" within that unit.
- he2006a, p. 1, Introduction: "much progress has been made in dating the Jianshangou bed of the Yixian Formation (124.6 ± 0.3Ma; 125.0 ± 0.2 Ma) [Swisher et al., 1999, 2002]".
- he2006a, p. 3, Discussion and Conclusions: "the Lujiatun bed was most likely deposited at the same time as the Jianshangou bed of the lower Yixian Formation".
- swisher2002a, p. 136, Abstract: the dated sample is "a new sample of the Bed 6 Sihetun tuff from the Yixian Formation, previously dated by our team as middle Early Cretaceous"; the Sihetun sanidine "gave a mean age of 125.0 ± 0.18 (1SD) ± 0.04 (SE) Ma".
- wang2001c, p. 663, Abstract: "YL29 basalt-andesite was collected from the low part of the bed 2", with "a plateau age of 128.2±0.8 Ma"; "YL30 diabase was collected from the subvolcanic rock, which intruded into beds 5~9", with "a plateau age of 122.3±0.5 Ma".
- wang2001c, p. 666 (translated): "Member 3 (Beds 5–9) is a lacustrine deposit"; the early birds, feathered dinosaurs and *Zhangheotherium* "are buried in Bed 6 of Member 3".
- wang2001c, p. 667 (translated): vertebrates occur "in Beds 1, 6, 8 and 9 of the composite section, and especially abundantly in Bed 6"; "the age range of Beds 2–9 is 128–122 Ma"; the diabase was emplaced before 122 Ma, so the Sihetun vertebrates are no younger than that figure; the 124.6 Ma sanidine age of Swisher and colleagues (1999) is "from the Bed 6 tuff".

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Jurassic, or "Upper Jurassic or Lower Cretaceous".* Chen and colleagues (1998) report a suggestion from the primitive birds that the beds could be Tithonian, conclude only that they are probably younger than the Solnhofen, and hedge the horizon line across the two periods, while their text favors the Early Cretaceous. The "older ages" they mention are earlier radiometric results that the Ar-Ar and U-Pb dates supersede (see the Yixian note).
- *128–122 Ma as the member's age.* Wang and colleagues' (2001) dates are a lava below member 3 and a diabase that cuts it: a floor and a ceiling, which license no stage (see [methods](../../methods.md)). The paper itself reads the diabase only as a minimum for the vertebrates.
- *Bed 8 alone as the Jianshangou.* This is the narrow use of Wang and colleagues (1998). The formal member of Wang and colleagues (2016) is the broad interval, so Sihetun bed 6 and its dates belong to it.

**Open.** Wang and colleagues (2001) list *Zhangheotherium quinquecuspidens* among the fossils of bed 6 (p. 666), where Wang and colleagues (1998) put its holotype in bed 8 at Jianshangou (p. 92). Both beds lie in the member, so the unit is unaffected, but a horizon given as one of the two beds should be checked against the other paper.

# Jinhua

## Does the Jinhua take the Albian and drop the Coniacian?

*2026-10-08*

**Conclusion.** Yes to both, applying the 2026-10-04 entry: the formation is Albian to Turonian. The Albian rests on Yu and colleagues' dated lower basalt and their Albian-to-early-Late-Cretaceous range for the dinosaur beds; the Coniacian rests only on a passing, unsourced label. Neither taxon has a horizon dated more finely than the lower Jinhua, so both take the unit's range. Governs `period: [Early Cretaceous, Late Cretaceous]` and `stages: [Albian, Cenomanian, Turonian]` on [Jinhua](../../../stratigraphy/j/jinhua.yml), and the same on [*Jiangshanosaurus lixianensis*](../../../genera/J/Jiangshanosaurus.yml) and [*Dongyangosaurus sinensis*](../../../genera/D/Dongyangosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- yu2010a, p. 98: the lower Jinhua basalt is "(101.8±3.2) Ma"; p. 99: the dinosaur- and egg-bearing strata range "早白垩世晚期 albian 期至晚白垩世早期" ("from the late Early Cretaceous Albian to the early Late Cretaceous", translated).

**Ruled out.**
- *The Coniacian.* Mayer and colleagues' "Turonian-Coniacian Jinhua Formation" (mayer2026a, p. 26) gives no source, and a passing label is not a reading.

**Open.** Nothing.

## What is the age of the Jinhua Formation?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** Late Albian to Turonian on the sources read here. The registry's Cenomanian–Coniacian drops an Albian that the cited dating paper leaves possible for the lower formation and keeps a Coniacian with a single unsourced label behind it. Governs `stages` on Jinhua (recommended Albian–Turonian, with a note on the Coniacian) and the stages of [*Jiangshanosaurus lixianensis*](../../../genera/J/Jiangshanosaurus.yml) and [*Dongyangosaurus sinensis*](../../../genera/D/Dongyangosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- yu2010a, p. 98: the Jinhua's lower basalt is "(101.8±3.2) Ma", relayed from its reference 19; on the current chart this is late Albian at its central value, reaching the Cenomanian within error.
- yu2010a, p. 97: honeycomb-egg horizons in "金华组…的下部" ("the lower part of the Jinhua Formation", translated); p. 99: those horizons "可能为早白垩世晚期" ("may be late Early Cretaceous", translated), and the dinosaur- and egg-bearing strata range "早白垩世晚期 albian 期至晚白垩世早期" ("from the late Early Cretaceous Albian to the early Late Cretaceous", translated), within an overall "105 Ma~90 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *Albian on the 105 Ma Rb-Sr date alone.* Tang and colleagues' 105 Ma (tang2001a, p. 272) is the superseded basis; the Albian survives on Yu and colleagues' later evidence, not on it.
- *Aptian–Albian.* Wilson (2005) lists "Jianshanosaurus" from the Jinhua as "Aptian-Albian" (wilson2005a, p. 314, Table 2), crediting Tang and colleagues, who give Albian.

**Open.** The Coniacian. Its only source read for this note is a passing "Turonian-Coniacian Jinhua Formation" (mayer2026a, p. 26), with no citation; Yu and colleagues' range ends at about 90 Ma, in the Turonian.

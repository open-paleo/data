# Jiufotang

## What does He and colleagues' (2004) date give the Jiufotang Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The Aptian, and no other stage. He and colleagues (2004) date K-feldspar from tuffs interbedded with the fossil-bearing shales at Shangheshou, Chaoyang, to 120.3 ± 0.7 Ma, and say the result confirms an Aptian age reached mainly on biostratigraphy, early Aptian in their conclusions. Their paper is the primary behind the 120.3 ± 0.7 Ma that Zhou and Zhang (2006) relay, so the two count as one statement of the age ([methods](../../methods.md#count-each-original-statement-of-an-age-once)). The Albian appears nowhere in it; among the works cited for the formation it comes from Turner and colleagues (2012). Governs the Aptian in `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on [Jiufotang](../../../stratigraphy/j/jiufotang.yml).

**Evidence.**
- he2004a, p. 1, Abstract: "40Ar/39Ar step heating analyses of K-feldspar and the SHRIMP U-Pb zircon data indicate that tuffs at the Shangheshou section erupted at 120.3 ± 0.7 million years ago. This result confirms an Aptian age for the Jiufotang Formation that was mainly based on biostratigraphic evidence."
- he2004a, p. 1, Abstract: "It also places stringent controls on the age of the fossils from the formation, providing a minimum age (120 Ma) for the four-winged dinosaur, Microraptor, and the seed-eating bird, Jeholornis."
- he2004a, p. 1, Introduction: a basalt dated "110.59 ± 0.52 million years obtained from intrusive basalt within the formation [Eberth et al., 1993], which probably provides a top limit for the Jiufotang Formation".
- he2004a, p. 3, Discussion and Conclusions: the tuffs give "the direct age for the fossil-bearing sediments"; the basalt "most likely represents a much later volcanic event"; the age "corresponds to an early Aptian age of the late Early Cretaceous".

**Ruled out.**
- *120 Ma as a minimum age for the formation.* Where the paper calls the figure a minimum age it speaks of the fossils, *Microraptor* and *Jeholornis* among them, not of the unit; the tuffs are interbedded with the fossil-bearing shales and date them directly.
- *An Albian from the Tebch basalt.* The 110.59 Ma intrusion that He and colleagues relay from Eberth and colleagues (1993) is at most "a top limit", and the paper treats it as "a much later volcanic event"; a limit is not an age ([methods](../../methods.md#rules-stated-in-contributingmd)).

**Open.** He and colleagues (2004) date one section; whether the formation reaches the Albian elsewhere is not addressed by them, and rests on Turner and colleagues (2012).

## Do the Meileyingzi Beds have a stage of their own?

*2026-10-06*

Supersedes the Aptian the Meileyingzi Beds and *Psittacosaurus meileyingensis* carried until 2026-10-06.

**Conclusion.** No. Sereno's (2010) "(Aptian)" follows the formation's name in the type horizon line, so the beds take the Jiufotang's epoch and no stage (see [methods](../../methods.md#a-parent-units-range-repeated-for-a-child-unit-is-the-parents-statement)). No source read dates the horizon of *P. meileyingensis* itself, so the species takes the formation's range, Aptian–Albian (see [methods](../../methods.md#decide-a-species-stage-on-statements-about-its-horizon-not-about-its-formation)). Governs `period: [Early Cretaceous]` and the absence of `stages` on the Meileyingzi Beds of [Jiufotang](../../../stratigraphy/j/jiufotang.yml), and `period.stage: [Aptian, Albian]` on [*Psittacosaurus meileyingensis*](../../../genera/P/Psittacosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- sereno2010a, p. 31, Type Horizon and Locality: "Meileyingzi Beds, Jiufotang Formation (Aptian); Beipiao, Liaoning Province, People's Republic of China".
- sereno1988b, p. 377: the species "is known only from the type locality", in "the same beds (Jiufotang Formation)" as a skull of *P. mongoliensis*; the paper gives no stage.

**Ruled out.**
- *Meileyingzi Beds, Aptian.* The parenthesis follows "Jiufotang Formation", not "Meileyingzi Beds".
- *P. meileyingensis, Aptian alone.* It rested on the same formation-level parenthesis, which is one of the readings that make up the Jiufotang's Aptian–Albian.

**Open.** A dating of the Meileyingzi Beds within the Jiufotang would narrow both the beds and the species.

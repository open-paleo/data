# Navajo Sandstone

## On what evidence does Baird (1980) date the Navajo Sandstone Sinemurian to Pliensbachian?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Baird (1980) dates the Navajo Sandstone Sinemurian to Pliensbachian on two lines of evidence. One is Galton's (1971) correlation of its reptiles, *Ammosaurus* and *Protosuchus*, with those of the Portland Formation, which Baird keeps while noting that the Portland has been redated as lower Liassic. The other is a *Corollina*-dominated microflora from the Whitmore Point Member of the Moenave, another Glen Canyon Group unit, correlated with the lower Liassic. Baird also relays Olsen and Galton's (1977) chart, which runs the Glen Canyon Group from the Hettangian well into the Pliensbachian, and gives the *Navahopus falcipollex* type trackway, from the upper Navajo, the same Sinemurian–Pliensbachian age. The Toarcian comes from Tykoski and Rowe (2004). Governs the Sinemurian and Pliensbachian in `stages` on [Navajo Sandstone](../../../stratigraphy/n/navajo-sandstone.yml).

**Evidence.**
- baird1980a, p. 219: "Until recently most authors have assigned the Navajo Sandstone, wholly or in part, to the Upper Triassic"; Galton's "assignment of a Late Triassic age to the Navajo was based on its reptile fauna".
- baird1980a, p. 221: "While Galton's correlation of the Navajo with the Portland remains valid, the latter is no longer Triassic but has been redated as lower Liassic".
- baird1980a, p. 221: in Olsen and Galton's chart the Glen Canyon is shown "as beginning in Hettangian time and continuing through the Sinemurian until well along in the Pliensbachian".
- baird1980a, p. 221: the Whitmore Point microflora is correlated "with the lower Liassic on the basis of a preponderance of Corollina (more than 90 percent of palynoflora). This evidence suggests a Sinemurian to Pliensbachian age for the Navajo Sandstone."
- baird1980a, p. 221: the *Navahopus* type trackway comes from "Cross-stratified dune sand of the upper Navajo Sandstone, Glen Canyon Group, Lower Jurassic (Sinemurian-Pliensbachian)", on the Kaibito Plateau.
- tykoski2004a, p. 68: *Segisaurus halli* comes "from the Navajo Sandstone (Pliensbachian-Toarcian) of northeastern Arizona".
- sertich2010a, p. 2, Horizon and Age: "The Navajo Sandstone is the uppermost unit of the Glen Canyon Group with an age no earlier than Pleinsbachian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Late Triassic.* Galton (1971) assigned the Navajo to the Late Triassic because its reptiles tie it to the Portland Formation, then held to be Triassic. Baird (1980) keeps the correlation, and with the Portland redated as lower Liassic the same evidence gives an Early Jurassic age.

**Open.** The Sinemurian rests on Baird's correlation of a microflora from the Moenave, while the Navajo is the uppermost unit of the Glen Canyon Group; that the Moenave age need not reach the Navajo is an inference from the two papers, not a statement in either. Sertich and Loewen (2010) give the Navajo an age no earlier than Pliensbachian, and Tykoski and Rowe (2004) Pliensbachian–Toarcian. The review that Sertich and Loewen cite for their age, Irmis (2005), would show whether the Sinemurian should stay.

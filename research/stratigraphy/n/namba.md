# Namba

## Does the Namba keep the Maastrichtian?

*2026-11-27*

**Conclusion.** No; the member is Cenomanian to Campanian (see [Galula](../g/galula.md)). Widlansky and colleagues (2018) reach the lowermost Maastrichtian only in a conditional, and commit to "Cenomanian – Campanian" for the member. Governs `stages`, Cenomanian to Campanian, and the note on widlansky2018a on [Namba](../../../stratigraphy/n/namba.yml); [*Rukwatitan*](../../../genera/R/Rukwatitan.yml) and [*Shingopana*](../../../genera/S/Shingopana.yml) are unaffected.

**Evidence.**
- widlansky2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 36: "Together these interpretations constrain the age of the Namba Member to Cenomanian – Campanian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Maastrichtian.* A conditional.

**Open.** Unchanged: a radiometric age for the upper member.

## Does Widlansky and colleagues' Aptian–Cenomanian bear on the Namba?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No. Widlansky and colleagues (2018) read the Galula Formation's geological constraints as pointing to an Aptian–Cenomanian age while leaving it "unclear how the individual members" fit, and Roberts and colleagues (2010) give the formation the same age; neither assigns it to a member, and it lies within the [Galula](../../../stratigraphy/g/galula.yml)'s Aptian–Maastrichtian. The Galula note on Widlansky and colleagues now gives the sentence. Governs nothing on [Namba](../../../stratigraphy/n/namba.yml); `stages` unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- widlansky2018a, pp. 28–29: the constraints "point to an Aptian – Cenomanian age, though it remains unclear how the individual members of the Galula Formation fit into this age framework".
- roberts2010a, p. 209: "A middle Cretaceous age (Aptian– Cenomanian) and a late Paleogene (Oligocene) age are assigned to the Galula and Nsungwe Formations, respectively".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Unchanged: a radiometric age for the upper Namba Member.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Namba Member?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian through Campanian. Widlansky and colleagues (2018) correlate three short reversals in the upper Namba Member either with Chron C32 (Campanian), the correlation they favor, or with short excursions late in the Cretaceous Normal Superchron (C34n), and give the two together as a bracket of "Cenomanian – Campanian". A paper's own either-or contributes both readings and every stage between, under the rule in [methods](../../methods.md) that a reported spread is other authors' readings, so all five stages stand. Gorscak and O'Connor (2019) take the favored correlation, "late Campanian (Chron c32)", for their analyses. The bracket is a magnetostratigraphic age, not a biostratigraphic one: the paper reports that no comprehensive paleomagnetic study of the Galula Formation preceded it, and calls its bracket "a significant refinement over previous constraints". Governs `stages` on [Namba](../../../stratigraphy/n/namba.yml).

**Evidence.**
- widlansky2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 36, 6. Conclusions: "We propose two potential correlations for relating these reversals to the GPTS, with the first one favored here: (1) These reversals may be equivalent to Chron C32 (Campanian), or (2) the reversals may represent short excursions during the upper part of the CNS (C34n). Together these interpretations constrain the age of the Namba Member to Cenomanian – Campanian, a significant refinement over previous constraints."
- widlansky2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 15, 2.2 Previous Paleomagnetic Studies from the Cretaceous of Africa: "To date, no comprehensive paleomagnetic studies have been conducted for the Galula Formation in the Rukwa Rift Basin".
- widlansky2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 8, 2.1.2 Age Estimates for the Galula Formation: "a single ~118 Ma grain was recovered from the upper Namba Member (E. M. Roberts, unpublished data), suggesting deposition after the middle Early Cretaceous if supported by subsequent observations".
- widlansky2018a, accepted manuscript, p. 29, 5.1 Correlation to the Geomagnetic Polarity Timescale (GPTS): "Together, these point to an Aptian – Cenomanian age, though it remains unclear how the individual members of the Galula Formation fit into this age framework".
- gorscak2019a, p. 6, Age and distribution: "a younger date for the overlying Namba Member as either Campanian or Cenomanian–Santonian".
- gorscak2019a, p. 42, Phylogenetics: "the Namba Member is constrained to the preferred hypothesis of late Campanian (Chron c32) according to Widlansky et al."

**Ruled out.**
- *A post-Aptian maximum depositional age from the ~118 Ma zircon.* Widlansky and colleagues (2018) report one grain from the upper member, from unpublished data, and hedge it twice: it only suggests deposition after the middle Early Cretaceous, and only "if supported by subsequent observations". Unpublished data, like a personal communication, is not a published determination, and one grain is not a population. It would not change the stages in any case, since about 118 Ma falls below the Cenomanian floor of the bracket.
- *Campanian alone.* Widlansky and colleagues (2018) favor the C32 correlation but keep the C34n one, and state the age as the bracket the two give together; Gorscak and colleagues (2022) still raise the older "Cenomanian-Santonian" alternative. Cutting the stages to the favored correlation would drop a reading the dating paper itself keeps.

**Open.** The same paper's Discussion reads the formation's geological constraints (carbonatite emplacement, seismic velocity, detrital zircons, the Malawi Dinosaur Beds) as pointing to an Aptian–Cenomanian age for at least part of the Galula Formation, without saying how the members fit. A published radiometric age for the upper Namba Member would settle where in the bracket the member lies.

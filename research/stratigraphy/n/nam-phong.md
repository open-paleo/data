# Nam Phong

## Which Racey (2009) do Laojumpon and colleagues cite?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** Racey's single-author chapter, by author and title; the Pliensbachian bound traces to Racey and Goodall (2009) either way. Laojumpon and colleagues' reference list gives "Racey, A., 2009" with the words of the chapter's title, though its pages, 67–81, match neither Racey paper. That chapter gives the Upper Nam Phong's "cannot be older than Pliensbachian" from the well palynology of Racey and Goodall, who state the same bound. Governs nothing on [Nam Phong](../../../stratigraphy/n/nam-phong.yml); the 2026-11-23 entry's reading stands.

**Evidence.**
- laojumpon2017a, p. 1169: "Palynological evidences indicated that the formation is not older than Pliensbachian (Late Early Jurassic period) (Racey, 2009)".
- laojumpon2017a, p. 1178, References: "Racey, A., 2009. The Khorat Group of NE Thailand Mesozoic red bed sequences from SE Asia and the significance of Thailand."
- racey2009a, p. 52: "Limited palynological data from the Phu Horm-1 well presented by Racey & Goodall (2009) suggest that the Upper Nam Phong Formation cannot be older than Pliensbachian".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Unchanged.

## What ages do the cited works give the two parts of the Nam Phong?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The Lower Nam Phong is late Norian to Rhaetian and the Upper Nam Phong Pliensbachian to Late Jurassic, tentatively Late Jurassic. Racey and colleagues (1996) dated the formation late Norian or Rhaetian from outcrop palynology before it was divided, and placed a probable hiatus, possibly spanning the whole Jurassic, between it and the Phu Kradung Formation above. Racey and Goodall (2009) then divided it from subsurface data into a Rhaetian Lower and a Pliensbachian-to-Late Jurassic Upper Nam Phong, and moved that hiatus to the top of the Lower Nam Phong. No cited work dates either part to the Hettangian or Sinemurian: those stages fall in the hiatus between the two parts, and the unit lists them only because its stages run unbroken from the oldest to the youngest. Laojumpon and colleagues (2017) apply the Pliensbachian bound to the whole formation and place two sauropod sites in the upper part from their resemblance to Early Jurassic sauropods. Governs `period` and `stages` on [Nam Phong](../../../stratigraphy/n/nam-phong.yml).

**Evidence.**
- racey1996a, p. 5, Abstract: "The Nam Phong Formation is dated accurately for the first time as late Norian to Rhaetian".
- racey1996a, p. 13: "the occurrence of common *Ovalipollis ovalis* … and striate bisaccate pollen (undifferentiated) in the Nam Phong Fm indicates an age no younger than Rhaetian and no older than Ladinian"; "The general lack of Rhaetian marker taxa tends to suggest an older, Norian age for the Huai Hin Lat Formation. Consequently, the Nam Phong Formation must be late Norian or Rhaetian in age."
- racey1996a, p. 32: "Petrographically, the Nam Phong Formation has more in common with the underlying Huai Hin Lat Formation than with the overlying Khorat Group"; p. 33: "the Triassic Nam Phong Formation and the Khorat Group may be separated by a diastem of unknown status (possibly representing much of the Jurassic)".
- racey1996a, p. 36, Conclusions: "a probable hiatus of unknown duration (possibly spanning the entire Jurassic) between the Nam Phong Formation and the overlying Phu Kradung Formation".
- racey2009b, p. 69, Abstract: "From subsurface seismic data it is clear that the Nam Phong Formation comprises two distinct units separated by an unconformity".
- racey2009b, p. 81: "This unconformity has yet to be identified at outcrop, and as the red beds straddling the unconformity are lithologically similar it would be difficult to detect at outcrop".
- racey2009b, p. 82: the data "support the original view of Racey et al. (1994, 1996) that a hiatus separates the Late Triassic Lower Nam Phong Formation from the remainder of the Khorat Group"; the Upper Nam Phong has "an age no older than Pliensbachian and no younger than Late Jurassic".
- racey2009a, p. 52: the Upper Nam Phong "cannot be older than Pliensbachian (middle Early Jurassic) based on the presence of *C. turbatus*".
- laojumpon2017a, p. 1169: "Palynological evidences indicated that the formation is not older than Pliensbachian (Late Early Jurassic period) (Racey, 2009)"; the seismic analysis "clearly divides this formation into two sections: 1) The Lower Nam Phong Formation which was formed in Upper Triassic period and 2) the Upper Nam Phong Formation, which is neither older than Plienbaschian nor younger than Late Jurassic period".
- laojumpon2017a, p. 1177: "sauropods in this study are more similar to the Early Jurassic than Triassic dinosaurs. Thus, Phu Hin Tan and Non Sra Ard village locality should be belonging to the upper part of the Nam Phong Formation in the Early Jurassic age" (the sentence ends on p. 1178).

**Ruled out.**
- *A Pliensbachian floor for the whole formation.* Laojumpon and colleagues (2017) write that palynology makes "the formation" no older than Pliensbachian, citing Racey (2009). Racey gives that bound for the Upper Nam Phong only (racey2009a, p. 52), and Laojumpon and colleagues' own next sentence puts the Lower Nam Phong in the Late Triassic.
- *A hiatus spanning the whole Jurassic above the Nam Phong.* Racey and colleagues (1996) proposed it before the formation was divided. Racey and Goodall (2009) place the hiatus above the Lower Nam Phong, and the Upper Nam Phong, Pliensbachian to Late Jurassic, falls inside the interval it was once thought to span.

**Open.** Whether any outcrop belongs to the Upper Nam Phong. The unconformity has not been found at outcrop, and Laojumpon and colleagues assign their sauropod sites to the upper part from the sauropods alone. Palynology or a radiometric age from those beds would settle it.

## Is the Nam Phong one formation, and which part is Isanosaurus from?

*2026-10-01*

**Conclusion.** Racey and Goodall (2009) split it, from subsurface data, into a Rhaetian Lower and a Late Jurassic(?) Upper Nam Phong separated by an unconformity that has not been found at outcrop; the [*Isanosaurus attavipachi*](../../../genera/I/Isanosaurus.yml) site cannot be assigned to either part. Governs keeping Nam Phong as one entry with the union of both parts' ages, and the same span on *Isanosaurus*.

**Evidence.**
- racey2009b, p. 69: "the Nam Phong Formation comprises two distinct units separated by an unconformity"; "The age of the Lower Nam Phong Formation based on palynology is Late Triassic (Rhaetian)"; p. 81: the Upper Nam Phong is "considered to be no older than Pliensbachian"; p. 82: "a Late Jurassic age is tentatively proposed".
- racey2009b, pp. 80–81: "This unconformity has yet to be identified at outcrop"; p. 78: "The Nam Phong Formation at outcrop has, to date, yielded only Late Triassic (late Norian to Rhaetian) palynomorphs".

**Ruled out.**
- *Upper Nam Phong, Early Jurassic, by Racey and Goodall.* Rauhut and colleagues (2020) write that "the Upper Nam Phong Formation that has yielded *Isanosaurus* has recently been dated as Early Jurassic (Racey and Goodall 2009)" (rauhut2020a, p. 2). Racey and Goodall never mention *Isanosaurus* and give the upper part as no older than Pliensbachian, tentatively Late Jurassic.
- *Early Middle Jurassic.* Hanta and colleagues' title gives the site an early Middle Jurassic age (hanta2023a, p. 222), but the paper places the beds only "likely" in the Upper Nam Phong by rock type (p. 238, partly after Booth and Sattayarak 2011) and cites a lower bound "no earlier than Pliensbachian" from an ostracod (p. 224, after Chitnarin and colleagues 2022); nothing supports a Middle Jurassic age.

**Open.** Which part holds the *Isanosaurus* site. Jia and colleagues (2022) report a detrital-zircon maximum of about 208 Ma for the Nam Phong in another basin (jia2022a, p. 3481), which was not weighed here.

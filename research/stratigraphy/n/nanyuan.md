# Nanyuan

## Does the 148 Ma ignimbrite belong to the Nanyuan?

*2026-11-26*

**Conclusion.** No work read says so, and nothing places it outside; the Tithonian stays. Xu and colleagues (2023) put their excavated section in "the lower part of the Nanyuan Formation" and date ignimbrites that "underlie (Bdx03) and overlie (Bdx04) the fossil-bearing sediments", without naming their unit. Xi and colleagues (2021) carry the formation up to ">145—130 Ma", after Cao and colleagues (2020), so a bed of 148 ± 1.9 Ma above beds in its lower part falls within the formation's span as published; the placement remains an inference, not a statement. Governs `stages` on [Nanyuan](../../../stratigraphy/n/nanyuan.yml), Kimmeridgian to Tithonian, unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- xu2023a, p. ?, Methods: "The section that we excavated is 3.6 m in depth and belongs to the lower part of the Nanyuan Formation."
- xu2023a, p. ?, Methods: "We also collected ignimbrite samples that underlie (Bdx03) and overlie (Bdx04) the fossil-bearing sediments."
- xi2021a, p. 385: "其岩性以火山岩为主, 年龄>145—130 Ma (曹明轩等, 2020)" ["its lithology is dominated by volcanic rocks, and its age is >145–130 Ma", translated].

**Ruled out.**
- *The 148 Ma ignimbrite as a limit above the formation.* No work places a formation top between the fossil beds and the ignimbrite.

**Open.** A section that names the unit of the Bdx04 ignimbrite would settle it. Xi and colleagues' Early Cretaceous for the formation, relayed from Cao and colleagues (2020), is not among the stages; that work has not been read.

## What age do Xu and colleagues' (2023) dates give the Nanyuan Formation?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Kimmeridgian to Tithonian. Xu and colleagues (2023) date two tuffites below and above the *Fujianvenator*-bearing layers in the lower part of the formation by zircon U-Pb to 150.2 ± 1.1 and 149.9 ± 1.1 Ma, and ignimbrites below and above the fossil-bearing sediments to 150 ± 1.8 and 148 ± 1.9 Ma. On the ICS chart v2024/12, with the base of the Tithonian at 149.2 Ma, the two tuffite dates are Kimmeridgian at their central values and their uncertainties reach into the Tithonian, and the upper ignimbrite is Tithonian at its central value. The paper's own "Tithonian stage" for the horizon is a label from an older chart, as set out in [Morrison Tithonian labels](../../topics/morrison-tithonian-labels.md); the same dates put the *Fujianvenator* layers in the Kimmeridgian and *Zhengheornis*, above the highest dated tuffite, in the Kimmeridgian–Tithonian (see [*Zhengheornis*](../../genera/Z/Zhengheornis.md)). Governs `stages: [Kimmeridgian, Tithonian]` on [Nanyuan](../../../stratigraphy/n/nanyuan.yml).

**Evidence.**
- xu2023a, p. 337: "Near Yangyuan Village, Zhenghe Country, Nanping City, Fujian Province; Upper Jurassic, Nanyuan Formation (Tithonian stage; 149.9–150.2 Ma; see Methods)".
- xu2023a, p. 342: "The *Fujianvenator*-bearing horizon of the Nanyuan Formation is composed of black carbonaceous mudstone and shale, interstratified with tuffite".
- xu2023a, Methods, Age of the IVPP V31985-bearing horizon and the Zhenghe Fauna: "concordant zircon U-Pb ages of 150.2 ± 1.1 Ma (PM702-1) and 149.9 ± 1.1 Ma (PM701-4) for the lower and upper layers of the IVPP V31985-bearing horizons"; "Concordant zircon U-Pb ages of 150 ± 1.8 Ma (Bdx03) and 148 ± 1.9 Ma (Bdx04) were obtained from the ignimbrite samples"; "Together, these results constrain the Zhenghe Fauna to a time interval of 148–150 Ma."

**Ruled out.**
- *Tithonian alone, from the paper's label.* "Tithonian stage" is printed beside 149.9–150.2 Ma, dates that fall below the current Tithonian base of 149.2 Ma; the label comes from a chart that put the base lower, and the dates, not the label, place the beds.
- *Kimmeridgian alone.* The tuffite dates' uncertainties reach the Tithonian, and the upper ignimbrite, at 148 ± 1.9 Ma, is Tithonian at its central value.

**Open.** Two points. First, Xu and colleagues say the ignimbrites "underlie (Bdx03) and overlie (Bdx04) the fossil-bearing sediments" but not whether they belong to the Nanyuan Formation; the formation is mainly volcanic, which suggests they do (inference). If the upper ignimbrite lies above the formation, it is a ceiling and adds no stage. Second, all four dates come from the lower part of the formation; the age of its upper part is not dated in the works cited.

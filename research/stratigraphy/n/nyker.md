## What do the cited works say about the age and setting of the Nyker Group?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Christiansen and Bonde (2003) date the group "(Tithonian?) Berriasian to Valanginian". Lindgren and colleagues (2008) call it "principally Lower Cretaceous", place its terrestrial to marginal marine strata in six fault blocks of southwestern Bornholm, formed by repeated down-faulting and tilting along the Fennoscandian Border Zone in the Triassic and Jurassic, and divide its lowest formation, the Rabekke, into the Homanshald and Skyttegård Members, the Homanshald resting disconformably on the Lower Jurassic Hasle Formation. After Christensen (1974) they give "the entire sequence" as Berriasian–Valanginian, perhaps Tithonian at the base. Governs `stages` and `period` on [Nyker](../../../stratigraphy/n/nyker.yml).

**Evidence.**
- christiansen2003a, p. 288: "The Nyker Group (Fig. 2) spans from (Tithonian?) Berriasian to Valanginian in age".
- lindgren2008a, p. 254: "The terrestrial to marginal marine strata of the principally Lower Cretaceous Nyker Group crop out in the Holsterhus, Nyker, Knudsker, Salene, Bøsthøj and Arnager-Sose fault blocks"; "These blocks were formed during the Triassic and Jurassic as a result of repeated periods of down-faulting and tilting along the Fennoscandian Border Zone".
- lindgren2008a, p. 254: "The Rabekke Formation (the lowermost formation of the Nyker Group) is subdivided into a lower Homanshald Member and an upper Skyttegård Member"; the Homanshald "rests disconformably upon nearshore marine sandstones of the Lower Jurassic Hasle Formation"; "The entire sequence spans the Berriasian–Valanginian interval, although its lowermost parts may be of Tithonian age (Christensen, 1974)".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** Lindgren and colleagues' (2008) "entire sequence" sentence closes their paragraph on the Rabekke Formation in the Arnager-Sose block, so it may date the Rabekke's two members rather than the whole group; this is a reading of the paragraph, not something the paper states. Christensen (1974), the source Lindgren and colleagues cite for the span, would settle which unit it dates and whether it states a Tithonian age.

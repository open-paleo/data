# Neuquén

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Neuquén Group?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Cenomanian to Campanian. Hugo and Leanza (2001), Leanza and colleagues (2004) and Garrido (2010) all start the group at the unconformity at its base, which they place in the Lower Cenomanian, and end it at the Huantraiquican unconformity in the Campanian: early Campanian in the first two, Middle Campanian in Garrido. No work cited dates the group radiometrically; both bounds come from the ages given to the unconformities. Hugo and Leanza assign its three subgroups in turn to the Cenomanian to lower Turonian, the upper Turonian to Coniacian, and the Santonian to lower Campanian. Governs `stages: [Cenomanian, Turonian, Coniacian, Santonian, Campanian]` and `period` on [Neuquén](../../../stratigraphy/n/neuquén.yml).

**Evidence.**
- hugo2001a, p. 2, Abstract: "whose age ranges from early Cenomanian (97 ± 3 Ma) to early Campanian (74 ± 3 Ma)"; "the Cenomanian-lower Turonian Limay Subgroup", "the upper Turonian-Coniacian Río Neuquén Subgroup", "the Santonian-lower Campanian Río Colorado Subgroup".
- hugo2001a, p. 4: the group, "with all its continental origin units, accumulated in the Upper Cretaceous, more precisely between the Cenomanian and the lower Campanian" (translated).
- hugo2001a, p. 10: deposition begins after "the intercretaceous unconformity determined by the Main Miránica phase" (translated), which "can be homologated to the discontinuity of global character placed by Vail and Todd (1980) in the Lower Cenomanian (97 ± 3 Ma)" (translated); the top is the maximum age of the Malargüe Group, "which would have begun to be deposited from the Upper Campanian" (translated); "its deposition had a duration of approximately 23 Ma" (translated).
- leanza2004a, p. 62: "Radioisotopic dating is still lacking for the Neuquén Group"; the estimated ages "have been mainly obtained from the ages of the superimposed intervals limited by regional unconformities".
- leanza2004a, p. 64: the Neuquén and Malargüe groups "are separated from the previous strata by the Main Miranican unconformity"; "The Neuquén Group was laid down during nearly 20 myr from the Cenomanian through to the Early Campanian".
- leanza2004a, p. 65: "The Huantraiquican unconformity separates the Neuquén Group from the Malargüe Group made up of the Allen, Jagüel and Roca formations".
- leanza2004a, p. 67: "Stipanicic et al. (1968) were the first authors to use the name Neuquén Group for these strata", divided into the Río Limay, Río Neuquén and Río Colorado subgroups "(Cazau and Uliana, 1973)"; "It was laid down between the Cenomanian and Early Campanian, and thus represents a total duration of nearly 24.9 myr".
- garrido2010a, p. 121, Abstract: "developed in the Neuquén Basin between the Lower Cenomanian and the Middle Campanian (Upper Cretaceous)"; "This succession reaches 1,200 m of maximum thick, being bounded at the base and the top by the Patagonidican and Huantraiquican unconformities respectively"; "two new formations are proposed (Los Bastos and Sierra Barrosa Formations, nom. nov.)"; "three subgroups and nine formations".
- garrido2010a, p. 124: the two unconformities "have been placed at the base of the Cenomanian (Leanza, 2009; Tunik et al., 2010) and in the Middle Campanian (Dingus et al., 2009) respectively" (translated); the group is "a succession of red beds composed mainly of sandstones, mudstones and graywackes; with a reduced participation of conglomeratic horizons" (translated).
- garrido2010a, p. 132: south of the Huincul Ridge the Candeleros Formation rests on "deposits of the Lohan Cura Formation, the latter with an estimated age in the Albian" (translated).
- garrido2010a, p. 134: "the Main Miránica or Patagonidic Unconformity" (translated), the same surface under two names.
- bonaparte1979c, p. 393, Abstract: "The age of the Neuquén Groups (Senonian premaastrichtian) (Cazau and Uliana, 1973; Bertels, 1969) is in agreement with the specialization of the Sauropods present in it"; the sauropods of the two groups "indicate that the components of both lithologic groups cropping out in the Sierra San Bernardo are contemporaneous".
- bonaparte1979c, p. 396: a typical horizon line, "Neuquén Group, Río Colorado Formation, possibly Bajo de la Carpa Member. Pre-Maastrichtian Senonian" (translated); every Neuquén Group horizon in the paper carries the same age.
- bonaparte1979c, p. 404: "The pre-Maastrichtian Senonian age of the Neuquén Group (Cazau and Uliana, 1973; Bertels, 1969)" (translated), resting on the "Intersenonian discordance at the base of the group" and on microfossils from the Jagüel Formation at its roof "which indicate lower and middle Maastrichtian" (translated); "The sauropods from the Río Neuquén, Río Colorado, and Allen Formations of the Neuquén Group" (translated) correlate with those of the Castillo, Bajo Barreal and Laguna Palacios Formations of the Chubut Group.

**Ruled out.**
- *A Senonian group, with no Cenomanian or Turonian.* Bonaparte and Gasparini (1979) date the group "pre-Maastrichtian Senonian", that is Coniacian to Campanian, after Cazau and Uliana (1973) and Bertels (1969), reading the unconformity at its base as "Intersenonian". Hugo and Leanza (2001), Leanza and colleagues (2004) and Garrido (2010) place the same basal unconformity (the Main Miranic or Patagonidic) at the base of the Cenomanian, and none of them follows the Senonian reading. Bonaparte and Gasparini's group also includes the Allen Formation, which Hugo and Leanza (2001) and Leanza and colleagues (2004) put in the Malargüe Group above the Huantraiquican unconformity, so their age is for a differently bounded unit.
- *Albian at the base.* Garrido (2010) gives the Albian to the Lohan Cura Formation beneath the group, not to any part of it.

**Open.** The two bounds are ages given to unconformities by correlation, and none of the works cited dates the group's base or top radiometrically. The stated durations disagree: Leanza and colleagues (2004) give nearly 20 myr on p. 64 and nearly 24.9 myr on p. 67 for the same Cenomanian to Early Campanian span, and Hugo and Leanza (2001) about 23 Ma. Neither the durations nor the Early or Middle Campanian top changes the stages.

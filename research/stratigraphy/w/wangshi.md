# Wangshi

## Which scheme does the registry follow for the Wangshi Group's formations, and where are the Laiyang dinosaur beds?

*2026-10-23*

**Conclusion.** The provincial scheme: the Wangshi Group is the Linjiazhuang, Xingezhuang, Hongtuya, Jingangkou and Jiaozhou formations, and the Laiyang and Zhucheng dinosaur beds, including those at Jiangjunding and Jingangkou, are Hongtuya. The provincial lithostratigraphy (Zhang and Liu 1996) adopts these five formations, makes the Tianqiaotun–Jiangjunding section a parastratotype of the Hongtuya, and lists *Tsintaosaurus*, *Tanius* and *Chingkankousaurus* from it; its Jingangkou Formation is 105.5 m of lake beds above, with an invertebrate fauna. Jiangjunding and Changwangpu are not units in it. The national Cretaceous synthesis (Xi and colleagues 2021) follows the provincial volume. Hu and colleagues (2001) use a parallel scheme of Xingezhuang, Jiangjunding, Jingangkou and Changwangpu formations after a 1995 revision, in which "Jingangkou" names a 680 m unit holding the dinosaur beds, a homonym of the provincial formation. Governs `formation: Hongtuya` on [*Tanius sinensis*](../../../genera/T/Tanius.yml), [*Tsintaosaurus spinorhinus*](../../../genera/T/Tsintaosaurus.yml), [*Laiyangosaurus youngi*](../../../genera/L/Laiyangosaurus.yml) and [*Chingkankousaurus fragilis*](../../../genera/C/Chingkankousaurus.yml); the removal of the Jiangjunding entry, whose sources move to [Hongtuya](../../../stratigraphy/h/hongtuya.yml); `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on Hongtuya, taking in the upper Campanian–lower Maastrichtian that Borinder and colleagues give for the Jiangjunding beds; and the removal of the Jingangkou entry, which no record names once its dinosaur beds are Hongtuya, its sources kept on Hongtuya.

**Evidence.**
- zhang1996a, p. 233: the group "From bottom to top it comprises the Linjiazhuang Formation, Xingezhuang Formation, Hongtuya Formation, Jingangkou Formation, and Jiaozhou Formation" (translated); p. 320, Appendix I: Wangshi Group adopted, row 118.
- zhang1996a, p. 238: the Hongtuya lies "conformably above the Xingezhuang Formation and below the Jingangkou Formation" (translated); p. 239: "Tianqiaotun and Jiangjunding southwest of Laiyang City ... serve as the parastratotype of this formation", over 3,000 m thick, and "Large numbers of hadrosaurid ... dinosaur fossils have been found at Tianqiaotun, Jiangjunding, and Jingangkou" (translated); p. 247: "The area around Tianqiaotun, Laiyang, yields *Tsintaosaurus spinorhinus*, *Tanius chinkangkouensis*, *T. sinensis*, *Chinkangkousaurus fragivis*" (translated), in the Hongtuya fauna.
- zhang1996a, p. 239: the Jingangkou "Refers to local shallow-lake and lakeshore facies deposits conformably overlying the Hongtuya Formation" (translated); p. 240: holostratotype 105.5 m; p. 318, Appendix I: adopted, row 54. Jiangjunding and Changwangpu appear in neither appendix.
- xi2021a: the Wangshi "is divided, from bottom to top, into the Linjiazhuang Formation (Malanggou Formation), Xingezhuang Formation (Tianjialou Formation), Hongtuya Formation and Jiaozhou Formation", the Jiaolai framework following "*Lithostratigraphy of Shandong Province* (Shandong Bureau of Geology and Mineral Resources, 1996)".
- xu2019a: "the Hongtuya Formation of the Wangshi Group between Jiangjuding and Jingangkou".
- hu2001a, p. 88: the group of four formations, Xingezhuang, Jiangjunding, Jingangkou and Changwangpu; its Jingangkou is 681 m at Laiyang with *Tsintaosaurus*.

**Ruled out.**
- *Jiangjunding and Jingangkou as the dinosaur formations.* That is the scheme of Hu and colleagues (2001), followed by Zhang and colleagues (2019) and Borinder and colleagues (2021); the provincial lithostratigraphy does not adopt the Jiangjunding and restricts the Jingangkou to the lake beds above the dinosaur horizon.
- *Hongtuya equal to Jingangkou, or below the Jiangjunding.* Zhang and colleagues (2019) and Borinder and colleagues (2021) read the Hongtuya against Hu's units; in the provincial scheme Hongtuya and Jingangkou are successive formations and the Jiangjunding beds are Hongtuya.

**Open.** *Micropachycephalosaurus*, from a "red earth cliff southwest of Laiyang Railway Station", is not placed by the provincial volume and stays at group level. Liu Mingwei and colleagues' 1994 definitions have not been read.

## What stages does the Wangshi Group take on its published readings?

*2026-10-10*

**Conclusion.** Coniacian to Maastrichtian, the same range the registry already held, now resting on published readings rather than Sloan's personal communication. Rozhdestvensky's Coniacian–Santonian and the Campanian–Maastrichtian of the group-level sources make the union; the Early Cretaceous in `period` comes from Xu and colleagues' "upper Lower Cretaceous", which names no stage. This settles the Open line of the 2026-10-04 entry. Governs `stages` and `period` on [Wangshi](../../../stratigraphy/w/wangshi.yml).

**Evidence.**
- rozhdestvensky1968a, translation, p. 48: "the most likely location of the Wangshih Formation is, as the layer with Jaxartosaurus, in the Coniacian–Santonian interval", on *Tanius* being a later form than *Bactrosaurus*.
- brusatte2013a: the unit is "Campanian-?Maastrichtian".
- chen1983a, p. 138: "the upper part of the Wangshi Group may correspond to the Belly River beds or Edmonton beds of Northern America"; buffetaut1995a, p. 685, reads this as "Campanian to Maastrichtian".
- borinder2021a, p. e1914642-3: the Jiangjunding Formation, within the group, ranges "from the upper Campanian to lower Maastrichtian".
- xu2019a, abstract: "The lower part of the Wangshi Group should be assigned to the upper Lower Cretaceous."

**Ruled out.**
- *Campanian–Maastrichtian alone.* Rozhdestvensky's "most likely" qualifies his own conclusion from the hadrosaur evidence, which is a reading, not a passing possibility.
- *A stage from Chen's lower part or Xu's lower part.* "Early Late Cretaceous" and "upper Lower Cretaceous" name parts of epochs, not stages.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is there a published source for a Coniacian to Maastrichtian age for the Wangshi?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** No. The range goes back to a personal communication from Sloan reported by Dong (1992); it is one unpublished opinion, not a dating. The registry's stages for the Wangshi, Coniacian to Maastrichtian, match that range exactly and need re-deciding on the published readings. Governs `stages` on [Wangshi](../../../stratigraphy/w/wangshi.yml).

**Evidence.**
- buffetaut1995a, p. 685: "According to Sloan (quoted by Dong, 1992), the Wangshi 'Formation' may range in age from Coniacian to Maastrichtian."
- Dong (1992, pp. 128–129), read only as a search snippet: "…suggested that it could be divided into three members from Coniacian to Maastrichtian in age (Sloan pers. comm.)". The only Sloan in Dong's bibliography is "Sloan R E (1969) Cretaceous and Paleocene terrestrial communities of western North America" (p. 182).

**Ruled out.**
- *Sloan (1969) as the source.* That paper is about North America.

**Open.** The published readings to weigh instead: Rozhdestvensky (1968), Coniacian–Santonian; Chen (1983), upper part Campanian–Maastrichtian and lower part "early Late Cretaceous"; Borinder and colleagues (2021), late Campanian–early Maastrichtian for the Jiangjunding Formation; Xu and colleagues (2019), lower part late Early Cretaceous. Dong (1992) has not been read beyond the snippet, so whether Sloan's communication also covers the three-member division is unknown.

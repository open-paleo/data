# Wangshi

## What stages does the Wangshi Group take on its published readings?

*2026-10-10*

**Conclusion.** Coniacian to Maastrichtian, the same range the registry already held, now resting on published readings rather than Sloan's personal communication. Rozhdestvensky's Coniacian–Santonian and the Campanian–Maastrichtian of the group-level sources make the union; the Early Cretaceous in `period` comes from Xu and colleagues' "upper Lower Cretaceous", which names no stage. This settles the Open line of the 2026-10-04 entry. Governs `stages` and `period` on [Wangshi](../../../stratigraphy/w/wangshi.yml).

**Evidence.**
- rozhdestvensky1968a, translation, p. 48: "the most likely location of the Wangshih Formation is, as the layer with Jaxartosaurus, in the Coniacian–Santonian interval", on *Tanius* being a later form than *Bactrosaurus*.
- brusatte2013a: the unit is "Campanian-?Maastrichtian".
- chen1983a, p. 138: "the upper part of the Wangshi Group may correspond to the Belly River beds or Edmonton beds of Northern America"; buffetaut1995a, p. 685, reads this as "Campanian to Maastrichtian".
- borinder2021a, p. e1914642-3: the Jiangjunding Formation, within the group, ranges "from the upper Campanian to lower Maastrichtian".
- xu2019a, abstract: "The lower part of the Wangshi Group should be assigned to the upper Lower Cretaceous."

**Ruled out.**
- *Campanian–Maastrichtian alone.* Rozhdestvensky's "most likely" qualifies his own conclusion from the hadrosaur evidence, which is a reading, not a passing possibility.
- *A stage from Chen's lower part or Xu's lower part.* "Early Late Cretaceous" and "upper Lower Cretaceous" name parts of epochs, not stages.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is there a published source for a Coniacian to Maastrichtian age for the Wangshi?

*2026-10-04*

**Conclusion.** No. The range goes back to a personal communication from Sloan reported by Dong (1992); it is one unpublished opinion, not a dating. The registry's stages for the Wangshi, Coniacian to Maastrichtian, match that range exactly and need re-deciding on the published readings. Governs `stages` on [Wangshi](../../../stratigraphy/w/wangshi.yml).

**Evidence.**
- buffetaut1995a, p. 685: "According to Sloan (quoted by Dong, 1992), the Wangshi 'Formation' may range in age from Coniacian to Maastrichtian."
- Dong (1992, pp. 128–129), read only as a search snippet: "…suggested that it could be divided into three members from Coniacian to Maastrichtian in age (Sloan pers. comm.)". The only Sloan in Dong's bibliography is "Sloan R E (1969) Cretaceous and Paleocene terrestrial communities of western North America" (p. 182).

**Ruled out.**
- *Sloan (1969) as the source.* That paper is about North America.

**Open.** The published readings to weigh instead: Rozhdestvensky (1968), Coniacian–Santonian; Chen (1983), upper part Campanian–Maastrichtian and lower part "early Late Cretaceous"; Borinder and colleagues (2021), late Campanian–early Maastrichtian for the Jiangjunding Formation; Xu and colleagues (2019), lower part late Early Cretaceous. Dong (1992) has not been read beyond the snippet, so whether Sloan's communication also covers the three-member division is unknown.

# Wapiti

## What does Fanti and Catuneanu's (2009) stratigraphy give for the age of the Wapiti?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** Campanian to Maastrichtian. Supersedes the Open line of the 2026-11-23 entry. Fanti and Catuneanu (2009) give the lowest of the formation's five units a "lower–middle Campanian" age, infer that unit 3 is at least in part the updip equivalent of the Bearpaw Formation, and place at the top of unit 5 the Cutbank Coal Zone, whose palynomorphs, relayed from earlier palynological work, include "several Maastrichtian taxa" of the *Mancicorpus gibbus* subzone. Their own estimate puts the lower Red Willow coals, in the upper part of unit 4, at about 70.4 Ma, Maastrichtian on the ICS chart v2024/12, though it rests on the Pipestone Creek bentonite date that Currie and colleagues (2008) relay from Eberth. Their p. 264 phrase "the Campanian Wapiti beds" sits against their own Maastrichtian palynology for the top of the formation. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Wapiti](../../../stratigraphy/w/wapiti.yml), unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- fanti2009a, p. 272: "the early progradation of the Wapiti clastic wedge during the lower–middle Campanian".
- fanti2009a, p. 282: "unit 3 is inferred to represent at least in part the updip equivalent of the Bearpaw Formation".
- fanti2009a, p. 281: "A distinctive palynological assemblage (Dawson et al. 1994b) includes several Maastrichtian taxa, e.g., Scollardia trapaformis and Mancicorpus gibbus".
- fanti2009a, pp. 280–281: "this would suggest that lower Red Willow coal seams were deposited at ~70.4 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *Campanian only, from "the Campanian Wapiti beds".* A passing label that the same paper's palynology for the Cutbank Coal Zone contradicts.

**Open.** Both radiometric dates the paper uses, for units 3 and 4, are credited to Eberth within other works ("Eberth, in Fanti 2007"; "Eberth, in Currie et al. 2008"), not to a published dating study; the stages rest on the palynology and correlations, not on them.

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Wapiti?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian and Maastrichtian. Currie and colleagues (2008) and Bell and Currie (2015) both call the formation Campanian–Maastrichtian in passing, and neither dates a Maastrichtian part of it. Their horizon, the Pipestone Creek bone bed, is Late Campanian. Currie and colleagues relay a radiometric date of 73.27 ± 0.25 Ma for an ash near the bone bed, palynomorphs from the bone bed, and palynology from other levels along the Wapiti River "consistent with a Late Campanian age for this section"; Bell and Currie place the bone bed in the uppermost part of Unit 3, and all described material from the formation in Units 3 and 4, "latest Campanian". Before the bone bed was found, Currie and colleagues record, only a hadrosaur phalanx and fragmentary bones had been reported from the formation. Governs `stages: [Campanian, Maastrichtian]` on [Wapiti](../../../stratigraphy/w/wapiti.yml).

**Evidence.**
- currie2008a, p. 4: "only a waterworn hadrosaur pedal phalanx and fragmentary bones had been reported from the Campanian–Maastrichtian Wapiti Formation".
- currie2008a, p. 7: an ash "yielded a radiometric date of 73.27 million years ago ± 250 000 years (D. Eberth, personal communication)".
- currie2008a, p. 8: samples along the Wapiti River "are consistent with a Late Campanian age for this section of the Wapiti Formation (Dennis Braman, unpublished reports on file at the Tyrrell Museum)".
- currie2008a, p. 18, Locality and Horizon: "Wapiti Formation, in Upper Campanian … Late Cretaceous, Late Campanian age".
- bell2015a, p. 1, Introduction: "The Wapiti Formation (Campanian-Maastrichtian)".
- bell2015a, p. 3, Systematic Paleontology: the ash "revealed an age of 73.27 ± 0.25 million years (D. A. Eberth in Currie et al., 2008), placing it within uppermost part of Unit 3 of the Wapiti Formation".
- bell2015a, p. 9, Discussion: "all described material from the Wapiti Formation comes from Units 3 and 4 (latest Campanian; Fanti and Catuneanu, 2009)".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** The Maastrichtian rests on passing labels, and the Late Campanian of the Pipestone Creek section on a date relayed as a personal communication and on unpublished palynology, which under the rule on personal communications in [methods](../../methods.md) are not published determinations. Fanti and Catuneanu (2009), the stratigraphy of the formation that Bell and Currie cite for its units and their ages, has not been read and would settle both.

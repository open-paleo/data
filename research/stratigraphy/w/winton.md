# Winton

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Winton?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Albian to Turonian, with the Turonian in dispute. The palynology gives late Albian to Cenomanian: Gray and colleagues (2002) assign the formation's spore-pollen assemblages to units APK6 and APK7 and query the Albian, and Hocknull and colleagues (2021) relay the same span from the zonation compiled by Monteil (2006). The detrital zircon ages are maximum depositional ages, which bound the beds rather than date them (see [methods](../../methods.md)): Tucker and colleagues (2013) put the base no older than latest Albian and the upper, fossil-rich beds near or after the Cenomanian–Turonian boundary, and Hocknull and colleagues (2021), who ask that these results be "treated cautiously", read them as 103 to 92 Ma maxima for portions of the formation. The Turonian enters on stated ages, "Cenomanian-? Turonian" for the *Australotitan* horizon in Hocknull and colleagues (2021) and "Cenomanian–lowermost Turonian" in Poropat and colleagues (2020), who cite Tucker and colleagues (2013) for it; it is disputed because Korasidis and colleagues (2026) find no Turonian assemblages in two wells. Governs `stages`, `period` and `dispute` on [Winton](../../../stratigraphy/w/winton.yml).

**Evidence.**
- gray2002a, p. 55: "Mid-Cretaceous [late Albian(?)–Cenomanian] palynofloras of the Winton Formation encompass spore-pollen assemblages attributable to palynological units APK6 and APK7".
- gray2002a, p. 55: "The Winton Formation conformably overlies the Mackunda Formation and, in some areas, is unconformably overlain by the Tertiary Glendower Formation."
- hocknull2021a, p. 15, Winton Formation Age: "The Winton Formation was assigned a Late Albian to Cenomanian chronostratigraphic age on the basis of spore-pollen zonation (Monteil, 2006)".
- hocknull2021a, p. 16, Winton Formation Age: "the results so far produced for the Winton Formation need to be treated cautiously. Nevertheless, they all indicate a probable temporal age range of between 103 to 92 million years ago (Late Albian to earliest Turonian) for the maximal depositional ages of portions of the Winton Formation".
- hocknull2021a, p. 38, Systematic Palaeontology: "Age & Horizon. Cenomanian-? Turonian, Winton Formation."
- tucker2013a, p. 767, Abstract: "sedimentation of the Winton Formation commenced no earlier than latest Albian (~103.0–100.5 Ma)" and deposition of the upper fossil-rich portion "began roughly near or after the Cenomanian–Turonian boundary (93.9 Ma)".
- poropat2020a, p. 2, Geological Setting and Associated Biota: the holotype "was preserved in the Cenomanian–lowermost Turonian (Tucker et al., 2013) 'upper' Winton Formation".
- korasidis2026a, p. 730, Abstract: the PK7 assemblages suggest "a latest Albian to Cenomanian age for the Winton Formation in these wells, with no evidence of Turonian-aged assemblages".
- tucker2013a, p. 774: "the base of the Winton Formation appears to be at or near the Albian/Cenomanian boundary, whereas the upper Winton Formation very likely continued into or past the early Turonian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Tucker and colleagues' (2013) zircon ages as the formation's age range.* Each is a maximum depositional age, the oldest the beds can be. The base "no earlier than latest Albian" and the upper beds "into or past the early Turonian" are bounds, and Hocknull and colleagues (2021) present their 103 to 92 Ma reading of the same results as maxima "for portions" of the formation. Neither paper's zircon figures add or remove a stage on their own.

**Open.** Whether the upper Winton reaches the Turonian. The stated Turonian ages trace back to Tucker and colleagues' (2013) zircon maxima, which Poropat and colleagues (2020) cite and which Hocknull and colleagues (2021) query, and Korasidis and colleagues' (2026) palynology from two wells does not show it. A direct date, or palynology from the fossil-bearing outcrop beds, would settle it.

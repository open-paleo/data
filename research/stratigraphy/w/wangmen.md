# Wangmen

## What do Meng (2000) and Mo and colleagues (2025) say about the age of the Wangmen?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Early Jurassic and Middle Jurassic, by series only, with the dispute the record states. Meng (2000) keeps an early Early Jurassic age for the Wangmen of the Shiwandashan Basin itself, and on charophytes moves the red beds of the small and medium-sized basins on its northern margin, which had been put in the formation by lithology alone, to the Early–Middle Jurassic, with the upper part possibly Middle Jurassic; he adds that the margin basins formed later than the main basin. Mo and colleagues (2025) take Meng's range for the formation as a whole, set it against "previous work" giving early Early Jurassic, and add their eusauropod, later-diverging than Early Jurassic sauropods, as further evidence for it. Neither work names a stage. Governs `period: [Early Jurassic, Middle Jurassic]`, the absence of `stages` and the `dispute` on [Wangmen](../../../stratigraphy/w/wangmen.yml); the same reading for the horizon of *Huashanosaurus* is in the 2026-10-09 entry on [*Huashanosaurus*](../../genera/H/Huashanosaurus.md).

**Evidence.**
- meng2000a, p. 19: the margin-basin red beds were dated "based solely on lithological correlation" and "assigned to the early part of the Early Jurassic and placed in the Wangmen Formation", while the fossils from them "range from Jurassic to Early Cretaceous in age" (translated).
- meng2000a, p. 20, 2 Discussion of Age: "The Wangmen Formation in the Shiwandashan Basin is of early Early Jurassic age. Judging from the composition of the charophyte assemblage collected from the sections of the small and medium-sized basins on the northern margin of the basin, its geological age should provisionally be assigned to the Early–Middle Jurassic"; "This indicates that the upper part of this formation may be of Middle Jurassic age" (translated).
- meng2000a, p. 21, 3 Conclusions: "the Shiwandashan Basin and the small and medium-sized basins on its northern margin did not form at the same time; the latter may have been deposited somewhat later" (translated).
- mo2025a, p. 909, title: "the Lower and Middle Jurassic Wangmen Formation of Ningming County, Guangxi, South China"; Abstract: "Previous work showed that the Wangmen Fm. is early Early Jurassic in age whereas the new eusauropod discovery indicates that the Wangmen Fm. is probably somewhat younger Early–Middle Jurassic in age".
- mo2025a, p. 910: "Meng (2000) considered the Wangmen Fm. as Early to Middle Jurassic in age based on the discovery of charophytes"; "above the top are calcareous mudstone, mudstone and lithic sandstone of the Lower Jurassic Baixing Fm."
- mo2025a, p. 922: the eusauropod "provides additional evidence for an Early to Middle Jurassic age for the Wangmen Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Stages.* Meng (2000) and Mo and colleagues (2025) give series only, so the unit takes no stages; as the *Huashanosaurus* entry rules out for the horizon, no work cited names a stage.

**Open.** Two points. Whether the Ningming quarry lies in the Shiwandashan Basin proper, where Meng keeps the early Early Jurassic, or in one of his margin basins, as the *Huashanosaurus* entry also leaves open. And Mo and colleagues put the Lower Jurassic Baixing Formation above the Wangmen, as Meng's map legend also gives it, which a Middle Jurassic upper Wangmen would contradict unless the overlying beds in the margin basins are not the Baixing; none of the works cited takes this up.

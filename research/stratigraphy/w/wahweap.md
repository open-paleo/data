# Wahweap

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Wahweap?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Campanian. Jinnah and colleagues (2008) date a bentonite in the middle member, about 40 m above the base, at 80.1 ± 0.3 Ma by 40Ar-39Ar, the first such age for the formation. From it they extrapolate the start of deposition to about 80.6–80.4 Ma and its end to about 77.5–76.1 Ma, and they find the lower and middle members younger than the Milk River Formation of Alberta. Beveridge and colleagues (2022) date the Star Seep bentonite, which they interpret as the same horizon, at about 81.47 Ma by U-Pb CA-ID-TIMS, 0.9 to 1.6 million years older than the earlier ages, and model the base of the formation at 82.17 Ma. Both ranges fall within the Campanian. Governs `stages: [Campanian]` on [Wahweap](../../../stratigraphy/w/wahweap.yml).

**Evidence.**
- jinnah2008a, p. 287: "the first 40Ar-39Ar age for the Wahweap Formation"; the ash horizon "yields an age of 80.1 ± 0.3 Ma".
- jinnah2008a, p. 290: the bentonite "crops out in the middle member ..., approximately 40 m above the base of the Wahweap Formation".
- jinnah2008a, p. 292: "deposition of the Wahweap Formation, in the study area likely commenced somewhere between ~80.6–80.4 Ma and ended somewhere between ~77.5 and 76.1 Ma"; a top near 76.1 Ma "would mean that the formation fits wholly within the middle Campanian".
- jinnah2008a, p. 293: "The lower and middle members of the Wahweap Formation are found to be correlative with the Pakowki and Foremost formations in Alberta, and younger than the Milk River Formation".
- beveridge2022a, p. 9, 5.1 Results: High-precision CA-ID-TIMS bentonite ages: the dated Star Seep bed "is also presumably correlative with (or within ±5 m of) bentonite CF05-B from Jinnah et al. (2009)".
- beveridge2022a, p. 14, 6.2 Upper and basal formation boundaries: "~81.47 Ma for the Star Seep bentonite", "significantly older (~0.9 to 1.6 Myr) than previous ages for what is interpreted to be the same horizon"; "The new Bayesian model age for base of the Wahweap Formation is 82.17 +1.47/-0.63 Ma".

**Ruled out.**
- *A base near 80.5 Ma.* Jinnah and colleagues (2008) extrapolate it from the 80.1 Ma bentonite and an estimated sediment accumulation rate. Beveridge and colleagues (2022) date what they interpret as the same horizon at about 81.47 Ma and model the base at 82.17 Ma. The identity of the two beds is their correlation, not a re-analysis of the CF05-B sample. The stage is Campanian on either reading.

**Open.** Nothing.

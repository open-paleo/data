# Wessex

## What carries the Wessex Formation's Berriasian?

*2026-10-09*

**Conclusion.** The British Geological Survey Lexicon, now cited, extending the Valanginian–Barremian that Robinson and Hesselbo (2004) and Raven and colleagues (2020) give. This is how the reference-work rule in [methods](../../methods.md#a-reference-work-corroborates-but-almost-never-carries-a-value-alone) lets a lexicon act: it extends a range a primary source establishes. Pittman and colleagues' (2020) "Berriasian-Barremian Wessex Formation" corroborates it but is a label with no stated basis, and is not counted on its own. The 2026-10-02 entry stands. Governs the Berriasian in `stages` on [Wessex](../../../stratigraphy/w/wessex.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bgs2020a: "Age range: Berriasian Age (KR) — Barremian Age (KB)"; "In practice the boundary is taken at the top of the last significant limestone in the top of the underlying Peveril Point Member of the Durlston Formation".
- robinson2004a: the Wessex is "a non-marine unit within the Lower Cretaceous (Valanginian–Barremian) Wealden Group".
- pittman2020a: "the Berriasian-Barremian Wessex Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Valanginian–Barremian.* It leaves out the Lexicon's range for the unit it defines, which the reference-work rule allows to extend a primary range.

**Open.** As in the 2026-10-02 entry.

## Do the plant debris beds L5, L9 and L11 have stages of their own?

*2026-10-06*

Supersedes the Barremian these three beds carried until 2026-10-06.

**Conclusion.** No. Every Barremian printed beside these beds belongs to the Wessex Formation, the base of its exposure, or a specimen, never to the bed itself, so each bed takes the formation's epoch and no stage (see [methods](../../methods.md#a-parent-units-range-repeated-for-a-child-unit-is-the-parents-statement)). The species from them keep the Barremian on statements about their own horizons. Governs `period: [Early Cretaceous]` and the absence of `stages` on L5, L9 and L11 of [Wessex](../../../stratigraphy/w/wessex.yml).

**Evidence.**
- pond2023a, p. 6, Locality and horizon: "Plant debris bed L5 lies close to the base of the exposed Wessex Formation on the Island, which is thought to be earliest Barremian in age".
- lockwood2021a, p. 849, Locality and horizon: "Wessex Formation, early Barremian, Lower Cretaceous. MIWG 6344 was excavated during 1978, from a plant debris bed (L9 of Stewart 1978)".
- naish2022a, p. 7, Locality and horizon: "The holotype was recovered from Grange Chine on the south-west coast of the Isle of Wight, from the L11 plant debris bed above the Grange Chine Sandstone of the Wessex Formation of the Wealden Supergroup. It dates to the Barremian".
- lockwood2025a, p. 3: "on the Isle of Wight only the younger section, from the late Hauterivian–Barremian boundary to the lower Aptian, is exposed".

**Ruled out.**
- *L5, Barremian.* Pond and colleagues date "the base of the exposed Wessex Formation", which L5 lies "close to"; the relative clause does not date the bed.
- *L9, Barremian.* Lockwood and colleagues' "early Barremian" stands in the horizon line beside "Wessex Formation", a sentence before the bed is named.
- *L11, Barremian.* Naish and Cau's "It dates to the Barremian" follows a sentence whose subject is the holotype.

**Open.** No source read dates a numbered plant debris bed directly. A biostratigraphic or magnetostratigraphic study tying one of these beds to a stage would settle it.

## What stages does the Wessex Formation span?

*2026-10-02*

**Conclusion.** Berriasian–Barremian. The Berriasian comes from its base on the Durlston Formation, as defined by the British Geological Survey; the Aptian belongs to the overlying [Vectis Formation](../../../stratigraphy/v/vectis.yml). Governs `stages` (Berriasian–Barremian) on Wessex.

**Evidence.**
- The British Geological Survey Lexicon (Wessex Formation) gives "Berriasian Age (KR) to Barremian Age (KB)", with the base "taken at the top of the last significant limestone in the top of the underlying Peveril Point Member of the Durlston Formation".
- kerth1988a, p. 351: the Barremian–Aptian boundary "must lie within the Vectis Formation, at least 25-30m beneath the top of the Shepherd's Chine Member", if the reversed interval correlates with Chron CM-0 rather than CM-1, "the former being the more likely"; p. 352: "the Wessex Formation (formerly the Wealden Marls)".

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian in the Wessex.* On Kerth and Hailwood's correlation the boundary lies in the Vectis, above the Wessex.

**Open.** The Barremian–Aptian placement depends on Kerth and Hailwood's preferred correlation; the alternative would put the boundary lower. That the Durlston base makes the lowest Wessex Berriasian is our reading of the Lexicon's definition.

## Do Carrano and colleagues (2012) give the Wessex a Berriasian age?

*2026-09-21*

**Conclusion.** No. Their "Wessex Formation (Berriasian)" relays Benson and colleagues (2009), and their own bibliography gives that paper's title as "Wessex Formation (Barremian)". The Berriasian in the record rests on the British Geological Survey's definition of the base (see the entry above) and on Pittman and colleagues (2020), not on this paper. Governs the sources for the Berriasian in `stages: [Berriasian, Valanginian, Hauterivian, Barremian]` on [Wessex](../../../stratigraphy/w/wessex.yml).

**Evidence.**
- carrano2012a, p. 265: "Benson *et al.* (2009) recently reported a partial pubis and femur (MIWG 6350) from the Wessex Formation (Berriasian) of the Isle of Wight"; p. 277: "A new large basal tetanuran (Dinosauria: Theropoda) from the Wessex Formation (Barremian) of the Isle of Wight".
- pittman2020a, p. 50: "Six fused sacral vertebrae from the Berriasian-Barremian Wessex Formation of the U.K."

**Ruled out.**
- *Carrano and colleagues as a source for the Berriasian.* A slip that their own bibliography contradicts (see [methods](../../methods.md#a-slip-that-its-own-document-contradicts-is-not-a-reading)).

**Open.** Nothing.

# Wallangulla Sandstone

## Is the Wallangulla Sandstone a formal member, and how is it named?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** It is an informal member of the [Griman Creek Formation](../../../stratigraphy/g/griman-creek.yml), named "Wallangulla Sandstone", never bare "Wallangulla". Governs `rank` and informal status on Wallangulla Sandstone, and `location.member` on species from it, such as [*Fostoria dhimbangunmal*](../../../genera/F/Fostoria.yml) and [*Walgettosuchus woodwardi*](../../../genera/W/Walgettosuchus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bell2019b, accepted manuscript, Geological setting: the strata "have been divided into two informal members, the Wallangulla Sandstone member and the overlying Coocoran Claystone member (Moore, 2002)".
- bell2018a, p. 6: "the GCF is divided into two informal members, the Wallangulla Sandstone member and the overlying Coocoran Claystone member (Moore, 2002)".
- bell2018a, p. 8: the fossils come from "the claystone lenses (informally referred to as the 'Finch clay facies') within the Wallangulla Sandstone".

**Ruled out.**
- *A formally defined member.* The only source given for the members is Moore (2002), a Geological Survey report on "geophysical ground surveys over Lightning Ridge opal prospects" (GS2002/442, as listed in bell2019b and bell2018a). That it is not a stratigraphic definition is an inference from its title.
- *"Wallangalla."* Agnolín and colleagues (2010) print "Wallangalla Sandstone Member of the Griman Creek Formation" (agnolín2010a, p. 271) and a bare "Sandstone Member of the Griman Creek Formation" for *Walgettosuchus* (p. 278) and *Rapator* (p. 281), while printing "Wallangulla Sandstone Member" on p. 276. Boyd (2015) copies "Wallangalla Sandstone Member … (Agnolin et al., 2010)" (boyd2015a, p. 21).

**Open.** Nothing.

## How old is the Griman Creek fauna at Lightning Ridge?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Early to middle Cenomanian at the oldest, for the Lightning Ridge exposures and their fauna. Governs `stage: [Cenomanian]` on Lightning Ridge species. It does not remove the Albian from the formation as a whole.

**Evidence.**
- bell2019b, accepted manuscript, p. 21: "preferred maximum depositional age range is early to mid-Cenomanian, with a range of 100.2–96.6 Ma", from detrital zircon in a volcanogenic claystone; abstract: "confirm an early to mid-Cenomanian age for the fauna, rather than Albian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Albian for the Lightning Ridge fauna.* Agnolín and colleagues (2010) give "Albian" for the Wallangulla taxa (agnolín2010a, p. 271), relaying Scheibner and Basden (1998). The zircon maximum age is younger.
- *Early Cenomanian as Bell and colleagues' result.* "Early Cenomanian" is Bell and colleagues' (2018) shortening; the 2019 paper gives early to mid-Cenomanian.

**Open.** Bell and colleagues (2019) restrict the date to the Lightning Ridge exposures. Other parts of the formation keep Albian fission-track and palynological ages, which is why the formation's range stays Albian–Cenomanian.

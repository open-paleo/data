# Wallangulla Sandstone

## Where in the Wallangulla Sandstone does the dated claystone lie, and how does the member meet the Coocoran Claystone?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** The bed that Bell and colleagues (2019) dated, a 5–25 cm volcanogenic claystone known locally as the "waxy band", is part of the uppermost Finch Clay layer and is known only at the Molyneux and Hard Hill localities of the Coocoran opal field. It lies above the uppermost fossil- and opal-bearing claystone and below the "steel band" marker bed. Above the steel band, the upper 6–8 m of the member are coarser and yielded no claystone lenses in their study, and the member's contact with the overlying Coocoran Claystone member is sharp and unconformable. Bell and colleagues take the band's zircons to constrain the maximum age of the fossil biota at Lightning Ridge. Governs the Cenomanian in `stages` on [Wallangulla Sandstone](../../../stratigraphy/w/wallangulla-sandstone.yml), which rests on this date (see the 2026-09-30 entry on the age of the Lightning Ridge fauna).

**Evidence.**
- bell2019b, pp. 657–658: "the uppermost Finch Clay layer includes a 5–25 cm thick layer of dark grey, bentonitic clay locally referred to as the 'waxy band'".
- bell2019b, p. 658: "The upper 6–8 m of the Wallangulla Sandstone member mark a shift to coarser lithologies"; claystone lenses there "were not observed during the course of this study".
- bell2019b, p. 659: "the contact with the overlying Coocoran Claystone member is sharp and unconformable".
- bell2019b, p. 660: the band "lies between the 'steel band' marker bed (above) and the uppermost fossil- and opal-bearing claystone (below)"; "zircons obtained from this layer are taken to constrain the maximum age of the fossil biota in and around Lightning Ridge".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** The dated band overlies the fossil-bearing claystone, so its maximum depositional age bounds the band and the beds above it; it bounds the fossils below only if the band was deposited close to the age of its youngest zircons. Bell and colleagues (2019) read the zircons as reworked airfall tuff. A date from within or below the fossil-bearing Finch Clay lenses would settle the age of the fauna itself.

## Does Agnolín and colleagues' "Sandstone Member" give *Rapator* the Wallangulla Sandstone?

*2026-10-11*

**Conclusion.** Yes, as it does for *Walgettosuchus*: the two horizon lines are identical, and the Lightning Ridge fossils come from the Wallangulla Sandstone. Governs `location.member: Wallangulla Sandstone` on [*Rapator ornitholestoides*](../../../genera/R/Rapator.yml).

**Evidence.**
- agnolín2010a, p. 281, *Rapator*, and p. 278, *Walgettosuchus*: "Sandstone Member of the Griman Creek Formation (Albian: late Early Cretaceous; Byrnes 1977), Lightning Ridge"; p. 276: "Wallangulla Sandstone Member".
- bell2018a, p. 8: the fossils come from "the claystone lenses (informally referred to as the 'Finch clay facies') within the Wallangulla Sandstone".

**Ruled out.**
- *"Sandstone Member" as a descriptive label only.* The same paper names the Wallangulla Sandstone Member, and the record that shares the line already carries it.

**Open.** Nothing.

## Is the Wallangulla Sandstone a formal member, and how is it named?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** It is an informal member of the [Griman Creek Formation](../../../stratigraphy/g/griman-creek.yml), named "Wallangulla Sandstone", never bare "Wallangulla". Governs `rank` and informal status on Wallangulla Sandstone, and `location.member` on species from it, such as [*Fostoria dhimbangunmal*](../../../genera/F/Fostoria.yml) and [*Walgettosuchus woodwardi*](../../../genera/W/Walgettosuchus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- bell2019b, accepted manuscript, Geological setting: the strata "have been divided into two informal members, the Wallangulla Sandstone member and the overlying Coocoran Claystone member (Moore, 2002)".
- bell2018a, p. 6: "the GCF is divided into two informal members, the Wallangulla Sandstone member and the overlying Coocoran Claystone member (Moore, 2002)".
- bell2018a, p. 8: the fossils come from "the claystone lenses (informally referred to as the 'Finch clay facies') within the Wallangulla Sandstone".

**Ruled out.**
- *A formally defined member.* The only source given for the members is Moore (2002), a Geological Survey report on "geophysical ground surveys over Lightning Ridge opal prospects" (GS2002/442, as listed in bell2019b and bell2018a). That it is not a stratigraphic definition is an inference from its title.
- *"Wallangalla."* Agnolín and colleagues (2010) print "Wallangalla Sandstone Member of the Griman Creek Formation" (agnolín2010a, p. 271) and a bare "Sandstone Member of the Griman Creek Formation" for *Walgettosuchus* (p. 278) and *Rapator* (p. 281), while printing "Wallangulla Sandstone Member" on p. 276. Boyd (2015) copies "Wallangalla Sandstone Member … (Agnolin et al., 2010)" (boyd2015a, p. 21).

**Open.** Nothing.

## How old is the Griman Creek fauna at Lightning Ridge?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** Early to middle Cenomanian at the oldest, for the Lightning Ridge exposures and their fauna. Governs `stage: [Cenomanian]` on Lightning Ridge species. It does not remove the Albian from the formation as a whole.

**Evidence.**
- bell2019b, accepted manuscript, p. 21: "preferred maximum depositional age range is early to mid-Cenomanian, with a range of 100.2–96.6 Ma", from detrital zircon in a volcanogenic claystone; abstract: "confirm an early to mid-Cenomanian age for the fauna, rather than Albian".

**Ruled out.**
- *Albian for the Lightning Ridge fauna.* Agnolín and colleagues (2010) give "Albian" for the Wallangulla taxa (agnolín2010a, p. 271), relaying Scheibner and Basden (1998). The zircon maximum age is younger.
- *Early Cenomanian as Bell and colleagues' result.* "Early Cenomanian" is Bell and colleagues' (2018) shortening; the 2019 paper gives early to mid-Cenomanian.

**Open.** Bell and colleagues (2019) restrict the date to the Lightning Ridge exposures. Other parts of the formation keep Albian fission-track and palynological ages, which is why the formation's range stays Albian–Cenomanian.

# West Melbury Marly Chalk

## Does any source date the West Melbury Marly Chalk Albian?

*2026-11-25*

**Conclusion.** No; the Albian and the Early Cretaceous are dropped. Hopson (2005) dates the formation Cenomanian from the erosion surface at its base, and the only Albian fauna he places in it, in the Bookham Conglomerate Bed, is derived. Barrett and colleagues (2020) date the Cambridge Greensand Member Cenomanian on its foraminifera and give its Albian fossils as reworked from the underlying Gault, and Raven and colleagues (2020) give the formation as Cenomanian. The Albian is the age of the Gault, the source of the reworked fossils, and the age of a different unit does not date this one. Governs `period: [Late Cretaceous]` and `stages: [Cenomanian]` on [West Melbury Marly Chalk](../../../stratigraphy/w/west-melbury-marly-chalk.yml). [*Syngonosaurus macrocercus*](../../../genera/S/Syngonosaurus.yml), [*Macrurosaurus semnus*](../../../genera/M/Macrurosaurus.yml) and [*Eucercosaurus tanyspondylus*](../../../genera/E/Eucercosaurus.yml) keep their Albian as reworked material.

**Evidence.**
- hopson2005a, p. 16: "Upper Cretaceous, Cenomanian. Mantelliceras mantelli to Cunningtoniceras inerme zones."
- hopson2005a, p. 17: "The derived fauna is latest Albian; the indigenous fauna is early Cenomanian."
- barrett2020a, p. 2: "the depositional age of the Cambridge Greensand Member is thought to be Cenomanian on the basis of its foraminiferan fauna (Hart, 1973)"; many of its fossils "were reworked from the underlying Albian Gault Formation".

**Ruled out.**
- *Albian, from the reworked fossils.* Their age is the Gault's.

**Open.** Nothing.

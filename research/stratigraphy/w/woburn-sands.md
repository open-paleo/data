# Woburn Sands

## What is the Woburn Sands' parent?

*2026-10-08*

**Conclusion.** The Lower Greensand. Casey treats the Woburn or Potton Sands as the Lower Greensand of Bedfordshire. Governs `parent: Lower Greensand` on [Woburn Sands](../../../stratigraphy/w/woburn-sands.yml).

**Evidence.**
- casey1961a, p. 566: north of Aylesbury "the Lower Greensand reappears in Bedfordshire, forming a thickness of 200 feet of predominantly yellow sands in the Woburn and Leighton Buzzard districts. These deposits are known as the Woburn or Potton Sands"; p. 491: "Derived fossils in the Lower Greensand Woburn ( = Potton) Sands".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** The source age of the reworked vertebrates in the Potton nodule bed. Casey lists "blocks of Neocomian sandstone, bones of Iguanodon" among the derived material (p. 566), but holds that worn bones are no proof of a Wealden origin: "Iguanodon was still living in Aptian times and worn fossils are commonplace in contemporary Lower Greensand deposits" (p. 491).

## What is the age of the Woburn Sands and its Potton fossils?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** The Woburn Sands are Aptian–Albian; the dinosaur bones in the Potton Nodule Bed are reworked from older, Neocomian beds. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on Woburn Sands and its Potton Nodule Bed, and the source-bed stages of [*Craterosaurus pottonensis*](../../../genera/C/Craterosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton1985d, p. 213: "Potton Nodule Bed in upper part of Woburn Sands (Lower Cretaceous, probably reworked from beds of Neocomian age; Casey, 1961)".
- galton2009a, p. 214: "~112 Ma (reworked into late Aptian-early Albian) for the Potton Sandstone of Bedfordshire"; p. 246: "(reworked into late Aptian-early Albian, Rawson et al., 1978)".
- The British Geological Survey Lexicon (Woburn Sands Formation) gives Aptian–Albian, in the Lower Greensand Group.

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian as the fossils' age.* Naish and Martill write "the Aptian Potton Sands have yielded Iguanodon and the stegosaur Craterosaurus pottonensis" (naish2007a, p. 496), the depositional age of the bed, not of the reworked bones.
- *Cambridge Greensand.* Galton's Fig. 15 caption calls the Potton femora "eroded, from Lower Cambridge Greensand (Potton Sandstone)" (galton2009a, p. 247), against his own text.

**Open.** The source beds. Naish and Martill (2008) suggest the material "might be reworked from the Hastings Beds Group" (naish2008a, p. 616); Ulansky (2014) gives Valanginian in a table (ulansky2014a, p. 5).

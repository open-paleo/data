# Woburn Sands

## What do Seeley (1874) and Galton (2009) say about the age of the Woburn Sands?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Aptian–Albian, unchanged from the 2026-09-30 entry. Galton (2009) dates the Potton phosphate bed at about 112 Ma, late Aptian to early Albian, after Rawson and colleagues (1978); that is when the bed formed, not the age of the vertebrates reworked into it. Casey (1961) reports an Aptian flora from the sands. Seeley (1874) thought the nodule bed probably of Purbeck–Wealden age, a reading he hedged himself and which the derived Lower Aptian ammonites in the nodules rule out. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on [Woburn Sands](../../../stratigraphy/w/woburn-sands.yml) and its Potton Nodule Bed.

**Evidence.**
- galton2009a, p. 214: "~112 Ma (reworked into late Aptian-early Albian) for the Potton Sandstone of Bedfordshire".
- galton2009a, p. 246: a femur "came from the Phosphate bed, Potton Sandstone (reworked into late Aptian-early Albian, Rawson et al., 1978) of Potton, Bedfordshire".
- galton1985d, p. 213: "Potton Nodule Bed in upper part of Woburn Sands".
- casey1961a, p. 566: at Potton, "Derived material in the nodules near the base comprised many Upper Jurassic fossils, blocks of Neocomian sandstone, bones of Iguanodon, and the Lower Aptian ammonites Prodeshayesites fissicostatus and Australiceras gigas"; "An important Aptian flora has been obtained from the Woburn Sands, described from driftwood and cones found mostly in the basal nodule-beds".
- seeley1874a, p. 690: "The bed of phosphatic nodules which there occurs in the brown sands, named by Conybeare and Fitton Woburn sands, is probably of the age of the Purbeck-Wealden formation, though there is also a possibility that it may be as new as the upper part of the Lower Greensand."

**Ruled out.**
- *Purbeck–Wealden, from Seeley (1874).* Seeley gives it as probable and allows the upper Lower Greensand as the alternative; it follows the correlation of Seeley (1869), which sets the "Potton Sands and Wicken Beds" level with the Purbeck Series. The nodules hold derived Lower Aptian ammonites (Casey, 1961), so the bed is no older than the Lower Aptian, and Galton (2009) dates it late Aptian to early Albian.

**Open.** The position of the nodule bed in the formation. Galton (1985) puts the Potton Nodule Bed "in upper part of Woburn Sands", and on that placement the formation below it may begin earlier in the Aptian than the bed's late Aptian to early Albian. Casey (1961) instead describes the Potton derived material "in the nodules near the base" and the flora from "the basal nodule-beds". The stages do not depend on which is right, since the whole Aptian is already in the range; a measured section of the Potton workings would settle it.

## Are the Potton dinosaur bones reworked from Neocomian beds?

*2026-10-10*

**Conclusion.** Not established. Supersedes the 2026-09-30 entry's statement that the bones "are reworked from older, Neocomian beds"; the formation's own Aptian–Albian age stands. The reasoning is in the [*Craterosaurus*](../../genera/C/Craterosaurus.md) entry of the same date, which gives the holotype every stage from the Berriasian to the Aptian. Governs nothing on this unit.

**Evidence.**
- casey1961a, p. 491: "Iguanodon was still living in Aptian times and worn fossils are commonplace in contemporary Lower Greensand deposits".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** The same as the open item of the 2026-09-30 entry.

## What is the Woburn Sands' parent?

*2026-10-08*

**Conclusion.** The Lower Greensand. Casey treats the Woburn or Potton Sands as the Lower Greensand of Bedfordshire. Governs `parent: Lower Greensand` on [Woburn Sands](../../../stratigraphy/w/woburn-sands.yml).

**Evidence.**
- casey1961a, p. 566: north of Aylesbury "the Lower Greensand reappears in Bedfordshire, forming a thickness of 200 feet of predominantly yellow sands in the Woburn and Leighton Buzzard districts. These deposits are known as the Woburn or Potton Sands"; p. 491: "Derived fossils in the Lower Greensand Woburn ( = Potton) Sands".

**Ruled out.** Nothing.

**Open.** The source age of the reworked vertebrates in the Potton nodule bed. Casey lists "blocks of Neocomian sandstone, bones of Iguanodon" among the derived material (p. 566), but holds that worn bones are no proof of a Wealden origin: "Iguanodon was still living in Aptian times and worn fossils are commonplace in contemporary Lower Greensand deposits" (p. 491).

## What is the age of the Woburn Sands and its Potton fossils?

*2026-09-30*

**Conclusion.** The Woburn Sands are Aptian–Albian; the dinosaur bones in the Potton Nodule Bed are reworked from older, Neocomian beds. Governs `stages: [Aptian, Albian]` on Woburn Sands and its Potton Nodule Bed, and the source-bed stages of [*Craterosaurus pottonensis*](../../../genera/C/Craterosaurus.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton1985d, p. 213: "Potton Nodule Bed in upper part of Woburn Sands (Lower Cretaceous, probably reworked from beds of Neocomian age; Casey, 1961)".
- galton2009a, p. 214: "~112 Ma (reworked into late Aptian-early Albian) for the Potton Sandstone of Bedfordshire"; p. 246: "(reworked into late Aptian-early Albian, Rawson et al., 1978)".
- The British Geological Survey Lexicon (Woburn Sands Formation) gives Aptian–Albian, in the Lower Greensand Group.

**Ruled out.**
- *Aptian as the fossils' age.* Naish and Martill write "the Aptian Potton Sands have yielded Iguanodon and the stegosaur Craterosaurus pottonensis" (naish2007a, p. 496), the depositional age of the bed, not of the reworked bones.
- *Cambridge Greensand.* Galton's Fig. 15 caption calls the Potton femora "eroded, from Lower Cambridge Greensand (Potton Sandstone)" (galton2009a, p. 247), against his own text.

**Open.** The source beds. Naish and Martill (2008) suggest the material "might be reworked from the Hastings Beds Group" (naish2008a, p. 616); Ulansky (2014) gives Valanginian in a table (ulansky2014a, p. 5).

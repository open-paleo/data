# Wabiskaw

## How did Badgley (1952) define and correlate the Wabiskaw Member?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** As a member of the Clearwater formation in central Alberta, named from the Barnsdall West Wabiskaw No. 1 well: where typically developed, a massive unit of winnowed graywackes, moderately to highly glauconitic, porous and predominantly marine. A footnote says the name had appeared earlier in a publication of the Alberta Government Petroleum and Natural Gas Conservation Board, which the paper does not identify. Badgley correlates the member lithologically with the Islay member of eastern Alberta and with the "Glauconitic sandstone" of the Leduc–Stettler areas, and the paper's table of formations sets it level with the Bluesky formation of the Peace River area to the northwest. Governs `rank: member`, `parent: Clearwater` and `lithology` on [Wabiskaw](../../../stratigraphy/w/wabiskaw.yml).

**Evidence.**
- badgley1952a, p. 6: "This member of the Clearwater, named from the Barnsdall West Wabiskaw No. 1 well, consists, where typically developed, of a massive sandstone unit. The sandstones are typically winnowed greywackes, moderately to highly glauconitic in most wells"; "It is predominantly marine".
- badgley1952a, p. 6, footnote 1: "Referred to previously in a publication of the Alberta Government Petroleum and Natural Gas Conservation Board."
- badgley1952a, p. 6: "The member is correlated lithologically by the writer with the Islay member of eastern Alberta and with the so-called "Glauconitic sandstone" of the Leduc-Stettler areas."
- badgley1952a, p. 3, Table of formations in Lower Cretaceous series: the "Wabiskaw member" in the Central Alberta column stands opposite the "Bluesky formation" in the Peace River Area column.

**Ruled out.** Nothing among the works cited here.

**Open.** The name's first use. The Conservation Board publication that Badgley (1952) says used the name earlier is not identified in the paper; finding it would date the name.

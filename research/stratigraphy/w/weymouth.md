# Weymouth

## How do the cited works define and date the Weymouth Member?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Oxfordian, on Barron and colleagues (2012): their chronostratigraphic table sets the member against the Mariae Zone and part of the Cordatum Zone, in the early Oxfordian, and in the Cleveland Basin, where it is the only member of the Oxford Clay present, they call it "entirely Upper Jurassic (Oxfordian)". Cox and colleagues (1992), who erected the member for the traditional Upper Oxford Clay, date only the formation, "of Callovian-Oxfordian age", so their paper supports the Oxfordian only through the parent. The lithology is Cox and colleagues' description of the member; Barron and colleagues add silty limestone beds near the top. Governs `stages` and `lithology` on [Weymouth](../../../stratigraphy/w/weymouth.yml).

**Evidence.**
- cox1992a, p. 343: "The Oxford Clay is a marine argillaceous deposit of Callovian-Oxfordian age"; "The three traditional subdivisions - Lower, Middle and Upper - are endorsed as lithostratigraphic units and named the Peterborough Member, Stewartby Member and Weymouth Member, respectively"; "All three are present from Dorset to Humberside; the Weymouth Member is also present in North Yorkshire where it is the sole representative of the Oxford Clay Formation".
- cox1992a, p. 344: "Weymouth Member (formerly termed Upper Oxford Clay)"; "Predominantly pale-grey, blocky, smooth-textured, calcareous mudstone, generally only slightly silty; thin, dark-grey, carbonaceous beds with striking interburrowing occur at intervals"; "Generally rather poorly fossiliferous".
- barron2012a, p. 20: "The Oxford Clay Formation is represented here only by the uppermost part (Weymouth Member), which is entirely Upper Jurassic (Oxfordian)".
- barron2012a, p. 94: the three formal members proposed by Cox and colleagues in 1992, "corresponding respectively with the Lower, Middle and Upper Oxford Clay of traditional usage".
- barron2012a, p. 95: "Upper part (Weymouth Member) mudstone, mainly pale grey, calcareous, smooth, blocky with silty limestone beds, especially near the top".
- barron2012a, p. 96, Table 6: the Weymouth Member against the "MARIAE" and "CORDATUM (part)" zones of the "EARLY/LOWER OXFORDIAN (part)"; the late Callovian Lamberti Zone falls in the Stewartby Member below.

**Ruled out.**
- *A Callovian age for the member, from Cox and colleagues (1992).* Their "Callovian-Oxfordian" is the Oxford Clay Formation's range; the paper gives the member no age of its own, and a parent's range does not date a child (see [Methods](../../methods.md)). Barron and colleagues' table puts the Callovian zones in the Peterborough and Stewartby Members.

**Open.** Nothing.

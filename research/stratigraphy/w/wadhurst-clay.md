# Wadhurst Clay

## What do the cited works say about the age of the Wadhurst Clay?

*2026-11-23*

**Conclusion.** Valanginian. Galton (2009) gives the Hastings Beds in descending order as the Upper Tunbridge Wells Sands, Grinstead Clay, Lower Tunbridge Wells Sands, Wadhurst Clay and Ashdown Beds, and dates the Wadhurst Clay of East Sussex to "~137 Ma (middle Valanginian)". The paper takes its dates from Ogg and colleagues' (2004) Cretaceous chapter of the 2004 time scale and from Walker and Geissman (2009). The stated middle Valanginian is the reading, and it is carried over (see [methods](../../methods.md), "A stated stage is carried over; a bare number is placed on the current chart"). Governs `stages: [Valanginian]` on [Wadhurst Clay](../../../stratigraphy/w/wadhurst-clay.yml).

**Evidence.**
- galton2009a, p. 213: "Hastings Beds : in descending order the Upper Tunbridge Wells Sands, Grinstead Clay, Lower Tunbridge Wells Sands, Wadhurst Clay, Ashdown Beds"; "dates from Ogg et al., 2005 ; Walker & Geissman, 2009".
- galton2009a, p. 214: "~137 Ma (middle Valanginian) for the Wadhurst Clay of East Sussex".

**Ruled out.**
- *Earliest Valanginian, from the 137 Ma figure placed on the current chart.* On the ICS chart v2024/12, 137 Ma lies only 0.05 Myr above the base of the Valanginian. The figure is an approximate age that Galton attaches to a stated substage on the 2004 time scale, so the stage governs and the number is not re-placed.

**Open.** Nothing.

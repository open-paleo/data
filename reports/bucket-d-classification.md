# Bucket D Classification

This report classifies the open Bucket D ("Disputed") triage genera by
the *kind* of dispute, so each can be handled with the right intake
discipline. Bucket B (modern + obscure) and Bucket C (iconic legacy)
have well-defined per-genus pipelines; Bucket D is heterogeneous and
needs different treatment depending on the dispute type.

The distinction between Bucket C "Manual" and Bucket D "Disputed" is
not whether the genus has uncertain phylogenetic placement — that is
fine for Bucket C (e.g. Alocodon, a tooth-only genus with placement
uncertainty but no synonymy question is in Bucket C). Bucket D is
reserved for disputes that involve **genus identity** — validity,
synonymy, nomenclature, or fundamental reassignment.

Last verified: 2026-05-08. 37 open Disputed entries; processing order
follows the section numbering below.

## Category I — Stale "Disputed" flag, promote out of D entirely (3)

Modern literature treats these as uncontroversially valid; the
disputed flag is a triage artefact, often inherited from older lit or
from peripheral non-type-species controversies. Handle exactly like
Bucket C: full-fat multi-paper intake, type-species-only.

- Protoceratops (multi-species + Bagaceratops/Bainoceratops
  relationships, but the genus itself isn't in question)
- Psittacosaurus (13+ species, type of Psittacosauridae)
- Thecodontosaurus (one of the original five Owen/Riley 1836 genera;
  modern controversy minimal)

## Category II — Active validity dispute, multi-paper neutral intake (5)

Recent literature is actively contesting validity or proposed
synonymy, with papers on both sides. Per project taxonomy policy,
capture both sides without taking one. Multi-paper extraction
similar to Bucket C.

- Nanotyrannus — vs. juvenile T. rex; 2025 Zanno & Napoli + Griffin
  et al. re-validate; second species N. lethaeus also 2025
- Sauroniops — vs. Carcharodontosaurus synonym; 2013/2020/2022/2025
  papers go back and forth
- Tianzhenosaurus — vs. Saichania synonym (Sullivan 1999, Arbour &
  Currie 2015); 2024 Pang et al. described second species treating
  it as valid
- Trigonosaurus — vs. Baurutitan synonym (Silva Junior et al. 2022);
  Fronimos 2023 disputed
- Paulodon — 2025 split of Iguanodon galvensis; methodology
  contested informally; new name has not gained consensus

## Category III — Nomen dubium, minimal stub (17)

Type material too fragmentary (single tooth, vertebra, jaw fragment,
etc.) to diagnose to species. Genus name persists in literature but
can't be reliably referred to. Single-paper minimal intake with
`status: nomen dubium` on the type species. Brief description noting
the inadequate material and (where applicable) what genus the
material may belong to. No exhaustive diagnostic_features extraction.

- Arkharavia (somphospondylan caudal vertebra; referred material
  reassigned to indet. hadrosaurid)
- Asiamericana (tooth-taxon; possibly Richardoestesia asiatica)
- Asiatosaurus (tooth + scant remains; both species nomina dubia)
- Bainoceratops (Djadokhta ceratopsian; possibly Protoceratops
  andrewsi per Makovicky & Norell 2006)
- Bienosaurus (thyreophoran; possibly Tatisaurus per Raven et al.
  2019)
- Brachypodosaurus (Lameta humerus; element identification disputed)
- Brohisaurus (Sembar Fm Pakistan; no diagnostic synapomorphies)
- Calamospondylus (Fox 1866 sacrum + iliac fragments now lost)
- Chiayusaurus (two species, both teeth-only)
- Dryptosauroides (Lameta caudal vertebrae; indistinguishable from
  other Lameta theropods)
- Embasaurus (two vertebrae, Kazakhstan)
- Euronychodon (tooth-taxon; possibly Paronychodon)
- Fabrosaurus (Elliot Fm partial jaw; better material is
  Lesothosaurus)
- Gigantosaurus (Kimmeridge Clay sauropod fragments; later name-reuse
  led to Tornieria/Janenschia tangle)
- Hypsirhophus (Garden Park Morrison stegosaurid; sometimes
  Stegosaurus synonym, sometimes nomen dubium)
- Trachodon (1856 Leidy; first-named hadrosaurid; T. mirabilis nomen
  dubium on a single tooth)
- Ultrasaurus (Korean U. tabriensis nomen dubium; not the Jensen
  "Ultrasauros" specimens)

## Category IV — Type genus retained nomenclaturally (2)

Type species is nomen dubium, but the genus name is the formal type
of a still-used higher taxon and cannot be retired. Brief stub
emphasising the nomenclatural significance and the higher-taxon
dependency.

- Ceratops (type of Ceratopsidae; nomen dubium based on horn cores +
  occipital condyle)
- Titanosaurus (type of Titanosauria; T. indicus and T. blanfordi
  both nomina dubia per Wilson & Upchurch 2003; type material lost
  and rediscovered)

## Category V — Wastebasket genera with complex split history (3)

Genus has accumulated and shed multiple species over decades; type
species itself is sometimes valid sometimes dubious; the multi-genus
spinoff story matters. Bucket-C-style treatment but emphasising the
nomen-dubium status of the type and the post-revision species
distribution (these are the long-tailed splits, not single-paper
synonymies).

- Bothriospondylus (B. suffossus nomen dubium per Mannion 2010;
  non-type species split off as Marmarospondylus, Vouivria,
  Lapparentosaurus, Narindasaurus)
- Euskelosaurus (Elliot Fm sauropodomorph; Plateosauravus often used
  for the material instead)
- Pelorosaurus (Wealden titanosauriform; type species P. conybeari is
  junior objective synonym of Cetiosaurus brevis; many former species
  now in Haestasaurus, Duriatitan, etc.)

## Category VI — Identity / reassignment pending (3)

Material originally described as one type of animal; recent work
suggests it's something else. Handling depends on whether the
reassignment is well-established or fresh.

- Ornithodesmus — well-established reassignment (originally pterosaur
  Istiodactylus; 2007/2019 work treats as dromaeosaurid/unenlagiid).
  Capture the original-name story and the modern interpretation.
- Parrosaurus — contested (Neosaurus → Parrosaurus → Hypsibema; 2018
  some authors revived Parrosaurus). Stub with both interpretations.
- Taveirosaurus — fresh reassignment (pachycephalosaurid teeth →
  nodosaurid → ornithischian indet. → 2024 eutriconodont mammal
  hypothesis). If mammal reassignment is confirmed, this is no
  longer a dinosaur and should be removed from open-paleo. Hold for
  consensus.

## Category VII — Nomenclaturally invalid name (1)

ICZN-invalid publication; brief stub pointing at where the material
actually belongs.

- Natronasaurus — self-published Ulansky 2014 e-publication is
  ICZN-invalid (no ISSN, not archived). Material is now Alcovasaurus
  (Galton & Carpenter 2016, exists in tree). Status: nomen nudum,
  cross-reference to Alcovasaurus.

## Category VIII — Close without intake (3)

Purely administrative resolutions; no genus-file intake required.

- Tianchiasaurus — misspelling of Tianchisaurus that appears
  interchangeably in Dong's 1993 description; Dong published an
  erratum in 1994 confirming Tianchisaurus is correct. The receiving
  genus already exists at `genera/T/Tianchisaurus.yml`. Add
  `Tianchiasaurus` as a synonym entry (alternative spelling,
  ICZN-invalid) and close the issue.
- Elachistosuchus — Late Triassic Arnstadt Fm basal archosauromorph
  (per μCT 2015 Sobral et al.); originally described as pseudosuchian,
  then rhynchocephalian — never a dinosaur. Close issue with
  explanation; no intake.
- Galesaurus — Cynodont therapsid (synapsid) from Karoo Basin,
  mistakenly classified as dinosaur by Owen 1859. Close issue with
  explanation; no intake.

# Dismissed Candidates

A permanent log of intake-issue genera dismissed from the dataset.
Unlike the `bucket-*` reports, this file is institutional memory:
it is not cleaned up after processing.

Two dismissal categories are covered:

1. **Possibly non-Dinosauria** — peer-reviewed publications have
   concluded the type material cannot be diagnosed to Dinosauria,
   but the placement remains potentially dinosaurian under future
   redescription.
2. **Nomina nuda** — the name has appeared in print, manuscript,
   dissertation, or museum label but was never formally published
   under the ICZN, so it has no nomenclatural standing. Many of
   these are likely dinosaurs; they are excluded from the dataset
   until a valid description appears.

Per-entry format:

```
- {Genus} (#{ticket}): short summary of why it was dismissed.
  - {Paper title} ({Author, Year}): short summary of why this paper is relevant.
```

---

## Possibly non-Dinosauria

Scope: an entry belongs here when one or more peer-reviewed
publications have concluded that the type material cannot be
diagnosed to Dinosauria, but the placement remains potentially
dinosaurian in a future redescription. Entries for genera that have
been *definitely* shown to be non-dinosaurs (i.e. unambiguously
referred to a non-dinosaurian clade) do not belong here.

Reopen criteria: new peer-reviewed research and broad community
consensus that the genus is, in fact, a dinosaur.

Note on silesaurids: many entries below are described as silesaurid
dinosauriforms. Under the prevailing sister-group hypothesis,
Silesauridae sits just outside Dinosauria. The competing
paraphyletic-silesaurid hypothesis (Müller & Garcia 2020) would
place some silesaurids inside Ornithischia, but has not reached
broad consensus.

### A

- Agnosphitys (#33): Late Triassic dinosauriform from the
  Magnesian Conglomerate fissure-fill at Cromhall, Avon, England,
  known from a holotype ilium with a referred partial skeleton
  (maxilla, humerus, astragalus). Originally placed outside
  Dinosauria by Fraser, Padian, Walkden & Davis (2002); subsequent
  phylogenetic analyses give conflicting placements as a basal
  theropod (Yates 2007), a basal sauropodomorph (Ezcurra 2010), or
  a silesaurid. No broad consensus that the genus is a dinosaur.
  - Phylogenetic relationships of *Chromogisaurus novasi* and other
    basal dinosaurs (Ezcurra, 2010): includes Agnosphitys in a
    cladistic analysis and recovers it as a basal sauropodomorph
    in Guaibasauridae, but excludes the referred material after
    Langer (2004) and Bonaparte et al. (2007) flagged it as a
    possible chimera.

- Amanasaurus (#72): Late Triassic (Carnian) silesaurid
  dinosauriform from the Santa Maria Supersequence of Rio Grande
  do Sul, Brazil, described by Müller & Garcia (2024) for two
  partial femora (single species *A. nesbitti*). Treated as a
  silesaurid in the type description.

- Asilisaurus (#158): Middle Triassic silesaurid archosaur from
  the Manda Beds of Tanzania (Anisian–early Carnian), one of the
  oldest known dinosauromorphs, known from multiple partial
  skeletons. Treated as a silesaurid since description.

### C

- Cryptoraptor (#395): Manuscript name (*Cryptoraptor lockleyi*)
  coined by Adrian Hunt in his 1994 dissertation for NMMNH P-17375,
  a fragmentary partial skeleton from the Norian Bull Canyon
  Formation of Quay County, New Mexico. The dissertation was never
  formally published, so the name has no ICZN standing. The material
  was re-evaluated by Nesbitt, Irmis & Parker (2007) and found to
  lack any clear dinosaurian synapomorphies, with the pubis and
  vertebrae showing *Shuvosaurus*-like (non-dinosaurian archosaur)
  affinities.
  - A critical re-evaluation of the Late Triassic dinosaur taxa of
    North America (Nesbitt, Irmis & Parker, 2007): redescribes
    NMMNH P-17375 as Archosauria indet., concluding the material
    cannot be assigned to Dinosauria because it lacks any clear
    synapomorphies with the clade.

### D

- Diodorus (#447): Late Triassic silesaurid dinosauromorph from
  the Timezgadiouine Formation of the Argana Basin, Morocco,
  known from a partial dentary and referred teeth (single species
  *D. scytobrachion*). Treated as a silesaurid since description.

- Dravidosaurus (#466): Coniacian (Late Cretaceous) reptile from
  southern India, originally described as a late-surviving
  stegosaur by Yadagiri & Ayyasami (1979). Chatterjee (1991)
  reinterpreted the poorly preserved fossils as plesiosaurian
  remains. No broad consensus on dinosaurian vs. plesiosaurian
  affinity; the material is too fragmentary for confident
  placement.

### E

- Eucoelophysis (#550): Late Triassic (Norian) Chinle Formation
  archosaur from New Mexico, originally described by Sullivan &
  Lucas (1999) as a coelophysid theropod. Nesbitt, Irmis & Parker
  (2007) reinterpreted it as a close relative of *Silesaurus*;
  Ezcurra (2016) recovered it as the sister-group to Dinosauria.
  Currently treated as a silesaurid.
  - A critical re-evaluation of the Late Triassic dinosaur taxa of
    North America (Nesbitt, Irmis & Parker, 2007): re-examines
    Eucoelophysis material and removes it from Coelophysidae,
    placing it close to *Silesaurus* outside Dinosauria.

### G

- Gamatavus (#601): Middle Triassic silesaurid dinosauriform from
  the Santa Maria Formation of Dilermando de Aguiar Municipality,
  Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil, known from a partial ilium (single
  species *G. antiquus*). Among the oldest South American
  silesaurids. Treated as a silesaurid since description.

- Gondwanax (#641): Triassic silesaurid dinosauriform from the
  Pinheiros-Chiniquá Sequence of Brazil, known from a partial
  skeleton (single species *G. paraisensis*). Among the oldest
  known dinosauromorphs. Treated as a silesaurid since description.

### I

- Ignotosaurus (#747): Late Triassic (Carnian) silesaurid
  dinosauriform from the Cancha de Bochas Member of the
  Ischigualasto Formation, Argentina; contemporary with the
  earliest dinosaurs (e.g. *Herrerasaurus*). Treated as a
  silesaurid since description.

- Itaguyra (#777): Late Triassic (Carnian) dinosauromorph from
  the Santa Cruz Sequence of the Santa Maria Supersequence,
  Brazil, known from two isolated pelvic bones (single species
  *I. occulta*). Allied with taxa traditionally regarded as
  silesaurids (possibly earliest ornithischian dinosaurs); the
  silesaurid-or-ornithischian question remains open.

### K

- Kwanasaurus (#873): Late Triassic silesaurid dinosauromorph
  from the Eagle Basin (Chinle Formation), Colorado, known from a
  single species *K. williamparkeri*. Treated as a silesaurid
  since description, with deeper skull and stronger herbivorous
  specialization than other silesaurids.

### L

- Lewisuchus (#912): Late Triassic (early Carnian) silesaurid
  dinosauriform from the Chañares Formation, Argentina; about 1 m
  long, exhibiting dorsal osteoderms. Treated as a silesaurid in
  recent literature; sometimes considered a member of, or sister
  to, the silesaurids that may themselves be true dinosaurs.

- Lutungutali (#959): Middle Triassic (Anisian) silesaurid
  dinosauriform from the upper Ntawere Formation of Zambia, known
  from a holotype with hip bones and tail vertebrae (single
  species *L. sitwensis*). First known silesaurid from Zambia and,
  along with Asilisaurus and Nyasasaurus, among the oldest known
  dinosauromorphs. Treated as a silesaurid since description.

### N

- Nyasasaurus (#1130): Avemetatarsalian archosaur from the
  putatively Middle Triassic Manda Formation of Tanzania, first
  described in 1956 in Charig's doctoral thesis and formally named
  by Nesbitt et al. (2013). Wikipedia describes it as a taxon that
  "may be the earliest known dinosaur" — placement on either side
  of the dinosaur–silesaurid boundary is currently unresolved.

### S

- Sacisaurus (#1366): Late Triassic (Norian) silesaurid
  dinosauriform from the Caturrita Formation of southern Brazil
  (single species *S. agudoensis*), known from dozens of bones
  including 35 right femora. Treated as a silesaurid since
  description; recovered in the paraphyletic-silesaurid analysis
  of Agnolín & Rozadilla (2017) and Müller & Garcia (2020).
  - Phylogenetic reassessment of *Pisanosaurus mertii* Casamiquela,
    1967 (Agnolín & Rozadilla, 2017): part of the body of recent
    analyses placing several silesaurid genera, including
    Sacisaurus, near the base of Ornithischia.

### T

- Taveirosaurus (#1557): Late Cretaceous tooth-taxon from the
  Argilas de Aveiro Formation of Portugal (and Laño, Spain), named
  by Telles Antunes & Sigogneau-Russell (1991), originally
  interpreted as a pachycephalosaurid. After successive
  reassignments (Homalocephalidae → Pachycephalosauridae →
  Nodosauridae → Fabrosauridae → Ornithischia indet. as nomen
  dubium per Norman 2004), a recent revision found the teeth most
  similar to the early mammal group Eutriconodonta, lacking
  ornithischian features.

### Y

- Yaverlandia (#1748): Lower Cretaceous Wessex Formation (Isle of
  Wight, England) reptile, known from two partial fossil skulls.
  Galton (1971) recognized it as a distinct genus and assigned it
  to Pachycephalosauridae; Naish (2008) suggested maniraptoran
  theropod affinities instead; Naish & Sweetman (2026) describe a
  second specimen and conclude the identity remains enigmatic,
  noting that some anatomical features are comparable to
  non-dinosaurian reptiles such as crocodyliforms.

## Nomina nuda

Scope: the proposed name has appeared in print, manuscript,
dissertation, abstract, museum label, or popular-press book but
has never been formally published in a peer-reviewed venue
meeting ICZN requirements. Most of these are very likely
dinosaurian — they are excluded from the dataset purely on
nomenclatural grounds, not on phylogenetic grounds.

Reopen criteria: the name is formally published in a peer-reviewed
venue meeting ICZN requirements. If instead the material is
redescribed under a *different* valid genus, the informal name
should be recorded as a synonym on that genus's YAML rather than
added back here.

Note on Malkani's Pakistani titanosaurs: several entries below
(Imrankhanshaheen, Marisaurus, and related Vitakri-Member taxa
proposed by M. Sadiq Malkani) were either published only in
abstracts or in venues widely treated as predatory (e.g.
Scientific Research Publishing). Wilson, Barrett & Carrano (2011)
considered the 2004–2006 batch invalid; the post-2020 names sit
outside any peer-reviewed venue and are similarly treated.

### A

- Andhrasaurus (#98): Informal armored-ornithischian name coined
  by Ulansky (2014) for GSI material (skull elements, ~30
  osteoderms, vertebrae and limb extremities) from the Kota
  Formation of India, originally assigned to Ankylosauria by Nath
  et al. (2002). Galton & Carpenter (2016) declared it a nomen
  nudum on the grounds that it did not meet ICZN requirements,
  listing the material as Thyreophora indet. Subsequently
  redescribed (sans the name) by Galton (2019) as Ankylosauria.

- Angloposeidon (#100): Informal sauropod-vertebra name for
  MIWG.7306, a single cervical from the Barremian Wessex
  Formation, Isle of Wight, England — possibly a brachiosaurid
  of ≥20 m length. The original worker (Naish) recommended the
  name be kept informal, though he later used it in his 2010
  Tetrapod Zoology Book One.

- Angustungui (#103): Informal stegosaur name for SS V16001 (axial,
  pectoral, pelvic, limb and armor elements) and paratype SS
  V16002, from the Late Jurassic Qigu Formation, Shanshan County,
  China. Discovered 2016; the name first appeared in a 2024
  bioRxiv pre-print recovering it as sister to *Loricatosaurus*.

### B

- Bayosaurus (#219): Informal abelisauroid name coined by Coria,
  Currie & Paulina Carabajal (2006) for MCF-PVPH-237 (dorsal and
  sacral vertebrae, fragmentary pelvis, partial bones) from the
  Turonian Cerro Lisandro Formation of Neuquén, Argentina. The
  name was used only as a phylogenetic-analysis placeholder; no
  formal description has followed.

- Beelemodon (#221): Informal coelurosaur-grade theropod name
  applied to two teeth from the Late Jurassic of Wyoming, USA.
  Bakker (1997) mentioned it in an Academy of Natural Sciences
  symposium; the teeth resemble *Compsognathus* but share
  features with *Protarchaeopteryx* and dromaeosaurids and lack
  unique diagnostic characters.

- Biscoveosaurus (#235): Informal basal-ornithopod name for an
  Early Maastrichtian specimen (dentaries, teeth, braincase,
  partial maxillae, forelimb elements, vertebrae, pectoral
  girdle) from the Cape Lamb Member of the Snow Hill Island
  Formation, James Ross Island, Antarctica. Possibly a secondary
  specimen of *Morrosaurus* — overlap with the *Morrosaurus*
  holotype cannot be tested.

### C

- Cinizasaurus (#353): Nomen ex dissertationae proposed by Hunt
  (1997, unpublished thesis) for NMMNH P-18400 (a tibia, vertebrae,
  and other fragments) from the Late Triassic of New Mexico,
  initially interpreted as a theropod. Intended type species "*C.
  hunti*". Nesbitt, Irmis & Parker (2007) reclassified both
  NMMNH P-18400 and P-18401 as indeterminate archosauriforms.

- Comanchesaurus (#373): Nomen ex dissertationae proposed by
  Adrian Hunt in his unpublished dissertation for NMMNH P-4569
  (a partial skeleton with vertebral centra and hindlimb bones)
  from the Norian Bull Canyon Formation of Guadalupe County, New
  Mexico. Intended type species "*C. kuesi*". Nesbitt, Irmis &
  Parker (2007) found the material to belong to a possible
  indeterminate saurischian.

- Capitalsaurus (#296): Informal large-theropod name coined by
  Peter Kranz in the 1990s for a single caudal vertebra
  discovered in 1898 during sewer construction at First & F
  Streets SE, Washington, D.C. Earlier assigned to *Creosaurus
  potens* and *Dryptosaurus potens* (both overturned). Adopted by
  the District of Columbia as official dinosaur (1998), with F
  Street renamed Capitalsaurus Court (1999) and 28 January
  observed as Capitalsaurus Day — none of which constitutes ICZN
  publication.

- Changdusaurus (#321): Informal stegosaurid name (also
  "Changtusaurus"; type species "*C. laminoplacodus*") coined by
  Zhao in 1983 for material from the Late Jurassic of China.
  Never formally described; one source reports the fossils have
  been lost.

### D

- Dachongosaurus (#401): Informal sauropod name (alternate spelling
  "Dachungosaurus"; type species "*D. yunnanensis*") coined by
  Zhao (1985) for a partial articulated skeleton from the Lower
  Lufeng Series (Sinemurian Dark Red Beds), Yunnan, China.
  Possibly a cetiosaur; the material may since have been
  redescribed under another name.

- Damalasaurus (#407): Informal sauropod name ("Damala lizard")
  for Early Jurassic material, including a rib, from the Middle
  Daye Group of Tibet. Attributed species "*D. laticostalis*" and
  "*D. magnus*" may refer to the same animal. No formal
  description.

- Duranteceratops (#479): Informal chasmosaurine-ceratopsid name
  proposed for a ceratopsid skull collected in 2012 from the
  Hell Creek Formation of South Dakota by amateur collector John
  Carter. Announced in Prehistoric Times no. 121 (Spring 2017);
  no formal description has been published.

### E

- Echizensaurus (#494): Informal ceratopsian-grade ornithischian
  name applied to Late Cretaceous material from Echizen, Japan.
  Mentioned in Lida Xing's homepage as Ceratopsia incertae
  sedis; no formal publication.

- Eoplophysis (#525): "*Eoplophysis*" was coined by Ulansky
  (2014) as a new genus for *Omosaurus vetustus* von Huene
  (later *Dacentrurus vetustus*), based on OUM J.14000 — a 60
  cm juvenile right femur from the Bathonian Cornbrash Formation
  of Oxfordshire, England. Maidment et al.'s stegosaur review
  found the holotype lacks Stegosauria synapomorphies and
  reassigned it to Dinosauria indet., making the Ulansky genus
  redundant and informal.

- Eugongbusaurus (#551): Informal neornithischian name leaked as
  a replacement-genus proposal for "*Gongbusaurus*"
  *wucaiwanensis* Dong (1989), described for IVPP 8302 (partial
  lower jaw, three caudals, partial forelimb) and IVPP 8303 (two
  sacrals, eight caudals, two complete hindlimbs) from the
  Oxfordian Shishugou Formation of Xinjiang, China. Because
  *Gongbusaurus* is a non-diagnostic tooth taxon, several
  workers have suggested moving the species to a new genus;
  none has yet done so formally.

### F

- Fendusaurus (#563): Nomen ex dissertatione proposed by Fedak
  (2006) for FGM 998GF13-II (a skull) plus five referred
  specimens from the Hettangian McCoy Brook Formation of Wasson
  Bluff, Nova Scotia. Previously classified as cf. *Ammosaurus*;
  Fedak placed it in Massospondylidae. First non-avian dinosaur
  from Nova Scotia.

- Ferropectis (#570): Nomen ex dissertatione for a Cenomanian
  Eagle Ford Group nodosaurid ankylosaur from Texas, named in
  Matt Clemens's 2018 dissertation. Intended type species "*F.
  brysorum*"; the dissertation's phylogenetic analysis recovered
  it as sister to *Borealopelta* in a clade with *Hungarosaurus*,
  *Europelta*, and *Pawpawsaurus*.

- Francoposeidon (#576): Informal turiasaurian-sauropod name
  ("French earthquake god"; type species "*F. charantensis*")
  for at least 7 individuals' worth of material — braincase,
  skull bones, teeth, cervical/dorsal/caudal vertebrae,
  chevrons, pelvic girdle, all limb bones — from the
  Hauterivian Angeac-Charente bonebed of France. Femur length
  ~2.35 m suggests one of the largest European sauropods. No
  formal description has been published.

- Futabasaurus (#590): Informal theropod name converted by David
  Lambert (1990) from Hasegawa et al.'s (1987) Japanese nickname
  "Futaba-ryū" for a ~56 mm-wide partial shin bone from the
  Coniacian Ashizawa Formation (Futaba Group), Japan;
  considered indeterminate tyrannosaurid. The name *Futabasaurus*
  has since been used for a plesiosaur genus, so any future
  formal description will require a different name.

### G

- Gadolosaurus (#593): Informal hadrosauroid name (PIN
  3458/5) that originated as a 1979 Japanese-language
  mistranslation by Tsunemasa Saito of the Russian word
  *gadrosavr* (hadrosaur). Applied to a juvenile from the
  Bayan Shireh Formation of Baishin Tsav, Mongolia; never
  intended by the Soviet workers as a generic name. Averianov,
  Lopatin & Tsogtbaatar (2022) preliminarily described the
  specimen as a possible juvenile of a novel taxon closer to
  *Gobihadros*.

### H

- Hanwulosaurus (#673): Informal ankylosaur name (~9 m long; "long
  for an ankylosaur") for material from the Late Cretaceous of
  Inner Mongolia, China — a nearly complete skeleton including a
  complete skull, vertebrae, ribs, scapula, ulna, femora, lower-leg
  bones, and armor. Surfaced in news reports in 2001; Zhao Xijin
  has suggested it may warrant its own subgroup within Ankylosauria,
  but no formal description has followed.

- Heilongjiangosaurus (#682): Informal hadrosaurid name (possibly
  lambeosaurine; intended type species "*H. jiayinensis*") coined
  in 2001 by Li & Jin in a faunal list from the Maastrichtian of
  Heilongjiang, China. May be the same animal as *Charonosaurus*,
  and possibly connected to the nomen nudum "*Mandschurosaurus*"
  jiainensis (1983).

- Hironosaurus (#700): Informal hadrosaurid name for fragmentary
  teeth and a vertebra from the Late Cretaceous of Hirono,
  Fukushima, Japan. Mentioned by Hisa (1988) and brought to a
  wider audience by Lambert (1990); identified by Dong, Hasegawa
  & Azuma as Hadrosauridae indet., lacking characters for
  precise identification.

- Hisanohamasaurus (#701): Informal sauropod name for teeth from
  Iwaki, Fukushima, Japan, first appearing in Lambert's 1990
  general-audience book. Initially identified as a diplodocid,
  later as a nemegtosaurid similar to *Nemegtosaurus*.

### I

- Imrankhanshaheen (#757): Wikipedia redirects this name to
  "Imrankhanhero", an informal titanosaurian name proposed by
  Malkani (2023) in Scientific Research Publishing (a venue
  widely treated as predatory) for a humerus, femur, fibulae,
  tibia, metatarsal, and possibly nearby caudal vertebrae from
  the Maastrichtian Vitakri Formation of Pakistan. The intended
  type species is "*Imrankhanhero zilefatmi*".

### J

- Jindipelta (#802): Informal ankylosaur name (intended type
  species "*J. zouyunensis*") for a partial skeleton from the
  Cenomanian Zhumapu Formation of China. First announced in the
  2019 SVP abstract book (Lei et al., "in press"); no formal
  description has followed.

- Julieraptor (#815): Informal nickname for a dromaeosaurid
  fossil from the Judith River Formation of Montana (discovered
  2002); parts of the same skeleton were illegally excavated in
  2006 as "Sid Vicious", and the poacher served jail time. Also
  nicknamed "Kleptoraptor" by Bob Bakker. The specimen consists
  of an almost complete skeleton with well-preserved braincase
  but missing most of the skull, most caudal vertebrae, part of
  the femur, and most of the spinal/cervical vertebrae.

### K

- Kagasaurus (#821): Informal coelurosaur-grade theropod name
  coined by Hisa (1988) for two teeth from the Early Cretaceous
  of Kaga, Japan. Possibly a non-maniraptoriform coelurosaur or
  a dromaeosaurid; no formal description.

- Kunmingosaurus (#869): Informal primitive-sauropod name (type
  species "*K. wudingensis*") invalidly coined by Zhao (1985)
  for pelvic, hind-limb, and vertebral material from the
  Fengjiahe Formation (Lower Lufeng Series), Yunnan, China,
  found in 1954.

### L

- Lancanjiangosaurus (#887): Informal sauropod name (alternate
  spelling "Lanchanjiangosaurus"; type species "*L. cachuensis*";
  for the Lancangjiang River of China) coined by Zhou (1983) for
  Middle Jurassic material from the Dapuka Group of Tibet. No
  formal description.

### M

- Magulodon (#975): Informal tooth-taxon (type species "*M.
  muirkirkensis*"; possible basal ornithopod or basal
  ceratopsian) coined by Kranz (1996) for a single tooth from
  the Aptian–Albian Arundel Formation of Maryland, USA. A later
  paper cited the type specimen as indeterminate but avoided
  using the name to prevent taxonomic clutter.

- Maltaceratops (#989): Informal centrosaurine ceratopsid name
  (proposed type species "*M. hammondorum*"; previously
  nicknamed "Malta new taxon") for a possible skull from the
  Campanian Judith River Formation of Montana, USA. No formal
  description has been published.

- Marisaurus (#1001): Informal titanosaurian name ("Mari lizard",
  for the Mari tribe of Pakistan; type species "*M. jeffi*")
  described by Malkani (2004) on tail vertebrae from the
  Maastrichtian Vitakri Member of the Pab Formation,
  Balochistan, Pakistan. Considered invalid by Wilson, Barrett
  & Carrano (2011).

- Megacervixosaurus (#1011): Informal titanosaurian sauropod
  name ("big neck lizard"; type species "*M. tibetensis*") coined
  by Zhao Xijin (1983) for material from the Late Cretaceous
  Zonggo Formation of Tibet. Never formally described.

- Microcephale (#1028): Informal pachycephalosaurid name (also
  "*Mycocephale*"; "tiny head") for very small (<5 cm) skull
  caps from the Campanian Dinosaur Park Formation of Alberta,
  Canada — the "North American dwarf species". First mentioned
  by Paul Sereno (1997) in a list of pachycephalosaurids; no
  species epithet was given.

- Microdontosaurus (#1032): Informal sauropod name (intended
  type species "*M. dayensis*") coined by Zhao (1985) for
  Middle Jurassic material from the Dapuka Group of Xinjiang,
  China. Not used since; the material may have been
  redescribed under another name.

- Mifunesaurus (#1038): Informal tetanuran-theropod name
  coined by Hisa (1985, no species epithet) for a tibia,
  phalanx, metatarsus, and tooth (YNUGI 10003 / MDM 341)
  discovered in 1979 in the Cenomanian Kabu Formation of
  Japan. Tooth morphology suggests megalosauroid or carnosaur
  affinity.

### N

- Ngexisaurus (#1104): Informal avetheropod-grade theropod name
  (type species "*N. dapukaensis*") coined by Zhao (1983) for
  material from the Middle Jurassic Dapuka Group of Tibet, China.
  Zhao (1985) introduced an alternate name "*Megalosaurus*"
  *dapukaensis* for related material.

- Nurosaurus (#1128): Informal sauropod name (proposed binomial
  "*N. qaganensis*") presented as soon-to-be-described by
  Zhiming Dong (1992) for a partial skeleton from the Early
  Cretaceous Qagannur Formation of Inner Mongolia. Notable as
  the first specimen on which a stress fracture of the foot
  phalanx was identified in a sauropod.

### O

- Orcomimus (#1147): Informal ornithomimid name coined by
  Michael Triebold (1997) for a pelvis and hindlimb from the
  Late Cretaceous Hell Creek Formation of South Dakota; never
  formally described. May be referable to one of the
  ornithomimosaur species currently known from the Hell Creek.

- Oshanosaurus (#1165): Informal sauropod name ("Oshan lizard";
  intended type species "*O. youngi*") coined by Zhao (1985)
  for material from the Early Jurassic Lower Lufeng Series of
  Yunnan, China, discovered by Zhao in 1971 at Dianchung,
  Eshan County. Molina-Perez & Larramendi (2019) suggested
  without elaboration that it belongs to the theropod
  *Eshanosaurus*.

- Otogosaurus (#1171): Informally named sauropod (proposed
  type species "*O. sarulai*") from Inner Mongolia, China,
  known from partial postcrania (including a 2.2 m tibia) and
  several footprints. Despite occasional citation to Zhao (2004)
  or Zhao & Tan (2004), scholars have not located such a source.

### P

- Pakisaurus (#1188): Informal titanosaurian name ("Pakistan
  lizard"; proposed species "*P. balochistani*") named by Malkani
  (2006) for isolated tail vertebrae from the Maastrichtian
  Vitakri Member of the Pab Formation, Balochistan. A 2023 femur
  from the Lameta Formation of India was also assigned to it.
  Considered invalid by Wilson, Barrett & Carrano (2011).

### Q

- Qaikshaheen (#1310): Informal titanosaurian name (intended
  type species "*Q. masoomniazi*") proposed by Malkani (2023)
  in Scientific Research Publishing for fragmentary axial,
  pectoral, and pelvic material from the Maastrichtian Vitakri
  Formation of Pakistan.

### R

- Ronaldoraptor (#1356): Informal oviraptorid name (also "Mitrata
  Oviraptorid"; listed as "*Oviraptor* sp.") first used by Luis
  Rey in his 2003 book *A Field Guide to Dinosaurs* as the
  caption to an illustration. Material is undescribed; may be
  closely related to *Citipati osmolskae*.

### S

- Sabinosaurus (#1365): Informal hadrosaur name (also
  "Sabinosaurio") for PASAC-1, a partial saurolophine skeleton
  from the Sabinas Basin of Mexico discovered in 2001. Initially
  described as *Kritosaurus* sp. by Kirkland et al. (2006), and
  Prieto-Márquez (2014) considered it indeterminate saurolophine
  — the largest known well-documented North American
  saurolophine (~11 m).

- Saldamosaurus (#1369): Informal stegosaurid name (type species
  "*S. tuvensis*") proposed by Ulansky (2014) for a complete
  braincase from the Early Cretaceous Saldam Formation of
  Siberia, Russia. Galton & Carpenter (2016) found the name did
  not meet ICZN requirements, declaring it nomen nudum.

- Saltillomimus (#1371): Nomen ex dissertatione for an
  ornithomimid theropod from the Campanian Cerro del Pueblo
  Formation of Mexico, named by Aguillón Martinez in her 2010
  thesis (intended type species "*S. rapidus*"). Holotype
  SEPCP 16/237 (partial tail, most of a hindlimb, forelimb
  bones); a skeletal reconstruction is on display at the Museo
  del Desierto.

- Sidormimus (#1448): Informal noasaurid name (also "Gadoufaoua
  noasaurid", "unnamed Niger noasaurid", "digging raptor")
  for an articulated specimen from the Elrhaz Formation of
  Niger, discovered in 2000 by Chris Sidor. First named by Lyon
  on the Project Exploration website (also labelled
  "Dogosaurus" on the National Geographic site).

- Skaladromeus (#1474): Nomen ex dissertatione (also
  "Kaiparowits ornithopod"; intended type species "*S.
  goldenii*") named in Clint Boyd's 2012 thesis for an
  ornithopod from the Kaiparowits Formation.

- Stegotitanus (#1499): Informal replacement-genus name proposed
  by Gregory S. Paul in *The Princeton Field Guide to Dinosaurs*
  (3rd ed., 2024) for *Stegosaurus ungulatus* — yielding the
  new combination "*Stegotitanus*" *ungulatus*. Material from
  the Kimmeridgian upper Morrison Formation, Wyoming, USA;
  ~7 m, ~4.2 t. Field-guide proposal does not constitute a
  formal generic erection.

- Sugiyamasaurus (#1521): Informal titanosauriform name
  ("Sugiyama lizard") for spatulate teeth from the Early
  Cretaceous of Japan (near Katsuyama City); first printed by
  David Lambert (1990) in the *Dinosaur Data Book*. Possibly
  referable to *Fukuititan*, with which the teeth co-occur.

- Sulaimanisaurus (#1522): Informal titanosaurian name
  ("Sulaiman lizard"; also "Sulaimansaurus"; proposed species
  "*S. gingerichi*") described by Malkani (2006) for seven
  Maastrichtian tail vertebrae from the Vitakri Member of the
  Pab Formation, Balochistan. Placed in "Pakisauridae" alongside
  *Pakisaurus* and *Khetranisaurus*; considered invalid by
  Wilson, Barrett & Carrano (2011).

### T

- Tonouchisaurus (#1613): Informal coelurosaurian name ("Tonouchi
  lizard"; suggested type species "*T. mongoliensis*") for limb
  material (less than 0.91 m long) from the Early Cretaceous of
  Mongolia; first mentioned in a Japanese news article.
  Barsbold initially read the manus as didactyl (a tyrannosauroid
  trait) but later noted it is tridactyl with a sub-arctometatarsalian
  pes.

### V

- Vectispinus (#1681): Wikipedia redirects this name to "White
  Rock spinosaurid" — a giant spinosaurid (>10 m, vertebrae
  comparable to *Spinosaurus*) described in 2022 from the
  Vectis Formation of the Isle of Wight, England. The describers
  considered the name "Vectispinus" but refrained from naming
  the taxon because the remains are too fragmentary.

- Vitakridrinda (#1690): Informal abelisaurid name (intended
  type species "*V. sulaimani*") first used in a Malkani (2004)
  abstract for two thigh bones and a tooth from the Maastrichtian
  Vitakri Member of the Pab Formation, Balochistan. The originally
  referred snout has since been reclassified as the mesoeucrocodylian
  *Induszalim*, and the braincase as *Gspsaurus*.

- Vitakrisaurus (#1691): Informal noasaurid name (intended type
  species "*V. saraiki*"; honoring the Saraiki people of southern
  Pakistan) for MSM-303-2, a right pes from the Maastrichtian
  Vitakri Formation of Pakistan. Brief description and lack of
  comparative material make formal classification difficult.

### X

- Xinghesaurus (#1725): Informal sauropod name (possibly
  titanosauriform; no species epithet) coined by Hasegawa et al.
  (2009) in the *Miracle of Deserts* expo guidebook. Mount-based
  estimates: ~15.4 m, ~6 t.
